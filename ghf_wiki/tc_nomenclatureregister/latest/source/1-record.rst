Event Nomenclature Register — 1988 Great House Farm removal, seizure and occupation
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. _1988-removal:

Event identity
--------------

**Working event:** late-November 1988 entry by BP enforcement agents/bailiffs, removal of the occupying family from Great House Farm, subsequent injunction proceedings, and the later demolition of the farmhouse in early December 1988.

**Identity-control note:** the family’s interpretation that one or more people in the audiovisual footage were SAS personnel acting undercover as BP bailiffs is kept as a separate, unconfirmed allegation at `Allegation: SAS or undercover personnel acted as BP bailiffs in 1988 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/allegation-sas-undercover-personnel-bp-bailiffs-1988-unverified/>`__. The supporting evidence target is `GHF-E181 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e181-identity-role-persons-1988-eviction-footage-pending/>`__, which remains pending verification. Do not use the event label or the existence of footage as proof of a person’s identity or role.

.. _1988-april-stand-off:

Separate April 1988 failed enforcement attempt
----------------------------------------------

The 29 April 1988 chainsaw stand-off is a distinct event from the late-November removal. Contemporary press evidence describes bailiffs and police attending, the drive being blocked and the enforcement attempt ending without the family being removed. `GHF-E090-N07 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e090-n07-chainsaw-farmer-vows-to-fight-on/>`__ is the evidential route.

Different nomenclatures for the same event
------------------------------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Nomenclature
     - How it should be treated
   * - **“Eviction” / “evicted”**
     - Contemporary press and legal/enforcement shorthand. Link through this page rather than treating “eviction” as the only description.
   * - **“Possession enforcement”**
     - Legal/enforcement description of the process by which possession was asserted following the court proceedings.
   * - **“Forced removal”**
     - Descriptive terminology focusing on the physical removal of the occupants.
   * - **“Taking control” / “took control”**
     - Property-control terminology useful when describing physical control of the premises without prejudging the ultimate title question.
   * - **“Seizure”**
     - Family narrative describing what happened from the family’s perspective if their two-parcel ownership case is correct.
   * - **“Occupation” / “occupied”**
     - Family terminology for the subsequent physical control of the house by BP or its agents.
   * - **“Illegal occupation” / “unlawful occupation”**
     - Family’s legal position / issue for counsel, conditional on the proposition that the House Plot remained outside BP’s title and that the possession/enforcement process therefore operated against land belonging to the family.
   * - **“Regaining possession”**
     - BP/press-style wording that assumes or adopts a prior legal entitlement to possession. This is not a neutral substitute for the event description.
   * - **“SAS-style operation”**
     - Contemporary programme wording describing the manner of the operation. It does not establish that the personnel were SAS. :sup:`🕒`
   * - **“Siege” / “stand-off”**
     - Useful where referring to the separate April 1988 failed enforcement attempt; do not collapse that event into the November removal.

Ownership contingency
---------------------

The House Plot (Parcel A) is treated here as distinct from the eastern agricultural land (Parcel B): Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 statement records the 1877 sale of the house to Daniel Thomas (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__), and the 1939 plan shows Parcel B only [`EV-0023 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0023-1939-wgr-john-williams-tenancy-plan/>`__]. The later title and possession chain may have treated those parcels as one. Accordingly, terms such as **“seized”**, **“occupied”**, **“illegally occupied”** or **“unlawfully occupied”** are recorded here as the family’s narrative/legal position, not as independently established conclusions. The underlying title and enforcement evidence must determine the legal description.
