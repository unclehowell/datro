Current high-level working theory: Cedwyn Estate and repeated parcel capture
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The family’s hypothesis is that Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr may have belonged to a wider historic Cedwyn/Cydfin estate connected with Llandough and Leckwith, and that successive land transactions, possession events and registrations may have fragmented or obscured older parcel interests. The proposed forty-parcel extent and any Wales-wide colonial/imperial mechanism remain unproved hypotheses. See the `Cedwyn Estate Working Theory <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/top-level-understanding-williams-parcel-a-title-sabotaged-severed-and-buried-while-a-synthetic-mimic-estate-was-forged/>`__ for the supporting leads, competing explanations and decisive document tests.

**Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr), beside St… .. read more**

`Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr) <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/great-house-farm/>`__, beside `St Dochdwy’s Church <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/archaeology/>`__ at Llandough-juxta-Penarth, was the Williams family home. Mary (Nancy) Williams’s witness statement c.1974 (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__) traces its occupation to at least 1667. The central dispute is whether the farmhouse plot remained a distinct parcel and was later improperly folded into another title.

Original Parcel A and Parcel B — then three later Parcel-A strands
------------------------------------------------------------------

**The working reconstruction is that the 1877… .. read more**

The working reconstruction is that the 1877 Bute–Thomas transaction marks the separation between `Parcel A <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/#parcel-a>`__—the farmhouse, garden, buildings and associated land—and `Parcel B <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/#parcel-b>`__, the eastern agricultural fields. The family’s working title theory treats the end of quarrying and last rent in 1928 as the point at which Williams became the “virtual freeholder” of Parcel A; the original title documents remain unresolved. The `1877 Bute–Thomas agreement <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e115-1877-bute-thomas-agreement/>`__, the `1938 Bute–WGR conveyance <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e121-1938-bute-wgr-conveyance-and-plan/>`__ and the `1939 tenancy and plan <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/dbdt123-7-1939-wgr-john-williams-tenancy-agreement-plan/>`__ are key records for testing that reconstruction [PENDING: `EV-0019 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0019-bute-wgr-conveyance-plan-1938/>`__].

Three proposed Parcel-A successor routes: Forest, BP centre and Council/VG41
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

**BP’s documented title chain concerned Parcel… .. read more**

BP’s documented title chain concerned Parcel B. The family now tests a three-strand Parcel-A model: a northern woodland route associated with a 1961 deed and later Forest of Cardiff title history; a south-eastern route associated with VG41’s 1970 village-green registration; and the occupied central house-and-garden route associated with BP’s 1982–83 registration and the 1987 amalgamation with Parcel B. In this reconstruction, the BP synthetic Parcel A that this Wiki identifies concerns the middle component, not the entirety of the historic Parcel A. The 1961 deed and 1970 register remain separate records whose mapped scope and title effect must be checked; neither by itself proves a freehold transfer or coordinated action. The family identifies a sequence that must be kept distinct: the 1949–1953 unwritten agricultural tenancy (which the Court of Appeal later treated as a protected periodic tenancy, while the family disputes any extension to Parcel A); a separate unsuccessful attempt to make Frederick a tenant in 1954; and later tenancy offers and proceedings in 1959, 1962, 1965 and 1974. This remains subject to source-by-source analysis and is not a finding of deliberate wrongdoing. The allegation of a synthetic title must be tested against the original instruments, plans, court records and registration files—not treated as a court finding. See the `permissive-occupation claim <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/claim-repeated-attempts-to-make-mary-williams-occupancy-of-parcel-a-appear-permissive>`__, the `Parcel A/B title claim <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/claim-parcel-a-parcel-b-distinct-histories-1987-amalgamation>`__ and the `Land Registry title history <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/land-registry-titles/>`__.

Parcel map: Bute → A/B → three proposed Parcel-A successor routes
-----------------------------------------------------------------

**How to read it:** follow the lines vertically. Each elbow shows only a year and parcel label; select it to expand the evidence, source links and limits. The faded grey line is the historic Williams Parcel-A estate as claimed in the working reconstruction. The proposed later routes are Forest of Cardiff (upper/north), BP’s house-and-garden registration (middle), and the Council/VG41 green-record route (lower/south-east), alongside Parcel B. This is a hypothesis that the old Parcel A was later represented by three distinct successor or administrative strands; only BP’s middle registration is described here as the synthetic registration (this Wiki’s case, not a court finding). The exact polygons and title effects remain to be proved against the original plans.

**BUTE · ROOT**

**Starting point: Bute Estate.** The tube-map model starts with a single Bute freeholder line before the reported 1877 division. The precise instruments, conveyed rights and boundaries must be read from the original deeds and plans; the graphic records the present working model, not an established legal finding.

See `Bute Estate <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bute-estate/>`__ and the `1877 Agreement <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1877-agreement/>`__.

**1877 · A**

**Reported split in Mary (Nancy) Williams’s account.** Mary (Nancy) Williams’s witness statement c.1974 (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__) says that a Bute-to-Daniel Thomas & Son arrangement concerned the house parcel for quarrying, with a route by which the freehold was to pass to Williams when quarrying ended. The original 1877 Great House Farm instrument has not been located; the related EV-0006 entry remains pending. A separate 1877 99-year lease identified for a Clyde Street house plot in Roath is not Great House Farm title evidence and is excluded.

Sources: `EV-0006 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0006-bute-thomas-agreement-1877/>`__ · `Daniel Thomas and Alfred Thomas <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/daniel-thomas/>`__ · `Mary (Nancy) Williams’s statement <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/mary-williams-statement/>`__.

**1877 · B**

**Parcel B track.** On the working model, the eastern agricultural fields remain on the Bute side of the division until the 1938 transfer to Western Ground Rents. This is the separate-fields route, not evidence that the same instrument conveyed the whole farmhouse plot.

Compare the `1938 Bute–WGR conveyance and plan (EV-0019) <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0019-bute-wgr-conveyance-plan-1938/>`__ and `The Two Parcels <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__.

**1916 · A+B**

**A+B in one reported agricultural tenancy.** In February 1916 the Marquess of Bute granted John Williams a yearly agricultural tenancy described in the judicial history as concerning the “whole farm”. On Mary (Nancy) Williams’s witness statement c.1974 (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__), the house parcel had already been separated by the reported 1877 arrangement; that makes the extent of the 1916 tenancy a central scope conflict, not something this line resolves. The 1939 plan later depicts Parcel B only.

**Family’s “virtual freeholder” position.** Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, recalled that quarrying ended and the last rent to Alfred Thomas was paid in 1928. The family treats this as the point at which its Parcel-A interest became the Williams family’s own freehold/beneficial estate. The receipt and original 1877 instrument are not presently held, so the exact legal effect remains a title proposition to prove rather than a completed registration finding.
