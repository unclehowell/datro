**The 1877 agreement is the family’s name for… … read more**

The **1877 agreement** is the family’s name for the arrangement under which the `Bute Estate <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bute-estate/>`__ sold the house parcel of `Great House Farm <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/great-house-farm/>`__ to `Daniel Thomas <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/daniel-thomas/>`__ for quarrying, with the freehold to pass to the `Williams family <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/williams-buckler-family/>`__ when quarrying ended [PENDING: `EV-0006 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0006-bute-thomas-agreement-1877/>`__]. It is the foundation of the family’s title claim. No copy was ever put before a court: the family’s copy was taken from the farmhouse in 1950–52, and the library copy went missing in 1984, the year BP served its writ. Its terms are known from `Mary (Nancy) Williams <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/mary-williams-statement/>`__, who saw it. :sup:`🕒`

The 1928 title effect and later Parcel-A/Parcel-B conflation
------------------------------------------------------------

**Family title position. The 1877 arrangement… … read more**

**Family title position.** The 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 when quarrying ceased and rent to Alfred Thomas ended. On the family’s established case, that event carried the whole Parcel-A house/quarrying estate to the Williams family—not merely the farmhouse and garden, but also the forest north of the house and the ground south-east of the house/garden that later became village green VG41. WGR’s later rights related to the distinct Parcel-B eastern fields; later use of “whole farm” did not itself enlarge those rights into Parcel A.

\* Yellow text on olive-green highlight = presumption used where primary evidence is missing.

PRESUMPTION — The reported 1877 Bute–Thomas arrangement made the end of quarrying and rent in 1928 the trigger for the Williams family to take the freehold of the whole Parcel-A house/quarrying estate, distinct from Parcel B, the eastern land retained by Bute.

This is the working premise for the chronology while the original 1877 instrument remains unavailable. The separate 15 May 1924 drainage agreement and its missing plan are catalogued at `GHF-E119 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e119-15-may-1924-bute-corbett-drain-agreement-and-plan/>`__; they are not treated as the sale contract.

**Chronology of the permissive-occupation… … read more**

**Chronology of the permissive-occupation narrative.** The family’s case is that the 1949 step was the first later attempt to make Mary’s occupation appear permissive. It preceded the reported loss of the title deed in 1950–52 and failed. The 1955 order’s “whole farm” wording then blurred Parcel B’s agricultural tenancy with the family’s Parcel A, and enforcement on 4 July 1955 seized the north forest and south-east green along with Parcel B. Mary remained in the farmhouse/buildings/garden group because she resisted removal after a leg amputation, with seven children to care for, and was not physically able to defend Parcel A’s entire perimeter.

**Do not misread Mary’s practical defence.… … read more**

**Do not misread Mary’s practical defence.** Later accounts that Mary fought for the “house and garden” describe the part she could physically hold onto after the forest and green were seized. They do not establish that the title claim was limited to the farmhouse and garden. Read with the 1877 arrangement, Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, witness statement c.1974 (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__) supports an underlying claim covering the whole Parcel-A estate, while her litigation posture and the coercive circumstances made the defended area appear narrower. The family’s alternative limitation case is that twelve years from 1928 would have matured by 1940 if the legally relevant requirements were satisfied; the 1955 recharacterisation instead made the clock appear to start in 1955. The underlying-title and limitation arguments must remain distinct.

   “I remember that the agreement provided that the rent should progressively decrease as land was taken for quarrying. It was also provided that when the quarrying ceased the freehold should belong to my grandfather.”

**Evidence description:** Bute sold the freehold of the farmhouse, buildings and about 10 acres to Daniel Thomas and kept about 9 acres “up on the top”. Mary’s grandfather took tenancies from both. The Thomas tenancy’s rent fell as land was quarried, and the freehold was to pass to her grandfather when quarrying ceased. It was kept in the blanket box at the farmhouse.

**Evidence:** `Mary (Nancy) Williams’s statement (extract) <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/mary-williams-statement/#:~:text=1877%3A%20The%20terms%20of%20the%20Thomas%20tenancy>`__

**1928: Quarrying ends and the last rent is paid**

Estate record **Receipt for the last rent to Alfred Thomas, 1928; rent receipts or rent book, 1916–39**

Missing: the 1928 receipt was seen by Mary; to be traced

Catalogue: GHF-E012-06

**Evidence description:** the last quarry machinery left in 1928 and the last rent, about £4 a year, was paid by John Williams to `Alfred Thomas <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/daniel-thomas/>`__. “I saw the receipt for this last payment of rent. After this my father regarded the farm as his own.” Rent continued to be paid on the fields only. HM Land Registry holds a record of 15 May 1924 (a Bute–Corbett agreement about a drain) on the WA231076 file [PENDING: `EV-0015 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0015-bute-corbett-drain-agreement-1924/>`__]. Whether it is connected to this step is one of the questions the family is pressing HM Land Registry to answer (`Land Registry titles <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/land-registry-titles/#deeds-before-registration>`__; analysis in the `Legal Review, section 2 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/scrutiny-and-accountability/legal-review-fraud-land-human-rights/#land>`__).

**Evidence:** `Mary (Nancy) Williams’s statement (extract) <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/mary-williams-statement/#:~:text=1928%3A%20The%20last%20rent%20to%20Alfred%20Thomas>`__

Parcel AThe end of rent to the Thomases for the house parcel.

**Research note.** On the 1877 terms as Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 statement records them (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__), this is when John Williams became freeholder of Parcel A under the 1877 terms, and the house parcel left any tenancy. In the alternative, twelve years’ rent-free occupation from 1928 gave the family title by about 1940.

**1950 – 1952: Papers taken from the farmhouse**

**Evidence description:** a lodger placed at the farmhouse in June 1950 by a Penarth estate agent stayed about two years. He later told Mary he had taken “the papers which were in the blanket box including the agreement relating to the farm” and given them to the agent. She found them gone, and thought their loss “was in some way connected” with WGR’s first possession application. When she told WGR in 1959 that documents proved her title, none could be produced (`1959 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1962-possession-order/#refusal-1959>`__), and the 1987 judgment records that they were “never produced” (`1987 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bp-properties-v-buckler/#court-of-appeal>`__) [`EV-0080 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0080-bp-properties-v-buckler-judgment/>`__]. Stolen documents are not subject to a time limit for their return (Limitation Act 1980, s.4); see the `Legal Review, section 1.4 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/scrutiny-and-accountability/legal-review-fraud-land-human-rights/#s4>`__.

**2026: Glamorgan Archives response and Bute X finding aid — Ref. 2026/4097b**

Archive correspondence + finding aid **Glamorgan Archives, Ref. 2026/4097b; Louise Hunt, Archivist, response received 8 September 2026**

Catalogue: GHF-E019-13

Location: Family archive / privately held email and attached Bute X catalogue extract

**Documentary fact:** Glamorgan Archives stated that it does not appear to hold deeds relating to the 1877 Daniel Thomas transaction after a catalogue search. It nevertheless identified **Bute X.6(12)**, an abstract of payments and receipts by the trustees of the Marquis of Bute’s properties for **1877 (copy)**, as a record that could be worth searching. The archivist stated that other similar Bute X records may also be relevant and that the records were then with Cardiff Library.

**Attached finding-aid evidence:** the Bute X catalogue supplied with the response identifies a substantial run of Bute estate financial material, including annual abstracts for 1871–1880; 1867–69 notes concerning the Glamorgan estate and **minerals**; 1832 bills and receipts including **improvements on Llandough Farm**; and 1833–35 bills and receipts concerning building and repairs on the estate, farms, woods and plantations. The catalogue also includes earlier files concerning manorial evidence, disputed properties and mineral surveys.

**Research significance:** these references give the family a specific, independently identified Bute Estate documentary trail to test against the property reconstruction. In particular, D31.49 (1832), the 1867–69 mineral and accounting material and Bute X.6(12) (1877) are targeted archival leads. The 1877 item is especially important because it falls in the same year as the Bute–Thomas transaction: a capital receipt from Daniel Thomas would corroborate the sale.

**Archive search status:** the response distinguishes between a negative catalogue search for deeds and a positive identification of potentially relevant Bute Estate accounting records. It therefore should not be recorded as “the 1877 transaction was not found anywhere”; the narrower fact is that Glamorgan Archives did not identify surviving deeds for the transaction in its search and directed the research toward Cardiff Library’s Bute X material. :sup:`🕒`

1877 source-control addendum — do not substitute the Cardiff leases
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The family’s reported Bute–Thomas arrangement, and the separate reported lease to John Williams, remain unresolved because the matching Llandough originals/plans have not been identified. The photographed 6 November 1877 Clyde Street, Roath counterpart is a 99-year building lease for a plot in Cardiff, granted to a builder of System Street, Roath; it is **not** the missing farm instrument. The 2 May 1877 DBDT/79/1 lease is likewise a Cardiff/Pembroke Terrace instrument.

The date “6 November 1877” should not be attributed to a specific Llandough deed unless the source passage or matching instrument is quoted and verified. Preserve the family’s reported 1877 arrangement as an unresolved lead; do not use the Cardiff deed to fill the gap. See `EV-0006 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0006-bute-thomas-agreement-1877/>`__, `EV-0008 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0008-reported-1877-lease-john-williams/>`__, `EV-0213 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0213-clyde-street-roath-lease-6-nov-1877/>`__, and the `Kissed Frogs register <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/anomalies-register/bpvsbuckler-archive-research-leads/#kissed-frogs-register>`__.

The separate NLW catalogue distinction also matters: Cydfin / Tŷ Mawr Farm is listed at 107 acres, while Llandough Farm is a different 166-acre holding. See the `NLW D 219 catalogue <https://archives.library.wales/index.php/llandough-manorial-leases-and-agreements>`__. None of these catalogue entries alone proves the 1877 transfer or fraud/concealment.
