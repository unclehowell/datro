**The Williams family , later Williams /… … read more**

The **Williams family**, later **Williams / Buckler**, lived at `Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr) <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/great-house-farm/>`__ until after the death of `Mary (Nancy) Williams <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/mary-williams/>`__ in 1983; the house was `demolished in 1988 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/demolition-1988/>`__. Her descendants run the `restoration campaign <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/restoration-campaign/>`__ and this archive.

**SOURCE PENDING: Marconi and Lavernock Point, 1897**

**SOURCE PENDING.** No document or named speaker yet supports the reported Williams-family connection with the Marconi wireless experiments at Lavernock Point in 1897. The historical Marconi–Lavernock experiments themselves are independently documented; the specific Williams-family connection has not yet been proved on this site.

**PRIMARY-EVIDENCE REQUEST.** The Wiki is seeking contemporary correspondence, household papers, photographs, Post Office records or other independently attributable material capable of supporting the reported family connection. Until such evidence is located, this remains SOURCE PENDING, not a documentary fact.

**Independent context:** Amgueddfa Cymru records the May 1897 GPO trials linking Lavernock Point and Flat Holm. `Museum Wales collection record → <https://museum.wales/collections/online/object/5f7f51a9-7b6b-3956-84c9-31b5f96214aa/GPO-trials-of-Marconi-in-1897-booklet/>`__

**Evidence description:** on 31 December 1969 `WGR <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/western-ground-rents/>`__ conveyed to `BP Pension Trust <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bp-properties/>`__.

`‹ <#p14-farm>`__\ `A + B as one <#p14-current>`__\ `› <#p14-ghf>`__\ **Parcel reading.** The conveyance itself is missing, but it appears to have passed the farm as one, although `WGR <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/western-ground-rents/>`__ had never obtained possession of, or any signature for, `Parcel A <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__. :sup:`🕒`

**Interpretation & wider context: when.** Frederick was alive when the sale came out, so it came before his death in 1967, and before the son’s household left in September 1968 – September 1969. The eldest son was probably born about 1937–40 (after the 1936 marriage), so he was old enough to sign a deed; William, born around 1948–49, was under the age of majority (21 until 1 January 1970) throughout. The tightest fit is spring 1965, just after Mary refused `WGR <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/western-ground-rents/>`__\ ‘s tenancy, to 1967. The 1961 deed is an earlier candidate.

**Interpretation & wider context: what.** He could not have sold Mary’s house (`Parcel A <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__): she was alive, in occupation, asserting ownership, and never signed. A solicitor could have completed a release or surrender of whatever interest he claimed as Frederick’s heir; a sale of a share he believed he had; a deal over the fields (`Parcel B <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__); a quitclaim `WGR <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/western-ground-rents/>`__ wanted on file before selling to BP; or a sale of a different parcel. `WGR <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/western-ground-rents/>`__ had just failed to get Mary’s signature, and a paper signed by another family member is exactly what it lacked. On this view the 1965 offer to Mary and the son’s sale were two routes to the same end: making the family’s hold on the farm look permissive.

**To find:** copy of the 29 March 1961 deed (`HM Land Registry <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/land-registry-titles/>`__) · `WGR <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/western-ground-rents/>`__ and BP conveyancing papers 1964–69, including the missing 1969 conveyance and BP’s abstract of title: any Buckler party other than Frederick, Mary or William · a solicitor’s file or deed of 1964–67 in the eldest son’s name · General Register Office entries for the eldest son’s birth (Buckler, about 1936–41, Cardiff or East Glamorgan) and Frederick’s 1967 death. :sup:`🕒`

**1983 – 1990: Defending the house, and William’s death**

| Human-rights decision **European Commission of Human Rights, Buckler v United Kingdom, 14 April** `1989 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/demolition-1988/>`__
| `Open the document → <https://greathousefarmwiki.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/wp-1790112811838.pdf>`__

Catalogue: GHF-E014-13

Location: `HUDOC / European Court of Human Rights <https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/ENG>`__

**Evidence description:** after Mary’s death on 26 March 1983 her son William inherited her estate and defended `BP Properties Ltd v Buckler <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bp-properties-v-buckler/>`__. BP won in the High Court (heard 10 July 1986, judgment 24 July 1986) and the Court of Appeal on 31 July `1987 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bp-properties-v-buckler/>`__. Demolition of the farmhouse began on 6 December `1988 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/demolition-1988/>`__.

`Parcel A <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__\ **Parcel reading.** What William inherited and defended was the house, garden and yard (`Parcel A <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__), and it was the farmhouse on `Parcel A <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/the-two-parcels/>`__ that was demolished. BP ultimately relied on a registered title that brought both parcels together in 1982/83; the disputed question is the historic root for the Parcel A component.
