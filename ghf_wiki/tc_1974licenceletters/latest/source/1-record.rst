Names: the family’s copies of the 31 October 1974 letters are addressed to **“Mary Buckler”** (image SOURCE PENDING). The Court of Appeal describes them as addressed to “Mrs Buckler (described again as Mrs Williams)”. The elder woman is **Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler**. Her youngest daughter, **Mary Buckler (later Godfrey)**, had married by 1972; an older daughter, born 1940, was also registered Mary. Which Mary the letters name is an open question: see `Two Mary Bucklers? Name and identity timeline <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/two-mary-bucklers-name-identity-timeline/>`__.

On 31 October 1974 `BP <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bp-properties/>`__ wrote to “Mrs Buckler (Mrs Williams)” saying she could remain in the farmhouse at `Great House Farm <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/great-house-farm/>`__ under licence [PENDING: `EV-0050 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0050-1974-bp-licence-letters/>`__]. The Court of Appeal later held these letters decisive against the family. The letters came at the end of an autumn in which BP started two court routes against `Mary <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/mary-williams/>`__ and finished neither.

**DOCUMENTED FACT (press).** On 15 April 1974 Mary, then 60, opened the farmhouse to the public “to show what the nation is losing”, and hundreds came [`EV-0048 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0048-telegraph-open-day-ancient-house/>`__]. She said the former Glamorgan County Council had given the BP Petroleum Pension Trust planning permission for about 13 houses, “But I will refuse to move because not only is this my home it is part of the nation’s heritage.” She added: “The ownership of the house has been in dispute for 25 years but my family has lived here for more than 400 years and my solicitors tell me I have a valid claim to it.” About 1,000 people had signed a petition, and the report says the family had acquired Ty Mawr in about 1667.

**Research note.** She never accepted the licence and continued to assert ownership in writing and in the press.

**Analysis: why 1974 matters**

**Research note.** If BP had a clean title to the house parcel, 1974 was the moment to prove it: by asking the court for a declaration of title, or by taking judgment on Mary’s plea. BP did neither. It adjourned the title fight, abandoned the warrant and sent a licence instead. The reasons the family gives for avoiding a trial of title are the open questions about the 1962 order (single-unit framing, jurisdiction, Mary as owner-claimant rather than a tenant holding over: `questions about the 1962 order <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1962-possession-order/#questions>`__) and the running of time after Mary refused the `1965 offer <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1962-possession-order/#offer-1965>`__.

**Research note.** The letters are treated as the last identified step in a 1949–1974 sequence that repeatedly sought to characterise Mary’s occupation of Parcel A as permissive. That sequence is distinct from proof of the underlying freehold title, and moral fairness is kept distinct from legal effect: what the 1974 proceedings actually decided, and what evidence of Parcel A title was before the court, remain the open questions.

**What happened next:** the High Court (July 1986) and the `Court of Appeal (31 July 1987) <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bp-properties-v-buckler/#court-of-appeal>`__ held that the letters made her possession permissive from 1974, whether or not she accepted them, so 12 years of adverse possession never ran [`EV-0080 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0080-bp-properties-v-buckler-judgment/>`__; PENDING: `EV-0074 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0074-high-court-trial-judgment-1986/>`__]. The judgment as presently identified did not finally adjudicate the historic Parcel A title in the adjourned 1974 action. Her title documents were not produced because, as Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 witness statement (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__) records, they had been `taken from the farmhouse in 1950–52 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1877-agreement/#papers-taken>`__.
