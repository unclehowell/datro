**Limitation clock — 1928, 1940, 1952, 1955 and 1962.** Twelve years was already the statutory period for recovery of land under section 1 of the `Real Property Limitation Act 1874 <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/37-38/57/pdfs/ukpga_18740057_en.pdf>`__; it did not begin in 1940. If Parcel-A possession became adverse in 1928, the family’s alternative theory is potential expiry around 1940. If it became adverse only in 1940, twelve years would run to about 1952. Rent non-payment alone does not prove the date of accrual or adverse possession. A failed 1954 proposal would not itself undo a period already completed; the 1955 order against Frederick and 1962 order against Frederick and Mary require independent analysis of the land covered, title pleaded, issues actually decided and any binding/preclusive effect. The accessible 1987 appeal report does not mention 1954; it mentions a 1952 letting letter and nevertheless recognises a protected periodic tenancy for Frederick. Track the separate 1954 event at `GHF-E182 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e182-1954-frederick-buckler-tenancy-attempt/>`__ as source-pending.

**NEW PARADIGM — CASCADE OF ACCEPTED PREMISES**

The investigation should not begin by assuming a conspiracy between institutions. It should ask: **Where did the first materially wrong, incomplete or unconfirmed proposition enter the chain; who introduced it; who relied upon it next; what independent check should have detected it; and what evidence shows whether that check occurred?** A single early error can be amplified by later institutional reliance without every later actor knowing that the underlying premise was defective.

Failure-mode tests
------------------

- **Identity failure:** the wrong parcel, person or legal entity was identified.
- **Evidence failure:** a material document existed but was not obtained, considered or disclosed.
- **Authority failure:** a person acted without the required power, delegation or instruction.
- **Verification failure:** a decision-maker relied on a representation without an appropriate independent check.
- **Boundary failure:** one legal instrument was treated as covering land that another document treated differently.
- **Record-lifecycle failure:** the documentary trail needed to audit a decision cannot now be demonstrated.
- **Remedy failure:** a present institution receives credible new evidence but does not identify or use the appropriate available mechanism.

Party-specific accountability rule
----------------------------------

Each failure mode must be tested on the dedicated actor page for the particular institution, company or individual concerned. Cross-references identify chronology or evidence only; they do not merge the assessments. A person is not treated as personally liable merely because they worked for an organisation, signed a document or appear in a chain. The evidence must establish their actual role, act or omission, authority, knowledge where legally relevant, and connection to the consequence.

Decision outcomes

Every control point should ultimately be classified as **confirmed correct**, **defect established**, **defect plausible but unproved**, **not material**, or **unresolved because primary evidence is missing**. “Unresolved” is not a finding of fault. :sup:`🕒`

Registration nexus — updated 15 October 2026
--------------------------------------------

The critical unresolved link is not merely the date of the “root”. It is the land-description and title chain across the 1938 WGR acquisition, the December 1969 WGR–BP Pension Trust conveyance, the 1975 BP Pension Trust–BP Properties conveyance, and the exact 1983 WA240304 registration extent. If the 1938 deed did not include Parcel A and no other effective instrument gave WGR title to it, a gap may exist upstream of the 1969 transfer.
