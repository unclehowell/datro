1. National Library of Wales: ex 642 — William Rees’s Bute archive list
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Reported item:** Prof. William Rees’s original typed list of the Bute archive, reportedly received in 1983–84. **Status: lead requiring direct inspection.** The exact archival description and extent have not been independently verified from a publicly indexed catalogue in this research pass.

**Why it matters:** compare the Llandough-related entries in this historical list with the current NLW catalogue. This may establish what records were listed at the time of transfer and how their descriptions or locations have since changed. A difference between an old list and today’s catalogue is not, by itself, proof that a record was destroyed, removed, concealed or unlawfully withheld; the comparison must account for renumbering, re-cataloguing, series-level descriptions, transfers, access restrictions and records listed under alternative place or estate names.

**Next test:** request a full-resolution copy of the original list and any covering accession / transfer documentation; transcribe every Llandough, Tŷ Mawr, Great House Farm, Daniel Thomas, Williams and Bute entry; record folio/page, wording, former reference, current reference and any explanatory catalogue note. Ask NLW to explain each unmatched item rather than treating unmatched entries as missing records.

2. National Library of Wales: D95/32–6 — 1793 sale of Llandough manor
---------------------------------------------------------------------

**Reported item:** deeds associated with the sale of Llandough manor to the Bute estate in 1793 for £19,000, shelfmark reported as NLW D95/32–6. The wiki previously referred to this transaction through a historian’s letter; the original deed group is the preferred source for the transaction’s actual terms.

**What to establish from the original:** the parties and their legal capacities; whether the instrument is a conveyance, counterpart, abstract or associated deed; the precise manor and lands included; schedules, plans, reservations and exceptions; and whether Tŷ Mawr / Great House Farm or any relevant parcel is expressly identified. A manor sale does not establish that every later parcel at Llandough was included in, or remained within, the same title.

**Status:** reported archival reference, not an inspected transcription. Do not use the £19,000 figure or the manor description as proof of a particular Parcel A boundary until checked against the deed itself.

5. WA240304 / 1983 farmhouse registration — the original application file
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The wiki’s existing analysis of `WA240304 and the 1983 first registration <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/scrutiny-and-accountability/procedural-fairness-notice/1983-first-registration-parcel-a-title-basis-alleged-hmlr-mistake/>`__ remains the controlling page for the title-chain issue. The reported legal framework identifies rule 29 of the Land Registration Rules 1925 as “Certificate by a solicitor as to title on first registration”. Section 14 of the Land Registration Act 1925 separately provided for the registrar, where required, to require the applicant and solicitor to make an affidavit or declaration concerning disclosure of deeds, charges, encumbrances and material facts.

**Important correction to the working hypothesis:** it is not yet established that the WA240304 file necessarily contains both a Linklaters solicitor’s certificate under rule 29 and a sworn declaration by BP Properties. Section 14 made the affidavit/declaration conditional on being required by the registrar; its presence, maker, wording and execution must be confirmed from the actual file. The rule 29 certificate is a strong targeted retrieval request, not evidence that the certificate has already been seen.

**Priority request:** ask HM Land Registry to identify and disclose the application form, the solicitor’s rule 29 certificate and any affidavit/statutory declaration, with dates, signatories, exhibits and the title documents / plans referred to. If any item is absent or withheld, ask whether it was never lodged, has been destroyed under a retention policy, is held elsewhere, or is withheld on a stated legal basis. Compare each assertion in the certificate/declaration with the operative deeds and plans, especially the 1938 WGR acquisition, 1969 conveyance, 1971 abstract and 1975 BP conveyance.

**Legal caution:** a knowingly false sworn statement or statutory declaration may have serious legal consequences depending on its form, the applicable offence and the evidence of knowledge and intent. No offence can be inferred merely because a declaration is suspected to exist or because a title-chain issue remains unresolved. First obtain the document; then test the precise representation against the contemporaneous record and applicable law.

8. Four retrospective leads — vetted 18 October 2026
----------------------------------------------------

**Overall status: research leads, not court-ready findings.** The four items below differ in evidential status. A newspaper report can evidence what a person said if authenticated; a will report does not identify land unless the clause is read; a covering email identifying an attachment does not substitute for examining the deed; and a general minute-book catalogue entry does not prove that a particular planning committee discussed Great House Farm.

8.1 Western Mail, 20 November 1958, p. 7 — “It’s a Crichel Down, says woman farmer”
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A research note supplies this citation and reports that Mrs Buckler told a county planning committee that Glamorgan County Council, Penarth Urban Council, Cardiff Rural Council and Western Ground Rents had “walked in and taken” the family’s land. The exact article, page and quotation have not yet been recovered and independently checked during this review. Keep this as an unconfirmed press lead pending the page image or a full transcript; do not assign it a new evidence-library item number until a source copy is identified.

**Potential significance if authenticated:** the article would show that Mrs Buckler publicly made an allegation about the taking of family land in 1958, approximately 29 years before the 1987 Court of Appeal proceedings. It would not by itself prove the family’s title, establish that any named body unlawfully took land, or amount to a judicial finding. The reported words “Cardiff Rural Council” require careful checking: the Wiki’s `Source Conflicts Register SC-22 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/records-access-chronology/source-conflicts-register/#sc-22>`__ records that the relevant area was within Penarth Urban District Council rather than Cardiff Rural District Council. Do not silently amend the newspaper quotation; verify what it says and reconcile that wording against the administrative history.

**Retrieval:** search the `Western Mail archive <https://www.findmypast.com/newspapers/wales/western-mail>`__ for 20 November 1958, page 7, using the headline and Mrs Buckler’s name. The archive catalogue establishes that the paper’s digital run includes 1958; it does not establish that this particular article has been found. Once obtained, preserve the complete page image, headline, byline (if any), surrounding context and exact quoted passage. Cross-reference: `Press Archive — Newspaper Transcripts <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/press-archive-transcripts/>`__ and `Master Timeline <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/timeline/>`__.

8.2 Reported 1900 will / Lord Colum and the 23 February 1905 conveyance
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A supplied research lead says that press coverage in 1900 reported that Lord Bute’s will left Llandough land to his son Lord Colum. The exact article and will/probate clause identifying that land have not been inspected in this review. The existence of 1900 newspaper coverage of Lord Bute’s will is independently traceable in the `Western Mail archive <https://www.findmypast.com/newspapers/wales/western-mail>`__; the widely reported title “Lord Bute’s Will — Bequest to the Seaman’s Hospital — Explanation of the Conditions” (19 October 1900, p. 5) does not itself verify a Llandough bequest. The family’s more specific “Llandough land to Lord Colum” proposition remains a lead until the relevant wording is identified.

**Inference correction:** an inheritance to Lord Colum would not, without more, “break” the title chain. It could be an intermediate succession event. The forensic question is whether the will/probate identifies the same Llandough lands or rights, what passed under it, and how the later instruments describe those same lands.

**Priority document:** order the **23 February 1905 conveyance recorded on the WA231076 historic file** from HM Land Registry, together with its schedule and plan (Wiki catalogue GHF-E026-03; `Land Registry Titles — deeds before registration <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/land-registry-titles/#deeds-before-registration>`__). The existing Wiki notes that WA735527 refers to this 1905 instrument and that it concerns rights over land Bute retained, but the 1905 instrument itself and its plan have not been inspected here. Compare the actual parties, capacity, consideration, estate/title recitals, rights granted, parcel description, boundaries and reservations against the 1900 will/probate and the 1938 Bute–Western Ground Rents conveyance and plan. Do not infer that it dealt with the whole of Great House Farm, Parcel A, Parcel B or the full Llandough estate before that comparison is complete.

The covering email names the parties as (1) Lakeside and Llandough Development Company Limited, (2) Western Ground Rents Limited and (3) Glamorgan County Council, and reports consideration of £16,800. The Council said that it had not identified a valuation report, a gift/undervalue record, committee reports authorising the acquisition, or correspondence/minutes with BP within its current records-management systems; it noted that surviving historic records may have been transferred to Glamorgan Archives. These are statements from the covering email. The underlying deed and plan have not been read here.

**File status:** the Wiki’s FOI register records the attachment names *“Conveyance dated 11 June 1965.pdf”* and *“1965 Contract.pdf”*, but states that the 27 MB email with attachments was not retrieved by the connector. The actual PDFs are not present in the Wiki’s searchable media library, so this page cannot provide a download of the conveyance. Ask the Vale to resend the conveyance and plan, and the contract, as separate files or through a secure transfer link. Once obtained, preserve the original files and transcribe the recitals, parties, title source, plan, property description, rights, reservations, consideration and any references to earlier WGR title.

**What it may establish:** if the plan and title recitals identify the exact school parcel and state WGR’s root of title, the deed may illuminate the chain for that particular tract. WGR’s appearance as a party in a school-land conveyance does not by itself prove how it first acquired the school land, nor does it establish its title to other land at Great House Farm. Compare the original deed/plan with the 1938 Bute–WGR conveyance and the registered title plans; do not extend the result to Parcel A or B without spatial and documentary matching. See the `Vale correspondence catalogue, Thread 8 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/records-access-chronology/foi-reply-register/vale-of-glamorgan-council-email-correspondence-catalogue/#t8>`__, `Lakeside and Llandough Development Company assessment <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/scrutiny-and-accountability/government-public-authority-involvement/lakeside-and-llandough-development-company-forensic-institutional-review/>`__ and `Glamorgan County Council assessment <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/scrutiny-and-accountability/government-public-authority-involvement/glamorgan-county-council-forensic-institutional-review/>`__.

**Search request:** ask Glamorgan Archives to identify the relevant planning / development-control committee minute books, agendas, indexes, officer reports, correspondence and appendices covering 1988. Search the general-minute volumes for June–November 1988 and December 1988–February 1989 as well as any separate committee series, using Great House Farm, Tŷ Mawr, Llandough, listing/retention, demolition, and application 89/01396/OUT as terms. A discussion, if found, could clarify what was known and considered. Silence in one general-minute volume would not establish that no consideration occurred: it may have been recorded in a different committee series, officer file, Cadw correspondence or planning file. The minutes are a retrieval target, not presently proof either that protection was considered or that it was not.
