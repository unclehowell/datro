Woodland — the live loss question
---------------------------------

**This page isolates the woodland loss from… … read more**

This page isolates the woodland loss from the wider Parcel A/B reconstruction. The project is no longer asking whether the 1939 tenancy included Parcel A: the 1939 agreement and plan have already been obtained and the plan is established as **Parcel B only**.

What was actually lost
----------------------

**The loss case concerns the forest north of… … read more**

The loss case concerns the **forest north of the house** and the **ground south-east of the house and garden that later became village green VG41**. Both are parts of the full Parcel-A house/quarrying estate, alongside the farmhouse, garden, barns, stable and yard west of the house. The family’s established title case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 when quarrying and rent to Thomas ended, carrying the full Parcel A—including forest and green ground—into the Williams family’s freehold. WGR’s later right to possession of Parcel B did not make those Parcel-A areas part of the agricultural tenancy.

The 1955 order was described as covering the “whole farm”. The family’s established account is that enforcement on 4 July 1955 took Parcel B and also seized Parcel-A woodland north of the house and the south-eastern green ground, while Mary physically held onto the farmhouse/buildings/garden group. The fact that she defended that occupied centre does not mean the forest and green were outside her Parcel-A title. The task now is to tie that established account to the order wording, enforcement record, annotated geometry and downstream title instruments—not to treat the taking as an unanswered generic question.

What the documentary work must now prove
----------------------------------------

**The 1939 agreement and plan are already held… … read more**

The 1939 agreement and plan are already held and establish Parcel B only; no repeat acquisition task is needed. The primary records now have a defined purpose: corroborate the boundary between Parcel B and the forest/green parts of Parcel A, document how the 1955 “whole farm” wording was applied, and establish how those exact Parcel-A areas later entered the woodland title. The family’s chronology places the 1928 end of the 1877/Thomas arrangement before the 1949 attempt to characterise occupancy as permissive, the report of missing title papers in 1950–52, and the 1955 enforcement/conflation. On the family’s limitation case, the twelve-year period from 1928 would have matured by 1940 if the relevant statutory conditions were satisfied; counsel must distinguish that historic position from the later attempt to make 1955 appear to be the starting point.

Second stage — how the woodland later became WA735527
-----------------------------------------------------

**The present woodland chain is separately… … read more**

The present woodland chain is separately important. WA735527 contains land from more than one historic strand. The critical documents are the 1975 conveyance/plan, the 1982 registration plan, the 10 November 1994 Forest of Cardiff transfer and plan, the underlying Ideal Homes acquisition, and the 2 December 2005 excision/transfer and plans.

**The Forest of Cardiff says it bought the… … read more**

The Forest of Cardiff says it bought the woodland from Ideal Homes after due diligence and says the remaining woodland may be put on the open market in early 2027. That statement creates an immediate preservation issue, not a finding against the charity.
