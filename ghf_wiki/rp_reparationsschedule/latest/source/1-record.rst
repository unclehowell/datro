5. Limitation and what must be proven
-------------------------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Claim
     - Time limit
     - What would need proving
   * - Recovering the land
     - Limitation Act 1980 s.15: 12 years; s.17 extinguishes title after that.
     - s.32(1)(a)/(b): time runs from when fraud or deliberate concealment was discovered, or could with reasonable diligence have been discovered. The claimant must prove it. s.32(3): no recovery of the property or its value from an innocent purchaser for value (current homeowners).
   * - Setting aside the 1987 judgment
     - Court’s power to set aside a judgment obtained by fraud.
     - Takhar v Gracefield [2019] UKSC 13: no need to show the fraud could not have been found earlier, but the new evidence must show conscious and deliberate dishonesty that materially caused the judgment. Until set aside, the 1987 judgment [EV-0080] stands against the family.
   * - Deceit, trespass, negligence (money claims)
     - s.2: 6 years from the damage.
     - Postponed only under s.32. For negligence, s.14A gives 3 years from knowledge, with a 15-year longstop (s.14B) that s.32 deliberate concealment can override (s.32(5)).
   * - Personal injury
     - s.11: 3 years from injury or knowledge.
     - s.33: the court may disapply the time limit. Causation needs medical evidence.
   * - Goods (contents, heirlooms)
     - s.3: 6 years for conversion.
     - s.4: no time limit against the thief for stolen goods, and the owner’s title is not extinguished. Each item must be identified and shown to belong to the family.
   * - HMLR indemnity
     - LRA 2002 Sch 8 para 8: 6 years from when the claimant knew, or ought to have known, of the claim.
     - Needs a register mistake proven, and the loss caused by it. Indemnity is reduced for the claimant’s own lack of care (Sch 8 para 5). 1983 law (Land Registration Act 1925) applies to the mistake: solicitor to confirm the transition.
   * - Human rights
     - Human Rights Act 1998 s.7(5): 1 year.
     - The Act does not apply to events before 2 Oct 2000. A claim would need a continuing or later act by a public authority (e.g. a decision in 2025–26).
   * - Claims of nan and dad
     - Their claims survive for the benefit of their estates (Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1934 s.1).
     - Needs a grant of probate or letters of administration, and the death certificates. The same time limits apply to the estates. Verified: `BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 <https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWCA/Civ/1987/2.html>`__ records that “no grant of representation to the estate of Mrs Buckler has so far been obtained” (as of 1987).
