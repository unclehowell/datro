**Llandough limeworks and the unconfirmed 33-acre lead… … read more**

The **Llandough Limeworks** are independently associated with Daniel Thomas and his son Alfred in the `People’s Collection Wales account <https://www.peoplescollection.wales/items/1888101>`__. Mary (Nancy) Williams’s later account links quarrying to part of the Great House Farm holding from 1876. A separate, uninspected archive lead refers to about 33 acres; the relationship between that acreage, Great House Farm and the 1877 house-parcel arrangement is not established. See `The 1877 Agreement <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1877-agreement/>`__ and `The Bute Estate <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/bute-estate/>`__.

.. _identity-distinction:

Source distinction: two Williams lines and two quarry histories
---------------------------------------------------------------

**Great House Farm / Llandough:** Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 account concerns her own family’s occupation at Great House Farm. She describes the Bute transaction involving the farmhouse/buildings and about 10 acres, plus land retained by Bute. The original 1877 instrument and plan have not been produced here.

**William Evans Williams of Pwll-y-Pant, Caerphilly:** `History Points’ account of former Pwll-y-Pant House <https://ipv6.historypoints.org/index.php?page=former-pwllypant-house-caerphilly>`__ identifies a different Williams, who died in February 1870 and left his estate to the Marquess of Bute. The `National Library of Wales catalogue for D242/1–27 <https://archives.library.wales/index.php/deeds-and-documents-relating-mainly-to-title-of-pwllypant-estate-including-copy-probate-of-will-of-david-evan%3Bisad?sf_culture=en>`__ identifies the 1870 probate of William Evans Williams’s will at **D242/17**, notes on his real estate at **D242/18**, and related succession-duty papers/accounts at **D242/19–23**. Separately, `D258/1–110 <https://archives.library.wales/index.php/papers-relating-to-personal-and-real-estates-of-william-evans-williams-dec-including-account-of-aberdare-iron-co>`__ is specifically catalogued as papers relating to his personal and real estates, including the Pwllypant estate audit (D258/98–101); Pwll-y-Pant estate rentals are catalogued in **D255**. The catalogue summary for D242/18 names several other parishes but does not name Llandough in that particular property note. That is not proof that no related land transaction occurred elsewhere in the wider estate papers.

**Independent Llandough limeworks context:** `People’s Collection Wales <https://www.peoplescollection.wales/items/1888101>`__ records that Daniel Thomas started the limeworks at Llandough in 1851 and that his son Alfred continued them. This corroborates the Thomases’ connection to the Llandough works, not the title owner or extent of the 33 acres.

**Brickworks next door:** a published history of Welsh brickworks gives: “Llandough Limeworks were owned in 1871 by D Thomas and Son and the brickworks had been opened next door by 1899. J E Billups of Billups Brick Co Ltd was the proprietor, becoming the Llandough Brick Co by 1923. The limeworks had gone and the brickworks is marked ‘disused’ by 1947.” (secondary; its sources are not cited). J. E. Billups died in November 1896; his obituary names “the Penarth Brickworks, which were started by him” (South Wales Daily News, 26 Nov 1896, p.6). Both in `EV-0292 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0292-llandough-lime-works-and-brickworks-industrial-sequence-secondary-and-billups-obituary-1896/>`__.

**What the catalogue now establishes:** the `NLW Bute title-deeds file list <https://archives.library.wales/downloads/reports/title-deeds-4-fileList.html>`__ describes **D153/1–8** as deeds relating to premises in the parish of Llandough, including a dwelling house adjoining the churchyard in 1811 (D153/1–2), and dates the series 1811–1912. The file’s own catalogue record reads: “… land (33 a.), formerly part of Great House Farm, for the Llandough Limeworks (33 a.), 1876-1912 (D153/4-8). Plan engrossed on D153/3 (1875) and 4 (1876).” It does not name William Evans Williams (`EV-0291 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0291-nlw-bute-catalogue-d153-llandough-33-acres-great-house-farm-limeworks-and-pwllypant-d242-d247-d258/>`__). The `same NLW list <https://archives.library.wales/downloads/reports/title-deeds-4-fileList.html>`__ separately describes D247/1–69 as deeds for Penyrheol-las, Quarry Mawr and the woodland later known as Pwll-y-Pant Quarry in Eglwysilan (1723–1895). The 33 acres are in the Llandough entry (D153/4-8); the Eglwysilan entries (D242, D247, D258) state no acreage (`EV-0291 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0291-nlw-bute-catalogue-d153-llandough-33-acres-great-house-farm-limeworks-and-pwllypant-d242-d247-d258/>`__). The catalogue does not name the parties to D153/4-8 or say which part of Great House Farm the 33 acres were; the deeds and the 1876 plan are unread.

**Repository and scope correction:** the Wiki’s `SC-21 entry <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/records-access-chronology/source-conflicts-register/#sc-21>`__ records that Glamorgan Archives said it did not hold the D153 reference and identified it as an NLW reference. The NLW title-deeds list confirms that the parent file D153/1–8 concerns premises in Llandough, but the individual contents of D153/4–8 and the Wiki’s “limeworks severance” label have not been verified against the original documents.

**Related identity test:** a separate reported 6 November 1877 lease to John Williams remains unlocated. The inspected DBDT/79/1 instrument is a 2 May 1877 Cardiff lease, not a Llandough instrument; see `SC-20 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/records-access-chronology/source-conflicts-register/#sc-20>`__. The identity of John Williams of Newport and the Great House Farm John Williams is unresolved.
