Fresh remedy architecture — 6 October 2026
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Reframing: the case is now divided into… … read more**

**Reframing:** the case is now divided into separate questions: title, registration, judgment, limitation, woodland recovery and public-authority accountability. No single limitation argument is being asked to do all the work.

- **Limitation Act 1980 s.32:** qualifying fraud, deliberate concealment or mistake can postpone limitation until discovery or when it could reasonably have been discovered.
- **Takhar:** a fraud-based challenge requires evidence that fraud affected the obtaining of the judgment; earlier document loss alone is insufficient.
- **CPR 52.30:** reopening a final appeal requires real injustice, exceptional circumstances and no alternative effective remedy.
- **CPR 31.16:** pre-action disclosure may be available where its conditions are met.
- **Registered land:** LRA 2002 s.96 disapplies ordinary Limitation Act s.15 recovery-of-land limitation against registered estates and Schedule 6 governs adverse possession.

**Woodland: where primary evidence proves a… … read more**

**Woodland:** where primary evidence proves a precise woodland polygon was already family land, the requested remedy can be framed as declaration/rectification/recovery rather than purchase, subject to the 1994 transfer, consideration, notice and purchaser-protection analysis.
