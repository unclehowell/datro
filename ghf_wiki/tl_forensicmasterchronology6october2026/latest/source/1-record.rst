**Updated scope control — 9 October 2026.** The controlling family case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 when quarrying and rent to Thomas ended; the full Parcel A included the farmhouse/buildings, barns, stable and yard west of the house, garden, northern forest and south-eastern ground later registered as VG41. Parcel B was the separate eastern agricultural fields, and the already-obtained 1939 agreement/plan depicts Parcel B only. The family’s established account is that the 1949 attempt to characterise Mary’s Parcel-A occupation as permissive failed, the title deed was reported missing in 1950–52, and the 1955 “whole farm” wording blurred the parcel boundary. On 4 July 1955, the north forest and south-east green were seized along with Parcel B, while Mary fought to remain in the occupied house group. The work now corroborates that account, traces the two physical areas through later titles, and assesses remedies; it does not reopen the 1939 scope finding. :sup:`🕒`

1. Status discipline
--------------------

- **DOCUMENTED** — directly shown by a surviving document or a checked catalogue/register entry.
- **INFERENCE** — a reasoned reconstruction from multiple records which remains open to rebuttal.
- **SPECULATION / TEST REQUIRED** — a possible explanation kept because it gives a clear evidential test; it must never be presented as fact.

2. 1877 — the first decisive fork
---------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED / TESTIMONY · Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, witness statement c.1974 (**\ `Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__\ **).** Mary (Nancy) Williams said the Bute Estate sold the greater part of the farm, including the farmhouse and about 10 acres, to Daniel Thomas and another portion of about 9 acres was retained by Bute. She also said the Thomas tenancy provided for rent to fall as quarrying took land and that the freehold should belong to her grandfather when quarrying ceased.

**Family title case, supported by Mary’s statement and the 1928 rent/quarry chronology.** The family’s position is that the 1877 arrangement provided for the full house/quarrying parcel to pass to the Williams family when quarrying ended, and that this took effect in 1928. Parcel A therefore included the house/buildings group and garden as well as the northern forest and south-eastern green ground later registered as VG41. The missing original instruments remain relevant to corroborating the documentary chain; their absence should not cause Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 statement and the 1928 rent/quarry chronology to be recast as merely a hypothesis that rent-free occupation might have matured into title. :sup:`🕒`

**CORRECTION.** The 15 May 1924 drain agreement cannot be treated as an “earlier candidate” date for a Williams freehold. It is an agreement about a drain; its importance is spatial and evidential, not as a conveyance.

3. 1891–1928 — occupation, quarrying and the hidden parcel question
-------------------------------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** Contemporary evidence places John Williams at Great House Farm by 1891. Bute Estate material continues to show estate/quarry activity through the early twentieth century.

**INFERENCE.** The family occupation and the Bute/Thomas interests need to be mapped independently of later “whole farm” descriptions.

**DOCUMENTED / TESTIMONY · Mary (Nancy) Williams, witness statement c.1974 (**\ `Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__\ **).** Mary says the quarry machinery left in 1928 and the last rent of about £4 was paid to Alfred Thomas, after which her father regarded the house parcel as his own; rent continued on the eastern fields.

**Title/limitation distinction.** The family’s primary case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and passed Parcel A to the Williams family. Separately, if a court were to treat ownership as unproven and analyse possession limitation instead, the family’s alternative case is that twelve years from 1928 would have matured by 1940 if the relevant statutory conditions were satisfied. The alternative limitation calculation does not replace or narrow the underlying-title case.

4. 1916 and 1938–39 — the “whole farm” problem
----------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** Later records recite a 1916 tenancy describing the “whole farm”. The 1939 revised tenancy has a surveyed plan which, on the Wiki’s present reconstruction, shows the eastern agricultural fields rather than the farmhouse parcel.

**Closed parcel finding.** The 1939 WGR–John Williams agreement and plan have already been obtained and analysed: the plan depicts Parcel B (the eastern agricultural fields) only, not Parcel A. Frederick’s 1949 agricultural tenancy followed that Parcel-B line. This is not a fresh research question and should not be used as proof that the north forest, south-east green or house group became WGR land.

**TEST.** The complete 1938 conveyance, 1939 tenancy, schedules and plans must be read together. The complete 1938 conveyance, 1939 tenancy, schedules and plans are needed to establish the precise extent conveyed.

5. 1949–1955 — the paper trail and missing documents
----------------------------------------------------

**1949–1955 — the permissive recharacterisation (legal case; SOURCE PENDING for the 1949 step).** The 1949 step was the first later attempt, on the family’s case, to make Mary’s Parcel-A occupation appear permissive despite the 1928 title effect; that attempt failed and preceded the report that the title deed was missing in 1950–52. The 1955 order’s “whole farm” wording then blurred the distinct Parcel-A estate and Parcel-B agricultural tenancy. During enforcement on 4 July 1955 the north forest and south-east green of Parcel A were seized along with Parcel B. The later summary that possession was taken of everything except the farmhouse and garden refers to the portion Mary held onto; it does not mean the entire Parcel A remained with her. :sup:`🕒`

**TESTIMONY · Mary (Nancy) Williams, witness statement c.1974 (**\ `Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__\ **).** Mary said the family papers, including the farm agreement, were removed from the blanket box in about 1950–52. The identity of the person named as the remover, Bruce Sutherland, and the Penarth estate agent Knapp are open corroboration targets.

**FORENSIC TEST.** Determine whether the 1952 WGR agents named in the records were connected with Knapp; obtain petty-sessions/press/probate evidence for Sutherland; identify Knapp’s firm and clients; and trace any surviving custodial record.

6. 1955–1965 — WGR’s treatment of Mary over the house
-----------------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** WGR subsequently dealt directly with Mary concerning the farmhouse and garden, including tenancy offers in 1959 and 1965, while the 1962 proceeding separately concerned the farmhouse and garden.

**Spatial reading.** WGR’s 1959 and 1965 offers and 1962 proceedings concerned the farmhouse and garden—the occupied centre Mary defended after the north forest and south-east green had been seized. “House and garden” is not interchangeable with the full Parcel A. Mary’s determined resistance, recent leg amputation and responsibility for seven children explain why she fought hardest for the remaining house group; these circumstances should not be used to infer that she surrendered or never claimed Parcel-A woodland and green.

**OPEN QUESTION.** The complete 1962 file is essential. The complete 1962 file is needed before concluding whether the ownership question was tried in that proceeding.

7. 1969–1975 — the BP documentary chain
---------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** Later documents recite a 31 December 1969 WGR → BP Pension Trust conveyance and a 1971 abstract of title. The 30 July 2026 HMLR schedule does not list those two items as surviving documents on the registration files. The 23 May 1975 BP Pension Trust → BP Properties conveyance is associated with WA240304, the farmhouse-and-garden title.

**FORENSIC QUESTION.** What exact land did the 1969 root convey? The answer cannot be supplied by repeating the recital in a later deed. The actual parcels clause and plan are required.

**DOCUMENTED CORRECTION.** The 29 March 1961 deed in the woodland chain is between WGR, South Western Brick & Tile Co. Ltd and Cardiff City Corporation. It is not evidence of a “Buckee” sale. The Brick & Tile company’s underlying interest should be separately researched. :sup:`🕒`

8. 1974 — the six-week deadline hypothesis
------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** The 1962 order is dated 11 December 1962. The record refers to a July 1974 action and ownership plea, leave to enforce in September, a warrant not before 31 October, and licence letters dated 31 October 1974.

**INFERENCE — strong but not proved.** If the 1962 proceeding had reset the relevant limitation clock and if the relevant adverse-possession period was running against the person entitled to possession, 11 December 1974 becomes a potentially important date. The clustering of the October 1974 steps immediately before that date is therefore a serious forensic question.

**DO NOT OVERSTATE.** The proposition that BP “avoided a trial of title because of the deadline” must remain an inference until the 1974 pleadings, correspondence and the original judgments are read.

9. 1982–1984 — first registration: the pivotal administrative stage
-------------------------------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** WA231076 was applied for on 30 November 1982. WA240304, the farmhouse-and-garden title, was applied for on 23 February 1983. The files contain post-death correspondence on 8 and 12 April 1983 and a further Linklaters batch on 12 May 1983. An A3 application and new register edition appear on 13 January 1984.

**NEW FORENSIC PRIORITY.** WA231076 item 17 is form A13, the list of documents lodged at first registration. This is the key document for reconstructing exactly what BP/Linklaters said was its root of title. It should be treated as a first-order evidence item.

**CRITICAL QUESTIONS.** What class of title was granted? What did the original register say? What notices were served on Mary? What was recorded about occupation? What did the Registrar ask? What did Linklaters answer? What happened after Mary died? What did the January 1984 application change?

**HISTORICAL-LAW TEST.** The Land Registration Act 1925 contained specific offences concerning suppression of documents/facts in registration proceedings (s.115) and fraudulent procurement of entries in the register (s.116). These are not findings of fraud; they define the historical legal test against which the surviving 1982–84 file should be examined.

10. 1987 — amalgamation during live litigation
----------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** An amalgamation application was made on 2 February 1987 while the BP v Buckler appeal process was still live. The farmhouse title was then absorbed into the main title chronology.

**INFERENCE.** The significance is not that amalgamation was inherently improper. The forensic issue is whether the court, parties and advisers were aware of the exact pre-amalgamation title position and whether the merger changed the documentary frame within which the disputed house parcel was subsequently described.

11. 1984–1989 — possession litigation and what was actually decided
-------------------------------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** The Court of Appeal decided issues concerning limitation and the 1974 licence. Its judgment records that the claimed title documents were not produced. The Wiki’s present research position is that the documentary title to Parcel A was not independently examined.

**CORRECTION.** Replace the categorical phrase “ownership was never tried in any court” with: **“The documentary title and root of title to Parcel A have not yet been shown to have been fully examined in the surviving court record.”** The 1962 and 1974 files must be checked before going further.

**TAKHAR TEST.** A fraud-based challenge to the 1987 judgment depends upon proving fraud in obtaining that judgment, not merely proving that documents were lost decades earlier. The 1984–87 litigation file is therefore more important than general historical reconstruction.

12. 1988 — demolition and heritage loss
---------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** The farmhouse was demolished on 6 December 1988, one day after the recorded Cadw decision not to list it. Independent HER material records that the building was completely demolished on that date and notes that the structure had only been partly recorded because of problems created by the ownership dispute.

**FORENSIC QUESTION.** The listing/inspection file, field notes, photographs, decision-maker record, destruction/transfer trail and contemporaneous planning correspondence should be reconstructed separately from the title dispute.

13. 1989–1994 — redevelopment and archaeology
---------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED / OPEN.** The record contains archaeological warnings, the 1990 assessment, later excavation and a substantial burial assemblage. The archaeology should be analysed independently of ownership: the question is what was known, when it was known, who advised whom, what conditions existed, what was omitted from planning files, and what was done when the archaeological risk became apparent.

**IMPORTANT LIMIT.** Archaeological anomalies do not prove title fraud. They become relevant only where a document, actor, decision or omission connects them to land ownership, planning or record handling. :sup:`🕒`

14. 1994–2005 — the woodland chain
----------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** Woodland title WA735527 was transferred to Forest of Cardiff in 1994 and later changed by a 2005 excision. The current title-plan reconstruction distinguishes blue and pink strands within the woodland title.

**CORE PROPRIETARY TEST.** Overlay the historic Parcel A plan, 1975 farmhouse plan, 1982 title plan, 1994 WA735527 transfer/plan and 2005 excision. The family should claim only the precisely identified area which the documentary chain supports.

**1994 TRANSFER TEST.** Obtain the 10 November 1994 transfer and consideration. If the transfer was for valuable consideration, purchaser protections need analysis. If it was a gift or non-value transfer, the legal position may be materially different.

15. 2026 — discovery, disclosure and institutional testing
----------------------------------------------------------

**DOCUMENTED.** New material identified in 2026 includes HMLR schedules and registers, House of Lords archive information, RCAHMW and Wales Office correspondence, museum records and archaeological reports.

**FORENSIC PRINCIPLE.** Discovery dates must be recorded by fact: *what was unknown, when it was discovered, where it came from, why it matters, and why it could not reasonably have been obtained earlier.* This is the correct evidential form for any later limitation argument. :sup:`🕒`

16. Present working reconstruction — without pretending certainty
-----------------------------------------------------------------

#. **Family title case.** The 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and transferred the full Parcel-A house/quarrying estate to the Williams family, including the north forest and south-east green ground. Mary’s account and the rent/quarry chronology are the central supporting evidence; later title records must be tested against that existing case.
#. **Closed Parcel-B finding.** The already-obtained 1939 agreement/plan depicts Parcel B only. The 1949 agricultural tenancy did not transfer Parcel A merely because later records used “whole farm” wording.
#. **Downstream title-chain question.** The later WGR/BP registration chain must be tested against the family’s 1928 Parcel-A title case and the 1955 seizure of its north forest and south-east green. The 1969 root deed/1971 abstract gap affects the chain descriptions for both parcels and is not, by itself, Parcel-A-specific proof.
#. **Critical administrative stage.** The 1982–84 first-registration files may contain the answer because they show the actual evidence lodged, declarations made, notices issued and title class granted.
#. **Potential judgment problem.** If the 1984–87 litigation file shows BP possessed material evidence inconsistent with the case advanced to the court and failed to disclose it in circumstances that amounted to fraud, a genuine *Takhar*-type issue arises.
#. **Potential woodland route.** If the exact woodland polygon is shown to belong to the family’s superior historic title and the later registration/transfer is a mistake or ineffective against that title, the family can pursue proprietary restoration/rectification rather than treating the land as something that must be purchased.

17. What would falsify the family case?
---------------------------------------

- A primary 1877 instrument showing the house parcel remained Bute-owned in a way inconsistent with the family’s title theory.
- A 1938/1969/1975 instrument and plan that clearly and lawfully carries Parcel A into the BP chain.
- An A13/first-registration file showing full disclosure of Mary’s claim and a lawful title basis for the exact Parcel A extent.
- The 1962 or 1974 file showing the ownership issue was finally adjudicated on the documentary title.
- The 1984–87 litigation file showing the supposedly missing title evidence was actually available to the family/BP and fairly deployed. :sup:`🕒`
- A 1994 woodland transfer for value to a protected purchaser combined with a title chain defeating the claimed prior interest.

18. Central working conclusion
------------------------------

**Assessment:** A coordinated-conspiracy hypothesis is not established. The open question is whether individually explainable administrative, conveyancing, litigation, custody and heritage events cumulatively left the family’s claimed Parcel A title untested and embedded later records as if established. Each proposed link requires independent documentary support.

The record describes Miles Fletcher pieces to camera, bailiffs removing furniture, removal vans and Branwen leaving the farm; its abstract says the High Court granted an injunction stopping BP demolishing the farm. The cataloguer’s note says “Not viewed”; the tape contains unrelated clips and the position of this clip is unknown, so the description may derive from the broadcaster’s log.

**Catalogue-date limit.** The record information supplied contains no catalogue-created date or online-publication date. Its 1988 recording/broadcast dates cannot be used to date when the catalogue entry or online page first appeared. Stated access conditions (on-site digital viewing for Reader’s Ticket holders and written ITV Wales permission for reuse) do not, by themselves, establish exceptional restriction or concealment.

Treat that as the recorded date for the viewing copy only. The present NLW catalogue information does not establish that date as the creation or online-publication date of item 507738, nor does it establish that the YouTube copy is the same item or identify who uploaded it.

**5 December 1988 — separate material sought.** The separate “Wales at Six” demolition headline/report remains a distinct research target. It must not be conflated with the 30 November eviction/injunction item. A negative result from a targeted online search is not proof that a 5 December record does not exist; NLW should confirm the search scope and any candidate item identifiers.
