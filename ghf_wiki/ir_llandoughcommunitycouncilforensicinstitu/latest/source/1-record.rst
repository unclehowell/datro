Forensic Assessment — Llandough Community Council
-------------------------------------------------

**Scope:** local community-council records and representations.

Role
~~~~

The Community Council is relevant principally to local representations, heritage/community knowledge and later public discussion. It did not adjudicate the private title dispute.

Paul Egan — documented role and casework assessment

**Current public status (checked 18 October 2026).** The Council’s own `Contact Us page <https://www.llandough-cc.co.uk/Contact_Us_20128.aspx>`__ continues to name Paul Egan as Clerk to the Council. **Historical public-role record.** Paul Egan / Paul R. Egan is identified as representing Llandough Community Council in `Welsh Government’s 2018 local-government reform consultation response documents <https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/consultations/2018-01/160222greenpaperannexa.pdf>`__. The Council’s own `2018 newsletter <https://www.llandough-cc.co.uk/_UserFiles/Files/Newsletter%20Late%20Spring%202018%20Special%20Edition.pdf>`__ and `Winter 2023 newsletter <https://www.llandough-cc.co.uk/_UserFiles/Files/Your%20Council/Newsletter%20Winter%202023.pdf>`__ also identify him as Clerk. These are dated snapshots, not proof of uninterrupted service between them. A family understanding in June 2026 that he might be retiring was explicitly unconfirmed and arose from a public social-media post; the present Council contact listing and 11 October signature show that he was still publicly listed/signing as Clerk at those later dates. The source reviewed here does not include a Council notice establishing the effective date of any retirement, reappointment, locum arrangement, handover or records-custody change.

Dated events and source links
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

1877–1928

Parcel A — early history

The family reconstruction links the Bute–Thomas arrangement to the house plot and treats the end of quarrying/rent in 1928 as the point at which Parcel A passed to the Williams family. The title effect remains to be established from the original instrument and related evidence.

`1877 agreement <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e115-1877-bute-thomas-agreement/>`__ · `1928 rent/quarrying record <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e120-1928-last-rent-receipt-and-end-of-quarrying/>`__

1938–1939

Parcel B — eastern agricultural land

The 1938 conveyance and 1939 WGR–John Williams tenancy establish the separate agricultural-field strand in the current reconstruction. The Wiki’s reading of the 1939 plan is that it depicts Parcel B, not the farmhouse plot.

`1938 conveyance <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e121-1938-bute-wgr-conveyance-and-plan/>`__ · `1939 tenancy and plan <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/dbdt123-7-1939-wgr-john-williams-tenancy-agreement-plan/>`__

1955 / 1962

Possession proceedings and parcel extent

The family case is that 1955 enforcement of Parcel B also took the northern woodland and south-eastern ground later registered as VG41. The 1962 proceedings concern a later stage of the occupied house group. These are disputed land-extent questions, not findings against the Community Council.

`1955 Parcel-A loss dossier <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/family-legal-case-control-room/1955-parcel-a-loss-dossier-woodland-vg41-green/>`__ · `1962 order <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/1962-possession-order/>`__

WGR → BP Pension Trust

The recorded company/title chain proceeds from Western Ground Rents to BP Pension Trust. That transfer and later registrations must not be treated, without instrument-level analysis, as proof that every part of Parcel A was conveyed.
