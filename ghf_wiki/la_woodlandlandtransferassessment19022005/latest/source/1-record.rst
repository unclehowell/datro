Thesis tested
-------------

A place name is not a parcel identity. “Leckwith Woods”, “Cwm Cydfin”, “Tŷ Mawr”, “the farm” and “the woods” may cover different ground or different interests. Separate parcels may have passed by separate instruments. No two records below are treated as the same land until their plans are overlaid.

**Result so far:** not one plan in the chain is held. Every link between these records is therefore unproven. The thesis stands as a method, not a finding.

VG41 and the Commons Registration Act 1965
------------------------------------------

Verified on legislation.gov.uk (Act as enacted). Registration did not by itself vest a green in a local authority. `s.1(3) <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1965/64/section/1/enacted>`__: a green with no registered owner was to “be vested in accordance with the following provisions of this Act”. `s.8(1) <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1965/64/section/8/enacted>`__: once the registration “has become final but no person is registered … as the owner”, then “unless the land is registered under the Land Registration Acts”, the authority “shall refer the question of the ownership of the land to a Commons Commissioner”. s.8(2)–(3): the Commissioner registers a proven owner; if not satisfied, he directs registration of the local authority. s.8(4): the land vests only “on the registration under this section”. s.8(5)(a): in an urban district, the vesting body is the urban district council. A registration became final under `s.7 <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1965/64/section/7/enacted>`__ if no objection stood at the end of the objection period. Section 8 was repealed in Wales from 6 Sep 2007 (`current text <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1965/64/section/8>`__).

**What the original VG41 file should hold** (by the Act; not yet seen): the application and map; the land section; the ownership section, showing whether any owner was registered; any objections and the date the entry became final; and, only if no owner was registered and the land was unregistered at HM Land Registry, the s.8 referral and the Commons Commissioner’s decision. In 1970 Llandough lay in Penarth Urban District, so an s.8 vesting would have been in Penarth UDC, not Llandough Community Council (formed 1982; before that the village was administered as part of Penarth). How the Community Council came to own the green (it states it “owns and maintains the village green”) is not established. If an owner was registered, no Commissioner referral would exist. The registers are open to public inspection (`s.3(2) <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1965/64/section/3/enacted>`__).

What cuts against the family’s case
-----------------------------------

- The 1876 rate book (provisional reading, `EV-0212 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0212-llandough-poor-rate-books-1870s/>`__) shows the farm (about 76 acres) owned by Bute, and 32 acres of woods in Bute’s own hands.
- The 1940–42 gift is placed “at Leckwith” in every record found; the mapped wood has 0 ha in Llandough (`EV-0220 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0220-leckwith-woods-wgr-gift-1940-1942/>`__).
- The Glamorgan Archives catalogue (DBDT123/7–13) lists WGR letting land at Llandough from 1939 to 1963. WGR treated land at Llandough as its own; `EV-0023 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0023-1939-wgr-john-williams-tenancy-plan/>`__\ ’s held plan shows Parcel B.
- DBDT121/2–3 show WGR succeeding to a Bute (Mountjoy) farm tenancy in Leckwith, so the 1938 sale was not urban ground rents only.
- If an owner was entered in VG41’s ownership section, the s.8 route never ran.
- The Forest of Cardiff chain runs through BP, the title the court accepted in 1987.

**The family’s case** is that Parcel A was not conveyed to WGR in 1938, so later transfers of it could not pass good title. Not proven by any record above. It is testable only against the 1938 plan.
