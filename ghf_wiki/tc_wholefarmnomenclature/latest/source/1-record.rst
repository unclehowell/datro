**Identity:** This page controls the historical wording “the farm”, “whole farm” and “the whole farm”. These are descriptions found in records, not self-proving legal boundaries or conveyances.

**Controlling interpretation — 9 October 2026.** Parcel A is the full 1877 house/quarrying estate, including the farmhouse/buildings, barn, stable and west-side yard, garden, north forest, and south-east ground later registered as VG41. Parcel B is the separate eastern agricultural fields. The family’s established title case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and Parcel A passed to the Williams family. The 1939 agreement/plan is already held and depicts Parcel B only. [SOURCE PENDING] Later “whole farm” wording blurred that distinction, and the 4 July 1955 enforcement of Parcel B also seized Parcel-A north forest and south-east green. A “whole farm” description must not be treated as proof that WGR had a proprietary or possessory right over the full Parcel A.

Combined-holding nomenclature
-----------------------------

- **the farm** — shorthand that, when used for this project, should mean the combined holding only where the surrounding evidence supports that reading.
- **whole farm** — explicit combined-holding formulation appearing in the historical record/reconstruction. In 1955, the family’s case is that this wording blurred the already distinct Parcel-A estate and Parcel-B agricultural possession; it does not itself prove an entitlement to both.
- **the whole farm** — expanded formulation with the same combined-holding meaning where the evidence supports it.
- **whole-farm** — adjectival form; link it to this page when it denotes the combined Great House Farm holding rather than a generic concept.
- **Parcels A and B** — an analytical description of the combined physical farm only; it does not mean that the parcels had the same owner, tenure or legal title at every date.

Temporal rule
-------------

**“The farm” is not treated here as a single continuous legal parcel throughout the entire chronology.** The combined expression can describe the earlier holding when a source genuinely treats it as one farm, including the pre-1928 context, and can describe the combined post-1987 registration position after the HMLR amalgamation. Between those points, references to “the farm” must be tested against the two-parcel structure, separate tenure/possession history and separate registration evidence. This is a nomenclature/control rule, not a claim that the 1987 amalgamation itself proves every earlier title question.
