My concern is the conduct, decision-making, record-keeping and accountability of public bodies responsible for heritage protection, planning and local government. The surviving evidence presents a series of unusually important questions. Great House Farm was professionally surveyed by the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of Wales in 1974. In December 1988, Cadw was considering the building for emergency spot-listing, with a contemporary report stating that inspectors were due to visit and consider the case. The European Commission of Human Rights records that the Convention application had been introduced on 3 May 1988; BP obtained possession on 29 November 1988; demolition began on 6 December 1988; and the application was not registered by the Commission until 12 December 1988. The evidence therefore establishes a very compressed sequence in which emergency heritage protection was being considered, domestic interim relief was refused, possession was obtained and the building was then destroyed within days. That chronology does not, by itself, establish coordination, deliberate suppression or unlawful conduct. It does, however, warrant investigation. There are several specific questions which I believe require answers from an authority independent of the present local authority: 1. What information and records were actually considered by Cadw when the 1988 spot-listing decision was made? 2. Was the 1974 RCAHMW architectural survey considered, and if not, why not? 3. What communications took place between Cadw, South Glamorgan County Council, BP Properties Ltd, the courts and any other public bodies during the critical period immediately preceding demolition? 4. What consideration was given by the local planning authority to the available statutory powers of preservation in circumstances where demolition was imminent? 5. Why was the building destroyed before there could be an independent retrospective architectural examination of the fabric? 6. How should the later archaeological evidence, including the discovery of approximately 1,026 burials in 1994, be understood in assessing the historic significance of the site and the adequacy of the earlier decision-making? I recognise that this later discovery cannot retrospectively prove that the 1988 listing decision was unlawful. 7.
