1. Manorial and ownership chain before 1877
-------------------------------------------

**PARTLY ARCHIVE-SUPPORTED — see the dedicated source page.** The pre-1877 Llandough chronology can now be anchored to the Bute Estate’s own archival catalogue and to published historical and archaeological sources. The identity lock remains **Llandough-juxta-Penarth / Cardiff, beside St Dochdwy’s**; Cowbridge-area Llandough material is excluded. The individual D219 deeds still need to be inspected before the 107-acre Cydfin/Tŷ Mawr holding is mapped precisely onto the later Great House footprint.

- **c.1106:** the published 1994 excavation report states that Llandough was granted to Tewkesbury Abbey at the close of the 11th century and that a grange was subsequently established.
- **1535:** *Cardiff Records* states that the manor of Llandough East belonged to the Abbot of Tewkesbury, with free and villein tenants paying rents of assize.
- **1543–1545:** *Cardiff Records* states that Henry VIII granted Llandough manor in 1543 to Lord Clynton and Say and Robert Turwitt; by 1545 it had been acquired by Sir George Herbert, as shown by a rent-roll; it further states that Sir George appears to have conveyed the manor to his grandson Sir William Herbert.
- **1596:** the same source records Landoche-juxta-Cardif as having free tenants, demesnes and copyholds.
- **1767–1793:** *Cardiff Records* places Llandough East, Dinas Powis and Cogan in the hands of William Hurst of Gabalva and Calvert Richard Jones in 1767 and says the subsequent history of Llandough followed that of Cogan, which was purchased by the Earl of Bute in 1793. This is **wider manorial context**, not proof by itself of a Great House conveyance. :sup:`🕒`
- **1552–1829 — major new archival lead:** NLW Bute Estate Records series **D 219** is a Llandough manorial lease series. Its catalogue explicitly identifies leases of **“Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)”**, 1552–1824, including D219/1, D219/4–5 and D219/8 onward. The Bute Estate’s M17 description separately identifies the Llandough manorial material as being in Llandough near Penarth.
- **Later D219 material:** the later deeds in the series are a priority target for reconstructing the early-19th-century ownership, tenancy, acreage and boundaries of Cydfin/Tŷ Mawr and testing the link to Great House Farm.
- **Research page:** the detailed evidence, source links, caveats and outstanding checks are now recorded at `Llandough (Penarth) — Cydfin / Tŷ Mawr and Pre-1877 Manorial Evidence <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/anomalies-register/llandough-penarth-cydfin-ty-mawr-manorial-evidence/>`__.

**Dating note.** The archive’s own research notes withdraw an earlier 1215 construction date and suggest c.1500–1530 (attributed to Sir Mathew Cradock). Neither date is sourced. RCAHMW classes the house as sub-medieval, probably seventeenth-century, possibly with a medieval core (`Where things stand <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/where-things-stand/>`__).

Kissed Frogs: source-screening and scope register
-------------------------------------------------

`What this page is about → Read the Docs <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/read-the-docs/#catalogue-pages>`__

**Scope test.** Every item is assessed against three distinct questions: (1) title and parcel identity; (2) an evidenced act of fraud, concealment or knowing misrepresentation; and (3) a documented public-authority decision, handling step or involvement. A source may be irrelevant to title but useful to the government-involvement chronology. A source-code assertion, a repeated Wiki paragraph or an archive search result is not independent proof of the claim it repeats.
