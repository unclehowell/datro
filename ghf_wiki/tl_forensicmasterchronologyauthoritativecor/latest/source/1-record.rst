**Authoritative working version.** This page supersedes any earlier 6 October chronology draft on this Wiki. It is a forensic reconstruction, not a finding of fact. Statuses are DOCUMENTED, INFERENCE, or SPECULATION / TEST REQUIRED. :sup:`🕒`

**Update — 15 October 2026.** Added a conditional 1928–1940 limitation checkpoint based on the pre-1940 twelve-year recovery period; distinguished the 1949–1953 periodic agricultural tenancy recognised in the 1987 judgment from the separately reported 1954 failed tenancy-making attempt; and recorded the source limitation that the accessible published Court of Appeal report does not mention 1954 but does refer to a 10 October 1952 agents’ letter. These are forensic distinctions and lines of enquiry, not findings that adverse possession completed or that anyone acted improperly.

**Fresh paradigm.** The central account is now the 1928 title effect and the 1955 seizure of parts of Parcel A under the “whole farm” wording used for Parcel B. The family’s established case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 when quarrying and rent to Thomas ended, vesting the full Parcel-A house/quarrying estate—including forest north of the house and the south-eastern ground later registered as VG41—in the Williams family. WGR’s later agricultural possession of Parcel B did not create a right to Parcel-A forest or green. The focus is how the 1949 permissive-occupancy attempt and the 1955 wording blurred that separation, and how the forest and green then entered later titles and records.

Core chronology
---------------

#. **1877 — DOCUMENTED / FAMILY EVIDENCE.** Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, described a Bute transaction separating the farmhouse and about 10 acres from about 9 acres retained by Bute, with a Thomas quarrying arrangement. **INFERENCE:** the legal character remains open: freehold sale versus mineral/quarry lease. Test Bute X.6(12), Bute rentals, the reported but unlocated 6 November 1877 lease lead, the uninspected D153/NLW record (see `SC-21 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/records-access-chronology/source-conflicts-register/#sc-21>`__), and Thomas records.
#. **1891–1928 — DOCUMENTED / FAMILY TITLE CASE.** Contemporary evidence places John Williams at Great House Farm by 1891 [`EV-0010 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0010-llandough-farmyard-theft-geese-1891/>`__]. Mary’s account and the rent/quarrying chronology identify 1928 as the point when quarrying ended and the last house-parcel rent was paid to Alfred Thomas [PENDING: `EV-0016 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0016-1928-last-rent-receipt-quarrying/>`__]. The family’s case is that this was the operative completion of the 1877 arrangement and that the family’s freehold covered the full Parcel A: farmhouse/buildings, barns, stable and yard west of the house, garden, forest north, and the south-eastern green ground later registered as VG41. Adverse possession is an alternative limitation analysis, not a replacement for this underlying-title case.
#. **1916–1939 — DOCUMENTED / CLOSED PARCEL CONTROL.** Later records use “whole farm”, but the 1939 WGR–John Williams agreement and plan are already held and the plan depicts Parcel B only: the eastern agricultural fields [`EV-0023 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0023-1939-wgr-john-williams-tenancy-plan/>`__]. Do not reopen the 1939 plan as a discovery task or use the wider wording in later records to turn Parcel A into part of that tenancy. The 1938 conveyance’s exact operative scope remains a separate downstream title-chain check, but the established Parcel-B-only finding is closed.
#. **1928–1940 — ALTERNATIVE LIMITATION CASE / LEGAL TEST REQUIRED.** The 12-year limitation period for recovering land pre-dated 1940: section 1 of the `Real Property Limitation Act 1874 <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/37-38/57/pdfs/ukpga_18740057_en.pdf>`__ already prescribed twelve years; the Limitation Act 1939 later consolidated the applicable law. If the right to recover Parcel A accrued when possession became adverse by about 1928 and all statutory requirements were met, the former owner’s right of action could potentially have been barred by about 1940. If adverse possession only began in 1940, the twelve-year period would ordinarily reach about 1952, not 1940. Rent-free occupation alone is not enough: identify the true title holder, the exact parcel, the accrual date, factual possession and intention to possess, any tenancy/permission, acknowledgement, disability or proceedings, and the legal effect of any order. A failed 1954 proposal would not by itself restart a period already completed, but the 1955/1962 orders and any issue-preclusion consequences require separate analysis.
#. **1949–1953 — DOCUMENTED TENANCY / FAMILY PARCEL-A POSITION.** An unwritten yearly agricultural tenancy to Frederick began on 2 February 1949 [PENDING: `EV-0025 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0025-frederick-buckler-unwritten-tenancy-1949/>`__]. The Court of Appeal later held that, despite the lack of a written agreement, Frederick had a periodic agricultural tenancy protected by the Agricultural Holdings Act; the last rent is recorded as paid in 1953. The family’s case, reading the 1939 tenancy plan as Parcel B only, is that the tenancy did not lawfully make the separate Parcel-A occupation permissive. The tenancy’s existence and its parcel scope are different questions; do not describe the whole tenancy as fictitious or as a failed agreement.
#. **1950–52 — FAMILY EVIDENCE.** Mary said papers including the farm agreement were removed from the farmhouse blanket box and the family title deed was reported missing. The chronology places this report after the 1949 tenancy began. Missing papers are important to title proof and later litigation availability; by themselves they do not prove who removed them or establish concealment. :sup:`🕒`
#. **1954 — FAMILY CHRONOLOGY / ADDITIONAL FAILED ATTEMPT; SOURCE ATTRIBUTION UNRESOLVED.** A separate further attempt to make Frederick a tenant is identified for 1954, and the family treats it as an unsuccessful effort to characterise Parcel-A occupation as permissive, distinct from the 1949–1953 agricultural tenancy. See `GHF-E182 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e182-1954-frederick-buckler-tenancy-attempt/>`__. The accessible published Court of Appeal report checked on 15 October 2026 does not mention 1954; it records a 10 October 1952 agents’ letter saying the letting would not proceed, but Dillon LJ nevertheless found that Frederick had a protected periodic tenancy. The 1954 event remains a separate family-chronology lead until its original source is attached; do not attribute it to Dillon’s judgment without a distinct source. The terms, proposed parcel, response and reason for failure remain to be established.
#. **1955–1965 — DOCUMENTED EVENT / FAMILY TITLE CASE.** On 4 July 1955, while enforcing possession of Parcel B under wording describing the “whole farm”, WGR seized the forest north of the house and the south-eastern ground later registered as VG41—both parts of Parcel A, on the family’s established account [PENDING: `EV-0034 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0034-writ-possession-execution-return-1955/>`__]. Mary held onto the farmhouse/buildings/garden group after fiercely resisting removal, in the context of a recent leg amputation and seven children; she could not physically defend the whole Parcel-A perimeter. The 1959/1965 tenancy offers and 1962 proceedings referred to the remaining occupied house-and-garden area. That narrower practical defence must not be misread as a title claim limited to the house and garden. The family’s alternative limitation case is that twelve years from 1928 would have matured by 1940 if the relevant legal conditions were met; the 1955 wording made the clock appear to begin later.
#. **1969–1975 — DOCUMENTED.** Later instruments recite the 31 December 1969 WGR→BP Pension Trust conveyance and a 1971 abstract [PENDING: `EV-0043 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0043-wgr-bp-pension-trust-conveyance/>`__; PENDING: `EV-0045 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0045-1971-abstract-title-bp-pension/>`__; PENDING: `EV-0052 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0052-bp-pension-trust-bp-properties-conveyance/>`__]. The actual root and abstract are not presently on the 30 July 2026 HMLR schedule. The 23 May 1975 BP Pension Trust→BP Properties conveyance is associated with the farmhouse title WA240304. Exact parcels remain to be proved from the instruments and plans.
#. **1974 — INFERENCE.** The 11 December 1974 date may be important because the 1974 steps culminated shortly before it. Treat the “deadline explains the October licence letters” proposition as an inference pending the original 1974 papers.
#. **1982–84 — DOCUMENTED / pivotal.** WA231076 was applied for on 30 November 1982; WA240304 on 23 February 1983 [PENDING: `EV-0066 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0066-hmlr-wa240304-item-10-register-1983/>`__; PENDING: `EV-0069 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0069-hmlr-wa240304-item-7-d23-1983/>`__; PENDING: `EV-0070 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0070-hmlr-wa240304-item-4-correspondence-1983/>`__]. The files contain post-death correspondence on 8 and 12 April 1983, a further 12 May 1983 Linklaters batch, and a 13 January 1984 A3 application/new register edition. **Highest priority:** WA231076 item 17, Form A13, the list of documents lodged at first registration. Establish the title class, notices, occupation questions, requisitions and applicant responses.
#. **1987 — DOCUMENTED.** An amalgamation application was made on 2 February 1987 while the appeal process was live [PENDING: `EV-0078 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0078-1987-title-amalgamation-application-plan/>`__]. The question is the exact legal and spatial effect of the merger, not whether amalgamation was inherently improper.
#. **1984–89 — DOCUMENTED / LEGAL TEST.** The Court of Appeal dealt with limitation and licence issues and recorded that the claimed title documents were not produced [`EV-0080 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0080-bp-properties-v-buckler-judgment/>`__]. The Wiki now uses: **“The documentary title and root of title to Parcel A have not yet been shown to have been fully examined in the surviving court record.”** Any *Takhar* route must prove fraud in obtaining the judgment, not merely historic document loss.
#. **1988 — DOCUMENTED.** The farmhouse was demolished on 6 December 1988, immediately after the recorded 5 December Cadw listing decision. Heritage records must be reconstructed separately from the title case.
#. **1989–1994 — DOCUMENTED / OPEN.** Planning, heritage and archaeology records reveal consultation, condition and archive questions. These become relevant to an accountability case only where a document establishes actor, information available, decision, power and causal effect.
#. **1994–2005 — DOCUMENTED / OPEN.** WA735527 was transferred to Forest of Cardiff in 1994 and changed by a 2005 excision [PENDING: `EV-0136 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0136-forest-of-cardiff-woodland-transfer-1994/>`__; PENDING: `EV-0139 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0139-forest-cardiff-goldsworthy-transfer-excision/>`__]. Historic Parcel A woodland must be overlaid against the 1975, 1982, 1994 and 2005 plans before any recovery claim is quantified.
#. **2026 — DOCUMENTED.** HMLR schedules, title material, judicial archive identification, RCAHMW responses, museum material and archaeological evidence are now being assembled. Each discovery date should state what was unknown, when it was found, where it came from and why it matters. :sup:`🕒`

Fraud / concealment evidential ladder
-------------------------------------

**Do not leap from absence to scandal.** The proof ladder is: missing record → identifiable custodian → expected existence → timing → competing claim → knowledge → intentional withholding/suppression → causation → legal consequence. This applies equally to private custodians and public authorities. :sup:`🕒`

Woodland recovery theory
------------------------

The family should seek to identify an exact woodland polygon supported by primary title evidence. If that polygon is shown to be family land which later entered a registered title by mistake or an ineffective disposition, the legal question becomes declaration/rectification/recovery rather than purchase. The 1994 transfer, consideration, notice and 2005 excision must be established first.

Legal routes
------------

- **Limitation Act 1980 s.32:** potentially postpones limitation where the action is based on fraud, deliberate concealment or mistake until discovery or when it could reasonably have been discovered. Deliberate concealment requires intentional conduct; recklessness alone is insufficient.
- **Takhar v Gracefield Developments Ltd [2019] UKSC 13:** potentially supports a fresh fraud-based challenge to a judgment, but the fraud must concern obtaining the judgment and the facts must fit the abuse-of-process safeguards.
- **CPR 52.30:** final appeals can be reopened only in exceptional circumstances where necessary to avoid real injustice and there is no alternative effective remedy. :sup:`🕒`
- **CPR 31.16:** pre-action disclosure may be available where its statutory conditions are satisfied.
- **LRA 2002:** registered-land adverse possession is governed by Schedule 6 and s.96 disapplies Limitation Act 1980 s.15 against registered estates; historic pre-2003 events require the applicable historical/transitional analysis.
- **LRA 1925 ss.115–116:** historical provisions expressly addressed suppression of documents/facts in registration proceedings and fraudulent procurement of register entries. They are legal tests for historical conduct, not findings that fraud occurred.
