**INFERENCE / PROVISIONAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT A TRANSCRIPTION OR QUOTATION FROM THE AGREEMENT**

The recorded examination of the plan identifies the mapped holding as the eastern agricultural fields (working label **Parcel B**), not the farmhouse plot (**Parcel A**). Read alongside the `1938 Bute–WGR conveyance and plan <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e121-1938-bute-wgr-conveyance-and-plan/>`__, this is strong evidence for testing the proposition that the 1939 tenancy dealt with the fields rather than the house parcel. It does not, by itself, prove root title to Parcel A or establish any wording, covenant, rent, term or other clause not visible in the source.

Compare the `Parcel A / Parcel B title analysis <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/claim-parcel-a-parcel-b-distinct-histories-1987-amalgamation/>`__.

Status update — 17 October 2026
-------------------------------

**Previous status “CLOSED” is now qualified.** The plan still depicts Parcel B only. However, the typed terms visible on the source photo (monthly let at 8s, kept “as meadow or pasture”, John Williams tenant, WGR landlord) and the TNA DBDT catalogue placement among small Llandough plot lets (not farm tenancies) mean the document reads as a separate grazing let rather than a revised farm tenancy. This undercuts reliance on it for the 1928 last-rent limitation point. Page 1 (parcels and acreage) is not online; family copy, if held, remains an active retrieval priority.

Why it remains in the evidence chain
------------------------------------

It fixes the documentary character of the 1939 land let. It supports testing that WGR documentation dealt with Parcel B; it does not independently prove the family’s title to Parcel A or the 1955 taking of Parcel A.

The family position remains that rent on Parcel A had ceased in 1928, while Parcel B was the agricultural tenancy subject lost in 1955.

Active next step
----------------

Obtain Page 1 of the 1939 agreement (parcels + acreage). Until then treat the grazing-let reading as the stronger catalogue-based interpretation.
