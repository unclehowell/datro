.. _who:

Who holds what
--------------

- **1968–2008:** HTV (Harlech Television) held the ITV franchise for Wales and the West. In 1974 and 1988 the ITV news service in Wales was HTV Wales.
- **2002:** the HTV service in Wales was renamed ITV1 Wales. **2014:** renamed ITV Cymru Wales.
- **2012:** ITV Wales donated its archive, about 250,000 items from 1958 onward including TWW and HTV material and a daily log of major news stories, to the National Library of Wales Screen and Sound Archive. ITV kept full access and commercial rights. The Library makes catalogued items available to readers.
- **Today:** ITV Cymru Wales has an archivist based in Aberystwyth who handles research requests.

*Sources: Wikipedia, “Timeline of ITV in Wales”; National Library of Wales press release, “ITV Wales archive donated to the National Library of Wales” (2012); National Library of Wales, Screen and Sound Archive, ITV collection page.*

14 October 2026 — ITV phone call: open questions, risks and potential remedy
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Evidence basis:** Sion Buckler’s account of his phone conversation with Owen at ITV. The account is not an independently verified transcript. Statements below are questions for investigation, not findings of wrongdoing.

The unresolved editorial questions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

- **Family context and possible omission:** Sion states that the clip he has viewed does not mention that his mother was pregnant or that three infant children were part of the family context. Was that information included in the original broadcast? If not, was its absence an editorial choice, an instruction, a time/format limitation, or simply a difference between the surviving clip and the transmitted report?
- **Did the clip air in this form?** The exact programme, transmission date, running order, original broadcast copy and length have not been identified. The extant viewing copy cannot by itself establish what viewers saw in 1988.
- **Abrupt cut and later editing:** After the voice-over describes the land as “prime development land overlooking Cardiff” and the clip shows a person the family has interpreted as a BP bailiff with a paramilitary appearance, the copy cuts abruptly. Was this the original broadcast edit, a later re-edit, a rights/licensing extract, a technical cut or a later editorial decision? Was any material removed before the clip was published online in 2025? No censorship finding is made.
- **Instructions, confidentiality or NDA:** Is there documentary evidence of any instruction to omit the family context, or any confidentiality agreement that covered the story? No NDA has been identified. If an agreement existed, who was a party, when was it made, what material and subject matter did it cover, and was it story-specific or a standard/general agreement? It would be unsafe to assume that Nicola Heywood-Thomas was bound by any such agreement.
- **Presenter’s personal connection:** Sion’s personal recollection is that Nicola Heywood-Thomas was the mother of his best friend and felt like a second mother to him in approximately 2001–2003. He does not recall her mentioning the Great House Farm coverage or footage. That recollection explains why the question matters to him, but neither silence nor personal connection proves that she knew of the 1988 case, had a duty to disclose anything, or was subject to restrictions.

What the 14 October call did — and did not — establish
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Sion appreciated that Owen phoned, asked about the story and sought to understand how ITV might help. Sion said that practical help would include addressing the family’s specific questions. Sion reports that the call then ended abruptly but politely. On the present account, no specific archive search, disclosure undertaking, correction, compensation proposal, settlement offer or admission was made. The call may be a constructive opening for a voluntary, pre-litigation resolution, but it is not yet evidence that ITV offered a remedy without admission of liability. That question should be put directly and neutrally.

PRINCE2 issues, risks, concerns and opportunity
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

- **Issue:** the original transmission and edit history remain unresolved, and the case chronology must keep the 30 November NLW catalogue item, the YouTube viewing copy recorded as published on 25 July 2025, and Owen’s separate reported December 2025 reference distinct until source metadata confirms their relationship.
- **Risk — evidence degradation:** broadcast logs, scripts, edit notes, rushes, upload records and correspondence may be difficult to locate after nearly four decades; prompt preservation and a defined search are valuable.
- **Risk — over-inference:** treating an abrupt cut, a missing reply, or the presenter’s personal connection as proof of censorship, a secret agreement or dishonest intent would exceed the evidence. Keep allegation, memory, inference and documented fact distinct.
- **Opportunity — present-day cooperation:** ITV may be able to provide an accurate transmission history, search surviving archives, identify the source and edit date of the YouTube copy, locate scripts/running orders, explain the 2025 request-handling chronology, and state what can or cannot be established. Such assistance could have greater practical value to the family than pursuing compensation for an unproven historic wrong.
- **Parallel dependency:** Sion told Owen that BBC Wales producer Elenid Hatcher had been made aware of the story weeks earlier and was reviewing it. This is Sion’s report of the status of that separate BBC Wales enquiry; it is not a finding about BBC or ITV.

16 October 2026 — separate NLW catalogue item and chronology controls

A National Library of Wales Watch and Listen catalogue record has been identified at `item 507738 <https://gwylio-gwrando.llyfrgell.cymru/detail/507738>`__, titled “Eviction News Material, 30 November 1988”. The supplied record describes a one-minute HTV Wales Betacam item whose recording and broadcast dates are both 30 November 1988. Its abstract describes Miles Fletcher pieces to camera, bailiffs removing furniture, removal vans and Branwen leaving the farm, and says the High Court granted an injunction stopping BP demolishing the farm.

The catalogue itself says “Not viewed”; it notes that the tape contains unrelated clips and that this clip’s position is unknown, so the description may come from a broadcaster’s log. No catalogue-created or online-publication date appears in the record as supplied. It is not the separate 5 December 1988 “Wales at Six” demolition headline/report. Neither the record’s existence nor its normal rights restrictions prove concealment, deliberate editing or an exceptional NDA.

**Open questions to NLW:** ask Beryl Evans / the Enquiries team when the catalogue record was created and when it first became available online; search Watch and Listen and the wider catalogue for separate 5 December 1988 HTV Wales / “Wales at Six” demolition material; identify any candidate records, their catalogue identifiers, stated recording/broadcast dates and whether they represent a full programme, off-air recording, raw clip or compilation. Do not treat a negative search as proof of non-existence without recording the search scope and date.

**Open questions to ITV:** ask whether the full 5 December report or its transmission log, running order, script, rushes/outtakes and edit history survive; whether the apparent “prime development land” cut belongs to the original broadcast or a later copy; and whether footage involving Nicola Heywood-Thomas was broadcast or retained in another version. Ask neutrally whether relevant editorial or confidentiality records exist; no case-specific NDA has been identified.

**Chronology control:** keep separate fields for event date, recording date, broadcast date, catalogue creation date, online-publication date, disclosure-request/response date, and later YouTube upload date. Where a field is not supplied or verified, record “unknown” rather than inferring it.
