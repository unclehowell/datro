Anticipated objections and the family’s answers
-----------------------------------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Objection
     - The family’s answer
   * - **“The courts have already decided this.”**
     - The 1987 Court of Appeal decided BP’s possession claim, including its limitation analysis and the effect of BP’s 1974 letters. The family’s current case concerns the full Parcel-A extent and the asserted 1928 title basis, while the surviving judicial summaries focus on possession, limitation and permission. The exact issues pleaded and decided must be described from the available judgments and pleadings; do not state categorically that no title issue was ever put or that the full Parcel-A boundary was exhaustively adjudicated. Fraud-based reopening and CPR 52.30 are separate, demanding routes and must not be asserted without the required evidence and legal advice.
   * - **“It is far too late.”**
     - Under section 32 of the Limitation Act 1980, time does not run while fraud or deliberate concealment keeps the facts hidden. Key records are only now coming to light: the 1924 record held by HM Land Registry, and the collections records Amgueddfa Cymru found in October 2026. Others are still being withheld or reported missing. :sup:`🕒`
   * - **“The family were only tenants or licensees.”**
     - The already-obtained 1939 WGR–John Williams agreement/plan depicts Parcel B, the eastern agricultural fields, only. It does not include Parcel A. On the family’s established title case, the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and the full Parcel A—including north forest and south-east green ground—passed to the Williams family. The alternative limitation case is that twelve years from 1928 would have matured by 1940 if the applicable statutory conditions were met. [SOURCE PENDING] The 1949 attempt to cast Mary’s occupancy as permissive failed; the 1955 “whole farm” wording then obscured that earlier position.
   * - **“BP is the registered owner, and the register is conclusive.”**
     - The register can be rectified to correct a mistake (Land Registration Act 2002, Schedule 4). When BP first registered in 1982–83, the family was in actual occupation, and the rights of people in actual occupation overrode first registration under section 70(1)(g) of the Land Registration Act 1925. Where rectification is refused, indemnity is available under Schedule 8.
   * - **“Missing records prove nothing.”** :sup:`🕒`
     - The records are missing from the files of bodies that had a duty to keep them: Cadw’s 1988 listing file, the planning appendices, GGAT’s correspondence, and the 1969 root conveyance from the Land Registry schedule. Those bodies have to account for the gaps. Where a party has lost or destroyed evidence, the courts may draw inferences against it. :sup:`🕒`
   * - **“Strasbourg rejected the case in 1989.”**
     - The European Commission of Human Rights ruled the application inadmissible on the 1988 record, without the title documents. That decision does not bar domestic proceedings on new evidence, or complaints about how public bodies have handled the records since 2000.
   * - **“The family members’ statements are self-serving.”**
     - Mary (Nancy) Williams’s statement dates from about 1974, more than a decade before the 1986–87 litigation. A 1974 newspaper report independently records her ownership claim and a lawyer’s view that she had a case. William Buckler’s 1983 diary records the Daniel Thomas receipts. These are contemporaneous records, not accounts made up for a lawsuit.
