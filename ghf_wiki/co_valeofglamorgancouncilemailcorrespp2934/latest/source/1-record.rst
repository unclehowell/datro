Editorial warning — 1938 “Cowbridge and Llandough Great House Farm” name trap
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**[PRESS REPORT; source references reported in the research file]** Do not identify the 1938 auction of the 185-acre “Cowbridge and Llandough Great House Farm”, reported in the Western Mail on 12 and 16 November 1938, as the Llandough-juxta-Penarth Tŷ Mawr property. The supplied research identifies it as a different farm, sold to L. D. Thomas. Until the sale particulars and plan are inspected, this is a strong identity warning, not a substitute for the primary plan.

**[CATALOGUE ENTRY ONLY]** Glamorgan Archives DSA/12/5400, described as a transfer of tenancy from “J. Williams”, concerns a different J. Williams and must not be used as a record of the Great House Farm family absent evidence matching the person, farm and parcel.

The correspondence catalogue below preserves the original requests/messages as historical records. A reference appearing in an old request is not confirmation that the transaction belongs to this case.

   | Buckler (Court of Appeal, 1987)\*.

   | I appreciate that the Local Planning Authority is not responsible for
   | determining land ownership, nor am I asking it to do so. My purpose is to
   | place on record a \*chronological summary of unresolved land-governance
   | issues\* which may be material to the integrity of the planning history
   | relied upon for that site.

   In brief and as reflected in public records:

   | – In \*July 1974\*, proceedings involving my grandmother and BP both
   | asserting ownership, were adjourned and did not result in a judicial final
   | determination.

   | – On \*31 October 1974\*, a further listed hearing did not proceed
   | substantively, following correspondence sent that morning by BP entities.
   | No judgment on title was issued. My nan requested the audience with the
   | judge the day before, with her lawyer. But that got leaked, the press
   | descended on our farm (not that I can find evidence of news articles from
   | said press), and the following morning the two letters from BP arrived to
   | our solicitor, effectively squashing the court hearing and audience with
   | the judge my nan had sought, to make her case of ownership. Her reply to
   | both BP letters (one from BP Pension Trust Ltd and one from BP Properties
   | Ltd), that morning of the 31st of October, was also 'I own the land'.

   | – Throughout this period, my grandmother was \*excluded from dealings\*
   | concerning land we asserted we owned, while the opposing party
   | continued to \*assert
   | ownership\*, notwithstanding the absence of a concluded judicial finding.

   | – In or around \*1982\*, the land was registered in BP’s name, despite the
   | earlier proceedings never having resolved the competing claims nor having
   | resulted in findings adverse to my grandmother.

   | – My grandmother then died in \*1983\*, after the registration had taken
   | place, without compensation and without any final adjudication of her claim.

   | – In the \*1987 appeal\*, the Court referred to correspondence as having
   | been replied to (albeit not to the correct corporate entity – which is
   | ultimately why we lost BP vs Buckler 1987), yet in my fathers appeal that
   | followed, whereby he argued that surely BP Pension Trust should have been
   | the claimant not BP Properties, the same judge from the case dismissed the
   | appeal citing that both companies were effectively the same. This is all
   | evidence on the face of the public record of these proceedings.

   | – By that stage, and in the absence of a documentary title which had
   | effectively been overtaken by registration, my family was driven to advance
   | arguments in adverse possession as a \*fallback position\*, which itself
   | should have assured our continued occupancy.

   | The cumulative effect was that my family was \*displaced\*, the land was
   | developed, and no compensation was ever paid, despite ownership never
   | having been conclusively decided in court. Whether intentionally or not,
   | the process operated in a manner where asserted ownership became treated as
   | settled fact.

   | I am not alleging misconduct, nor am I seeking retrospective remedies from
   | the Authority. I am simply asking whether, given the above sequence, it
   | would be appropriate for the Authority to:

   | – note that historic planning decisions were taken on the basis of \*assumed
   | title\*, and

   | – record that the underlying ownership dispute was \*never finally
   | adjudicated\*, for the sake of transparency and future governance
   | integrity.

   | This correspondence is intended to be factual, measured, and
   | non-adversarial. I am happy to supply references to the relevant hearings,
   | registrations, or judgments if that assists your records. But what I'm
   | highlighting here is all on public record and in the public eye.

   Yours faithfully,

   Sion Buckler
