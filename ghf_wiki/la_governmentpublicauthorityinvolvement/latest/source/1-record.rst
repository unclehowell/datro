This branch is the master index for party-by-party forensic assessments of every identified third-party actor appearing in the Great House Farm event, litigation, historical record chain or present recovery campaign. The unit of analysis is the legally or factually distinct institution, company, professional firm, court, police/prosecution body, public authority, named individual or other identifiable actor—not a generic category such as “government”. Each review has its own chronology, identified actors, specific acts or omissions, evidence trail, legal-function context, disputed issues and potential responsibility questions.

Actor-by-actor forensic standard
--------------------------------

- **Legal/entity identity:** identify the precise institution, corporate entity, partnership, court, police/prosecution body or other legal person involved.
- **Named individuals:** identify officers, judges, solicitors, surveyors, archaeologists, investigators, agents, directors and other individuals where the evidence actually identifies them.
- **Specific conduct:** record the particular act, decision, omission, instruction, communication, filing, report, search, enforcement step or failure—not merely that an organisation was “involved”.
- **Authority and duty:** identify the statutory, contractual, professional, procedural or other function that applied at the relevant time, where established.
- **Evidence and causation:** link each proposition to the existing Wiki evidence and chronology and identify the asserted or demonstrated consequence.
- **Liability discipline:** distinguish established fact, reasonable inference, allegation, unresolved question and evidence still required. The existence of a review is not itself an allegation of wrongdoing or liability. :sup:`🕒10-10-2026`

Court and judicial-decision review standard
-------------------------------------------

A court review must assess the court’s **adjudicative role** as well as the custody or administration of its records. For each relevant order or judgment, identify the issues pleaded and argued; the evidence and parcel plans actually before the decision-maker; the legal and factual premises used; the propositions decided versus treated as assumption or background; whether a party had a fair opportunity to challenge a material proposition; whether later registration or title events could have affected the record before the court; and the order’s legal and physical scope. Apply this to the 1955 High Court possession order, Hollis J’s 1986 trial, the 1962 Cardiff County Court proceedings, the 1987 Court of Appeal appeal, the House of Lords leave process and the Strasbourg admissibility decision.

This standard is not an accusation of judicial wrongdoing. An adverse result or a judge’s legal conclusion does not prove misconduct; conversely, courts are not exempt from evidence-led scrutiny of the material before them, the reasoning given and the consequences of any demonstrable mistake or procedural deficiency.

Liability / responsibility identification register
--------------------------------------------------

Every dedicated review should answer, as far as the evidence permits: **Who?** **What entity or individual?** **What did they do or fail to do?** **When?** **What power, duty, contract or professional function applied?** **What evidence proves it?** **What consequence followed?** **What remains disputed?** and **what further evidence is required before attributing liability?** Where an individual is not yet identified, the review records the role or office and makes identification an explicit evidence-recovery task rather than guessing a name.
