**BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ… … read more**

**BP Properties Ltd v Buckler** [1987] EWCA Civ 2, (1988) 55 P&CR 337, [1987] 2 EGLR 168, is the Court of Appeal decision that ended the principal 1980s possession appeal. It is a leading English case on adverse possession and on the effect of a licence the occupier never accepted. The reported appeal addressed possession, limitation and the effect of the 1974 letters. The family’s current contention concerns the full Parcel-A title said to arise when the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928: farmhouse/buildings; barn, stable and yard west of the house; garden; the northern forest; and the south-eastern green later registered as VG41. The complete pleadings and judgment should be used to identify precisely which title issues and parcel extents were put and decided; the page should not state categorically that title was never examined unless the record supports that formulation. The family then took the case to the European Commission of Human Rights, which declared it inadmissible in 1989 (`below <#echr>`__).

Interpretation: 1954 tenancy attempt, the court’s reasoning and the 1987 amalgamation
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**The alternative 1928–1940 limitation calculation.** The twelve-year period for recovery of land was already provided by section 1 of the `Real Property Limitation Act 1874 <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/37-38/57/pdfs/ukpga_18740057_en.pdf>`__; it did not first come into existence in 1940. If, as Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, witness statement c.1974 (`Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/testimony/mary-williams/>`__) indicates (last rent paid 1928), the relevant owner’s right to recover Parcel A accrued when occupation became adverse by about 1928, a twelve-year period could potentially have completed by about 1940. If the clock began only in 1940, twelve years would reach about 1952—not 1940. These are calculations conditional on proof: non-payment of rent alone does not establish adverse possession, and the true owner, parcel, possession, intention, permission/tenancy, acknowledgment and relevant proceedings must be established.

A failed attempt to make occupation permissive in 1954 would not, merely by being proposed, restart a limitation period already completed. But the legal effect of the 1955 and 1962 possession orders is separate and potentially decisive. The 1955 order was against Frederick and is described in the appeal report as covering the whole farm; the 1962 proceedings were brought against Frederick and Mary for the farmhouse and garden [PENDING: `EV-0041 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0041-cardiff-county-court-possession-order-1962/>`__]. The orders’ exact land, pleadings, issues decided and consequences for any earlier Parcel-A title must be tested directly, rather than assumed from the words “whole farm” or from the later adverse-possession calculation.

**Attribution check:** the accessible published 1987 Court of Appeal report does not mention the year 1954. It records a 10 October 1952 agents’ letter saying WGR did not propose to proceed with the letting, yet Dillon LJ nevertheless concluded that Frederick had a protected periodic agricultural tenancy. The separate 1954 failed attempt remains tracked at `GHF-E182 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e182-1954-frederick-buckler-tenancy-attempt/>`__ pending the source supporting it; it should not be attributed to Dillon’s judgment without a separate, identified passage.

The family chronology identifies a further failed attempt to make Frederick Buckler a tenant in 1954, distinct from the unwritten 1949–1953 agricultural tenancy. This is tracked in `GHF-E182 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence-library/ghf-e182-1954-frederick-buckler-tenancy-attempt/>`__; the original source is pending. The published appeal judgment treated the 1949 tenancy as a real periodic tenancy protected by the Agricultural Holdings Act despite the absence of a written agreement. The question is whether that tenancy’s land-scope—and the distinct 1954 proposal—could properly be used to describe the separate Parcel-A occupation as permissive.

The Court of Appeal did not simply accept Hollis J’s view that Frederick’s possible willingness to take a tenancy defeated adverse possession. Dillon LJ described the trial judge’s treatment of Mary’s intention as paradoxical and found that Frederick and Mary were jointly in adverse possession of the farmhouse and garden from 2 February 1955. The appeal was nevertheless dismissed because the 1962 possession order could still be enforced and the 1974 letters made Mary a licensee for limitation purposes.

The judgment’s references to BP’s paper title and registration must be tested separately from those possession findings. The current Wiki title chronology records two separate title numbers (WA231076 and WA240304) still live after the 1986 trial, and an amalgamation application dated 2 February 1987 while the appeal was pending [PENDING: `EV-0078 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0078-1987-title-amalgamation-application-plan/>`__]. That is a question about the exact parcel extent and what was before the court—not proof that the court or parties knew of a defect. Two title numbers do not, by themselves, prove different ownership.

The published appeal’s statement that the result might have been different concerned Mary expressly repudiating the 1974 licence letters. It should not be restated as a finding that a full trial of the 1877 root title would have yielded a different result unless that proposition is sourced independently. The complete pleadings, trial evidence, appeal bundle, registers/plans and amalgamation file are needed before concluding that a material fact was misstated or that procedure was unfair.

**Evidence description:** William Buckler applied to the Commission on 3 May 1988 (registered 12 December 1988, No. 14464/88) [`EV-0122 <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/ev-0122-echr-application-14464-88-inadmissibility/>`__]. He complained of an unfair trial and judicial bias (Article 6), including that the judge ignored a previous court ruling and failed to distinguish between the current and previous title holders; interference with his home (Article 8) and his possessions (Article 1 of Protocol 1); and lack of an effective remedy (Article 13). While the application was pending BP took possession and demolished the farmhouse (`1988 possession and demolition <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/demolition-1988/#demolition>`__).

On 14 April 1989 the Commission declared the application inadmissible: the fair-trial complaint was not substantiated; the loss of the home was lawful and justified to protect BP’s rights as owner; he had no “possession” in law because the courts held the property belonged to another; and unsuccessful appeals are still an effective remedy. The Commission ruled on the domestic courts’ findings; it did not see the title documents either.

Occupied house groupThe farmhouse was his home within the full Parcel A, whose extent also included the north forest and south-east ground later registered as VG41 on the family’s case.

**What the judgments’ history omits**

**Research note.** The available judgment and Commission summaries start from the 1916 tenancy and the later possession chronology. The family’s separate title case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and carried the full Parcel-A house/quarrying estate—not just the occupied house group—to the Williams family. The family also relies on Mary’s statement, the 1938 conveyance, the already-obtained 1939 Parcel-B-only tenancy plan, the reported loss of title papers in 1950–52, the failed 1949 permissive-occupancy attempt and the later 1955 “whole farm” description. These points should be compared against the exact issues and evidence before the courts; their omission from a summary alone does not prove concealment or establish that no title issue was raised.
