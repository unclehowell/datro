1. Description
--------------

- **14–20 Nov 2025.** Sion wrote to Kanishka Narayan MP about BP Properties v Buckler (`M01 <#m01>`__, report attached). The Clerk replied on 20 Nov (6 days later) to Sion and the MP: “I regret to inform you that we are unable to assist with the matter you raise” (`M02 <#m02>`__). Sion acknowledged it the same morning (`M03 <#m03>`__).

- **18 May 2026.** Sion asked for disclosure of all LCC records on Ty Mawr, the farm lands, the Williams and Buckler families, BP v Buckler, and any meeting at which Billy Buckler addressed the Council c.1988 (`M04 <#m04>`__). The Clerk acknowledged it on 20 May as an FOI request (`M05 <#m05>`__, 2 days).

- **14 Jun 2026.** The Clerk answered (`M06 <#m06>`__, 27 calendar days, inside the 20-working-day limit): minutes from 1982, when LCC was formed, were deposited at Glamorgan Archives in 2002; before 1982 Llandough came under Penarth Town Council; section 21 FOIA (information reasonably accessible elsewhere) applied; and he pointed to the Council’s “Buildings of Significance” web page for history. Sion replied the same day (`M07 <#m07>`__), asking whether LCC holds any records **other than** the deposited minutes, and whether any record exists of Billy Buckler addressing the Council. **No direct answer to** `M07 <#m07>`__ **appears in the mailbox: 126 days to 18 Oct 2026.** Sion also sent a thank-you note that evening (`M08 <#m08>`__).

- **20 Sep 2026.** Sion wrote to Councillor Misra with the family history and a heritage proposal (`M09 <#m09>`__, PDF attached). The Chair replied the same day (`M10 <#m10>`__): the Clerk “has already directed you to all the information we have … We have nothing more to add.”

- **21 Sep 2026.** Sion made a narrower FOIA/EIR request (`M11 <#m11>`__): post-2002 records; photographs of any date; what passed from Penarth Town Council in 1982. On 28 Sep the Chair passed it to the Clerk (`M12 <#m12>`__, 7 days).

- **11 Oct 2026.** The Clerk refused the 21 Sep request under section 12 (cost limit £450 = 18 hours) (`M15 <#m15>`__, 20 days after the request; 13 days after the hand-over). His estimate: 224 sets of minutes 2002–2016 at “about 20 minutes to read each” and “about 10 minutes to assess each”, stated as “a total of over 2240 minutes which equates to 37 hours”. (224 × 10 min = 2,240 min, about 37 h; at 20 + 10 = 30 min each the total would be 6,720 min, 112 h. The email’s arithmetic counts only the 10 minutes.) He suggested narrowing to e.g. 2002–2006 and offered internal review and the ICO. The other matters “will be considered at the October meeting of the council”; **no date was given.**

- **13 Oct 2026.** Sion asked for an internal review (`M16 <#m16>`__, then a replacement version, `M17 <#m17>`__). The same evening the Clerk acknowledged it (“a full response will be sent to you in due course”) and sent the agenda and a Zoom link (`M18 <#m18>`__). **The attached agenda (dated 13 Oct) says the meeting is on Thu 22 Oct 2026 at 7.00pm “in the Council Chamber located at the rear of the Llandough War Memorial Hall. Any Councillor or member of the press or public may attend either in person or remotely.”** Item 5 is “Meeting with Councillor G. Carroll”. Item 10 is “a request from Mr SB” (Sion’s two requests: passing his details to former members and residents, and an interpretation board near the green).

- **16 Oct 2026.** Sion sent two emails at 08:45 and 08:54 (`M20 <#m20>`__, `M21 <#m21>`__). At 09:53 the Clerk wrote: “the Council meeting on 22 October will now be held remotely only” (`M22 <#m22>`__). **That is a change from the 13 Oct agenda (in person or remote) to remote only, 3 days later and 6 days before the meeting.** No reason was given in the email. Sion objected at 13:31 (`M23 <#m23>`__), asking who decided it, when, and under what policy. **No reply by 18 Oct 2026 (2 days).**

- **Open as of 18 Oct 2026:** the internal review requested 13 Oct (acknowledged, no outcome); the 14 Jun questions (`M07 <#m07>`__); the 16 Oct objection (`M23 <#m23>`__); the Clerk’s promise (`M15 <#m15>`__) to report the Council’s views after the October meeting.

- **Cllr George Carroll** is a Vale of Glamorgan councillor, not an LCC member; his emails are on the `Vale of Glamorgan Council correspondence page <https://research.bucklerfamily.estate/evidence/vale-of-glamorgan-council-correspondence/>`__. **M15 · 11 Oct 2026 09:42 UTC · Paul Egan (Clerk, Llandough Community Council) · Re: Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr), Llandough — request for information under FOIA 2000 / EIR 2004**

Dear Mr Buckler, Thank you for your email of 21 September 2026 making a request for information. We are treating your correspondence as a request for information under the Freedom of Information Act 2000 (the act). Under the provisions of the act and as defined by “The Freedom of Information and Data Protection (Appropriate Limit and Fees) Regulations 2004” (the regulations) , an authority is not obliged to comply with a request for information if the authority estimates that the cost of complying with the request would exceed the “appropriate limit”. This appropriate limit is set by the regulations at £450 for our type of authority. In the regulations our staff time is valued at £25 per hour. This has the effect of imposing a time limit of 18 hours of staff time for activities specified in the regulations. The regulations make the following activities by an authority countable towards the cost limit. \* determining whether it holds the information, \* locating the information, or a document which may contain the information, \* retrieving the information, or a document which may contain the information, and \* extracting the information from a document containing it. We have estimated the time and therefore costs as provided by the regulations, and in doing so have taken into account the above activities. We estimate that this work will take in excess of 18 hours of effort by our staff. As we have a significant amount of documents on file from the period 2002-2016 where minutes are in documents rather than published on the website or in archives. We estimate this will exceed the limit provided for by the regulations and therefore your request is refused on the grounds provided for under section 12 of the act. Our estimate of time is based on the requirement to search all minutes of the council and its committees for a period of 14 years. It is estimated that it will take about 20 minutes to read each set of minutes of which there are estimated to 224 sets of minutes.
