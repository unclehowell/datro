

To whom it may concern,

I am writing as the child of Branwen and William (“Billy”) Buckler 
regarding the destruction of Great House Farm, Llandough, and the 
long-running dispute surrounding BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987].

I have compiled a substantial historical archive, including newspaper 
reports, court references, family testimony, archaeological material, 
estate history, and public records relating to Great House Farm (“Ty 
Mawr”), a site associated with medieval, monastic, and Roman occupation.

The archive is currently summarized at:

bpvsbuckler.datro.xyz

The material raises serious unresolved questions regarding:

• The treatment of the Williams/Buckler family’s longstanding occupation 
and asserted ownership claims. • The repeated use of possession proceedings 
without a full adjudication of superior title. • Missing or allegedly 
suppressed title documentation connected to Bute estate transfers and later 
transactions. • The demolition of an allegedly medieval structure during an 
active heritage controversy. • Whether the archaeological significance of 
the site was fully disclosed during planning and demolition processes. • 
The wider public interest and cultural significance of the site and its 
destruction.

Contemporaneous newspaper coverage from the 1970s and 1980s demonstrates 
that these concerns were publicly known at the time, including heritage 
objections, petitions, and public claims of ancestral ownership.

I am requesting:

   1. Review and preservation consideration of all surviving records 
   connected to Great House Farm.
   2. Clarification as to whether any formal heritage review or 
   investigation into the demolition process was ever completed.
   3. Guidance regarding access to archival and planning materials relevant 
   to the site.
   4. Consideration of whether this matter warrants renewed historical or 
   heritage examination.

I appreciate that some claims remain disputed or unproven, and I am not 
asking recipients to accept every allegation as established fact. However, 
I believe the totality of the historical material warrants serious review 
rather than dismissal.

I would be grateful for acknowledgement of receipt and guidance on the 
appropriate next steps.

Kind regards,

Sion Buckler Grandson of Mary Williams / son of Branwen and William Buckler
