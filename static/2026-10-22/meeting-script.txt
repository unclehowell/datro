GREAT HOUSE FARM / TŶ MAWR — STATEMENT TO LLANDOUGH COMMUNITY COUNCIL
Thursday 22 October 2026, 7.00pm. About three minutes.

Chair, councillors, thank you.

My grandmother was Mary Williams of Great House Farm, Tŷ Mawr, beside St Dochdwy's Church. Our family account is that we lived in that house for 320 consecutive years, from 1667 until it was demolished at about four in the morning on 6 December 1988.

I am grateful this is on the agenda. But Item 10 describes it as a plaque and a mailing list, and I want you to see the size of what sits under Church View Close.

It is the likely ground of St Dochdwy's monastery, one of the great early Christian centres of Wales, whose site has never been formally located. It is where 1,026 people were buried in the early medieval period, the largest such population yet recovered in Wales; their remains are in museum storage. It is the house where, according to the official Historic Environment Record, the manorial courts of Llandough and Leckwith were held. Our family tradition ties it to Marconi's 1897 wireless trials at Lavernock, and published sources record Peerless Jim Driscoll's first fight in its barn.

None of that is on the Council's own history page. Great House is not mentioned at all.

In 1988 the story was told as the eviction of squatters. We were not squatters. The courts decided who could live in the house. No court has ever decided who owned it. The house came down the morning after Cadw declined to list it, while our application to the European Commission of Human Rights was still pending.

For eighteen months my family has asked twenty organisations for the records. Cadw says its 1988 file was probably destroyed but holds no record of destroying it. The Land Registry's own schedule of 43 documents does not include the deed its title rests on. This Council refused our request on cost, with figures that do not add up. Freedom of information, environmental information and our own personal data are three separate rights, and each should be answered on its own terms.

I am not asking this Council to try the case. I am asking you to do three things. Put our statement and pack on the public record. Make Great House Farm a standing item, with a named councillor. And write to the bodies holding the records, asking them to disclose them fully. The detail can follow, meeting by meeting, in the open.

Your mission is “Serving our community, preserving our identity”. Tŷ Mawr is part of that identity. For thirty-eight years it has had no voice. Tonight, please give it one.

Thank you.
