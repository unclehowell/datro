Llandough Community CouncilThursday 22 October 2026, 7.00pm

Preserving our identity.

That is the Council's own mission statement. On 22 October it considers Tŷ Mawr, the Great House of Llandough: a home our family traces back to 1667 (as the Western Mail also reported in 1958), demolished at about 4am on 6 December 1988. Its wider heritage and the family's unresolved title questions are still largely absent from the Council's published story.

The briefing.

Nine compartments, in order. Say the line in bold, then move on. Open a compartment only if a councillor asks: the record is underneath, with what is still being tested and the answer to the obvious objection.

C1Who we areWe are the Williams–Buckler family of Tŷ Mawr, the Great House of Llandough. Our family lived there for 320 years.

On the record

  • Heneb's Historic Environment Record says the manorial courts of Llandough and Leckwith were held at Great House. On record
  • Mary Williams's statement of about 1974, and the 1974 press, record the family at Great House since 1667. Buckler case
  • William Buckler was born in the house. In 1988 three small children lived there. On record
  • Trade directories from 1868 to 1901 list “Williams John, Great house”; an 1892 press report names “Mr. John Williams, Great House Farm, Llandough” (wiki EV-0225, EV-0226). On record
  • The 1881 census lists John Williams, farmer, at “Llandough Home Farm” (RG11/5288 p.2, wiki EV-0224). Per Sion Buckler, this is Great House Farm under another name. On record
  • The Western Mail, 20 November 1958 (snippet only, wiki EV-0037), reported the land as “farmed by her family since 1667”. On record

Still being tested

  • No deed of 1667 has yet been found. The 1670 hearth tax and Bute estate leases are being searched. Being sought

What cuts against us

  • The heritage record says the Vaughans held Great House as the chief freehold farm from the mid-16th to the late 18th century. Both accounts can be true only if the Williamses held under them. On record
  • A 1904 report says John Williams “succeeded his father in the tenancy of the farm in 1855” (wiki EV-0227): a tenancy, and of “Llandough Farm”, matched to Great House by inference. On record
  • “Home Farm” appears in no other source, and the 1881 acreage may read 170, closer to the neighbouring Llandough Farm of the Rees family (wiki EV-0228). Being sought

If asked “Weren't you tenants?” The 1939 tenancy plan, now held, depicts the eastern fields only, not the house. Mary refused the tenancies offered for the house.

Evidence: Wiki: The Williams / Buckler Family · Wiki: Mary Williams's Statement (c. 1974) · Heneb HER GGAT02038s

C2What lies thereThe ground at Church View Close held one of the great early Christian cemeteries of Wales. Nothing marks it.

On the record

  • A published study reports 1,026 burials from the early medieval cemetery excavated there in 1994, the largest such population recovered in Wales. On record
  • St Dochdwy's monastery is attested from the 7th century. Heneb says its exact site has not been established. On record
  • Medieval pottery was found on the slope beside the house in 1962–63. The Roman villa south of the church is a separate site. On record
  • Published sources record Peerless Jim Driscoll's first fight in a barn at Great House Farm. On record
  • Marconi's 1897 wireless trials crossed from Lavernock to Flat Holm. On record

Still being tested

  • Family tradition that Thomas Williams of Great House carted Marconi's equipment. Buckler case
  • Where the monastery stood. It is not proven to lie beneath Tŷ Mawr. Being sought

What cuts against us

  • Museum Wales traces the medieval spur and spearhead linked to the “armour” story to Church Farm, 1858, not to Great House. On record
  • It has not been shown that the 1994 burials lay on the family's own parcel. Being sought

If asked “Isn't this history already told?” The Council's own village history says 800 burials and does not mention Great House at all.

Evidence: Holbrook and Thomas, Medieval Archaeology 49 (2005) · Wiki: Archaeology and Heritage · Stead and Williams, Wales and its Boxers (2008)

C3Mary's parcelFor decades Mary Williams said the house was hers. She was treated as a tenant, and in 1983 her home was registered to BP while she lived in it.

On the record

  • April 1974: Mary opened the house to the public and told the Daily Telegraph it was hers. Her solicitors said she had a valid claim. On record
  • She refused the tenancies offered for the house in 1959 and 1965. On record
  • BP's 1974 action, in which she pleaded ownership, was adjourned and not relisted. On record
  • 23 February 1983: BP's solicitors applied to register the farmhouse as a separate title, certifying no question or doubt. Mary died on 26 March 1983. On record
  • 2 February 1987: that title was amalgamated into the fields title, before the Court of Appeal hearing. On record
  • The 1939 tenancy plan depicts the eastern fields, Parcel B, only. On record
  • The Land Registry's 43-item schedule does not list the 1969 deed recited as the root of BP's title. On record

Still being tested

  • The family's case: two parcels since 1877, the house parcel theirs from 1928. Buckler case
  • A 15 May 1924 agreement on the Land Registry file, not yet read. Being sought
  • The “synthetic Parcel A” working hypothesis: that registration and merger made the house appear to follow the fields' title. Being sought
  • Per the Buckler case: notice of registration reached them only after Mary's death. Buckler case

What cuts against us

  • The 1916 tenancy described the farm as one holding, and later documents repeated that wording. On record
  • HM Land Registry says there is no evidence of a mistake; its stage-two decision says the title is registered correctly. That decision is with the Independent Complaints Reviewer. On record

If asked “The register is conclusive.” The register can be corrected for mistake, and a person in actual occupation in 1982 had protected rights.

Evidence: Wiki: The Two Parcels · Wiki: Land Registry Titles · Wiki: Anomalies A-008 to A-012, A-025, A-026 · Wiki: 1987 HMLR amalgamation during appeal

C4The courtsThe family lost every round on who could stay. No court has ruled on who owned the house.

On the record

  • High Court 1986 and Court of Appeal 1987 decided possession. The House of Lords refused leave in 1988; Strasbourg found the application inadmissible in 1989. On record
  • The Court of Appeal counted the possession period from a 1955 order. On record
  • The appeal judgment notes certain documents were never produced. Mary's statement says the family's papers went missing in 1950–52. Buckler case
  • On 5 December 1988 the court refused to wait for Strasbourg. The house came down the next morning. On record
  • The House of Lords papers were identified on 23 September 2026 after earlier searches missed them. The Strasbourg file was destroyed in 2000. On record

Still being tested

  • Procedural unfairness, court error and non-disclosure. Reopening needs new primary evidence; the Lords papers are not yet read. Being sought

What cuts against us

  • The Court of Appeal found the 1974 letters made later occupation permissive, whether or not Mary accepted them. On record
  • Strasbourg held the interference with William Buckler's home lawful and justified. On record

If asked “The courts have decided this.” They decided possession. The family's title case on the house parcel has not been tried.

Evidence: BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 · Wiki: BP v Buckler, Scope, Decided and Undecided · Wiki: Potential Miscarriage of Justice register

C5The dispossessionIn 1988 a Welsh family was removed from its home and the house demolished before dawn. It was reported as an eviction.

On the record

  • Press, 29 April 1988: five bailiffs and about fifteen police; doors broken with poleaxes, children behind them. On record
  • Press, December 1988: William Buckler injured, taken to hospital, and charged from his hospital bed. On record
  • Press, 6 December 1988: bulldozers from about 4am; William's wife and their three children watched; nothing standing by breakfast. On record
  • Press, 1989: the family's possessions lost and the family rehoused with relatives. On record
  • Press: a Vale councillor called the demolition “disgusting”; villagers met their councillors. On record

Still being tested

  • Per the Buckler case: police searched the house and removed documents. Buckler case

What cuts against us

  • Press, 1989: one charge was withdrawn as out of time; William Buckler pleaded guilty to the others. On record

If asked “Why has it taken so long?” Witnesses died, papers were lost and the trauma silenced the family. Key records surfaced only in 2026.

Evidence: Wiki: 1988, Possession and Demolition · Press transcripts GHF-E090-N07 to N15

C6The heritage erasedA Welsh manor house beside an early Christian site was cleared, and the official record was left saying almost nothing.

On the record

  • The Local Plan said the buildings should be retained. GGAT's 1989 report described the farmhouse as a “county treasure”. On record
  • Cadw refused to list it on 5 December 1988. No assessment file or destruction record has been produced. On record
  • GGAT warned in 1982. In 1990 untested ground was zoned low potential. Burials were found there in 1994. On record
  • The heritage record classes the site “Post Medieval – Farm”. The Royal Commission recorded it only in part. On record
  • A 1983 fire destroyed relics from years of excavations (South Wales Echo, 18 March 1983). On record

Still being tested

  • Per the Buckler case: the fire was at a Neath store and Llandough material was lost. Buckler case
  • Whether the 1990 evaluation was adequate on what was known then. Being sought

What cuts against us

  • GGAT disclosed its dual role in 1990, recommended recording and protection, and did not advise demolition. On record
  • Cadw said in 1988 that the house was not of sufficient architectural merit to list. On record

If asked “Missing records prove nothing.” Agreed, on their own. These are files public bodies had a duty to keep, and they should account for the gaps.

FOIFreedom of Information Act 2000

General recorded information. Twenty working days. A council may refuse if compliance would cost more than £450 (18 hours at £25), but must estimate reasonably and offer advice to narrow the request.

EIREnvironmental Information Regulations 2004

Information on land, development and the historic environment. Twenty working days. No automatic cost cap: refusal only if “manifestly unreasonable”, and then only if the public interest favours it.

SARUK GDPR, Article 15

Information about the person asking. One month, free. Covers what the Council holds about the living family members; it does not cover records about people who have died.

The three-minute statementRead it

Chair, councillors, thank you for placing this on the agenda.

My grandmother was Mary Williams of Great House Farm, Tŷ Mawr, beside St Dochdwy's Church. Our family's case is that we lived in that house from 1667 until it was demolished at about four in the morning on 6 December 1988.

Item 10 describes a plaque and a mailing list. The subject is wider than that, so I will take it in short parts.

The house. Heneb's record says the manorial courts of Llandough and Leckwith sat at Great House. The Local Plan said its buildings should be retained. It was never fully recorded before it came down.

The ground. A published study reports 1,026 burials from the early medieval cemetery excavated there in 1994, the largest such population recovered in Wales. The monastery's exact site has never been established. Nothing on the ground marks any of it, and the Council's own village history does not mention Great House.

My grandmother. For decades she said the house was hers. She refused every tenancy offered for it. In February 1983, while she lived there, BP applied to register the farmhouse as its own title; she died that March. In 1987, while the appeal was running, that title was merged into the fields.

The courts. They decided who could stay. The family lost. They did not decide who owned the house. The house came down the morning after Cadw refused to list it, while our Strasbourg application was pending.

The records. More than twenty bodies have been asked. Cadw's 1988 file is missing with no record of destruction. The Land Registry's list of 43 documents does not include the 1969 deed BP's title rests on. The Ministry of Justice has not yet answered our MP.

Our research has only started. Nothing yet meets the threshold to reopen the case, and we do not claim it does. That is why the records matter.

We ask for four things. Put our statement on the public record. Make Great House Farm a standing item, with a named councillor. Recommend to the Senedd that an independent inquiry be considered. And write in support of disclosure and preservation of the records. We also ask you to recognise what three generations of my family have been through. We are not seeking anyone's home at Church View Close.

Your mission is “Serving our community, preserving our identity”. Tŷ Mawr is part of that identity. Thank you.
