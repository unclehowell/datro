1. Executive Summary

This dossier documents the case of BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2, concerning the dispossession of the Williams-Buckler family from Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough, Vale of Glamorgan. The family occupied the property continuously from 1667 to 1988 — a period of 321 years. In 1987, the Court of Appeal granted BP possession based on a unilateral 1974 licence letter addressed to 'Mrs Buckler' — a name Mary Williams never used. The farmhouse was demolished by BP Properties Ltd on 6 December 1988 amid considerable local controversy. Ownership was never determined by any court.

5. Principal Contradictions

  1. BP Companies: Court said BP Pension Trust and BP Properties were 'different' (to block the family's challenge) and 'same' (to defeat the family's adverse possession claim).
  2. Identity: Mary Williams was addressed as 'Mrs Buckler' despite never adopting that name. The Williams name carried the land claim rights.
  3. 1916 Tenancy: Exists only in paragraph 36 of the 1987 judgment. No original document has been located.
  4. Ownership: Courts consistently avoided determining ownership, ruling only on possession.
  5. 1974 Licence: Court held a unilateral, unaccepted letter ended adverse possession — a novel legal principle.
  6. Archaeology: 1870 Roman soldier discovery ignored; 1994 excavation confirmed over 800 burials.
  7. Missing Deeds: Cardiff Library deed copies removed in 1984, never recovered.
  8. Excavation Delay: Largest dig in Wales, report took 11 years to publish (2005).
  9. Planning Omission: Medium-high archaeological potential site got planning permission with no condition for archaeology.
  10. Marconi Ignored: Farm's connection to first over-sea wireless transmission never factored into heritage protection.
  11. Press Campaign (1974): Major press/TV campaign and 1,700-signature petition failed to secure any heritage protection for the farmhouse.
  12. Roman Villa (1979): Nationally important Roman villa emergency-excavated and destroyed for housing within months; no scheduling protection.
