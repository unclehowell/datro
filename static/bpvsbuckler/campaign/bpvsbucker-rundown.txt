1938 - Reversion Conveyed
The reversion was conveyed to Western Ground Rents Ltd. 
Western Ground Rents Ltd was a Cardiff-based ground rent company that operated one of the most extensive and controversial property portfolios in South Wales during the mid-twentieth century. The company's business model centered on acquiring freehold reversions from original property developers, thereby becoming an intermediary landlord that collected ground rents from leasehold occupiers while maintaining ultimate ownership of the underlying land. This operational approach generated substantial profits—shareholders could recover their capital more than twice within three years through dividends of 50% (1951–52, 1952–53), 25% (1953–56), a capital distribution equivalent to 60% (1956–57), and 75% (1957, 1958)—but also attracted intense criticism from tenants, politicians, and housing reform advocates .
The company's litigation strategy in the early 1960s reflected systematic enforcement of ground rent claims. Rather than accepting negotiated settlements, Western Ground Rents pursued aggressive legal action to recover possession from tenants in arrears. The 1962 action against Mr. Frederick Buckler and Mrs. Buckler (sued as Mrs. Williams) for possession of Great House Farm must be understood within this broader pattern of enforcement activity, with the Cardiff County Court serving as the primary venue for the company's numerous possession claims
