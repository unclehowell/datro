TŶ MAWR — THE GREAT HOUSE FARM STORY
Narration script: 102 scenes in date order. Release v0.0.0.56, 2026-10-06. Source: https://greathousefarmwiki.wordpress.com/

PROLOGUE: TO 1857 (c. 650 – 1857)

P.1  C. AD 650 — St Dochdwy's church (St Dochdwy's, Llandough)

NARRATOR: St Dochdwy's church stands at Llandough, on the site of an early monastery. Great House stands beside it.

P.2  12TH – 14TH CENTURY — Medieval occupation (Great House — north-east slope)

NARRATOR: Pottery of the 12th to 14th centuries is later found on the slope north-east of the house. The Royal Commission classes the house as sub-medieval, probably 17th century.

P.3  1552 – 1829 — Manorial leases (Llandough manorial leases)

NARRATOR: The Llandough manorial leases, 1552–1829, list "Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm (107 a.)".

P.4  MID 16TH – LATE 18TH CENTURY — The Vaughans (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: From the mid-16th to the late 18th century the Vaughan family hold Great House, the chief freehold farm of the parish.

P.5  1667 — The Williams family at Great House (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams's statement (c. 1974) records her family at Great House from 1667.

P.6  EARLY 19TH CENTURY — The Bute Estate (Great House)

NARRATOR: Early in the 19th century the Bute Estate acquires the freehold of Great House. The manorial courts of Llandough and Leckwith are held in the house.

P.7  C. 1840 — Great House Farm (Census and tithe records)

NARRATOR: Census and tithe records begin to describe the house as "Great House Farm".

ACT I: DIVISION (1858 – 1915)

I.1  C. 1858 — Burials at Church Farm (Church Farm, Llandough)

NARRATOR: Workmen at Llandough Church Farm find about six skeletons, a medieval spearhead and a spur. The bones are reburied by the church stile.

I.2  C. 1870 — The armour under the floor (Great House — dining room)

NARRATOR: Family tradition records a soldier in armour found beneath the dining-room floor. The Daily Telegraph (1974) and the Royal Commission (1988) record the account.

I.3  1876 – C. 1912 — The limeworks (Llandough Limeworks)

NARRATOR: The Bute Estate lets about 33 acres of the farm to the Llandough Limeworks, which work it until about 1912.

I.4  1877 — Division of the farm (Bute Estate Office)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams's statement records that in 1877 the Bute Estate sold the farmhouse, buildings and about 10 acres to Daniel Thomas, and kept about 9 acres. The Williamses remained as tenants of both. The deed is missing.

I.5  11 NOVEMBER 1891 — Theft of geese (Llandough — court report)

NARRATOR: Three geese are stolen from "Mr. John Williams, Great House Farm, Llandough". The theft is reported from court.

I.6  MAY 1897 — Marconi at Lavernock (Lavernock Point)

NARRATOR: Marconi's wireless trials at Lavernock Point, May 1897. Family tradition records that Thomas Williams of Great House carted his equipment.

I.7  1908 — John Williams (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary's grandfather dies. John Williams takes over the house from the Thomases and the fields from Bute.

I.8  10 AUGUST 1913 — Birth of Mary Williams (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams is born in the farmhouse on 10 August 1913.

ACT II: TENANCIES (1916 – 1948)

II.1  FEBRUARY 1916 — The 1916 tenancy (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Bute grants John Williams a yearly tenancy described as "the whole farm". The document has not been found.

II.2  1928 — Last rent on the house (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams's statement records that in 1928 the last quarry machinery left, and John Williams paid a final rent of about £4 to Alfred Thomas. She saw the receipt. It is missing.

II.3  1936 — Marriage (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams marries Frederick Buckler, who comes to live at the farmhouse. They have seven children.

II.4  1938 — Sale to Western Ground Rents (Bute Estate Office)

NARRATOR: The Bute Estate conveys its reversion on the 1916 tenancy to Western Ground Rents Ltd.

II.5  MAY 1939 — The 1939 tenancy (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Western Ground Rents grants John Williams a revised tenancy, with a plan.

II.6  C. 1940 — Twelve years (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: By 1940, twelve years after 1928, no rent has been paid on the house.

II.7  1944 — Farm management (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: The War Agricultural Executive Committee is dissatisfied with the farm. Frederick Buckler takes over running it as manager for John Williams.

II.8  C. 1948 – 49 — Birth of William Buckler (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: William (Billy) Buckler is born at the farm.

ACT III: POSSESSION PROCEEDINGS (1949 – 1968)

III.1  2 FEBRUARY 1949 — The 1949 tenancy (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: John Williams surrenders his tenancy. Frederick Buckler's yearly tenancy from Western Ground Rents begins on 2 February 1949. It is not put in writing.

III.2  JUNE 1950 – C. 1952 — The blanket box (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams's statement records that a lodger placed by a Penarth estate agent stayed about two years and told her he had taken "the papers which were in the blanket box including the agreement relating to the farm" and given them to the agent.

III.3  1952 – 53 — Last rent on the fields (Landlords' agents)

NARRATOR: On 10 October 1952 the landlord's agents write that they will not proceed with a letting to Frederick. He pays his last rent in 1953.

III.4  2 FEBRUARY 1955 — The 1955 possession order (High Court / Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 2 February 1955 Western Ground Rents obtains a High Court order for possession against Frederick Buckler, described as "the whole of the farm". On 4 July 1955 possession is taken of everything except the farmhouse and garden. Mary Williams had recently returned from hospital.

III.5  1959 — 1959 tenancy offer (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Western Ground Rents offers Mary a tenancy of the farmhouse and garden. She refuses, stating that the house is hers through her grandfather and that documents prove it.

III.6  11 DECEMBER 1962 — The 1962 possession order (Cardiff County Court)

NARRATOR: On 11 December 1962 Cardiff County Court (Judge Temple Morris QC) orders possession of the farmhouse and garden, with mesne profits from 1955, against Frederick and Mary, on Western Ground Rents' claim. The order is not enforced.

III.7  JUNE 1963 — Committal proceedings (Cardiff County Court)

NARRATOR: The mesne profits under the 1962 order are partly enforced against Frederick, and committal proceedings follow.

III.8  JANUARY – MARCH 1965 — 1965 tenancy offer (Landlords' agents)

NARRATOR: On 2 March 1965 the landlord's agents offer "Mrs Williams" a weekly tenancy of the farmhouse and garden at £2 a week. It is not signed or returned and no rent is paid.

III.9  1964 – 68 — Reported sale by the eldest son (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: A cousin's account says the eldest son sold "our land parcel" through a solicitor without his parents' knowledge. No document has been found. In 1968 his household leaves the farm.

III.10  1967 — Death of Frederick Buckler (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Frederick Buckler dies, aged 56. Mary takes back the name Williams and remains in the farmhouse.

ACT IV: BP (1969 – 1983)

IV.1  31 DECEMBER 1969 — Conveyance to BP Pension Trust (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 31 December 1969 Western Ground Rents conveys its interest at Great House Farm to BP Pension Trust Ltd. The conveyance is not on the Land Registry files.

IV.2  1970 – 72 — Village green and planning permission (Planning office)

NARRATOR: On 16 June 1970 land beside Leckwith Road is registered as village green VG41. On 25 July 1972 planning permission is granted for housing on the site, while Mary lives in the farmhouse.

IV.3  1974 — Royal Commission survey (Great House — interior)

NARRATOR: H. J. Thomas surveys the ground floor for the Royal Commission. The house "remained partially recorded because of problems of access created by an ownership dispute".

IV.4  15 APRIL 1974 — Open day (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 15 April 1974 Mary opens the farmhouse to the public. The Daily Telegraph reports her saying ownership has been disputed for 25 years and her solicitors tell her she has a valid claim.

IV.5  3 JULY 1974 — 1974 possession action (County court)

NARRATOR: BP Pension Trust brings a county court action for possession. On 3 July 1974 Mary pleads ownership and the Limitation Act 1939. Her statement describes the 1877 sale. The case is part heard and adjourned, and not relisted.

IV.6  SEPTEMBER – OCTOBER 1974 — The 1974 letters (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 19 September 1974 BP is given leave to enforce the 1962 order; Mary was not given notice. On 31 October the warrant is withdrawn and BP writes to Mary allowing her to remain in the farmhouse rent-free for life. She does not accept.

IV.7  19 NOVEMBER, 1970S (YEAR UNCLEAR) — Letter to the Western Mail (Western Mail — letters)

NARRATOR: A letter to the Western Mail, dated 19 November (year unclear), warns that demolition would erase the site of St Dochdwy's monastery and traces the land's descent.

IV.8  23 MAY 1975 — The 1975 conveyance (BP Pension Trust / BP Properties)

NARRATOR: On 23 May 1975 BP Pension Trust conveys property at Great House Farm, with a plan, to BP Properties Ltd. The Court of Appeal later calls it "the actual conveyance of the farmhouse and garden".

IV.9  22 MAY 1980 — Hansard (House of Commons)

NARRATOR: On 22 May 1980 Ted Rowlands MP tells the House of Commons that Western Ground Rents, "the most rapacious ground landlord" in South Wales, has been bought by the BP pension fund.

IV.10  1 OCTOBER 1982 — GGAT advice (GGAT)

NARRATOR: On 1 October 1982 GGAT advises the planners that occupation may continue north of the church and asks for investigation before development.

IV.11  19 – 30 NOVEMBER 1982 — First registration (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 30 November 1982 Linklaters & Paines apply to register BP Properties as owner of the land, title WA231076. Mary is living in the farmhouse.

IV.12  23 FEBRUARY 1983 — Farmhouse title (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 23 February 1983 BP's solicitors apply to register the farmhouse and garden as a separate title, WA240304, on the 1975 conveyance, certifying they know of no question or doubt affecting the title.

IV.13  26 MARCH 1983 — Death of Mary Williams (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: Mary Williams dies in the farmhouse on 26 March 1983. Her son William inherits her estate.

IV.14  8 APRIL – 12 MAY 1983 — Registration correspondence (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 8 and 12 April and 12 May 1983 Linklaters and the Land Registry exchange letters on the farmhouse file, including form D23.

ACT V: BP PROPERTIES LTD V BUCKLER (1984 – March 1988)

V.1  1984 — The writ (BP Properties)

NARRATOR: On 22 May 1984 BP Properties issues a writ for possession of Great House Farm and adjoining land against William Buckler. The defence relies on adverse possession from 1955.

V.2  10 JULY 1986 — High Court judgment (High Court, Cardiff)

NARRATOR: On 10 July 1986 Mr Justice Hollis gives judgment for BP, holding that the 1974 letters gave Mary a licence.

V.3  1986 – 87 — Royal Commission correspondence (Royal Commission (RCAHMW))

NARRATOR: During the trial and appeal, the Royal Commission's correspondence with Mrs Buckler goes missing.

V.4  2 FEBRUARY 1987 — Amalgamation of titles (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 2 February 1987, with the appeal pending, an application is made to amalgamate WA240304 (farmhouse and garden) into WA231076.

V.5  31 JULY 1987 — Court of Appeal (Court of Appeal, London)

NARRATOR: On 31 July 1987 the Court of Appeal (Dillon LJ, Mustill LJ, Sir Edward Eveleigh) dismisses the appeal in BP Properties Ltd v Buckler. It finds adverse possession from 1955, but holds that the 1962 action stopped time and the 1974 letters made the possession permissive.

V.6  3 MARCH 1988 — House of Lords (House of Lords)

NARRATOR: On 3 March 1988 the House of Lords refuses leave to appeal. The family are given 28 days to leave.

ACT VI: EVICTION AND DEMOLITION (April – December 1988)

VI.1  29 APRIL 1988, MORNING — First eviction attempt (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 29 April 1988 five bailiffs and about fifteen police arrive. William Buckler blocks the drive with a tractor and secures the house.

VI.2  29 APRIL 1988 — The chainsaw (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: The bailiffs break the gate and doors. William Buckler pushes a running chainsaw through a door. After four hours the bailiffs withdraw. BP tells the press Mary had been allowed to stay rent-free because of ill health.

VI.3  MAY 1988 — Strasbourg and the MP (Alun Michael MP's office)

NARRATOR: On 3 May 1988 William Buckler applies to the European Commission of Human Rights. On 12 May Alun Michael MP asks the Lord Chancellor to review the case.

VI.4  29 JULY 1988 — Cadw inspection (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 29 July 1988 Cadw inspects and photographs the farmhouse and barn while considering them for listing.

VI.5  29 – 30 NOVEMBER 1988 — Eviction (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 29–30 November 1988 bailiffs enter the farmhouse. William Buckler is taken to Llandough Hospital. Eight other residents are made homeless.

VI.6  1 DECEMBER 1988 — Belongings (Llandough Hospital)

NARRATOR: The family's belongings, including Mary's papers and photographs, are left in the house or put in the road.

VI.7  2 DECEMBER 1988 — Injunction (High Court)

NARRATOR: On 2 December 1988 a High Court judge orders that the farmhouse and its contents must not be touched. BP states it plans to demolish and build homes.

VI.8  3 DECEMBER 1988 — Charges (Llandough Hospital / magistrates)

NARRATOR: William Buckler is taken from hospital and charged with assaulting two bailiffs, criminal damage, and wanton or furious driving.

VI.9  3 – 5 DECEMBER 1988 — Spot-listing (Cadw)

NARRATOR: Over the weekend of 3–5 December 1988 Cadw considers spot-listing without access to the interior.

VI.10  MONDAY 5 DECEMBER 1988 — Final hearing; listing refused (Cardiff)

NARRATOR: On 5 December 1988 Judge Norman Francis refuses to continue the injunction pending the European Commission. The same day Cadw states the house is not of sufficient merit to list.

VI.11  6 DECEMBER 1988, 4AM — Demolition (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: From about 4am on 6 December 1988 the farmhouse and buildings are demolished.

VI.12  7 – 9 DECEMBER 1988 — Local reaction and bail (Llandough)

NARRATOR: A Vale of Glamorgan councillor calls the demolition "disgusting". Villagers meet their councillors on 8 December. On 9 December William Buckler is bailed on condition he stays away from the site.

VI.13  DECEMBER 1988 — Royal Commission in the rubble (Great House Farm — rubble)

NARRATOR: R. F. Suggett of the Royal Commission examines the rubble, records a fireplace jamb and an ogee-stopped beam, and notes the loss of a carved stone capital by the front door.

ACT VII: DEVELOPMENT (1989 – 2019)

VII.1  20 MARCH 1989 — Site clearance (Great House Farm)

NARRATOR: On 20 March 1989 lorries clear the site.

VII.2  JANUARY – MARCH 1989 — Charges resolved (Court)

NARRATOR: In January 1989 one charge is dropped as out of time and William Buckler is barred from within half a mile of the site. On 23 March he pleads guilty to the remaining charges and is freed.

VII.3  1989 — Rehousing (Penarth)

NARRATOR: The family live with William Buckler's sister in Penarth. He plans to live in a converted bus and says about £30,000 of possessions were taken from the site.

VII.4  14 APRIL 1989 — European Commission decision (European Commission of Human Rights)

NARRATOR: On 14 April 1989 the European Commission of Human Rights declares Buckler v United Kingdom inadmissible.

VII.5  OCTOBER – NOVEMBER 1989 — Outline application (Vale of Glamorgan planning)

NARRATOR: BP applies for outline planning permission for housing (89/01396/OUT, received 8 November 1989). GGAT advises an archaeological assessment and is then commissioned by BP to carry it out. Its report calls the farmhouse "a county treasure".

VII.6  FEBRUARY – MARCH 1990 — Outline permission (Vale of Glamorgan planning)

NARRATOR: On 13 March 1990 outline permission is granted with 18 conditions. The officer's report notes the farm buildings "were unfortunately demolished a little while ago". GGAT's further comments of 8 February 1990 are missing from the file.

VII.7  AUGUST – OCTOBER 1990 — Evaluation trenches (Former farm site)

NARRATOR: In August 1990 GGAT digs eight trenches for BP and finds early medieval burials. An area that could not be investigated is marked low potential. In October the council records the condition as met.

VII.8  21 AUGUST 1991 — Heritage record entry (Historic Environment Record)

NARRATOR: The Historic Environment Record records that the house and buildings "were suddenly and completely demolished by B.P. Properties Ltd. on 6th December 1988 amid considerable local controversy".

VII.9  14 MAY 1992 — Reserved matters application (Vale of Glamorgan planning)

NARRATOR: On 14 May 1992 Ideal Homes Wales applies for approval of 20 houses (92/00671/RES).

VII.10  15 MAY 1992 — Death of William Buckler (Llandough)

NARRATOR: William Buckler dies on 15 May 1992 in Llandough Hospital and is buried at Michaelston-le-Pit.

VII.11  27 JULY 1992 — GGAT letter (GGAT)

NARRATOR: On 27 July 1992 GGAT writes to the council that the 1990 assessment showed significant deposits, including human bone, and urges mitigation. Page 2 is missing from the file.

VII.12  3 SEPTEMBER 1992 — Reserved matters approved (Vale of Glamorgan planning)

NARRATOR: On 3 September 1992 the 20 houses are approved without a new archaeological condition.

VII.13  DECEMBER 1993 — Transfer to Ideal Homes (Former farm site)

NARRATOR: On 3 December 1993 BP transfers the site to Ideal Homes and groundworks begin. On 13 December GGAT asks the council for a breach-of-condition notice. The council accepts the developer's counsel's opinion that the condition is discharged.

VII.14  MARCH – JULY 1994 — Cemetery excavation (Former farm site)

NARRATOR: March–July 1994: the Cotswold Archaeological Trust excavates an early medieval cemetery on the site. Published totals reach 1,026 burials. The Home Office licence lapsed between 21 April and at least 3 May while work continued.

VII.15  28 JULY 1994 — Finds to the National Museum (National Museum of Wales)

NARRATOR: On 28 July 1994 Ideal Homes Wales, as landowner, gives the human remains and finds to the National Museum of Wales (95.56H).

VII.16  1994 – 96 — Woodland title and houses (Woodland — WA735527)

NARRATOR: On 10 November 1994 the woodland passes to the Forest of Cardiff (WA735527). In 1995–96 rights are granted to South Wales Electricity. The houses of Church View Close are built.

VII.17  2005 — Publication (Medieval Archaeology)

NARRATOR: The excavation is published in Medieval Archaeology 49 (2005): 1,026 burials, 814 articulated. On 22 December 2005 part of the woodland title is divided off.

VII.18  20 MARCH 2019 — 1990 assessment archived (RCAHMW national archive)

NARRATOR: On 20 March 2019 GGAT's 1990 assessment is added to the national record.

ACT VIII: RECORDS (2025 – 2026)

VIII.1  NOVEMBER 2025 – JANUARY 2026 — Records requests begin (Royal Commission and others)

NARRATOR: From November 2025 Mary's grandchildren request records from public bodies. On 29 January 2026 they apply to HM Land Registry to record the interest omitted at first registration.

VIII.2  FEBRUARY – MARCH 2026 — Land Registry reply (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 23 March 2026 HM Land Registry sets out the registration history and states there is "no evidence of a mistake in the register".

VIII.3  19 APRIL – 25 MAY 2026 — Complaint decisions (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 19 April 2026 HM Land Registry refuses the stage-one complaint. On 25 May the stage-two decision states the title is "registered correctly".

VIII.4  21 MAY 2026 — Cadw disclosure (Cadw)

NARRATOR: On 21 May 2026 Cadw releases two photographs of 29 July 1988, coded "YYY". It says its paper file is "quite likely" destroyed; in July, "most likely".

VIII.5  JUNE – JULY 2026 — Schedule of registration documents (HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: On 30 July 2026 HM Land Registry supplies a schedule of 43 documents on the first-registration files. The 31 December 1969 conveyance and the 1971 abstract are not listed.

VIII.6  25 AUGUST 2026 — Museum Wales reply (Museum Wales)

NARRATOR: On 25 August 2026 Museum Wales replies that the information is "not held", and in the same letter says it has found records of other discoveries at Great House Farm, which it withholds as "not in scope".

VIII.7  8 SEPTEMBER 2026 — Glamorgan Archives reply (Glamorgan Archives)

NARRATOR: On 8 September 2026 Glamorgan Archives report no deeds for the 1877 sale, and identify Bute X.6(12), the trustees' 1877 book of payments, at Cardiff Library.

VIII.8  17 – 21 SEPTEMBER 2026 — Complaints and correspondence (Cadw / Stephen Doughty MP)

NARRATOR: 17–21 September 2026: formal complaint to Cadw; letters to Stephen Doughty MP; records request to Heneb. A Land Registry agent states the title was "closed in 2005".

VIII.9  23 SEPTEMBER 2026 — House of Lords papers (The National Archives)

NARRATOR: On 23 September 2026 The National Archives identifies the House of Lords papers in BP Properties Ltd v Buckler, catalogued in May 2026.

VIII.10  25 – 27 SEPTEMBER 2026 — Woodland offer (Forest of Cardiff)

NARRATOR: On 25 September 2026 the Forest of Cardiff offers the family a route to ownership of the woodland. On 27 September the family decline.

VIII.11  27 – 29 SEPTEMBER 2026 — Complaints to Heneb and the Reviewer (Heneb / HM Land Registry)

NARRATOR: 27–29 September 2026: complaint to Heneb about GGAT's role, 1982–1995; referral of HM Land Registry to the Independent Complaints Reviewer (ICR/090/26); preservation notice to eleven public bodies.

VIII.12  29 SEPTEMBER – 5 OCTOBER 2026 — Archive releases (Cadw / RCAHMW / Museum Wales)

NARRATOR: On 29 September 2026 the Royal Commission releases its records, including the 1974 newspaper report and a note of the armour. On 5 October Museum Wales releases its Church Farm file.

VIII.13  SEPTEMBER – OCTOBER 2026 — Publication of the record (Great House Farm Wiki)

NARRATOR: September–October 2026: the family publish the documents and press cuttings on the Great House Farm Wiki, with a legal review.

EPILOGUE: TODAY (Now)

E.1  TODAY — The sequence (The two titles)

NARRATOR: 1916 to 1987: a tenancy, two possession orders, three tenancy offers, licence letters, a conveyance, two registrations and an amalgamation.

E.2  TODAY — The two parcels in the record (The two parcels)

NARRATOR: Records of 1877–1987 describe the property both as one farm and as separate parts.

E.3  TODAY — The site today (Church View Close, Llandough)

NARRATOR: Church View Close, twenty houses, stands on the site. The woodland is registered to the Forest of Cardiff. The human remains are held by the National Museum of Wales.

E.4  TODAY — Village history (Llandough Community Council)

NARRATOR: Llandough Community Council's page on the village's history describes the Roman villa, the monastery and the 1994 burials. It does not mention Great House, the Williams family or the demolition.

E.5  TODAY — The family's requests (The family)

NARRATOR: The family ask for: a ruling on the ownership of Parcel A; the return of the woodland and the green, or reparation; reparation for the houses built on the land; the return of the estate's artefacts; a true heritage record; and a public inquiry.
