1. c.650 — St Dochdwy's, Llandough

3. 1200s — Llandough Church

The site beside St Dochdwy's church at Llandough is occupied in the medieval period: sherds of 12th- to early 14th-century pottery were later found on the slope just north-east of the house. The house itself, Tŷ Mawr ('Great House'), was classed by the Royal Commission as a sub-medieval house, probably 17th-century, possibly with a medieval core.

  • MASTER MASON: There has been building beside St Dochdwy's for generations.
  • LOCAL FARMER: The pottery on the slope says folk have lived here a long time.

5. 1215 — Great House Site — NE Slope

Archaeological evidence confirms medieval occupation of the Great House site: sherds of C12th, C13th and early C14th pottery on the steep slope immediately NE of the house. The HER classifies Great House as a sub-medieval direct-entry house with clear affinities to Brynwell, a medieval farmhouse in neighbouring Leckwith parish. A reported 'armour' find is dealt with under c.1870 and c.1880.

  • HER OFFICER: C12th-C14th pottery sherds on the NE slope confirm continuous occupation from that date.
  • RCAHMW INSPECTOR: The core of the house may have been medieval — cross-passage end-chimney plan with masonry partitions and external wall batter.
  • ARCHAEOLOGIST: Medieval ironwork found under the rear wing floor c.1880, now at the National Museum of Wales.
  • RCAHMW INSPECTOR: Great House has affinities with Brynwell, a medieval farmhouse in Leckwith, also modernised by the Bute Estate in the C19th.

Sources: GGAT HER PRN 02038s via Archwilio · RCAHMW Inventory Vol IV Part 2 · Great House Farm Wiki (greathousefarmwiki.wordpress.com)

11. 1560 — Great House Farm

The Vaughan family, a minor gentry family, become the chief freehold farmers of Great House from the mid C16th. They occupy the property for over two centuries, until the late C18th. Several memorial inscriptions relating to the family are preserved in Llandough parish church and churchyard. The Vaughans represent the established local gentry class who held the chief freehold farm of the parish for generations.

  • VAUGHAN PATRIARCH: Our family, the Vaughans, are now established as the chief freehold farmers of Great House.
  • PARISH CLERIC: The Vaughan family memorials will be preserved in our church and churchyard for centuries to come.
  • VAUGHAN PATRIARCH: We are minor gentry — not the highest rank, but we hold this land as freeholders, not tenants.
  • LOCAL RECORDER: The Vaughans will hold Great House from the mid C16th through to the late C18th — more than two centuries.

Sources: GGAT HER PRN 02038s via Archwilio

12. 1667 — Great House Farm

According to Mary Williams's own statement, the Williams family's occupation of Great House begins in 1667. No earlier written source has yet been found; family tradition remembers the entry as a purchase, while estate records treat the family as tenants.

CHALLENGE: The family contend that their occupation from 1667 amounted to ownership in all but name, and that this claim was asserted for more than 300 years. An earlier written source is still being sought.

  • WILLIAMS ANCESTOR: I paid a large court fine to enter Great House as our family's holding.
  • HERBERT STEWARD: Our roll shows the Williams entry as a manorial lease, not freehold.
  • WILLIAMS ANCESTOR: But this is our home now, purchased with honest coin for our heirs.
  • HERBERT STEWARD: The Court Baron record will show this as leasehold under our lordship.
  • WILLIAMS ANCESTOR: We have the receipt. Our children will know we bought this land.

1800s

16. 1800 — Great House

In the early C19th, the freehold of Great House is acquired by the Bute Estate — one of the largest landed estates in Wales, controlled by the Marquess of Bute. The manorial courts for Llandough and Leckwith are held in Great House, confirming its historic role as a seat of local governance. The Bute Estate modernises the property in the C19th, as it did with neighbouring Brynwell farmhouse in Leckwith.

  • BUTE ESTATE AGENT: The Bute Estate now holds the freehold of Great House. We will modernise the property.
  • COURT OFFICIAL: The manorial courts for Llandough and Leckwith shall be held in Great House from this time.
  • BUTE ESTATE AGENT: We will also modernise Brynwell in Leckwith, which shares Great House medieval origins.
  • LOCAL TENANT: The Bute Estate are new landlords. They hold the freehold now.

23. 1858 — Llandough Church Farm

About 1858, while foundations are dug for new buildings at Llandough Church Farm, workmen find a medieval iron spearhead and a rowel spur, with about six skeletons. 24. c.1870 — Great House — Dining Room

Family tradition holds that a soldier in armour, with horse, lance and small shield, was found under the dining-room floor. A Daily Telegraph article of 16 April 1974 dates the find to 'about 1870', and RCAHMW holds a note of the story. No primary record of the find has been located; Museum Wales says the similar medieval objects in its collection came from Church Farm, Llandough, in 1858.

CHALLENGE: The family regard the tradition as evidence of the site's antiquity that was never investigated. The Wiki records it as a documented tradition, not a proven find.

  • WILLIAMS FAMILY HEAD: We've found a soldier in armour under the floor!

25. 1876 — Estate Offices

From 1876 the Bute Estate lets about 33 acres that had formed part of Great House Farm for the Llandough Limeworks, which work until about 1912. How that land relates to the family's two parcels is still being tested.

CHALLENGE: The family allege they permitted quarrying in exchange for absolute freehold of their land parcel. This oral agreement was never formally recorded — a pattern of convenient documentation gaps.
