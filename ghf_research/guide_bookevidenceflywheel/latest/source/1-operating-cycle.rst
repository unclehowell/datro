Operating cycle
===============

Purpose and research questions
------------------------------

The cycle tests four questions separately:

1. **Ownership and direction of tenure.** Could the Williams family have held the relevant freehold or another proprietary interest and granted occupation or a lease to Bute? Test the direction of the transaction from the parties and instrument, not from a general account of Bute's presence in the district.
2. **Social standing.** Is there independent material supporting a Williams family's position as gentry or minor gentry? Look for contemporary records of office, landholding, inheritance, arms, taxation, marriage or estate administration. A surname in a county history or pedigree is a lead, not a status finding by itself.
3. **Vaughan succession.** Is there a documented chain linking a particular Vaughan person or estate to a particular Williams person or estate? Each generation and each transfer needs a bridge. Similar spellings and family names alone do not establish descent.
4. **Presence at the target holding.** Do contemporary sources place a Williams or Buckler household at Great House Farm itself? Keep the house, farm, garden, woods, fields and neighbouring holdings distinct unless a plan, boundary description or other record joins them.

A result may be useful for one question and irrelevant to the others. Record the question it addresses.

Cedwyn as a discovery index
---------------------------

Use the Cedwyn site to identify its existing source references, terminology, date ranges, variant place-names and open questions. It is a discovery index, not independent corroboration of the historical proposition being researched. Repetition across several Cedwyn pages remains one site-originated lead unless the pages point to distinct source material.

For every promising site reference, follow the citation to the underlying book, archive item, register, map or page image. Record whether that underlying material was actually opened. A site transcription, an archive catalogue description and an inspected original are three different retrieval states.

Search scope and query rotation
-------------------------------

Build query bundles across four dimensions:

* **Place forms:** Llandough-juxta-Penarth, Llandough-juxta-Cardiff, Landoche-juxta-Cardif, Llan-Doch and Llan-Doche-Penarth, together with Leckwith, Penarth and nearby historical forms.
* **Property forms:** Great House, Great House Farm, Tŷ Mawr, Ty Mawr, Cydfin, Cedfin and Cedwyn, combined with farm, house, manor, estate, field, lease and freehold terms.
* **Family forms:** Williams, Vaughan and relevant historic spelling variants, used with dates, places and named records rather than as stand-alone identity searches.
* **Record and transaction forms:** lessor, lessee, landlord, tenant, conveyance, lease, rent, freehold, manorial court, tithe, will, probate, estate account and land-tax terms.

Disambiguate the target parish from Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge. A hit from the Cowbridge parish, its castle or its families is not a Llandough-juxta-Penarth result unless an independent source establishes the connection.

Search books and printed histories, but also use library catalogues, archival catalogues, digitised volumes, journals, maps and primary record series. A catalogue result may identify an item worth obtaining without exposing the item's contents.

Keep the exact query, search date, platform or collection, query bundle, returned result count where available, and any pagination or coverage limit. Record the search that produced a lead so a later researcher can reproduce it.

Rotate exact search phrases rather than deleting useful names or place forms from the research vocabulary. Retire a phrase only after recording why it was productive or exhausted. Retain a small number of control searches and periodically re-run successful historic searches to detect newly indexed material. A previously productive term is never permanently excluded.

Retrieval and source inspection
-------------------------------

Use a retrieval ladder and record the highest stage actually completed:

1. **Bibliographic metadata:** title, author or compiler, edition, publication details and catalogue identifier. The text has not been inspected.
2. **Catalogue description:** a holding or item description has been read. It may identify a document, but it is not the document itself.
3. **Search result or snippet:** only the excerpt returned by a search interface has been seen.
4. **OCR or transcript:** machine-readable text has been searched or read. Note the transcript provider and that OCR may omit, merge or misread text.
5. **Full text:** the relevant section or page range has been read in the digitised text. Record the edition and page range; do not imply that the printed page image has been checked.
6. **Page image:** the relevant original printed page, manuscript leaf or scan has been viewed. Record the visible page or folio.
7. **Original record or complete surrogate:** the relevant instrument or complete reliable image set has been inspected and its provenance recorded.

Do not collapse these stages into a single status label. State exactly what was opened, what remains unseen and what must happen next. If page numbering in a scan differs from the printed pagination, record both.

Separate the creator's contemporary record from a later editor's summary, transcription, translation, catalogue abstract or modern website. A secondary work can provide a valuable lead while remaining secondary. A digitised copy of an older edition is not a new independent source merely because a different website hosts it.

Identity, locality and parcel checks
------------------------------------

Before joining a person, family or place to the target, test:

* the full name and its spelling in the source;
* dates that make the proposed identity possible;
* occupation, office, family relationships or other identifying details;
* the exact parish, settlement, house or farm named;
* the source's purpose and whether it refers to a manor, church, estate, house or individual parcel;
* whether a plan, schedule, acreage, boundary or neighbouring owner can be reconciled to the target holding.

Do not treat church patronage as landownership, manorial lordship as ownership of every parcel, or an estate reference as proof of the title to a particular house or field. For a proposed Williams-to-Bute letting, identify the lessor and lessee, date, term, rent, property description, plan and any later assignment. The opposite direction must be tested just as carefully.

Name resemblance is not a genealogical link. For example, a printed form resembling Vaughan must not be joined to a Vaughan family without independent identity evidence. A person with a common surname is not identified by proximity, by a later family name, or by an uploader's name alone.

Keep separate holdings separate. In particular, a Llandough Farm reference is not automatically the same holding as Cydfin or Tŷ Mawr. The current research notes distinguish a 166-acre Llandough Farm from Cydfin Farm / Tŷ Mawr; that distinction remains until a plan or other parcel evidence resolves it.

Register structure and assessment
----------------------------------

The canonical register is the book-research-findings.csv file in the Cedwyn research library. Preserve its existing columns and append new records rather than replacing its history. Use these record types consistently:

* **finding** — a specific passage or item inspected at the stated retrieval stage;
* **lead** — a bibliographic or catalogue route that still requires inspection;
* **negative_screen** — a defined search across a stated corpus that returned no relevant indexed result;
* **query_log** — the search terms, date, platform, coverage and result of a search cycle.

For each row, record title, author or compiler, year or edition, page or folio where known, a brief attention note, stable source URL or archive citation, source type, exact retrieval status, relevance and limits, query keywords and observation date. State a next action in the relevance or notes field until the row is resolved.

Keep source relevance separate from source quality. A highly reliable book about the wider manor may be irrelevant to the title of one farm; a weakly indexed catalogue entry may still point to a primary record worth obtaining. Record whether corroboration is independent, derived from the Cedwyn site, or not yet established. Detect duplicates by work, edition, page or folio and source URL before adding a row. A second website hosting the same edition is not a second independent work.

Counter-evidence and negative screens
-------------------------------------

Search for material that could weaken the preferred explanation as deliberately as material that could support it. Record contradictory dates, different family identities, competing property descriptions, alternative explanations and records that place a name in another parish.

A negative screen must name the volumes or collections searched, the variant spellings and terms, the retrieval method, the date and its limits. A zero-hit OCR search is a report about that search and that corpus; it is not proof that a person, record or connection did not exist. Note poor OCR, incomplete digitisation, missing pages and inaccessible catalogues.

Preserve unresolved differences rather than harmonising them. The current intake, for example, has a petition count in the provisional village-book identification that differs from another figure on the site. Keep both figures attributed to their respective locations until the printed pages and underlying petition are inspected.

Publication and close-out
-------------------------

Before a row is promoted into a public evidence page or a legal analysis:

1. confirm that its citation opens and identifies the correct work, edition and source;
2. confirm that any quotation is accurate against the stated retrieval level and page;
3. keep the source's recorded content separate from interpretation and legal effect;
4. state what the source does not establish;
5. link the row to related records without merging identities or parcels prematurely;
6. add counter-evidence and a next test where a material question remains;
7. use a short quotation or a paraphrase in the public register, with a citation and page locator.

The public CSV is a finding aid, not a substitute for the book or primary record. Keep extracts proportionate and use no more than a brief excerpt from any one published source. Use a separate evidence-register entry when an artefact has been obtained and meets the catalogue's entry requirements; a search result or catalogue abstract alone does not become a held primary record.

At the end of each cycle, record new distinct works, works already known, pages actually inspected, new primary-record routes, contradictions found, duplicate rows avoided and the next actions. Update the controlling project task with the run date, actual progress, owner, status and blockers. Leave it open while substantive retrieval, comparison or publication work remains. Never mark a recurrence or deployment live unless the scheduler or deployment record confirms it.

Review metrics
---------------

A useful cycle is measured by source quality and research movement, not raw search-result volume. Record:

* distinct new works or archival items located;
* distinct sources independently inspected;
* pages or folios read and page images reviewed;
* new primary-record routes that can be pursued;
* contradictions or identity and parcel traps found;
* duplicate results correctly excluded;
* unresolved questions moved to a concrete next action.

A cycle that records a well-defined negative screen or disproves a mistaken identity can be productive. A large bundle of repeated snippets is not.
