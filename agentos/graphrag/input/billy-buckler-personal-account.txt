THE PERSONAL ACCOUNT OF WILLIAM "BILLY" BUCKLER
Great House Farm, Llandough
29 April 1988 – 24 March 1989

---

Friday 29 April 1988

The bailiffs and police came again today. I am in poor health — my heart is bad — but I dared not leave the farm to go for a medical check-up. I told the South Wales Echo: I am refusing to leave Great House Farm at Llandough near Cardiff for medical attention because I fear the bailiffs will return the moment I am gone and take the house while I am away. A dozen police officers were on the farm as the bailiffs tried to evict us. They say I used threatening words. I say I was defending my home.

---

Wednesday 11 – Thursday 12 May 1988

They came at us again. Five bailiffs and around fifteen to twenty police officers arrived at Great House Farm, Llandough. I blocked the narrow drive with a tractor, locked the farmhouse doors and barred the windows. They smashed the lock on the gate and walked round the tractor. Then they started smashing the doors with poleaxes. My children — Hywel, Rhys and Amy — were right behind the door when the axe came through. That is when I started my chainsaw and pushed it through the door. They backed off then.

I told the reporters: I am here to stay and I will fight this to the death. This farm has been in the family for nearly 400 years. They have got no documents for it. If it is theirs, why have I never paid a penny in rent for it in my life? I am the owner, despite what any court says. They have no claim over this farm except for what has been obtained through the courts.

My wife Branwen was distressed. She left the farm with the children while I and several friends and tenants remained. A farm tenant, Christopher Thompson, told the press: This land would be worth a fortune. Luxury houses could be built up here to overlook Cardiff Bay. The rich would get richer and the poor poorer.

Cardiff MP Alun Michael asked the Lord Chancellor to look at my case.

---

Wednesday 30 November 1988

At five in the morning the bailiffs forced their way in. I was not there — I had been taken to Llandough Hospital. They evicted my wife and children while I was in my hospital bed. They stripped the outbuildings. They took documents, family photographs and the children’s clothes. Branwen watched them take half the roof off one of the outbuildings.

I wrote this later in my notebook: A notice they fastened to the gate of the farm on the 29th of November 1988 became the cause of my going to Llandough Hospital. The Police locked me up for two weeks while they searched my home and premises. They also took lots of documents from there and photos.

---

Thursday 1 – Friday 2 December 1988

The High Court in Cardiff granted an injunction preventing BP from demolishing the farm. But there were ugly scenes when my wife Branwen tried to serve the writ on the bailiffs who had evicted us the day before. The neighbours called it an SAS-style operation.

Branwen told the television cameras: We won a small victory today in getting the injunction and stopping them from doing any further damage to our home. And all we have to do now is hope that the judge will uphold his decision on Friday when there is a full hearing.

She says she was assaulted and her shoulder dislocated when she came to serve that injunction.

The judge allowed us limited access to collect belongings.

---

Saturday 3 December 1988

I was taken from my hospital bed at Llandough Hospital to face charges of assault on two bailiffs. I had been in hospital for three days. The press reported I was a 39-year-old father of three. I was still recovering, removed under arrest, knowing they were pressing the eviction while I was physically vulnerable and unable to defend the house.

The South Wales Echo reported that Cadw was considering an appeal to the Secretary of State for Wales to protect Great House Farm as a listed building. Inspectors were due to visit. The newspaper said if we failed to have the emergency order extended, the farmhouse could be razed to the ground within days.

---

Monday 5 December 1988

The High Court hearing failed. The emergency procedure was not extended. The judge said we had exhausted our remedies. Within hours, BP Properties moved in and began the demolition. The house was 400 years old, some say 800 years old. My mother Janet Harris told the press: It is reputed to be 800 years old and they want to destroy it. They will have it down as fast as they can now.

I watched from a distance, barred from going within half a mile of my own home. The farmhouse where my family had lived for centuries was flattened. My wife Branwen and our three young children were removed. Everything we had fought for was wiped out in a single day.

---

Tuesday 6 December 1988

The demolition continued. The Western Mail reported: A family's long and bitter battle for an historic farmhouse appeared to be over yesterday when a High Court decision went against them. The Welsh historic monuments organisation decided to take no further action. Mr William Buckler and his wife Branwen had been hoping to hang on to the reportedly 800-year-old Great House Farm at Llandough and its prime 10-acre site. But they lost.

The councillors called the area a battleground. A Vale of Glamorgan councillor and BP pensioner called BP’s decision to flatten the buildings "disgusting."

---

Tuesday 13 December 1988

I was released on bail and surveyed the ruins. I told the South Wales Echo: All I have got left are the clothes I stand up in. Standing among the rubble, I realised I had lost the house, the land, my livelihood and roughly £30,000 of personal possessions taken from the site. I was ordered by the court not to go within half a mile of the Great House Farm site.

---

Late January 1989

At Barry magistrates court, the Crown Prosecution Service withdrew the allegation against me from 29 April. The prosecutor, Mr Jeff Clarke, told the bench the summons was legally "out of time" and could not proceed. I am now living at an address in Penarth. The court has ordered me not to go within half a mile of the site.

I am due to appear again on 3 February to face charges of assault and wanton and furious driving arising from the events at the farm in November.

---

Monday 20 March 1989

W. J. Davies, the biggest contractor around this area, has started taking the stone of all the farm buildings down to the refuse tip. I watched the last physical traces of the farm being erased. I wrote in my notebook: A 1000 year of history is being lost on a tip.

---

Thursday 23 March 1989

I appeared in court in Cardiff. W. J. Davies has taken all the farm away and is loading all my farm machinery to resell. The police brought the case up again and threatened me with three years in jail. They said if I admitted to two of the charges against the bailiffs, they would drop the other charges. I could not stand any more pressure from that lot, so that is what I was forced to do. I was fined £375 for damages to the bailiffs and ten days in jail, which I had already done.

I walked out knowing the criminal cases are closing while the house and land are gone for good.

---

Friday 24 March 1989

I vowed to continue my fight. I told the South Wales Echo: I will fight on to the death. I have lost my historic family home and more than 70 acres of land. I shall just have to start from scratch. They have taken everything from me. I have no money and no home. But I am not finished.

---

POSTSCRIPT

My name is William Buckler. My mother was Mary Doreen Williams, daughter of John Williams. My father was Frederick Buckler. My mother fought this battle for thirty-three years before me. She believed she had title through her grandfather. She died on 14 March 1983, four years before the Court of Appeal ruled against us. The licence they claimed ended our adverse possession was addressed to "Mrs Buckler" — a name she never used. She was a Williams. The courts never ruled on who owned the land. They only ruled on possession. The farm is gone. The stones are on a tip. But the truth is not.
