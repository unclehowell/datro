### Description of Development

Residential development including new access road at Land at Great House Farm, Leckwith Road, Llandough, Penarth.

The Council hereby **GRANTS OUTLINE PERMISSION** for the carrying out of the proposed development as described above and in accordance with the application and plans received by the Council on 8th November, 1989, subject to the following conditions:

### Conditions

1. **Reserved matters.** Approval of the details of the siting, design and external appearance of the building(s), the means of access thereto and the landscaping of the site (hereinafter called "the reserved matters") shall be submitted to and approved by the Local Planning Authority before any development is commenced.

2. **Time limit for reserved matters.** Application for approval of the reserved matters hereinbefore referred to must be made not later than the expiration of three years beginning with the date of this permission.

3. **Commencement.** The development to which this permission relates must be begun not later than whichever is the later of the following dates:
   - (a) The expiration of five years from the date of this permission.
   - (b) The expiration of two years from the date of the final approval of the reserved matters or, in the case of approval on different dates the final approval of the last such matters to be approved.

4. **Level areas only.** All structures, including proposed dwellings, garages and other buildings sited within the curtilages of individual dwellings, shall be erected on the level areas of land and no structures shall be erected on the steep sloped or wooded areas of the site.

5. **Village green/amenity area.** The detail plans to be submitted shall provide for an area of 0.25 acres, located adjacent to Leckwith Road and the boundary with St. Dochdwys Church, to be laid out and landscaped as a village green/amenity area prior to the development hereby approved being brought into beneficial use.

6. **Vehicular access.** Vehicular access to the development, with sightlines of 4.5m x 60m shall be provided at the north western edge of the site onto the A4055 Leckwith Road.

7. **Estate roads.** Any estate roads for the development shall be a minimum of 5.5m in width with a minimum of 1.8m wide footways and 10.5m kerb radii at its junction with the A4055.

8. **Public right of way.** The proposed development shall not infringe upon or obstruct the existing public right of way which runs along the north western boundary over the site.

9. **Landscape scheme.** A landscape scheme for the site showing the trees and shrubs to be retained and further planting, the details of paved areas, verges and other open spaces, shall be submitted to and approved by the Local Planning Authority before any work commences on site. The scheme shall be implemented not later than the first planting season after and thereafter maintained and replaced as necessary for a period of 5 years to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority.

10. **Drainage.** Before the commencement of the development hereby permitted, a scheme showing the means by which foul sewage and surface water run off are to be catered for shall be submitted to and approved in writing by the Local Planning Authority.

11. **Floor levels.** Details of proposed finished floor levels in relation to the existing ground levels and the finished levels of the site, including cross sections, shall be submitted to and agreed in writing with the Planning Authority before development commences.

12. **Parking.** The detail plans shall provide for parking facilities to meet the standards of the Local Planning Authority.

13. **Private garden space.** The proposed dwellings shall be provided with private garden amenity space based on a minimum standard of 200 sq. ft. per habitable room.

14. **Privacy.** The proposed dwelling(s) shall be designed so that no main windows of habitable rooms shall directly overlook the main windows of habitable rooms of other dwellings at a distance of less than 70 feet.

15. **Stone wall.** The existing stone wall fronting the site, where not directly affected by building operations or the proposed access, shall be retained and made good, in accordance with a scheme to be agreed in writing with the Local Planning Authority, before the development hereby approved is occupied.

16. **Archaeologist access.** The developer shall afford access at all reasonable times to any archaeologist nominated by the Local Planning Authority, and shall allow him/her to observe the excavations and record items of interest and finds.

17. **Archaeological survey.** No development shall take place until a field evaluation survey and archaeological assessment report has been carried out by a qualified archaeologist to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority. This work shall be carried out within three months of the date of this consent.

18. **Archaeological findings.** The detail plans to be submitted shall pay full regard to the results and findings of the archaeological investigations referred to in condition 17 above.

## Document 3: Comments and Recommendations by The Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust Ltd

**Title:** Land at Great House Farm, Llandough — Planning Application 89/01396/OUT  
**Author:** The Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust Ltd  
**Format:** Typeset report (3 pages including cover)

> **Image:** Cover page features a pen-and-ink illustration of a rural village scene showing a church tower with spire, houses, barns, and a curved road. Below the illustration is the title block and the GGAT logo (stylised letters "G" and "A" forming a monogram).

### 1. Introduction

1.1 This report has been divided into sections which outline the archaeological interest of Llandough and the archaeological potential of the development site, the place of archaeological interests in the planning process and a summary of our recommendations. For further information on the archaeology of Llandough members are respectfully referred to *Biglis, Caldicot and Llandough* (ed D M Robinson) published in the British Archaeological Reports Series, No 188 (1988).

### 2. Summary of the Archaeological Interest of Llandough and the Archaeological Potential of the Development Site

2.1 The church of St Dochdwy and the surrounding area is of considerable archaeological and historical interest. The churchyard is almost certainly on the site of an early christian monastic foundation which may have been founded as early as 650 AD; the cross shaft near the church dates to the tenth or eleventh century, and is of a scale and style that would mark an important religious site.

2.2 In 1979 during the course of the Wales and West Housing Association Development to the south of the church, emergency archaeological excavation revealed evidence of high quality. In summary part of an Iron Age farmstead was located, together with a Roman villa of high status, a dark age cemetery, a medieval monastic grange belonging to Tewkesbury Abbey, a late medieval village and a post medieval mill and farm. It is important to note that there was no visible trace of the farmstead, villa, cemetery or grange prior to the commencement of development at this site. Although valuable information was recovered during the excavation, the circumstances of discovery were unsatisfactory to both archaeological interests and those of the developer. It would not be in the interests of archaeologists, developers or the local community were such a situation to arise again.

2.3 At present our information on the Great House Farm area is limited to the records of the recently demolished property which was listed as a 'county treasure'. There is a possibility that archaeological deposits relating to earlier activity on this site may survive, and we believe that this possibility should be tested by a suitably qualified archaeologist.* This could be achieved in a rapid cost effective way by the use of a mechanical excavator under archaeological supervision. The results of such an operation would form the basis of an assessment report which would detail the archaeological implications of re-development and suggest what, if any, further archaeological action would be desirable. We estimate that the investigation and report could be completed within a period of two weeks at a cost of £2,000.

\* *As listed in the Institute of Field Archaeologists Directory of Members 1988-9*

### 3. Archaeology and Planning

3.1 There are a number of planning provisions that are relevant to the archaeological interest of this particular site.

> **Image:** Page 2 of the report continues with planning policy references. The page is typeset with footnote asterisks.

#### 3.2 South Glamorgan Structure Plan (June 1989)

**Policy EV 15 — Sites and Buildings of Archaeological Interest**

> Sites and buildings of archaeological interest will be protected by management measures and the control of new development, and provision will be made for the recording and rescue of important archaeological features, where they are to be disturbed by new development.

#### 3.3 East Vale Local Plan (February 1987)

**Policy E9**

> Where proposals for development may affect areas of archaeological interest, efforts will be made to secure adequate time and access for survey, excavation and recording.

Parts of the plan area have considerable archaeological interest, and careful study can answer many questions about the area's long history. As new development can destroy valuable evidence, relevant planning proposals will be referred to the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust for their observations. If a site is considered to be of archaeological significance efforts will be made to ensure that information is gained from the site before the remains are lost.

(It should be noted that at the consultation stage in the preparation of the East Vale Local Plan the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust understood that this site was designated for housing and a recommendation was made that there should be provision for prior archaeological investigation. (Letter to John Maitland Evans 1 October 1982, extract reproduced in Appendix to this Report)).

#### 3.4 Welsh Office Planning Guidance and Statutory Instruments

**Circular 61/81**

Section 26 states: 'where application is made to the local planning authority for planning permission to carry out development which would affect any monument, whether scheduled or unscheduled, the desirability of preserving the monument and its setting is a material consideration.'

**Statutory Instrument 1988, No 1812**

Section 4 confirms that a Local Planning Authority is empowered to direct an applicant to provide further information that may be necessary to enable a planning application to be determined.

**Circular 1/85**

Appendix B, Section 3 states that 'if any part of the development is premature, the proper course is to refuse permission for it.'

As even an unscheduled monument is a 'material consideration' in the planning process we believe that in this particular case the Local Planning Authority has insufficient information to determine the application. We therefore recommend that this application should not be determined until BP Properties have provided an archaeological assessment report prepared by a qualified archaeologist.*

\* *As listed in the Institute of Field Archaeologists Directory of Members 1988-9*
