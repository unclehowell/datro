JOURNAL OF EVENTS
Great House Farm, Llandough
April 1988 – March 1989

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Late April 1988

Bailiffs and police attempt to confront Billy Buckler while he is in poor
health. He refuses to leave the farm for a medical check-up, fearing they
will seize the house the moment he is gone.

On 29 April he is alleged to have used threatening words and behaviour at
Great House Farm. The Crown Prosecution Service will later withdraw the
allegation at Barry magistrates court as legally "out of time."


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

~11–12 May 1988

Bailiffs try to force entry. Billy blocks the narrow drive with a tractor,
locks the farmhouse doors and bars the windows. When five bailiffs and
around fifteen police officers arrive at Great House Farm, Llandough, they
smash the lock on the gate, walk round the tractor and begin smashing the
doors with poleaxes.

His children — aged two, three and four — are right behind the door when
an axe comes through. That is when he starts the chainsaw and pushes it
through the door; the bailiffs back off.

He stands his ground for hours with his family behind him, telling
reporters:

    "I am here to stay and I will fight this to the death."

He is convinced the farm is still theirs after what the press records as a
thirty-three-year legal struggle and nearly four hundred years of family
occupation. His distressed wife, Branwen, later leaves with the children
while he and several friends and tenants remain. He remains adamant he is
the true owner and asks the press:

    "They have got no documents for it. If it's theirs, why have I never
    paid a penny in rent for it in my life?"

A farm tenant, Christopher Thompson, tells reporters the flare-up is driven
by the Cardiff Bay development:

    "This land would be worth a fortune… Luxury houses could be built up
    here to overlook Cardiff Bay. The rich would get richer and the poor
    poorer."

A South Wales Police spokesman emphasises the officers were present only to
prevent a breach of the peace, not to assist the bailiffs.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

30 November 1988

At 5 AM, bailiffs force their way in and the family is evicted while Billy
is away at a court hearing. He is locked out; personal documents, family
photographs and the children's clothes are left behind or removed. From his
hospital bed he learns that the outbuildings are being stripped and that
the first physical destruction has begun.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1–2 December 1988

ITV News reports from Cardiff:

    "Good evening. The High Court in Cardiff today granted an injunction
    preventing the oil giant BP from demolishing a farm outside the city
    that's been at the center of a 30-year legal battle. But there were
    ugly scenes when one of the occupants, Mrs. Branwen Buckler, tried to
    serve the writ today on bailiffs who had evicted her and her husband
    yesterday."

    "The Buckler family have lived here at Great House Farm for more than
    four centuries. But yesterday, the present occupants Billy and Branwen
    Buckler were evicted in what neighbors have described as an SAS style
    operation. The events that followed the eviction led to the arrest of
    Billy Buckler. Mr. Buckler, who suffers from a heart condition, is now
    in custody at Llandough Hospital."

    "The bailiffs moved in following BP's victory in the courts, where seven
    judges in all ruled in favor of the company's claim. BP claimed they'd
    bought the freehold in 1969 and allowed the Bucklers to stay there. But
    for their part, the Bucklers say that since they've never paid any rent,
    they've acquired squatters rights. The farm occupies a slice of prime
    development land overlooking Cardiff."

    "But this morning, Mrs. Buckler won a temporary stay of execution. A
    High Court judge in Cardiff granted an injunction preventing BP from
    demolishing or removing anything from the property. Mrs. Buckler says
    she was assaulted and her shoulder dislocated when she came to serve
    that injunction today, but she remains optimistic about regaining her
    home."

    Mrs. Branwen Buckler, speaking to ITV:

        "We won a small victory today in getting the injunction and
        stopping them from doing any further damage to our home. And all
        we have to do now is hope that the judge will uphold his decision
        on Friday when there's a full hearing."

A High Court judge issues the interim injunction ordering that the farmhouse
and its contents must not be touched. Billy realises the legal fight is
narrowing to a final hearing, but the family is already locked out of their
own home.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

3 December 1988

The South Wales Echo reports that Billy has been in Llandough Hospital
since his eviction on Wednesday (30 November). Cadw, the Welsh historic
monuments body, confirms it is considering an emergency "spot-listing" appeal
to the Secretary of State for Wales to protect the farmhouse, parts of which
the family believe date back to the 12th Century. Inspectors are due to
visit the threatened farmhouse at the weekend. The newspaper notes that on
Monday the High Court will decide whether to extend the emergency procedure;
if the family fail, the farmhouse could be razed within days.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

5 December 1988

The High Court hearing fails; the emergency procedure is not extended.
Demolition begins within hours. Billy sees the farmhouse — possibly
centuries old — flattened and his family removed, feeling that everything
they had fought for has been wiped out in a single day.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Early–mid December 1988

Billy is discharged from hospital and surveys the ruins. Standing among the
rubble with only the clothes he is wearing, he realises he has lost the
house, the land, his livelihood and roughly £30,000 of personal possessions
taken from the site.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Late January 1989

At Barry magistrates court, the Crown Prosecution Service withdraws the
29 April charge; the prosecutor, Mr Jeff Clarke, tells the bench the
summons is legally "out of time" and cannot proceed. By now Billy is living
at an address in Penarth. The court orders him not to go within half a mile
of the Great House Farm site. He is due to appear again on 3 February to
face charges of assault and wanton and furious driving arising from the
events at the farm in November 1988.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

3 February 1989

Billy appears in court to face the assault and wanton-driving charges from
the November eviction.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

20 March 1989

W. J. Davies, the biggest contractor in the area, begins carting the stone
from all the farm buildings away to a refuse tip. Billy watches the last
physical traces disappear and records in his own hand:

    "A 1000 year of history is being lost on a tip."


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

23 March 1989

Billy appears in court in Cardiff. The police bring the case again,
threatening him with three years in jail unless he admits two of the charges
against the bailiffs. Under that pressure he pleads guilty; the other
charges are dropped. He is fined £375 for damages to the bailiffs and
credited with the ten days he has already served in jail. All his farm
machinery is being loaded away to be resold. He walks out knowing the
criminal cases are closing while the house and land are gone for good, yet
still publicly vows to keep fighting.
