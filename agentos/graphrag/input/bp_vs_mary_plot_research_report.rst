================================================================================
Executive Summary
================================================================================

This report reconstructs the documentary chain surrounding Great House Farm
(Ty-mawr), Llandough, between 1955 and 2026. The available evidence establishes
two separate Land Registry titles (WA231076 and WA240304) derived from a common
BP root of title, later amalgamated. The principal unresolved issue is whether the
occupied farmhouse and garden formed part of those titles or represented a legally
distinct parcel whose history has not yet been reconstructed. The report identifies
the surviving evidence and highlights the remaining documentary gaps requiring
archival investigation.

This chronology is an investigative framework, not a conclusion. The BP Plot
and Mary Plot columns are analytical categories created to test the hypothesis
that the occupied farmhouse and garden may have constituted a legally distinct
parcel. They are not assertions that two legally distinct plots have already been
proved. The report invites the reader to assess whether the accumulated
documentary evidence supports, weakens, or refutes that hypothesis.

================================================================================
Established Documentary Facts
================================================================================

The following facts are supported directly by primary documents:

- A 1962 possession order existed, obtained against the Bucklers but not enforced.
- A 1974 licence to occupy was offered to Mrs Mary Buckler by BP.
- Two HMLR titles were created: WA231076 (30 November 1982) and WA240304
  (23 February 1983).
- The two titles were later amalgamated under WA231076; WA240304 was closed.
- The house and farm buildings were demolished on 6 December 1988.
- HMLR acknowledges no Buckler interest was disclosed at first registration.
- An RCAHMW survey (ST 16 80 73 31) was conducted in 1974 while the farmhouse
  was occupied.

================================================================================
Evolution of the Property Description
================================================================================

The surviving legal record shows a consistent evolution in how the property is
described across different proceedings:

**1955**
   Possession proceedings concerned Great House Farm. The possession order was
   executed against the farm except the farmhouse and garden, which remained
   occupied.

**1962**
   Proceedings concerned only the farmhouse and garden, indicating the occupied
   residential parcel was treated separately from the agricultural land already
   recovered.

**1974**
   The dispute continued to concern the farmhouse and garden. The proposed BP
   licence also referred only to the house and garden.

**1987**
   The Court of Appeal repeatedly refers to the farmhouse and garden, whilst
   also describing the wider property as Great House Farm. The appeal itself
   concerned the occupied farmhouse and garden.

**Observation**

Throughout the surviving record there is a consistent distinction between
"Great House Farm" (the wider holding) and "the farmhouse and garden" (the
occupied residential parcel). Whether this distinction reflects two legally
distinct parcels, a retained curtilage, or simply different descriptions of land
within one title cannot be determined from the published documents alone and
requires examination of the original court plans, pleadings and title documents.

================================================================================
Relationship between WA231076 and WA240304
================================================================================

Known
-----

- The two titles were registered separately (30 November 1982 and 23 February 1983).
- They had different application dates.
- They were later amalgamated under WA231076; WA240304 was closed.
- Both titles derived from the same root of title (Conveyance 31/12/1969).
- Both were conveyed to BP Properties Limited via separate conveyances
  (23/05/1975 for WA240304; 19/11/1982 for WA231076).

Unknown
-------

- The exact land comprised in each title.
- Why two separate conveyances and registrations were necessary.
- Whether one title corresponded to the occupied farmhouse and the other to
  surrounding farm land.
- Whether either title excluded the farmhouse and curtilage.
- Whether the 1962 possession order applied to one, both, or neither title.

================================================================================
Key Investigative Issues
================================================================================

1. **The 1962 possession order** is the key investigative issue. The order was
   obtained against the Bucklers but NEVER ENFORCED for 12 years. The Court of
   Appeal records that enforcement was not pursued because of Mrs Buckler's
   recent discharge from hospital, her vulnerable condition and fears that she
   might commit suicide if evicted. Those humanitarian reasons are expressly
   recorded in the judgment. However, the published judgment does not state that
   these were the only considerations affecting enforcement. The judgment also
   does not determine whether Mrs Buckler asserted any independent proprietary
   rights, possessed historic deeds, relied upon an unregistered title, or
   advanced any ownership claim beyond adverse possession. The absence of
   discussion of those matters should not be treated as evidence that they did
   not exist.

2. **The legal descriptions evolve from "the farm" to "the farmhouse and garden."**
   The relationship between those descriptions and the later Land Registry titles
   remains unresolved. Determining whether they represented separate legal parcels
   is one of the principal unresolved questions.

3. **HMLR admits they did not investigate Buckler interests.** "At the time of
   registration we did not receive or were aware of any interest that you or
   your family may or may not have had in the land." This means HMLR registered
   BP's title WITHOUT checking if the Bucklers had a claim to a SEPARATE parcel.

4. **No independent conveyance or registered title has yet been identified for the
   occupied farmhouse and garden.** Whether one existed remains unknown. This
   absence of documentary evidence is itself an important subject for further
   archival research.

================================================================================
Possible Alternative Explanations Not Addressed by the Published Judgment
================================================================================

The published Court of Appeal judgment explains the continued occupation
primarily by reference to humanitarian considerations surrounding Mrs Buckler's
health and threatened suicide. It does not examine whether any additional legal,
evidential or practical considerations may also have influenced the parties'
conduct. In particular, the judgment does not determine whether Mrs Buckler
possessed historic title documents, asserted ownership under an earlier
conveyance or lease, or relied upon any unregistered proprietary interest. The
surviving published record therefore leaves these questions unresolved.

Questions Requiring Archival Research
================================================================================

1. What land was included in the 1962 possession order?
2. What land was included in WA240304?
3. What land was included in WA231076?
4. Why were two first registrations necessary?
5. Did either title exclude the farmhouse and curtilage?
6. Was there ever a separate root of title for the farmhouse and curtilage?
7. What was the relationship between the OS plot numbers and the registered titles?
8. Did Mrs Buckler ever claim title to land OTHER than the farmhouse and garden?
9. What happened to the farm buildings/land after the 1988 demolition?
10. Was the 1974 "licence" actually a disguised recognition of Mary Buckler's
    separate title?
11. Can the 1955 writ or possession plan be obtained?
12. Can the complete 1962 County Court file be located?
13. Can the 1974 pleadings and licence correspondence be obtained in full?
14. Can the 1987 Statement of Claim and its referenced red plan be located?
15. Can the extent of WA231076 and WA240304 be overlaid against historic OS
    mapping and the RCAHMW survey?
16. Did Mrs Mary Buckler ever produce or refer to deeds during any litigation?
17. Were any deeds exhibited to the 1955, 1962 or 1974 proceedings but not
    reproduced in the reported judgment?
18. Did BP or its predecessors ever acknowledge that Mrs Buckler claimed
    ownership independently of adverse possession?
19. Are there surviving solicitors' files, counsel's briefs or witness
    statements referring to title deeds or ownership claims?
20. Can any missing title documents be traced through archive catalogues,
    probate records, solicitors' archives or estate papers?
