Step-by-Step Guide: Challenging Detention Visit Restrictions
Step 1 – Send an Urgent Human Rights Demand to the Hospital
The woman must immediately establish a formal legal paper trail by emailing the hospital's senior leadership and patient services.
Who to Email:
• To (PALS): Find the Patient Advice and Liaison Service email on the hospital's website (e.g., pals@hospitaltrust.nhs.uk).
• CC (Chief Executive): Search the NHS Trust name on CEO Email UK <https://ceoemail.com>_ to find the direct email of the Chief Executive.
Draft Email Template:
::
Subject: URGENT: Human Rights Act Challenge – Unlawful Restriction of Visiting Rights – [Partner's Full Name] – DOB: [Partner's Date of Birth]
Dear Chief Executive and Patient Advice and Liaison Team,
RE: Unlawful ban on conjugal visits for my partner, [Partner's Full Name], currently detained at [Ward Name / Hospital Name].
I am writing to formally challenge the blanket restriction currently preventing me from having conjugal and private visits with my partner. This decision is entirely disproportionate and directly breaches our fundamental rights under Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (Right to respect for private and family life), as incorporated into domestic law by the Human Rights Act 1998.
Under Section 6 of the Human Rights Act 1998, it is strictly unlawful for a public authority, including an NHS Hospital Trust, to act incompatibly with Convention rights.
Please note the following critical points regarding my partner's situation:
* Mental Capacity: My partner possesses full mental capacity to make decisions regarding intimacy and personal relationships. No formal capacity assessment stating otherwise has been issued.
* Lack of Justification: A blanket policy or generic ward restriction that completely blocks conjugal visits without a specific, individualized risk assessment is irrational and legally unsustainable.
As a public authority, you must show that any interference with our family life is necessary and proportionate. The current total ban fails this test.
My Urgent Requests:
1. Immediate Review: I request an immediate, individualized review of this visiting restriction by senior management.
2. Disclosure of Policy: Please provide the exact written policy document or clinical assessment being relied upon to enforce this separation.
3. Restoration of Visits: Please confirm in writing within 7 days how and when private, intimate visits between my partner and me will be safely accommodated.
I am currently seeking representation from a Civil Liberties and Public Law solicitor to initiate a formal judicial review of this policy. A swift resolution on your part will avoid unnecessary and costly litigation for the Trust.
I look forward to your urgent response.
Yours sincerely,
[The Woman's Full Name]
[Her Phone Number]
[Her Home Address]
Step 2 – Contact PALS
Submit the complaint to the hospital's Patient Advice and Liaison Service (PALS) using the email in Step 1.
Ask PALS to:
• Arrange an urgent review.
• Explain the legal and clinical basis for the restriction.
• Confirm the complaints procedure.
• Facilitate a meeting with the ward manager or Responsible Clinician.
Step 3 – Ask for a Multidisciplinary Review
Request a meeting with:
• The Responsible Clinician,
• The ward manager,
• The social worker (if there is one),
• And, if appropriate, the patient's Independent Mental Health Advocate (IMHA).
Key Question:
Ask whether less restrictive arrangements have been considered rather than a blanket prohibition.
Step 4 – Make a Formal NHS Complaint
If PALS does not resolve matters within the 7-day window, submit a formal complaint to the NHS Trust's designated Complaints Department.
Include:
• Dates and times of refused visits;
• Names of staff involved;
• Why she believes the decision is unfair;
• The effect on her partner, herself, and the children;
• What outcome she wants (for example, an immediate review and appropriate visiting arrangements).
Step 5 – Ensure Her Partner Has an Advocate
If he is detained under the Mental Health Act, ask the ward staff for an Independent Mental Health Advocate (IMHA) if he does not already have one. The advocate can help him challenge restrictions and ensure his views are heard.
Step 6 – Contact the Care Quality Commission (CQC)
If she believes the hospital is failing to respect patients' rights or is operating unfairly, she can notify the CQC.
• Contact Email: enquiries@cqc.org.uk
• Note: The CQC will not resolve an individual complaint but may investigate concerns about how the service is operating.
Step 7 – Contact a Specialist Solicitor (Legal Aid)
Seek advice from a solicitor specialising in mental health law, public law, and human rights. Because the woman is on benefits, she is highly likely to qualify for Free Legal Aid.
Top UK Legal Aid Firms to Contact:
• Wilson Solicitors LLP: public@wilsonllp.co.uk (Public Law & Human Rights Specialists)
• Duncan Lewis Solicitors: civilcomplaints@duncanlewis.com (Request the Public Law Department)
• The Public Law Project (Charity): enquiries@publiclawproject.org.uk
• Civil Legal Advice Helpline (Government): Call 0345 345 4 345 to be matched to a free lawyer.
Draft Email Template to Solicitors:
::
Subject: Legal Aid Inquiry: Urgent Judicial Review / Human Rights Act Claim (Article 8)
Dear Public Law Team,
I am writing to urgently request legal representation regarding a breach of the Human Rights Act 1998 by an NHS Hospital Trust.
My partner is currently medically detained at [Hospital Name]. The hospital has enforced a blanket ban preventing us from having conjugal/private visits, completely separating us. My partner has full mental capacity to consent to intimacy, and no individual risk assessment has been provided to justify this restriction. This is a severe and ongoing breach of our Article 8 rights (Right to respect for private and family life).
I am currently on receipt of public benefits ([Name of Benefit, e.g., Universal Credit]) and I believe I qualify for Legal Aid under public law.
I have today sent a formal letter to the Hospital Trust's Chief Executive giving them 7 days to resolve this. Due to the strict 3-month time limit for a Judicial Review, I require urgent advice and a solicitor to issue a formal Pre-Action Protocol letter on my behalf.
Could you please confirm if you have capacity to take on this case under Legal Aid? I can be reached immediately at [Your Phone Number].
Yours faithfully,
[The Woman's Full Name]
Step 8 – Contact Her MP
Write to her Member of Parliament (MP) to apply political pressure on the hospital trust.
Who to Email:
• Find her MP's direct email address by entering her home postcode at Find Your MP <https://parliament.uk>_.
Draft Email Template to MP:
::
Subject: Urgent Constituent Matter: Human Rights Breach by NHS Trust at [Hospital Name]
Dear [MP's Last Name],
As a constituent living at [Her Address], I am writing to urgently request your assistance regarding a severe breach of human rights being committed by [Name of NHS Trust / Hospital].
My partner is currently medically detained at [Hospital Name]. The hospital management has put a blanket ban on conjugal and private visits between us, keeping us entirely separated. This is causing immense distress to myself, my partner, and our children.
This blanket policy directly breaches our right to private and family life under Article 8 of the Human Rights Act 1998. My partner has full mental capacity, and no individual risk assessment has been done to justify this total ban. I am currently on benefits and attempting to secure a Legal Aid solicitor to bring a Judicial Review against the Trust.
I kindly ask that you contact the Chief Executive of the NHS Trust on my behalf to request an urgent, immediate review of these visiting restrictions.
Thank you for your time and urgent attention to this matter.
Yours sincerely,
[The Woman's Full Name]
[Her Phone Number]
Step 9 – Escalate to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman
If the NHS formal complaints process is completed and she remains dissatisfied, she can ask the Ombudsman to investigate.
• Contact Portal: PHSO Making a Complaint <https://ombudsman.org.uk>_
• Note: This step requires her MP to sign off on the form before submission.
Step 10 – Keep an Evidence File
Maintain a strict file to hand over to the Legal Aid lawyer:
• A diary of every refused visit;
• Copies of all emails and letters sent and received;
• Names of staff spoken to;
• Notes of meetings;
• Any written policies provided by the ward;
• Evidence of the impact on her partner, herself, and the children (e.g., doctor notes about stress, children's distress);
• Proof of benefits (e.g., a printed copy of her latest Universal Credit statement).
What You Should Ask For (The Remedies)
In all communications, she must focus on practical remedies, such as:
• An immediate review of the restriction;
• Written reasons for the decision;
• Disclosure of the relevant policy;
• An individual risk assessment rather than a blanket restriction;
• Appropriate arrangements for family contact if safe and clinically justified;
• An apology if mistakes have been made;
• Changes to hospital policy if it has been applied unfairly.
