PAGE 19 – 1938 DESCRIPTION OF PROPERTY
--------------------------------------
The Very Attractive Modern Farm House, which is well built of stone with a tiled roof, is very pleasantly situated, well planned, and in an excellent state of repair and decoration throughout. It contains Hall, Two Reception Rooms, Kitchen, Pantry, Dairy, Back Kitchen, Bath Room, Six Excellent Bedrooms and Dressing Room, together with the usual offices outside. Garden and Orchard.

The Very Excellent Buildings comprise a well-designed range, stone built and tiled, around the yard, including Four-Stall Stable and Loose Box, Barn, Two Modern Cow Sheds to tie nine cows each, Fodder House with Loose Box, and Calf Shed. Stone-built and Slated Cart Shed with Cow Shed to tie four, and loft overhead. Stone-built and Tiled Boiler House with two Pig Sties adjoining. Sheep Dip. Three Wooden and Galvanised Fowl Houses.

The Buildings on enclosure No. 73 comprise Stone Built Barn with slated roof, and wooden and galvanised Implement Shed.

The Electric Light is installed throughout the house and to most of the buildings, and the public supply of water is laid on to the house, to several of the buildings, and to water bowls in the cow-sheds. There is also an excellent soft water cistern at the house.

The Tithe Redemption Annuity is £25 5s. 0d.

IMAGE 19 OF 53
---------------
Description: Handwritten memoir page 1, cursive script on lined paper.

Extracted Data:
- Reference: DCO/WHS/2
- Content: "Glimpses into life amongst the farming community in the Vale of Glamorgan roundabout 1880 as related to me by my maternal grandfather Mr David Thomas of Great House Farm Llandough, nr Cowbridge."
- Describes childhood at "The Glebe Farm, St Hilary" where he was born; attended a dame's school in the village where reading, writing and arithmetic only were taught in Welsh.
- Witnessed the last hanging for sheep stealing in Glamorgan, when a poor wretch was brought bound to a cart from Cowbridge jail, and hanged on a tree near the clump of trees on Stalling Down.
- Before the birth of his brother, he vividly remembered being carried on the shoulders of a farm servant dressed in a dark smock embroidered with cart-wheels to his grandparents' home at Llanwithen (Llantwit Major).
- Later the family moved to Llanmihangel Place, once a Tudor mansion, then part of the Earl of Dunraven's estate. Still holding some treasures in tapestry and pictures belonging to the original owners. The house contained dungeons which he believed haunted by Cromwellian soldiers, whose chains rattled when the wind sighed through the oak grove behind the house. Queen Anne is supposed to have slept at Llanmihangel Place.

IMAGE 20 OF 53
---------------
Description: Handwritten memoir page 2.

Extracted Data:
- "I doubt this to be authentic"
- Before farm machinery, oxen were employed; ploughing by oxen, mostly white, at Llanmihangel Place in grandfather's youth.
- After leaving St Hilary he attended the Eagle School at Cowbridge, then the Edward VI Grammar School, then Jesus College Oxford.
- Thus he was able to teach many of the farm labourers to read and write.
- Iolo Morganwg used to visit my grandfather at Llandough when both were very old men; his name was Evan Lewis and his great hero was Lord Shaftesbury.
- Cowbridge was a highway town between the ports of Cardiff and Swansea; the stage coach stopped regularly at The Duke of Wellington's Inn; horses were driven trotting up the hill into The Butts.
- The town hall stood in the middle of the street, in front of the present building; the old name Pontfaen was changed to Cowbridge when a cow being led to a nearby slaughter house got caught by her horns and had to be removed from under the bridge.
- The road called Primrose Hill leading from the main road to the clump of trees at the top of Stalling Down.

IMAGE 21 OF 53
---------------
Description: Handwritten memoir page 3.

Extracted Data:
- About Iolo Morganwg: colourful stone mason, literary man, lived his last years and buried at Flemingston near St Athan.
- A wall built by him still stands on the left of the road leading to the village of Llandough where three generations of my family have lived at Great House Farm.
- This wall took 8 years to complete as Iolo was often in London executing commissions in tracing family trees and heraldic backgrounds for noblemen.
- On one such night he presented himself at the top of a flight of marble steps, knocked at the door of one of his client noblemen, only to be roughly handled by the powdered footman.
- Iolo shouted "Touch a Welshman if you dare! We were men of high renown before the Saxon wore the crown."
- Before gaining admission and receiving a commission, before returning to his stone mason's work at Llandough.
- This wall enclosed a field and orchard and stables as part of the hunting lodge of the Talbot family, known now as Llandough Cottage or The Cottage.
- Once visited by Thomas Carlyle, who described the village of Llandough as "the quietest, most deserted spot on earth."
- Iolo lived with his daughter Peggy in a ramshackle farm cottage at Flemingston, but they were often invited to dwell at the country houses of local gentry.
- On one occasion Peggy discovered a large hole in her well-worn black woollen stocking, but with ready wit covered the naked leg with soot, and accompanied her scholarly father.
- My grandfather very deeply regretted that many of Iolo's manuscripts must have been burnt, and much learning lost, when after Iolo's death the cottage and contents passed into the hands of a local farmer unable to appreciate their true value to Welsh literature.
