Ministry of Defence,

I am writing to request information under the Freedom of Information Act 2000 regarding any involvement of UK Armed Forces personnel in mine, my pregnant mother and heart conditioned fathers eviction/dispossession that took place in 1988 at Ty Mawr (Great House Farm), Llandough, near Penarth, Vale of Glamorgan.

Specifically, I request the following:

1. Whether any units of the UK Armed Forces, including but not limited to UK Special Forces (e.g. SAS), were deployed, tasked, or otherwise involved in this incident before, during or after the 1988 eviction (involving upto a reported 30 police officers).

2. Any records, logs, operational reports, orders, requests or communications referring to military support to civil authorities in relation to this event.

3. Any documentation relating to Military Aid to the Civil Authorities (MACA) requests connected to this incident.

4. Any correspondence between the Ministry of Defence and:

+ South Wales Police
+ Vale of Glamorgan Council
+ Welsh Office (as it existed at the time)
concerning the eviction or associated operations.

5. Any internal assessments, reviews, or after-action reports relating to the use of force or military involvement in this case.

For clarity, the incident involved the removal of myself and my family from Ty Mawr (Great House Farm) in 1988, reportedly involving a significant and coordinated enforcement presence.

If any of the requested information is held but exempt from disclosure, please specify the relevant exemption(s) and provide any information that can be released in redacted form.

If the cost of compliance exceeds the statutory limit, I would be grateful if you could advise how the scope of this request might be refined.

Please confirm receipt of this request and provide a reference number.

Regards,

Sion Buckler Esq. (Ret'd Sgt)
Manor of Llandough, Vale of Glamorgan
Service ID: 25148537

Dear Information Rights Compliance Team,

I am writing to request an internal review of FOI2026/09019, and to formally clarify that this correspondence should also be treated as a Subject Access Request under UK GDPR (Article 15) insofar as it relates to my personal data.

1. Dual legal basis (FOIA + UK GDPR)
My original request concerns an incident in which I was a child directly present in the household at the time of the 1988 eviction/dispossession. This engages not only FOIA considerations but also my right of access to personal data under UK GDPR, including any information from or about me held within MOD systems, historical records, liaison files, or operational documentation.

Accordingly, please ensure this review is handled with the correct distinction between:
FOIA exemptions relating to third-party or operational sensitivity, and
UK GDPR obligations regarding personal data concerning a data subject (myself)
Where information is mixed personal and third-party data, I expect proper consideration of redaction rather than blanket refusal or “no information held” responses where records exist.

2. Anticipated issue: “No information held”
Your response states that no information is held for the non–section 23 parts of my request.
Given the nature of the event described (a coordinated enforcement operation involving multiple police officers), this conclusion requires clear evidential support.
Please confirm whether the search performed included:
MACA (Military Aid to the Civil Authorities) liaison records and requests
Regional military headquarters or district command diaries (Wales / Western regions, circa 1988)
Civil contingency planning files involving police support or cross-agency assistance
Defence–Home Office / Welsh Office liaison correspondence streams
Archived operational logs later transferred to The National Archives (including DEFE/WO series material)
If these were not explicitly searched, please explain why they were considered out of scope.
A conclusion of “no information held” is only reliable if searches were targeted, reasonably comprehensive, and capable of identifying transferred archival holdings, not limited to centrally indexed MOD FOI repositories.

3. Anticipated issue: “Section 23(5) neither confirm nor deny”
I acknowledge the application of section 23(5) in relation to Special Forces information.
However, I request that the internal review confirms:
Whether the “NCND” position was applied narrowly to Special Forces only, or
Whether it has been used in a manner that may have inadvertently limited wider searches across non-exempt MACA or civil support records
It is important that section 23 is not treated as a proxy limitation on the scope of the entire request, as my request explicitly extends beyond Special Forces involvement.

4. Adequacy of search (core issue)
The key issue for review is not exemption alone, but whether the search strategy was capable of identifying relevant historical operational liaison records at all.
Given the passage of time (1988), I would expect a reasonable approach to include:
Historical / archived command records
Decommissioned or transferred MACA documentation
Cross-referencing of police support operations involving military liaison
Keyword and event-based archival searches rather than only current MOD holdings
If none of these methods were used, please set out the reasoning for limiting the search methodology.

5. UK GDPR – personal data scope
As I was a child present during the events described, I formally request confirmation of:
Whether any MOD-held records contain personal data relating to me
Whether any such data has been withheld under FOIA exemptions without assessment under UK GDPR
Whether a separate Article 15 disclosure search has been conducted or will now be initiated
If necessary, I request that this part of my request be processed independently as a SAR with appropriate identity verification steps.

6. Requested outcome of internal review
For clarity, I am seeking a review that specifically determines:
Whether the MOD’s search strategy was sufficiently wide to capture historical MACA / civil support records
Whether “no information held” remains a supportable conclusion after inclusion of archived and transferred record series
Whether FOIA and UK GDPR obligations have been correctly separated and applied
Please confirm receipt of this request for internal review and confirm whether it will also be logged as a Subject Access Request under UK GDPR.

Yours sincerely,

Mr Sion Buckler (Ret'd Sgt)

Dear Mr Buckler,

Receipt is acknowledged of your email of 9 June 2026 requesting an internal review of your request, our reference FOI2026/09019.

The Department's target for completing internal reviews is 20 working days from date of receipt. We therefore aim to have a response with you on or before 21 July 2026. However, in the interests of providing you with a realistic indication of when you should expect a response, the majority are currently taking longer depending on their complexity, but we will aim to complete the review between 20 and 40 working days.

The review will involve an independent assessment of the processing of the requests and the outcome.

Yours sincerely,
MOD Information Rights Compliance Team
