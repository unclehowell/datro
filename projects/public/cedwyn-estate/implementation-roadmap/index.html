The project is active as a digital and planning exercise. The physical museum follows staged evidence and approval gates; no opening date, land parcel or construction budget has been set.

Quality and change control

No silent scope expansion
  • All architectural and historical content receives a source, date, rights note and confidence/status label.
  • Concept drawings carry a permanent “not to scale / not a survey” label until replaced by verified survey products.
  • Land and construction decisions require written local specialist review and a costed option.
  • Every work package identifies product, owner role, cost cap, target date, acceptance test and risk controls before authorisation.
  • Any proposed scope change states effect on cost, time, quality, sustainability, risk and benefits before approval.
  • Public opening, prices, accommodation, weddings, equestrian facilities and visitor forecasts remain uncommitted until a relevant stage gate passes.

Reporting and decision cadence

Proposed governance

Monthly highlight report: stage status, deliverables accepted, spend and commitments by workstream, risks/issues, decisions needed, funding status and forecast. A stage-end report should include lessons, unresolved data gaps, updated business case and a recommendation to proceed, pause, amend or stop.

Until a local delivery team is appointed, role assignments are provisional. The Executive retains budget and continuation decisions; technical specialists certify their own scoped outputs; assurance is independent of the supplier who prepared the product.
