Identify whether a suitable, legally available and environmentally responsible site can be found south of Puerto Madryn. This is a search brief: no coordinates, parcel, owner, purchase option or preferred lot has been established.

Purpose and constraints

Define the search before fieldwork

The search is for a museum-house and interpreted landscape inspired by the original Tŷ Mawr estate. It should not assume a particular landowner, available property, boundary or planning status.

Geographic principle

The Cardiff–Tŷ Mawr–coast and Puerto Madryn–future site–coast relationship is a design hypothesis. Use defined points and identical distance methods for any comparison; label route distance separately from straight-line distance.

Desktop reconnaissance

Before visiting land
  1. Define search boundary and GIS method; document data source, date, coordinate reference system and accuracy.
  2. Map the historical Welsh site, Cardiff reference point, shoreline references and Puerto Madryn town centre so the geographic concept can be tested fairly.
  3. Develop a broad search area south of Puerto Madryn, then shortlist at least three plausible candidate areas if available data supports it.
  4. Screen cadastral information, current title, ownership and authority to negotiate through local counsel. Do not infer availability from an online map.
  5. Overlay planning/zoning, road access, rights of way, water supply, wastewater, electricity, telecoms, topography and public services.
  6. Identify conservation designations, sensitive habitats, wildlife corridors, shoreline/coastal rules, flood exposure, fire/weather exposure and any cultural or community constraints.
  7. Estimate travel times for visitors, construction access, material supply, emergency services and staff.

Site visits and due diligence

Written permission and local specialists

Field visits occur only after land access is authorised. Appoint local legal, survey, environmental and planning specialists under written scopes. A visit is not a title investigation, and a visual inspection cannot establish soil suitability, water rights or permission to build.

  • Confirm cadastral identification, seller authority, encumbrances, easements and permitted uses with local counsel.
  • Commission topographic survey and preliminary ground/soil assessment for shortlisted sites where proportionate.
  • Confirm water availability and lawful extraction, wastewater options, drainage, fire access and infrastructure costs.
  • Undertake a qualified ecological screen before any landscape, trail or equestrian plan.
  • Seek early feedback from municipal/regional planning staff and local cultural groups, without implying endorsement.

Required outputs and stage gate

Acceptance criteria
  • GIS atlas showing assumptions, distance measures, constraints and data quality.
  • Land schedule with cadastral identifier only where lawfully established, source date and due-diligence status.
  • Shortlist comparison with cost ranges, red flags, consultation notes and explicit information gaps.
  • Local professional opinions and recommendation to proceed, amend the search, use an existing building or stop.

Gate: no offer, deposit or purchase approval until title/authority, permitted use, environmental feasibility, access/utilities and a preliminary cost case have passed independent review.

Cost baseline: to be quoted by local professionals. Schedule baseline: set only after access to cadastral data and the applicable planning pathway is understood.
