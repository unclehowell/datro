Business Case — the digital estate

Estate level · version 1.0 · 20 October 2026

Reasons

The estate carries a public record that people rely on, and much of it was built in a few weeks by several workers at once. Without one register, assets go unnoticed when they break (7 addresses were down when this register was first checked), backups are uneven, and privacy slips are hard to catch everywhere at once.

Options

OptionSummaryAssessment
Do nothingKeep building sites ad hocRising risk of silent outages, lost data and privacy leaks
Do the minimumA list of sites onlyShows what exists, not whether it is safe
Do something (chosen)PRINCE2-lite control of the estate: register, registers, stages, reportsSmall ongoing cost; makes every later change safer

Expected benefits

  • Every asset known, owned (by role) and status-checked.
  • Faster recovery: each site rebuildable from Git or a recorded export.
  • Privacy protected across all sites by one check.
  • Independence: the public record survives the loss of any single platform.
  • Lower hosting waste (e.g. wasted preview builds removed on 19 Oct 2026).

Expected dis-benefits

  • Some time spent on records rather than new content.
  • Change control slows quick fixes slightly.

Timescale

Stages S0–S3, 19 October to 30 November 2026 (see stage plans).

Costs

Hosting stays on free plans except the existing paid storage. Possible small costs, each needing the Executive’s approval: an archive.org account is free; a larger hosted-wiki plan or paid hosting only if a limit is hit.

Major risks

See the Risk Register: single accounts, backups, privacy, rate limits, credential rotation, disk space on the worker machine.
