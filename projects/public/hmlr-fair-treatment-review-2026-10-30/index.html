HM Land Registry — fair-treatment and process-consistency assessment

  • Date 30 October 2026
  • Project P2 — Wales records and testimony recovery
  • Status AMBER · concern open for testing
  • Control R8 — proposed process-risk

This addendum records a concern about the handling and classification of public enquiries. It distinguishes the recorded chronology from the requester's experience and from questions that remain to be tested. It does not find unlawful discrimination, deliberate hostility or an improper motive.

1. Purpose and standard

Ordinary, consistent treatment — not preferential access

The requester is a descendant of the historic parties and is researching the matter as a member of the public and citizen researcher. The connection explains the subject matter and why the records are personally significant. It does not create a right to preferential access or a different evidential standard.

The standard to test is ordinary and consistent public-service treatment: clear routing, accurate classification, proportionate verification where required, and reasoned answers to properly scoped enquiries. Any concern about unequal treatment should be supported by a specific decision, the relevant policy and, where available, genuinely comparable cases.

3. The complaint-classification concern

A question about intake and routing, not a claim that Stage 2 was refused

The requester describes the initial move into Stage 1 complaint handling as abrupt, defensive and hostile. He says the original purpose was to research the register and obtain procedural and documentary answers as a member of the public. The record shows that a Stage 1 decision existed by 19 April and that he then expressly requested Stage 2 to exhaust the available internal route.

Those propositions can coexist. The fact that Stage 2 was later requested must not be rewritten as proof that the requester originally set out to complain. Conversely, the current correspondence does not yet prove that HMLR unilaterally converted the 31 January report to a complaint without explanation.

Records needed to settle this point

  • The original intake record and case-opening classification for the 31 January report.
  • The date, decision-maker role and recorded reason for assigning the Stage 1 complaint route.
  • The procedure or policy applied to a report about possible registration irregularity where the customer seeks factual records and procedural guidance.
  • Any explanation given to the requester before the Stage 1 decision was issued.

4. Identity verification and equal treatment

Separate SAR safeguard; necessity and proportionality to be tested

HMLR's 27 October email asked for a copy of photographic identity evidence (for example passport or driving licence) together with a recent utility bill before continuing SAR D260928. HMLR stated that it would make a file note and would not retain copies of the documents supplied.

Identity verification is a recognised SAR safeguard. The fair-treatment question is not whether HMLR may verify identity in principle. It is why these particular two forms of evidence were considered necessary in this case, whether a less intrusive alternative would have sufficed, how the documents would be handled, and whether the same standard is applied consistently to comparable requesters.

This request must not be described as a condition of FOI F261030, EIR F261029 or the distinct registration enquiry 260928-6762800 / N610CGV. Those processes have separate legal bases and reference numbers.

5. Pattern watch — observable indicators

Record each occurrence; do not infer motive from one event
  • Classification: an enquiry is labelled a complaint before the requester has asked to invoke a complaint route, without explanation or opportunity to correct the category.
  • Route conflation: a completed complaint is used to close or avoid a later properly scoped records, FOI/EIR, SAR or registration enquiry.
  • Verification scope: identity documents are demanded for a process that does not require identity verification, or the scope of an SAR check is expanded to unrelated enquiries.
  • Proportionality: two or more identity documents are required without an explained necessity or without considering available alternatives.
  • Routing and response: obsolete contact details, repeated generic referrals, unexplained legal referral or failure to identify the team handling a specific request.
  • Consistency: demonstrably different requirements or treatment from genuinely comparable public enquirers, supported by records rather than assumption.
  • Family-context handling: any recorded reference to the requester's descent from historic parties being used to discount, escalate or otherwise change the handling of an otherwise ordinary public enquiry.

For each occurrence, preserve the dated original, channel, reference, exact words, applicable procedure, response and any comparison evidence. Do not collect or publish unrelated individuals' personal data.

6. Assessment and limits

What can and cannot presently be concluded

Supported: a Stage 1 decision was issued; Stage 2 was expressly requested by the requester; he later distinguished factual records enquiries from reopening the complaint; the SAR identity request was issued on 27 October; HMLR stated that copies would not be retained; and several separate HMLR reference routes are active or have reached different endpoints.

Open: who first classified the registration-integrity report as a complaint; why it was routed to Stage 1; whether the identity check required both documents; what alternative verification was considered; and whether materially comparable public requesters were treated differently.

Not established: unlawful discrimination, differential treatment because of family connection, deliberate hostility, vexatious intent or an attempt to force litigation. The current material supports an auditable fairness concern, not a finding about motive or legal liability.

7. Control actions

P2 — open for follow-through
  1. Keep the HMLR routes separate in the register and all future reporting.
  2. Seek the original intake/classification record and reason for the Stage 1 route.
  3. For SAR D260928, request clarification of necessity, alternatives and handling of identity evidence. Do not upload passport, utility bill or private address material into HubSpot, the Legal Wiki or public project records.
  4. Continue to monitor HMLR correspondence for the indicators in section 5, including later public-body enquiries where the same process question arises.
  5. Ask independent Hive reviewers to test the chronology, proportionality and comparator evidence before any formal assertion of unequal treatment is made.
  6. Keep the issue open pending the intake record, SAR response and any comparable evidence. Escalate only against a specific documented act and the applicable procedure.
