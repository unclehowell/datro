Update — 30 October 2026: HM Land Registry fair treatment

P2 · process and equal-treatment concern · AMBER
  1. Background and classification. The first procedural email of 29 January 2026 was sent to an address that rejected delivery. A separate registration-integrity report was sent on 31 January and was acknowledged and triaged by HM Land Registry on 2 February. A Stage 1 decision followed on 19 April. The requester then expressly asked for Stage 2 on 19 April as part of exhausting the internal route. The available correspondence does not yet establish who first classified the earlier enquiry as a complaint, when that decision was made, or the reason recorded for it. The concern is the timing and explanation of that classification, not the fact that Stage 2 was later used.
  2. Subsequent distinction of routes. After the Stage 2 decision of 25 May, later requests sought access to or clarification of identified first-registration records and repeatedly distinguished those questions from reopening the complaint. The separate ICR/090/26 review of complaint handling ended with a final decision not to investigate further on 21 October.
  3. Identity verification — SAR only. On 27 October HM Land Registry asked for photographic identity evidence and a recent utility bill to continue subject access request D260928, stating that it would make a file note but would not retain copies. This request is not recorded as a condition of the FOI request F261030, EIR request F261029 or the separate registration enquiry 260928-6762800 / N610CGV, which was referred to a lawyer on 22 October. Do not merge these routes or describe the SAR check as a demand attached to all correspondence.
  4. Fair-treatment issue. The requester is a descendant of the historic parties and is researching the matter as a member of the public and citizen researcher. That connection is relevant context, not a basis for preferential treatment. The question is whether he received the ordinary, consistent treatment due to any member of the public: clear routing, accurate classification, proportionate identity checks where applicable, and reasoned answers to properly scoped enquiries.
  5. What remains unproven. The present record does not establish unlawful discrimination, differential treatment because of family connection, or deliberate hostility. The identity request may be a routine and proportionate SAR safeguard. The fair-treatment concern is to be tested against the original intake/classification record, the policy applied to D260928, any less intrusive verification options, and genuinely comparable cases.
