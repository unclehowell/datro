A mini-project inside Parcel A (Phase 1). It stays on standby until a trigger is confirmed, then runs the trigger-event SOP to a filing. Planning stance: hope for the best, plan for the worst.

Standby. No trigger confirmed. The clocks start on the discovery date (T0) once Gate 1 is passed.

Project Brief

Background, objective, scope

Background

The Court of Appeal gave judgment for BP Properties Ltd on 31 July 1987 ([1987] EWCA Civ 2); the House of Lords refused leave on 3 March 1988; possession was taken on 29 November 1988 and demolition began on 6 December 1988. Application 14464/88 to the European Commission of Human Rights was declared inadmissible on 14 April 1989. The reopen-loop scores possible triggers every 4 hours (reopen triggers).

Objective

File the right application, in time, within days of a confirmed trigger, with evidence that will stand up.

Scope

  • In: the SOP, evidence preservation, legal opinion, route choice, the filing.
  • Out: the FOI and SAR programme (WP-FOI), the press programme, Phase 2.

Constraints

  • No court step without a written legal opinion.
  • No outside letter that names a private individual.
  • Every clock in the SOP is met.

Benefits

  • The judgment set aside, title restored or compensation (route B, D).
  • A Strasbourg finding against the United Kingdom (route C1).
  • A full public record of what happened.

Costs

£0 until Gate 2. Counsel’s fee estimate is a Gate 2 product; public access, pro bono and conditional fee options are compared there. Adverse costs exposure is assessed in the opinion.

Planning stance: hope for the best, plan for the worst

Best, expected and worst paths
PathWhat happens
Best caseGate 1 passes on a primary record; pro bono or public access counsel within 2 weeks; set-aside claim issued within 8 weeks of T0; money and land actions under s.32 alongside; judgment set aside.
Expected caseThe record needs official copies (2–6 weeks); opinion by T0 + 6 to 10 weeks; letter before action, then a claim; defended; an appeal; a final domestic decision, then the Strasbourg four months if needed.
Worst caseThe record is not accepted, or the opinion finds no route; nothing filed; the evidence is kept, the reopen-loop continues, and the FOI, ICO and ombudsman routes carry on. Innocent purchasers since 1988 are outside reach (s.32(3)); money routes against those responsible remain.
