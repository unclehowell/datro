Dear Sion Buckler,

Thank you for contacting The National Archives of the United Kingdom.

It is unlikely The National Archives holds the records of this type of case, and the Central Land Registry , who hold records of land registration, is a separate body. Some records of land ownership can be found among records at local or county archives.

I recommend you consult our guide to searching for records of Land ownership records and deeds: an overview - The National Archives . Regarding the court cases you mention, I also recommend you consult: Civil court cases: Chancery Division since 1875 - The National Archives , and you may also find Civil courts: appeal cases after 1875 - The National Archives  of use to your research. These guides will help you to use our catalogue , or other online sources, effectively and focus your search.

Records are represented in our online catalogue by a title, a reference and a brief description. When you search our catalogue using keywords you are searching across these titles and descriptions, rather than the contents of the records themselves. By clicking on any search result, you can see whether the record is available online or if you need to visit us to view the original. You can also request copies of records from their catalogue description page.

If the record is held by another archive, you will need to contact them directly to find out how you can access it.

You can also watch this video  for more advice on using our online catalogue.

You are welcome to visit us  to continue your research here in person, where access to our records is free of charge. Before you visit, please check our website for information regarding our opening hours  and ID requirements . If you are unable to visit, or would like someone to pursue this research on your behalf, please see the available paid search options  on our website.

Yours sincerely,

Sirs/Ma'am,

I'm writing to raise a concern regarding the integrity of the historical record following disputed dispossession of land i.e. BP vs Buckler 1987

After forced removal and demolition of our home, archaeological excavation and removal of items took place on land whose ownership had never been conclusively adjudicated at the time of registration. BP asserted it, but it was never determined in a ruling beforehand as the July 1974 case between BP and my grandmother was adjourned and the October 31st 1974 appeal and audience with a judge she made just 24 hours before, was squashed by letters sent by BP that same morning.

I am not making allegations here, simply stating facts about this landmark legal case. And asking whether this type of circumstance is relevant to archival or historical record considerations.

Yours faithfully,


Sion Buckler
