Those location and map identifiers should be used as the primary means of identifying the site. The following farm, estate and locality wordings are supporting search terms drawn from different records and periods. They are not all interchangeable names, and their appearance in a source does not by itself establish that they refer to the same building, parcel, estate boundary or title.

Farm and farmhouse nomenclature

Please consider these forms when searching: “Great House Farm”; “Great House Farm, Llandough”; “Great House Farm (GGAT02038s)”; “Great House”; “Great house”; “Greathouse”; “Tŷ Mawr”; “Ty Mawr”; “Ty-mawr”; “Ty Mawr Farm”; “Ty Mawr (Great House)”; “Ty Mawr, Penarth Road, Llandough”; “Cydfin Farm”; “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)”; “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm”; “Cedfin”; “Cydwin”; “Cedwyn”; “Cedfyn”; “Cefin”; “Llandough Home Farm”; “Land Doc Home Farm”; “R-W-S-S Farm / Reese’s Farm”; “Llandough Church Farm”; “Church Farm”; “former Great House Farm”; “a 3.4 acre site located adjacent St. Dochdwy Parish Church”; and “LLANDOUGH FARM (Ideal Homes Wales)”. The map form “Ty-mawr” is also recorded on an Ordnance Survey 25-inch map, although the edition and its precise relationship to the site have not been confirmed.

The source status matters. The National Library of Wales Bute catalogue lists “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)” within a 1552 - 1824 catalogue range; that range is not a specific date for the Ty Mawr wording. The 1824 Stewart survey, plan 19, records “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm” (Glamorgan Archives DB/E/1). Historical directories use “Great house” and “Greathouse”. The checked 1881 census image reads “Llandough Home Farm”; “Land Doc Home Farm” is a reported competing transcription, and “R-W-S-S Farm / Reese’s Farm” does not appear on that checked page. “Cydwin” and “Cedwyn” are reported research leads whose underlying 16th-century documents have not been verified; “Cedfyn” and “Cefin” are retained as search forms without a confirmed primary occurrence. “Llandough Church Farm” and “Church Farm” are source-specific terms whose connection to this site is not established by the cited records. The 1994 Ideal Homes Wales plan uses “LLANDOUGH FARM” for this development site, while other records use Llandough Farm for a separate farmstead; please distinguish records by their mapped location rather than by that wording alone.

Other indexed phrases, including “an 800-year-old house”, “the ten-roomed house”, “ancient Welsh house” and “the farm / the whole of the farm”, are descriptions rather than established property names.

Estate and associated-place nomenclature

For relevant records concerning the wider surrounding area, please also consider “Cedwyn Estate”, “Cydwin”, “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)”, “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm” and “Cwm Cydfin Wood”. “Cedwyn Estate” is the family’s working name for the wider historic estate, not a period name established by the records cited here. “Cydwin” is reported from a 1547 family research note whose underlying document is not held; the Bute catalogue wording and the 1824 survey wording have the source limits described above. “Cwm Cydfin Wood” appears in a 1902 Bute sale catalogue. These names are search aids for relevant site and surrounding-area records, not a claim that each denotes the same holding or proves the estate’s extent or title.

Town and locality nomenclature

Please also consider “Llandough-juxta-Penarth”; “Llandough near Penarth”; “Llandough near Cardiff”; “Llandough, Penarth”; “Llandough, Cardiff”; “Llandough”; “Landoche-juxta-Cardif”; “Llandough / Leckwith”; “Penarth Road, Llandough”; “Urban District of Penarth”; “Llandochau”; and “CF64” / “Church View Close, [postcode redacted]”. “Landoche-juxta-Cardif” is a historical locator for the manor, not proof that every holding in a record bearing that locator is this farm. “Llandough / Leckwith” is neighbouring parish and manorial context, not another name for the farm. “Urban District of Penarth” is a historical local-government description. Advice and Assistance

Under the duty to provide information and assistance in accordance with section 16 of the FOIA, we can provide the following information which may assist you in this matter:

Your clarification response confirmed to us that the map we sent you with our email of 26 October 2026, is the correct region for the property, which is the subject of your request. We have found some archive maps. They show the area from: 1947 (published in 1950); 1965; 1971; 1977 and 1989.

This mapping shows we had the property named as Ty-Mawr in 1947 and it changed to Ty Mawr Farm between 1965 and 1971. It was still called that in 1989. There is no mention of any other name at the location. The name would only have been changed if there was clear evidence either from a sign on the property or confirmation from the owner / occupier.

Unfortunately, the relevant Object Name Book for the area, which would have shown any name changes and who authorised them is not held by OS and The National Archive record states ‘Missing at Transfer’  ST1673. Missing at transfer | The National Archives<https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C11414033>

We do not hold any archive records detailing the reasons that prompted any changes to our mapping.  OS maintain the National Map, which is over 600 million features of Britain’s landscape.  Once a change is made, the map itself serves as the record of any change.
