Sir/Madam,

I write to formally place before the Crown Prosecution Service evidence
concerning suspected historical fraud and related criminal conduct arising
from the dispossession and subsequent treatment of the former Great House
Farm, Llandough, Vale of Glamorgan.

I am currently investigating the matter and preparing proceedings
concerning the underlying proprietary dispute. I am also considering the
institution of a *private prosecution* in respect of any criminal offences
disclosed by the evidence.

I specifically invite the CPS to consider, at this stage, whether this is a
case in which the Director of Public Prosecutions should exercise the power
under *section 6(2) of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 to take over
the conduct of a private prosecution*, should proceedings be instituted.

The principal concern is not simply the disputed ownership of land. The
historical evidence appears to disclose a possible pattern in which the
family's established occupation and proprietary position were progressively
characterised as merely permissive.

Of particular significance is the apparent treatment of the occupation in
the *1955 proceedings*, followed by the more explicit attempt in *1974* to
establish a rent-free licence arrangement concerning the property. I am
investigating whether these were independent legal positions or whether
they formed part of a longer and coordinated course of conduct intended to
create or manufacture a documentary/legal basis for treating an established
proprietary occupation as permissive, thereby facilitating the acquisition
or retention of the land.

The subsequent involvement of *BP Properties Ltd and BP Pension Trust*, the
registration of title WA231076 in 1982, and the litigation culminating in
the 1987 Court of Appeal proceedings are material to that investigation.

I do not ask the CPS to determine the civil title dispute. The question I
ask is whether the documentary sequence discloses evidence capable of
supporting criminal offences involving fraud, conspiracy, false or
misleading representations, concealment of material information, or other
offences arising from the alleged acquisition or preservation of property
rights.

I am particularly concerned that conduct occurring at different stages may
only become intelligible when examined as a single historical sequence
rather than as isolated civil proceedings. The apparent attempt to
establish permissive occupation in 1955, the subsequent licence proposal in
1974, the later registration of title, and the representations made in the
litigation may therefore warrant consideration together.

I recognise that the CPS will need sufficient evidence to apply the Full
Code Test and that the existence of a civil dispute does not itself
establish criminal conduct. I am not presenting the allegation as proved. I
am presenting the documentary evidence and asking the prosecuting authority
to determine whether it warrants criminal investigation and/or prosecution.

The CPS's published guidance confirms that a private prosecutor may ask the
CPS to take over a private prosecution and that, when specifically
requested to intervene, the CPS must make a decision whether or not to take
it over. The guidance further recognises that the CPS may take over and
continue a private prosecution where the Full Code Test is satisfied and
there is a particular need for the CPS to conduct the prosecution,
including in serious or complex cases or cases involving substantial
disclosure issues.

I therefore expressly request that the CPS:

   1.

   *Consider whether it would be prepared to take over a private
   prosecution under section 6(2) of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 if
   proceedings are instituted;*
   2.

   *Confirm whether the matter should instead be referred to an
   investigative authority for investigation before the CPS considers taking
   over proceedings;*
   3.

   *Identify the appropriate CPS division or office to which the evidence
   should be submitted; and*
   4.

   *If the CPS is not prepared to consider taking over such proceedings,
   expressly confirm that position and the reason for it.*

I am able to provide an indexed evidential bundle containing the relevant
1955 and 1974 material, historical conveyances and plans, Land Registry
documentation concerning WA231076, relevant court records and a chronology
identifying the specific documentary inconsistencies and matters which I
consider require investigation.

I would prefer the CPS to have the opportunity to consider the matter at
this stage rather than for a private prosecution to be commenced without
first giving the public prosecuting authority an opportunity to determine
whether it considers the case sufficiently serious, complex or otherwise
appropriate for public prosecution.

Please therefore treat this correspondence as a *formal request for
consideration of CPS involvement and potential exercise of the DPP's
section 6(2) power*, and confirm the appropriate procedure for submitting
the evidence for that purpose.
