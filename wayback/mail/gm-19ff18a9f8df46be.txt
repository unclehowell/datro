Dear Ms James,

Thank you for your considered response.

I understand and accept that the Committee does not have a remit to
adjudicate upon an individual dispute, determine whether a historical
decision was lawful, or direct Cadw to reopen a particular case. My
submission was deliberately framed so as not to ask the Committee to do any
of those things.

I would, however, respectfully clarify one point, because I am concerned
that the nature of the request may have been characterised more narrowly
than intended.

The attached report expressly states that it is *not a legal challenge, a
forensic proof of wrongdoing, a complaint seeking adjudication, or a demand
for reversal of the 1988 decision*. Its stated purpose is historical record
correction, archival explanation and administrative acknowledgement. In
particular, it asks for an explanation of why almost none of the records
concerning the 1988 assessment survive, whether that reflects the
applicable record-retention arrangements, and whether the surviving
independent evidence should be properly reflected in the permanent public
record.

The central issue therefore extends beyond the merits of the individual
1988 listing assessment.

Cadw's 2026 FOI response confirms that an assessment took place, but that
only two exterior photographs now survive; the assessment report, reasons
for refusal, site notes, internal recommendations and consultation
correspondence are no longer available. The report does not assert that
this proves wrongdoing. Rather, it identifies a straightforward
public-administration question: *how can the adequacy and evidential basis
of an irreversible public heritage decision be understood or scrutinised
when the underlying administrative record no longer survives?*

That seems potentially relevant to the Committee's stated function of
scrutinising the administration and accountability of Welsh Government and
associated public bodies, even if the Committee cannot itself determine
whether the 1988 decision was correct.

I am therefore not asking the Committee to investigate the Buckler family's
historical dispute with BP, adjudicate upon the 1988 decision, or direct
Cadw to reverse anything. The specific administrative questions raised in
the report are instead:

   1. What record-retention arrangements applied to the 1988 assessment?
   2. Why do the assessment report, reasons, site notes and consultation
   records no longer survive?
   3. Were the records destroyed under an applicable retention policy or
   other authority, and were they considered for transfer to the appropriate
   archival institution?
   4. Is the present position consistent with Welsh Government/Cadw
   expectations for the preservation of records concerning significant
   heritage decisions?
   5. Are current arrangements sufficient to ensure that comparable
   heritage assessments, particularly where a building is under imminent
   threat of demolition, are properly documented and retained?

These are not questions that require the Committee to determine the rights
of either party to the original dispute. They concern *administrative
practice, institutional record-keeping and the ability of the Senedd and
the public to scrutinise decisions taken by public bodies*. The report
specifically asks Cadw to explain the archival position and review its
current record-keeping practices for this reason.

I would therefore be grateful if the Committee could reconsider whether the
broader administrative and accountability issue falls within its scrutiny
function, even if the Committee cannot examine the underlying individual
dispute.

If the Committee considers that it nevertheless cannot engage with the
matter, I would appreciate clarification as to *which Senedd or Welsh
Government scrutiny, oversight, archival or complaints mechanism is
considered appropriate for raising a documented concern about the absence
of the administrative record of a historical Cadw decision and the
implications this has for public accountability*.

For completeness, I should also emphasise that the report itself
deliberately recognises the limits of the surviving evidence. It does not
claim that Cadw acted unlawfully or in bad faith, and it expressly
acknowledges that the absence of the 1988 working papers means that the
assessment cannot now be reconstructed definitively.

My objective is therefore quite limited: *to ensure that the surviving
evidence is preserved, that the archival gap is properly explained, and
that the public record accurately distinguishes what is established from
what can no longer be verified.*

I hope that clarification assists, and I would be grateful if you could
confirm whether the Committee is able to consider the matter from that
wider public-administration and accountability perspective.

Kind regards,

Sion Buckler (Ret’d Sergeant)
