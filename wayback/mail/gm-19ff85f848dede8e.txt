Ms Davidson,

Thank you for your response.

I accept the distinction you draw between the Council's Corporate Concerns
and Complaints Policy and the separate statutory framework governing the
conduct of elected members. I am not seeking to use the corporate
complaints procedure as an appeal against historic planning, enforcement,
land or judicial decisions.

However, I do wish to clarify one important point concerning your decision
in your capacity as Monitoring Officer.

My complaint about Councillor George Carroll was not simply that the
Council should revisit historic decisions concerning Great House Farm. The
specific concern was his repeated failure to provide any substantive
response to correspondence sent to him in his capacity as the elected
councillor for Llandough, despite repeated approaches over a period of
approximately five months and despite the correspondence setting out
specific matters concerning his ward.

The chronology supplied with my complaint records correspondence on:

   - 16 February 2026;
   - 4 May 2026;
   - 7 June 2026;
   - 25 June 2026; and
   - 13 July 2026.

My complaint therefore concerned the conduct and handling of that
correspondence by an elected member, rather than asking the Monitoring
Officer to determine the underlying historical ownership or planning
dispute.

You state:

“Having reviewed the information you have provided, I have not identified
evidence which would indicate a breach of the Members Code of Conduct.”

I would therefore be grateful for clarification of the basis upon which
that conclusion was reached.

In particular, please confirm:

   1.

   *Which specific provisions of the Members' Code of Conduct were
   considered* in assessing my complaint.
   2.

   *Whether the complete correspondence chronology and the underlying
   emails and attachments were considered*, rather than merely my summary
   of them.
   3.

   *Whether Councillor Carroll was invited to provide an explanation or
   response* concerning the repeated absence of any substantive response to
   the correspondence.
   4.

   If so, whether his response was taken into account in reaching your
   conclusion.
   5.

   If not, please explain how the Monitoring Officer was able to determine
   that there was no evidence indicating a possible breach without obtaining
   the member's account of the conduct complained of.
   6.

   Please explain, in sufficient detail to enable me to understand the
   decision, *why the documented pattern of conduct was considered
   incapable of engaging any provision of the Members' Code of Conduct*.

I appreciate that failure to answer correspondence does not, by itself,
necessarily constitute a breach of the Code. I am not asserting that
proposition. My concern is that the Monitoring Officer appears to have
reached a definitive conclusion without identifying the relevant Code
provisions, the evidence considered, or the reasoning by which that
evidence was found insufficient.

I would also ask you to clarify whether you consider that the matter should
instead be referred to the *Public Services Ombudsman for Wales* for
consideration under its jurisdiction concerning complaints about the
conduct of elected members.

I note that your response states that no further action will be taken by
you as Monitoring Officer. I will therefore retain your letter, together
with my original complaint and the complete correspondence record, for
consideration of whether a complaint should be submitted to the Public
Services Ombudsman for Wales.

For the avoidance of doubt, I am not asking you to determine the historical
merits of the Great House Farm dispute through the corporate complaints
procedure. I am asking for the reasoning and evidential basis for the
separate determination you have made concerning the conduct of an elected
member.

I would be grateful if you could therefore provide the above clarification
and confirm the relevant Code provisions considered.

Regards,

Sion Hywel Buckler
