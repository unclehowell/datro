Internal Review
We have accepted your request for an internal review of FOI 2026-024.

The purpose of an internal review is to consider whether Amgueddfa Cymru handled the original request appropriately, including whether:

  *   the request was interpreted correctly;
  *   reasonable and proportionate searches were undertaken;
  *   relevant information held by the Museum was identified and considered; and
  *   the response complied with the requirements of the Freedom of Information Act 2000.

The internal review process is not ordinarily intended to redefine or broaden the scope of the original request, nor to conduct new searches solely on the basis of additional search terms or lines of enquiry proposed after the original response has been issued.
Nevertheless, the reviewer will consider the points raised in your correspondence when assessing whether the original request was interpreted appropriately and whether the searches undertaken were reasonable in the circumstances.

We aim to provide the outcome of the internal review within 20 working days of receipt of your request. If, exceptionally, we are unable to conclude the review within that period, we will write to you again explaining the position.

Under section 10 of the Freedom of Information Act 2000, we will respond to that request within 20 working days of receipt.   Based on the date your correspondence was received, we aim to respond to the new request by 23 September 2026.

The internal review and the new request will be considered separately and may result in separate responses.

Thank you for your response.

I am requesting an internal review of the handling of my request. I do not consider the response sufficient because it appears that the searches were conducted too narrowly around the later description of a "medieval visor, lance, helmet or other item of armour", rather than around the underlying historical discovery at Great House Farm itself.

The issue I am investigating is a reported nineteenth-century discovery at Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough. My understanding from contemporary or near-contemporary newspaper and historical accounts is that the discovery was described as involving the remains of a Roman soldier, with associated remains and objects. The dating and description of the individual objects as "medieval" may therefore itself be inaccurate, or may represent a later conflation or retelling of the original discovery.

Your response is particularly significant because it states:

<"Our searches identified records relating to ... other archaeological discoveries recorded at Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough. These records do not relate to, nor are they otherwise associated with, the discovery described in your request. We have therefore not considered them to fall within the scope of this request.">

I respectfully challenge that approach.

The central question is precisely whether the discovery described in later historical accounts can be identified, corroborated, corrected or explained through the Museum's records concerning archaeological discoveries at Great House Farm. If the Museum has identified records relating to other discoveries at the same historically significant site, it is not sufficient simply to conclude internally that they are unrelated without disclosing what those discoveries are, when they were recorded, and the basis upon which that conclusion was reached.

I therefore request that the internal review reconsider the scope of my request and undertake searches using, at minimum, the following terms and concepts:

- Llandough, Cardiff

- Roman grave
- Roman remains
- human remains
- skeleton

- cavalry burial

- 1870 and adjacent years

Please also search for relevant records under alternative historical place-name indexing, donor names, findspot descriptions, parish names and catalogue terminology where the precise modern name "Great House Farm" may not have been used.

Most importantly, I ask that you disclose sufficient information about the "other archaeological discoveries" which your searches identified at Great House Farm to enable me to understand and assess the Museum's conclusion that they are unrelated. This should include, so far as held:

1. A description or title of each relevant discovery or record.
2. The approximate date of the discovery or record.
3. The nature of the material or remains involved.
4. Any accession number, catalogue number or archival reference.
5. The recorded provenance or findspot.
6. Any dating attributed to the discovery.
7. The basis upon which Amgueddfa Cymru concluded that each record was unrelated to the nineteenth-century discovery described in my request.

I am not asking the Museum to create new archaeological opinions or undertake speculative research. I am asking for recorded information which has already been identified during the searches and which directly concerns archaeological discoveries at the specific site named in my request.

If the Museum maintains that any of those records fall outside the original scope, then please treat this email additionally as a refined request for the recorded information identified above.

I would also ask the reviewer to consider whether the original response gave insufficient weight to the possibility that the historical account may have inaccurately dated or described the find. A search which excludes Roman, nineteenth-century, burial and equine terminology risks missing the very records capable of establishing what was actually discovered beneath Great House Farm.

Given that your searches have already identified records concerning archaeological discoveries at Great House Farm itself, I would be grateful for a full reconsideration and disclosure of the recorded information requested above.
