This week, many residents have also reported disruption to postal services within Llandough.

Thank you to everyone who's shared their experiences.

I've raised these concerns with company bosses, asking them to investigate and provide details of action being taken to address existing problems.

You can read the full letter below.
FAO: Royal Mail

14th October 2026


Dear Michael

ROYAL MAIL DELIVERIES IN LLANDOUGH

I am writing to you regarding the above.

In recent weeks, several Llandough residents have reported delays and reliability issues with postal services.

In one incident, a resident waited several weeks to receive a blood test form that she had ordered. The paperwork in question only arrived two weeks after the medical appointment for which it was required.

Another resident missed a pre-surgery appointment because the relevant notification arrived a week late, while a third person has reported a medical subscription arrived several days after its scheduled delivery date.

Furthermore, another resident has not received any post since 7th September. Many others have reported that they have not received anything for several weeks, only for a large amount of mail to arrive all at once.

Royal Mail’s website advises customers that 2nd Class and other non-1st Class letters will be delivered every other weekday. Customers are told to expect 2nd Class mail to arrive within three weekdays. Reports from residents indicate these timescales are not being adhered to.

I would therefore be grateful if you would investigate current provision in the Llandough, Penarth and Eastern Vale of Glamorgan areas, and take action to address existing issues regarding service reliability.

Thank you for your assistance with this, and I look forward to hearing from you.

Kind regards,

Cllr George Carroll
Elected Member

Llandough ward
Vale of Glamorgan Council

Plans to build 250 houses on land just down from the Merrie Harrier

If you submitted representations during the consultation on the Vale of Glamorgan Replacement Local Development Plan, you will recently have received an invitation to register to speak at the public examination.

The deadline to do so is 30th October.

The RDLP includes a key site (below) to build 250 houses on land just down from the Merrie Harrier.

This will exacerbate existing congestion and safety issues at the Merrie Harrier junction.

Furthermore, it will eat into farmland that creates a natural boundary between Llandough and Dinas Powys, threatening both villages' distinct, separate identities.

I have registered to speak at the hearing, at which I will be speaking against the plans.

I would urge as many residents as possible to do so too.

The hearings are likely to take place in Spring or Summer 2027.

We must work together to protect our green spaces and prevent inappropriate development.

It's a key part of Our Plan for Llandough.

If you have further questions, please let me know.

Our community newsletter

Our community newsletter ensures everyone in our village is kept up to date with what’s happening in Llandough.

It’s been going for several years, with a team of us working together to make it happen.

It wouldn’t be possible without the hard work of our volunteers.

Maintaining good communication with residents is central to Our Plan for Llandough.

We do many things to help achieve this. Social media is an important way to share updates.

As is this weekly e-newsletter, that lands in subscribers’ inboxes every Sunday evening.

We also go door to door, asking residents if there’s anything they’d like to raise.

You may be surprised how many people we speak to who aren’t online.

It’s vital they’re kept in the loop.

We achieve this through a paper newsletter, that lands on every doormat in Llandough.

Our aim is to build a delivery network that covers our whole village, while I fill in the gaps.

Rail replacement bus service:
25th October to 1st November

The Vale of Glamorgan Line between Cardiff, Barry and Bridgend will be CLOSED from 10.00pm on Sunday 25th October, reopening on Monday 2nd November.

Buses will replace trains serving Cogan station during this time.

Network Rail have advised the closure is to allow for works across the line, including renewing switches and crossings in Cogan.

Passengers are advised to check before they travel and allow extra time.

Further information can be found on Transport for Wales' website.

Blocked drains

Blocked drains pose a serious safety risk to our village, with the potential to cause floods and ice hazards.

When I raised the leaks on Leckwith Road recently, the Council confirmed to me only has two gully tanker teams to maintain around 25,000 drains on its highway network.

The Council must invest in more capacity to ensure drain repairs can be carried out in a more timely manner.
