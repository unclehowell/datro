Dear Requester

Thank you for your request for an internal review of Amgueddfa Cymru's response to your Freedom of Information request concerning a reported archaeological discovery at Great House Farm, Llandough.

I have now completed my review of the handling of your request, including the searches undertaken and the concerns raised in your request for review.

Having reviewed the original request, the searches conducted and the response provided, I am satisfied that the request was handled appropriately and that reasonable and proportionate searches were undertaken in those locations where information falling within the scope of the request would reasonably be expected to be held.

In your request for review, you suggest that additional searches should have been undertaken using a wider range of terms and concepts, including references to Roman burials, military burials, cavalry burials, human remains, horse remains and other archaeological terminology. I have carefully considered this point. However, when assessing whether information is held for the purposes of the Freedom of Information Act 2000, the relevant issue is not whether every conceivable search term has been used, but whether the authority has undertaken searches that are reasonable and proportionate in the circumstances and reasonably likely to identify relevant information if held.

Your request concerned a specific reported discovery at a named location. Accordingly, the location identified in the request was used as the primary search criterion. Searches were undertaken across Amgueddfa Cymru's information holdings where records relating to acquisitions, accessions, archaeological finds, provenance information and associated correspondence would reasonably be expected to be held. This included collections management systems, accession records, archived documentation, shared electronic records and other organisational record repositories.

Records identified through those searches were reviewed by collections staff and the FOI team to determine whether they related to the reported discovery described in your request. As the search methodology was location-based rather than focused on a particular object type, archaeological period or interpretation, it was reasonably likely of identifying relevant records regardless of whether associated material had been recorded as Roman, medieval or otherwise.  I have therefore considered your suggestion that later historical accounts may have inaccurately described the nature, date or attribution of the reported discovery. I am satisfied that this possibility would not have affected the outcome of the searches undertaken.

I have also considered whether broader keyword searches would have been appropriate. Given the scale and diversity of Amgueddfa Cymru's collections and records, generic terms such as "horse", "burial" or "skeleton" would retrieve large volumes of records relating to many different sites and periods and would not provide a reliable or proportionate means of locating records relating to a specific reported discovery at a named location. I am satisfied that the location-based search strategy adopted was the most appropriate and proportionate approach.

The searches undertaken did not identify any records relating to:
* the acquisition, accession, donation, transfer or deposit of a visor, lance, helmet, armour or related objects associated with the discovery described in your request;
* associated human or animal remains connected with that discovery;
* reburial records relating to that discovery; or
* excavation reports, discovery reports or watching-brief records relating to the discovery described in your request.

Having reviewed both the scope of the original request and the searches undertaken, I am satisfied on the balance of probabilities that, if information relating to the reported discovery were held by Amgueddfa Cymru, those searches would have been likely to identify it.

You also raised concerns regarding the reference in the original response to other archaeological records identified during the course of the searches. Those records were reviewed and were not considered to relate to, or otherwise be associated with, the reported discovery described in your request. However, I can confirm that this aspect has been subject to further consideration and a separate response will be issued in relation to those records.

I therefore uphold the original conclusion that Amgueddfa Cymru does not hold recorded information falling within the scope of your request.

This concludes Amgueddfa Cymru's internal review process. If you remain dissatisfied, you may complain to the Information Commissioner's Office under section 50 of the Freedom of Information Act 2000.
