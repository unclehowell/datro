Dear Heneb: The Trust for Welsh Archaeology,

I am writing to request access to recorded information concerning the March
1983 fire at the former Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust (GGAT)
warehouse in Swansea, particularly the reported destruction of
archaeological finds, excavation records and photographic material from
Llandough, together with records concerning the subsequent custody,
preservation, transfer and integrity of archaeological records relating to
Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr), Llandough.

I recognise that Heneb is an independent private company limited by
guarantee and registered charity rather than a body ordinarily subject to
the Freedom of Information Act 2000.

However, Heneb now maintains the statutory Historic Environment Records for
Wales on behalf of the Welsh Ministers. Sections 194 and 195 of the
Historic Environment (Wales) Act 2023 require the Welsh Ministers to
maintain publicly accessible Historic Environment Records, including
information concerning archaeological investigations and their findings.

I therefore ask that this request be considered under any applicable
statutory information-access regime, including the Environmental
Information Regulations 2004 insofar as Heneb considers that it falls
within their scope.

If Heneb considers that the EIR do not apply to any or all of the
information requested, please state that expressly and identify the legal
basis for that position. I would nevertheless be grateful if Heneb would
consider the request voluntarily as an information-access request,
particularly where the information forms part of, or supports, the
statutory Historic Environment Record or associated archaeological archive
maintained on behalf of the Welsh Ministers.

I have copied Cadw and the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical
Monuments of Wales because the request concerns the statutory HER system
and records said to have been transferred or accessioned into the National
Monuments Record of Wales.

The request is for existing recorded information only. I am not asking
Heneb to create a new report or undertake fresh archaeological
interpretation.
Purpose and evidential context

This request arises from a wider forensic examination of the surviving
documentary record concerning Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr), Llandough.

Two companion forensic critiques, dated 21 September 2026, have been
prepared concerning respectively Cadw and GGAT/Heneb. They are not
presented as proof of dishonesty, bad faith, corruption or deliberate
destruction. Rather, they identify a series of documentary gaps,
inconsistent records and questions which cannot properly be resolved
without access to the underlying primary records.

In particular, the GGAT critique identifies questions concerning the
Trust's historical roles as archaeological adviser, contractor,
investigator, curator and record-holder, and concerning the provenance and
subsequent custody of the 1990 Great House Farm assessment and associated
material.

The purpose of this request is therefore to establish, from the records
themselves, what material existed, what was lost, what survived, where it
was held, when it was transferred, and whether the present HER and archive
accurately represent the historical record.
The two distinct archaeological areas at Llandough

For the avoidance of doubt, I ask that the following be treated as separate
investigations.

*1. Llandough Farm / Roman villa area south of St Dochdwy's church*

GGAT undertook excavation in this area in 1978–79, with the HER identifying
event GGATE001109 as "Llandough Farm". The associated site record concerns
an Iron Age/Roman villa complex with later early-medieval burial activity.

*2. Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr) area north of St Dochdwy's church*

GGAT undertook an archaeological assessment/evaluation of Great House Farm
in 1990, followed by the substantially larger Cotswold Archaeological Trust
excavation in 1994.

These should not be conflated in the records or in any response to this
request.
