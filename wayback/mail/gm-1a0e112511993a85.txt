I am trying to reconstruct what happened historically which led my late
grandmother and father, over many decades, to continue asserting that our
family owned the land at Great House Farm, while that position was
repeatedly contested and ultimately possession was awarded to BP in *BP
Properties Ltd v Buckler*.

What has caused me to revisit the documentary history is that the later
position is not as straightforward as I had previously assumed. Land
Registry has now told me that its position is not that it determined that
my family did not own the land; but it insists that no registration mistake
has been identified, which is what the MP forwarded me (they're fobbing him
off too).

Land registry will wake up to this soon I'm sure. Or I'm wrong and I need
to wake up. But I am a military warfare professional, and while I make
mistakes, or AI makes mistakes on my behalf, I have no issue holding my
hands up. I'm just trying to understand what happened to us and our home
and our land as children in the 80's, and why it all happened.

At the same time, I have so far found no convincing documentary evidence of
my family paying rent for the particular parcel which we occupied and
claimed as our own after the quarrying arrangement appears to have come to
an end.

I have therefore had one of my AI agents published my current
reconstruction, including the documentary sources behind it, here:

Great House Farm research <https://reference-url-citation.invalid/3>

I am not asking you to accept that reconstruction. It's clearly hit and
miss as an early technology. I am asking for your help in testing it
against the records held by Glamorgan Archives.
The central question

My present understanding is that what had originally been treated as one
agricultural holding can, when the surviving documents are examined
chronologically, be distinguished into two areas.

For simplicity, I have called them *Parcel A* and *Parcel B*:

   - *Parcel A* — the farmhouse, house plot, garden and associated land
   north/west of St Dochdwy's Church, occupied by my family; and
   - *Parcel B* — the agricultural land to the east which was subject to
   the agricultural tenancy and subsequently associated with Western Ground
   Rents.

I appreciate that “Parcel A” and “Parcel B” are my labels, not historical
descriptions appearing in the documents. But they aren't entirely just my
labels, parcel B is clearly what is in the 1939 map, which you hold, which
the council clerk for Llandough directed me to earlier this year, on his
day of retirement.

Also in 1983 two parcels were merged at the land registry, by BP. Which
confirms two parcels. I have purchased all those records avaliable and the
maps do confirm what I suspect.

The important question is whether the *underlying documents themselves
support the physical distinction*.

My working reconstruction is that the 1877 Daniel Thomas/Williams
arrangements ultimately resulted in my family acquiring the freehold
interest in the land around the farmhouse when the quarrying arrangement
came to an end, with the last machinery leaving in 1928.

If that is correct, it would explain something which otherwise appears very
difficult to reconcile.

The 1916 agricultural tenancy may have covered both parcels of the farm as
a whole at that time. But if part of that holding subsequently ceased to be
part of the agricultural tenancy because of my family's interest in the
parcel A crystallising into our freehold of parcel A, then the 1938 Western
Ground Rents acquisition and the *1939 revised agricultural tenancy* would
not necessarily concern our farmhouse land. Despite decades of it being
treated as though it did and our occupancy being allegedly permissive,
which is a narrative forced upon us to believe, when it most likely wasn't
the case. And why my nan rejected the premis and my dad too. Until their
dying breaths. I inherit this interest in this land parcel A as heir and
successor.

That is why the 1939 plan was so important and helpful.
The point I would particularly like you to confirm

*When you look at the actual 1939 Western Ground Rents revised tenancy and
its plan, I presume you see the same thing I see?*

My reading is that the plan depicts *Parcel B, the eastern agricultural
land, but does not depict or include Parcel A containing the farmhouse and
the land immediately associated with it.*

If that is what the plan shows, I would be very grateful if you could
simply confirm that.

If I am wrong, I would equally value you telling me that I am wrong and,
ideally, pointing me to the feature of the plan or accompanying document
which demonstrates that.

I am not asking you to determine what that means in law. Only whether *we
are looking at the same physical information in the same historical
document*.

That distinction is crucial because I am increasingly concerned that the
later history may have proceeded on the assumption that there was only one
undivided parcel, when the contemporary records may actually distinguish
two different interests.
I would like to do the same exercise with the other records

Do the surviving records contain any express documentary act which actually

events. I am asking whether such an express documentary act appears in the

*6. Later title history*

Do the surviving Glamorgan Archives records contain documentary evidence
showing how the farmhouse parcel itself passed from the earlier family

*I want to know whether the historical documentary evidence held by the
