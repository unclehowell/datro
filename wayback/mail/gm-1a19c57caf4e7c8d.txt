Related descriptions include “an 800-year-old house”, “the ten-roomed house”, “ancient Welsh house” and “the farm / the whole of the farm”.

The checked 1881 census page reads “Llandough Home Farm”; “Land Doc Home Farm” is a competing reported transcription and “R-W-S-S Farm / Reese’s Farm” is a reported lead not appearing on that page. “Cydwin” and “Cedwyn” are unverified research leads; “Cedfyn” and “Cefin” are search forms without a confirmed primary occurrence. “Church Farm” and “Llandough Church Farm” are source-specific terms whose identity is unresolved. The 1994 Ideal Homes Wales plan uses “LLANDOUGH FARM” for the development site, while the Rees family Llandough Farm is a separate farmstead. A shared wording alone does not establish that records refer to the same place.

Estate and associated-place terms: “Cedwyn Estate”, “Cydwin”, “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)”, “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm” and “Cwm Cydfin Wood”. Cedwyn Estate is the family's working name, not an established period name. The Bute catalogue's 1552 - 1824 range is not a specific date for the Ty Mawr wording; the 1824 Stewart survey records “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm”, and a 1902 sale catalogue names Cwm Cydfin Wood. These terms do not establish common boundaries or title.
