Dr Wigley,

This is a supplement to the complaint re-served on 20 September, not a
chaser. I do not expect a reply before the stage-one response. It adds
seven matters under the existing headings at section 7, and one addition to
the outcomes at section 9, set out at the end.

The public interest is engaged alongside my family's. Great House Farm
stood beside the Grade I church at Llandough, was recorded by the Royal
Commission in 1974, sat within a known archaeological site, and was
described in the post-demolition planning record as a "county treasure". I
do not ask Cadw to treat the 1994 discoveries as knowledge it had in 1988.
They show what was lost; the pre-1988 record is the measure of Cadw's
process.

On my family's account and the contemporary press report, protection was
being sought in the first days of December 1988 and the house was
demolished almost immediately afterwards. I do not ask Cadw to determine
responsibility for the demolition. The grievance is narrower: protection
was sought at the critical moment, and Cadw cannot now show what it
considered.

1. The adopted development plan (matter A)

The planning officer's report on application 89/01396/OUT records that the
supporting information to Policy H.2 of the East Vale Local Plan "states
that buildings on the site should be retained, however were unfortunately
demolished a little while ago." I do not say this bound Cadw or required
listing. I ask:
(a) was Cadw aware of it in December 1988;
(b) did the planning authority draw it to Cadw's attention; and
(c) if neither can be established, will Cadw say so expressly.

2. The search undertaken on 21 May (matters D and F)

Mr Weatherhead wrote that he had asked a colleague to search other paper
files, "including the Welsh Office files", and would write again with the
result, while fairly warning that some files had quite likely been
destroyed. I have not received that letter. Please confirm whether the
search was carried out, which series were searched, what was found, and
whether Cadw regards the ATISN 27021 response of 11 June as its report of
that search.

3. The meaning of "YYY" (matter J)

Cadw has given two accounts of the marking — that the buildings were
"marginally below the bar for listing" (21 May) and that the building did
not meet the criteria (10 July) — and one inference, that it did not meet
the criteria "as it was not included on the list" (29 July). I ask for one
settled meaning, the documentary source for it, and, if there is none, a
statement to that effect.

4. The retention period (matter J)

Your email of 27 August said that no records of the historic retention
arrangements are available, and that the three-year period is what current
colleagues advised from their knowledge of the systems then in place. I ask
only that the complaint response confirm expressly that this is an account
of general practice, not evidence that the Great House Farm file was in
fact destroyed after three years.

5. Professional archaeological knowledge of this site before December 1988
(matter C)

On 22 September 2026 Amgueddfa Cymru disclosed to me, under FOI 2026-032,
its collections records for two accessions from this site. The first,
82.44H, is the material from the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust's
excavation of 1979 on land formerly part of Great House Farm, carried out
in advance of housing development. The museum's records give the findspot
as ST 168 733, the donor as Wales & West Housing Association Ltd, and the
published report as Owen-John 1988. The accession runs to several hundred
entries and includes Roman coins, samian, glass, a bone scabbard slide and
querns, together with medieval pottery and ironwork.

This is not archaeology discovered after the event. By the time Cadw
considered this building, the site had been professionally excavated, the
finds had been accessioned by the national museum under a 1982 accession
number, and the excavation report had been published. I therefore ask, as
an addition to matter C:
(a) whether Cadw held, or had access to, the 1979 excavation record or its
published report when it considered the buildings in 1988;
(b) whether that material was drawn to its attention by GGAT, the planning
authority or the Royal Commission; and
(c) if neither can now be established, that Cadw say so in terms.

I do not say this material required the building to be listed. I say that
the conclusion recorded in December 1988, that the site was not of
sufficient archaeological interest, was reached against a professional
record that already existed and is still held by the national museum today.

6. The National Museum attribution (matter H)

The GGAT assessment of August 1990, which Cadw supplied to me on 21 May,
states that "it is likely that some medieval ironwork which is held by the
National Museum of Wales was discovered under the rear wing c 1880 (RCAHMW
1988)."

The museum's disclosure allows that sentence to be tested, and the position
is more precise than either of us has previously put it. The museum does
hold medieval ironwork from this site — knife blades, arrowheads, a buckle
and a pair of compasses within accession 82.44H — but as excavated material
from 1979, not as an 1880 discovery beneath the rear wing. Its searches
found no accession, donation or deposit answering the c.1880 provenance at
all. The museum's own explanation, given in its disclosure, is that objects
recorded as found at Church Farm, Llandough in 1858 and donated in 1963
under accession 63.24 "could well be the basis for such a local
story/tradition".

I therefore no longer ask Cadw to accept that the attribution is
uncorroborated in the round. I ask, more narrowly, whether Cadw holds
anything supporting the c.1880 rear-wing provenance beyond the GGAT
sentence it forwarded, and whether it will ask that the derived record note
the museum's findings rather than continue to carry the 1880 attribution
unqualified.

7. Correcting the record (matter H)

You directed me to Heneb as manager of the HER, which was reasonable. The
only substantive reply I have had from Heneb is its statement of 22 May
that it is not subject to FOI as a limited company; a schedule I sent on 19
September remains outstanding.

The museum's disclosure now gives a firm anchor for what the record should
show. Accession 95.56H comprises the material from the Cotswold
Archaeological Trust excavation of 1994, donated by Ideal Homes Wales Ltd,
and its locality is recorded in the museum's own collections database as
"Great House Farm Llandough" at ST 1681 7331 — the grid reference carried
by the historic environment record for this site. That accession runs to
several hundred sets of early medieval human remains from this ground.

As the statutory duty to keep historic environment records rests with the
Welsh Ministers, I ask:
(a) which body is responsible for correcting an inaccurate or incomplete
historic environment record;
(b) what Cadw's own role is where the record is managed by Heneb; and
(c) what Cadw will do if the entry for this site (GGAT02038s) is not
corrected to reflect the 1979 and 1994 excavations and the accessions
arising from them.
