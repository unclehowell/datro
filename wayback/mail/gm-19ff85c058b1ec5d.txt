My request was deliberately framed more broadly than a simple catalogue

You also correctly state that Discovery searches catalogue titles and
descriptions rather than the contents of records. That qualification is
important, because it means the search described cannot establish that
relevant records did not exist, were never transferred, or were destroyed.

categories expressly identified in my request, including searches or
recorded information relating to:

   - Western Ground Rents Ltd;

   - Mountjoy Ltd;

   records;

   - the 1988 eviction and demolition;
   - Cadw or central-government communications concerning the property;
   - European Commission of Human Rights Application 14464/88;
   - records concerning transfer, destruction or disposal of potentially
   relevant material; and
   - HMC9/311 or related archival references.

These alternative lines of enquiry are intentional. The purpose is to
establish whether relevant information can be identified through the
*government
bodies, corporate entities, court proceedings, administrative processes and
archival transfers associated with the property*, rather than relying
solely upon the modern name by which the property is known.

I am conducting corresponding enquiries with other repositories and public
bodies. This is specifically intended to triangulate the documentary
history where records may have been catalogued under a different creator,
department, legal proceeding, estate, corporate body or administrative
function.

I therefore ask that you do not treat the absence of a Discovery keyword
hit as an answer to the substantive questions raised.

Please clarify:

   1.

   *Whether the searches undertaken were limited to the PRO and RW
   catalogue series and the four property/location search terms identified in
   your email.*

   *Whether The National Archives searched for the named corporate bodies,
   court proceedings and application references identified in my request*,
   rather than only searching the property names.
   3.

   *Whether The National Archives holds any recorded transfer, accession,
   disposal, destruction or retention information* indicating that records
   concerning the relevant property, estate, companies, litigation or
   government involvement were transferred elsewhere, destroyed, or otherwise
   disposed of.
   4.

   If such information is not held, *whether this is because no such
   records are held, because no relevant records were ever transferred to TNA,
   or because TNA's catalogue/search systems contain no discoverable
   description of them.*
   5.

   Whether *HMC9/311* or related series have been checked, or whether that
   reference falls outside the search undertaken.
   6.

   Whether there are *central-government, court, departmental or
   administrative records relating to the litigation and entities identified
   above which may contain references to the property without using “Great
   House Farm”, “Ty Mawr”, “Llandough” or “Cedfin” in the catalogue
   description.*

For the avoidance of doubt, I am not asking The National Archives to infer
the contents of uncatalogued records or to undertake an unlimited research
project. I am asking for confirmation of the *recorded information held by
TNA concerning the existence, provenance, transfer, retention, destruction
or disposal of potentially relevant records*, and for the searches
undertaken to locate that information.

That distinction is particularly important because a catalogue search
returning no result is not equivalent to a finding that no relevant
historical, governmental or archival records existed.

I would therefore be grateful if this could be reconsidered as a response
to the scope of the original FOI enquiry rather than treated solely as a
request for assistance with a Discovery keyword search.
