Our family’s wider case, so the complaint is not read as a cataloguing
dispute only

Separately from the archaeological record:

I believe there has been fraud, concealment and government involvement
resulting in the dispossession of our home and land. The family has
occupied and claimed Great House Farm since at least 1667. In 1988, after
BP Properties Ltd v Buckler, we were removed. The farmhouse was demolished
on 6 December 1988. About twenty houses now stand on that ground. The
narrative that followed treated us as peasant squatters evicted from an old
farmhouse. That narrative is false.

In reconstructing the house as a Welsh heritage museum in Patagonia I found
that our ownership of the house-and-garden parcel was never extinguished.
The working case is two parcels: Parcel A (house and garden) belonging to
Mary Williams; Parcel B treated as if it were the whole farm; a synthetic
title built by decades of false assertion that occupation of Parcel A was
permissive. Limitation is postponed if concealment is made out.
