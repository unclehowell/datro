Customer Relations / Planning Complaints Officer,

I wish to make a formal complaint concerning the Vale of Glamorgan
Council's Dear Customer Relations / Planning Complaints Officer,

I wish to make a formal complaint concerning the Vale of Glamorgan
Council's handling of Great House Farm, Llandough-juxta-Penarth,
particularly the circumstances surrounding its demolition in December 1988
and the subsequent planning process.


Please register this as a formal complaint under the Council's corporate
complaints procedure and provide me with a complaint reference number.


This complaint concerns the Council's administration and exercise of its
planning and heritage responsibilities. It is not intended to constitute an
appeal against a properly made historic planning decision.


1. Great House Farm, Llandough (Cardiff not Cowbridge) was recognised as a
building of historic importance


The planning and archaeological documentation concerning the site
identifies Great House Farm as a building of heritage significance.


In particular, the archaeological material identifies the former building
as a "county treasure" associated with SMR 2038s, while earlier
architectural recording by RCAHMW describes a substantial historic
farmhouse with surviving historic fabric.


The later 1990 archaeological assessment states:


«"Owing to an ownership dispute and the subsequent sudden destruction of
the property in 1988 the building was incompletely understood."»


I regard that statement as highly significant because it records,
contemporaneously with the subsequent planning process, that the building
had been destroyed suddenly and that its archaeological and architectural
history had therefore not been adequately understood or recorded.


2. The Council's apparent failure to take protective action before the 1988
demolition


I ask the Council to investigate what action, if any, its planning and
heritage officers took when Great House Farm was at risk of demolition.


In particular, I ask the Council to establish:


1. Whether the Council knew that Great House Farm was at risk of demolition
before December 1988.

2. Whether the Council received any notification, complaint, application,
correspondence or other warning concerning the proposed demolition.

3. Whether the building had been considered for statutory listing or other
statutory protection.

4. Whether consideration was given to the use of a Building Preservation
Notice or any other available statutory or administrative mechanism capable
of preventing or delaying demolition pending assessment.

5. Whether the Council's heritage officers, planning officers or
archaeological advisers were consulted.

6. Whether any inspection or photographic/architectural record was
undertaken immediately before or after the demolition.

7. Why, given the building's recognised historic significance, the Council
did not secure a comprehensive standing-building record before the building
was lost.


I am not asserting at this stage that a particular statutory power
necessarily had to be exercised. I am asking the Council to investigate
what powers and procedures were available in 1988 and why they were, or
were not, used in this case.


3. The "sudden destruction" of the building requires investigation


The 1990 archaeological assessment expressly attributes the incomplete
understanding of the building to its "subsequent sudden destruction ... in
1988".


I therefore ask the Council to establish from its records:


- the date on which it first became aware of the proposed or actual
demolition;

- the identity of the relevant officers;

- whether the Council received complaints or reports concerning demolition
activity;

- whether any site visit took place;

- whether any notice, warning, enforcement action, preservation action or
other intervention was considered;

- and whether the Council has retained records explaining why the
demolition was allowed to proceed.


This is particularly important because the building was subsequently
treated within the planning process as an already-cleared site.


4. The 1989 planning application appears to contain a significant
historical discrepancy


The outline planning application submitted by BP Properties Ltd in 1989
states, in the section asking which buildings were to be demolished:


«"None."»


The same application describes the site as "Disused" and the previous use
as "Not known".


This requires explanation because the application followed the demolition
of Great House Farm in December 1988 by approximately one year.


I ask the Council to investigate whether its planning officers:


- were aware of the former Great House Farm building when the 1989
application was processed;

- knew when and how it had been demolished;

- checked the accuracy of the applicant's statement that no buildings were
to be demolished;

- identified the former farmhouse in the planning history and supporting
documentation;

- and considered whether the demolition itself raised any planning,
heritage or enforcement issue requiring investigation.


I am particularly concerned that the planning process appears to have
proceeded on the basis of a cleared site without first establishing the
circumstances in which the historic building had disappeared.


5. Failure to create an adequate record of the lost building


The subsequent archaeological documentation states that the building was
"incompletely understood".


I ask the Council to explain what mitigation, recording or investigation it
required in consequence.


The documentary evidence presently available indicates that substantial
historic fabric and multiple phases of the building existed, yet the
building was destroyed without a complete standing-building survey having
been secured.


I therefore ask the Council to identify:


- all surveys undertaken before demolition;

- all surveys undertaken after demolition;

- all archaeological or architectural advice obtained by the Council;

- all photographs held by the Council;

- all correspondence concerning the building's architectural or historic
importance;

- and the Council's policy or practice in 1988 concerning the recording of
significant unlisted historic buildings at risk.


6. Subsequent planning treatment of the site


Following demolition, the planning process concentrated heavily on
below-ground archaeology, ultimately resulting in archaeological
investigation of the cemetery and associated remains.


I do not suggest that archaeological excavation was inappropriate. My
concern is that the destruction of the standing historic building appears
to have been treated as a fait accompli rather than as an event requiring
accountability and an explanation of how the Council's heritage
responsibilities had been discharged.


The later planning documentation should therefore be reviewed alongside the
records relating to the 1988 demolition.


In particular, I ask the Council to identify precisely when the planning
authority first recorded the former farmhouse as demolished and what
explanation was placed on the planning file for its loss.


7. Matters I ask the Council to investigate


Please investigate and provide a substantive response to the following
questions:


A. What did Vale of Glamorgan Council know about the impending demolition
of Great House Farm before December 1988?


B. What statutory and non-statutory heritage protections were available to
the Council at the time?


C. Were any of those mechanisms considered or discussed?


D. Were any Building Preservation Notice, listing, enforcement, urgent
works, archaeological recording or other protective measures considered?


E. Why was the building apparently destroyed without a comprehensive
standing-building survey?


F. What records does the Council hold concerning the demolition itself?


G. Why did the 1989 planning application proceed despite the recent
demolition of a building identified in the archaeological record as a
"county treasure"?


H. Why was the applicant's answer "None" given to the question concerning
buildings to be demolished, and did the Council investigate the apparent
discrepancy?


I. Did any Council officer consider whether the 1988 demolition constituted
a breach of planning control or otherwise required investigation?


J. Did the Council investigate whether the building had been demolished
lawfully?


K. What heritage assessment was undertaken before the subsequent
development permissions were processed?


L. What lessons has the Council subsequently incorporated into its
procedures for County Treasures and other locally significant historic
buildings?


8. Records requested as part of the investigation


Please preserve and review all relevant records, including planning files,
enforcement records, heritage files, committee papers, officer reports,
internal correspondence, site inspection notes, photographs, archaeological
correspondence and consultation records concerning:


- Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr / Cedfin / Cydfin;

- SMR/HER reference 2038s / GGAT02038s;

- the December 1988 demolition;

- the 1989 outline planning application;

- the 1990 archaeological assessment;

- and the subsequent reserved matters/development applications.


I would also ask that the Council identify any material which has been
destroyed or cannot now be located, together with the applicable
retention/disposal record where one exists.


9. Outcome sought


I am seeking a formal investigation and a reasoned written explanation of
the Council's actions and omissions.


In particular, I ask the Council to determine:


1. Whether its officers discharged the heritage and planning
responsibilities applicable at the time.

2. Whether any failure occurred in relation to the identification,
protection, recording or investigation of Great House Farm.

3. Whether the Council's handling of the 1989 planning application
appropriately addressed the recent demolition of the building.

4. Whether any relevant records have been lost or destroyed and, if so,
when and under what retention arrangements.

5. What procedural lessons arise from the case.


I also request disclosure of the relevant non-exempt records identified
during the complaint investigation, where these are not otherwise
available. I recognise that requests for specific recorded information may
need to be dealt with separately under the appropriate information-access
regime.


I have only recently been able to examine and correlate the surviving
planning, archaeological and heritage documentation sufficiently to
identify these issues. I therefore ask that the Council accepts the
complaint for investigation notwithstanding the age of the events concerned.


Please acknowledge receipt and confirm the complaint reference and the
officer responsible for its investigation.


Yours faithfully,
