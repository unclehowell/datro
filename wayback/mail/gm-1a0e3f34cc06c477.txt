Sirs/Madam,

I write concerning the land now comprising Church View Close, Llandough,
formerly Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr, including title WA231076 and former
title WA240304.

Persimmon Homes (Wales) Ltd is the current registered proprietor of
WA231076. Persimmon is therefore a present link in the conveyancing chain
arising from the registration of this land in BP Properties Ltd's name in
1982–83, BP's subsequent transfer to Ideal Homes in 1993, and the later
development history.

I am conducting a documented investigation into the legitimacy of that
title chain. This is not a speculative historical enquiry. The issues arise
from identified Land Registry, conveyancing, court, planning and
public-authority records, and from specific documentary gaps which remain
unresolved.

The central issue is the historic identity and ownership of the land.

The evidence presently assembled indicates two historically distinct
parcels:

• Parcel A: the farmhouse, garden, buildings, yard and associated woodland,
which the Williams family maintains was acquired out of the Bute estate
through the Daniel Thomas transaction and subsequently became the family's
freehold interest; and

• Parcel B: the eastern agricultural fields, whose subsequent passage from
Bute to Western Ground Rents and then to BP is not presently disputed by
the family.

The significance of that distinction is that later documents repeatedly use
the expression “whole farm”, although the surviving 1939 tenancy plan is
understood to show the eastern fields only. The 1938 Bute-to-Western Ground
Rents conveyance is therefore a material title-chain question: what estate
was actually capable of being conveyed by Bute in 1938, and did that
conveyance include Parcel A?

There is further evidence requiring examination of the period from 1949 to
1974, including repeated attempts to characterise Mary's occupation of the
farmhouse parcel as tenancy or permission; the loss of the family's title
papers in about 1950–52; the 1955 possession order expressed in “whole
farm” terms but enforced everywhere except the farmhouse and garden; the
separate 1962 possession proceedings concerning the farmhouse and garden;
Mary's express assertion of ownership; and the 1974 proceedings, which were
adjourned part-heard and were never finally adjudicated on the family's
historic ownership claim.

The Court of Appeal's decision in BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA
Civ 2 subsequently concerned possession, limitation and the legal effect of
the October 1974 letters. The judgment records Mary's asserted ownership
and the absence of the documents she said supported it. The present
investigation therefore distinguishes carefully between what that judgment
decided concerning possession and limitation and the separate question of
whether the documentary root and extent of WGR/BP's title to Parcel A were
ever actually established.

That distinction becomes particularly important in relation to first
registration.

HM Land Registry records show BP's first-registration applications for
WA231076 and WA240304 in 1982–83. The surviving registration material also
identifies a 31 December 1969 conveyance from Western Ground Rents to BP
Pension Trust as a root of title, but that conveyance is not presently
included in the 30 July 2026 schedule of documents surviving on the
relevant first-registration files. The 1975 conveyance and plan, the
1982–83 registration material, the extent of the two titles and their later
amalgamation therefore require direct reconciliation against the underlying
documentary title.

There is also an unresolved procedural issue concerning notice and
opportunity to object during the 1982–83 first-registration process. Mary
Williams remained in occupation of the farmhouse. The family evidence
recorded on the evidence index is that notification was not received until
after her death on 26 March 1983. I am obtaining the primary notice,
service, application and registration records to establish exactly what
occurred.

The matters under investigation consequently include possible registration
mistake, procedural unfairness, material non-disclosure and, depending upon
what the primary records establish, possible fraud or deliberate
concealment. There are also unresolved public-authority record questions
involving HM Land Registry, court administration and other authorities.
These are identified as evidential questions, not asserted as established
findings against Persimmon or any individual.

Persimmon's position is therefore directly relevant because its present
registered title derives from the historic chain now being examined.

Please provide a substantive response to the following:

   2.

   3.

   4.

   6.

If Persimmon maintains that the documentary title to Parcel A is
