Our response

The initial response from our records enquiries team looked for the four terms (“Great House Farm”, “Ty Mawr”, “Llandough” or “Cedfin”) within the PRO and RW series and did not find any relevant information. We have now tried those terms and the further twelve terms that you have specified in a wider catalogue search. We have provided the results of our search below.

The crux of your query is local land ownership dispute in Glamorgan and the demolition of some buildings by a private company; generally speaking, these are not matters dealt with by central government and we would not expect to see anything within our collections. As such, there would have been no records to transfer. The exception is the court case relating to BP properties, which generated some papers. Links to the catalogue pages for these records can be seen below.

There are likely to be records of the farm in the 1940s as part of the National Farm Survey series but these cannot all currently be searched online by farm.

We are unable to conduct this research on the archival collections on your behalf without charging fees to conduct research on your behalf (Paid Search). A public authority is allowed to charge fees under section 9 of the Freedom of Information Act and within this section of the Act it states: "provision is made by or under any enactment as to the fee may be charged by the public authority for the disclosure of the information" (S9(5) FOIA).

The Public Records Act (1958) enables a statutory instrument to be made setting out the fees which may be made in connection with the supply of information from The National Archives. The prices we charge are set out by our Fees Regulations<https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1243/made>.

If you are interested in paying for research to be conducted on your behalf, then details of our Paid Search service and other options for paying for research are available here: Paying for research - The National Archives<https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/paying-for-research/>

If you would prefer to conduct this research yourself then you may find the following research guide to be of assistance: National Farm Survey of England and Wales 1941-1943 - The National Archives<https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/national-farm-survey-england-wales-1941-1943/>

There are Agricultural Executive Committee minutes and papers for Glamorgan; but again, these cannot be searched online by name of farm and we are unable to conduct this research without charging a fee: Browse records of other archives | The National Archives<https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/browse/r/h/C42711>

Your request

Original email dated 13/08/2026:

My request was deliberately framed more broadly than a simple catalogue search for the words “Great House Farm”, “Ty Mawr”, “Llandough” or “Cedfin”. I specifically asked The National Archives to identify recorded information concerning the existence, transfer, retention, destruction, disposal or absence of records, including records that might not be catalogued under the property's later or local names.

Your response confirms only that you searched the PRO and RW series using:

“Great House Farm” OR “Ty Mawr” OR Llandough OR Cedfin

combined with “Glamorgan”, and that this produced no catalogue results.

You also correctly state that Discovery searches catalogue titles and descriptions rather than the contents of records. That qualification is important, because it means the search described cannot establish that relevant records did not exist, were never transferred, or were destroyed.

In particular, your response does not appear to address the other categories expressly identified in my request, including searches or recorded information relating to:


   *   Western Ground Rents Ltd;
   *   BP Pension Trust Ltd;
   *   BP Properties Ltd;
   *   Mountjoy Ltd;
   *   the proceedings in BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2;
   *   the 1982–1983 registration and associated government or administrative records;
   *   the 1980–1988 public campaign and governmental correspondence;
   *   the 1988 eviction and demolition;
   *   Cadw or central-government communications concerning the property;
   *   European Commission of Human Rights Application 14464/88;
   *   records concerning transfer, destruction or disposal of potentially relevant material; and
   *   HMC9/311 or related archival references.

These alternative lines of enquiry are intentional. The purpose is to establish whether relevant information can be identified through the government bodies, corporate entities, court proceedings, administrative processes and archival transfers associated with the property, rather than relying solely upon the modern name by which the property is known.

I am conducting corresponding enquiries with other repositories and public bodies. This is specifically intended to triangulate the documentary history where records may have been catalogued under a different creator, department, legal proceeding, estate, corporate body or administrative function.

I therefore ask that you do not treat the absence of a Discovery keyword hit as an answer to the substantive questions raised.

Please clarify:


  1.  Whether the searches undertaken were limited to the PRO and RW catalogue series and the four property/location search terms identified in your email.


  2.  Whether The National Archives searched for the named corporate bodies, court proceedings and application references identified in my request, rather than only searching the property names.


  3.  Whether The National Archives holds any recorded transfer, accession, disposal, destruction or retention information indicating that records concerning the relevant property, estate, companies, litigation or government involvement were transferred elsewhere, destroyed, or otherwise disposed of.


  4.  If such information is not held, whether this is because no such records are held, because no relevant records were ever transferred to TNA, or because TNA's catalogue/search systems contain no discoverable description of them.


  5.  Whether HMC9/311 or related series have been checked, or whether that reference falls outside the search undertaken.


  6.  Whether there are central-government, court, departmental or administrative records relating to the litigation and entities identified above which may contain references to the property without using “Great House Farm”, “Ty Mawr”, “Llandough” or “Cedfin” in the catalogue description.

For the avoidance of doubt, I am not asking The National Archives to infer the contents of uncatalogued records or to undertake an unlimited research project. I am asking for confirmation of the recorded information held by TNA concerning the existence, provenance, transfer, retention, destruction or disposal of potentially relevant records, and for the searches undertaken to locate that information.

That distinction is particularly important because a catalogue search returning no result is not equivalent to a finding that no relevant historical, governmental or archival records existed.

I would therefore be grateful if this could be reconsidered as a response to the scope of the original FOI enquiry rather than treated solely as a request for assistance with a Discovery keyword search.

I am continuing parallel enquiries with the National Library of Wales, Glamorgan Archives, Cadw, RCAHMW, HM Land Registry and other relevant bodies. The purpose of these enquiries is to establish the documentary chain independently and to identify where records were created, transferred, retained or potentially destroyed.
