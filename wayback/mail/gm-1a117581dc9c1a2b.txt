FOI Team,

Thank you for the clarification.

Your email of 7 October materially clarifies the position, and I have now
corrected my records to distinguish the different Llandough farmsteads.
Ref 13.2 — Llandough Farm opposite the hospital

I understand and accept the distinction in your catalogue description:

“Waterwheel from Llandough Farm (opposite hospital)”

This refers to the separate *Llandough Farm in the Llandough near
Penarth/Cardiff locality*, not to Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr. I am not
seeking to attribute the waterwheel to Great House Farm.

I will therefore treat Ref 13.2 as a separate Llandough Farm record unless
a primary Museum record expressly establishes otherwise.
MS 3360/3 — Great House / Llandough Estate

The more significant clarification is the information you have now supplied
from MS 3360/3.

You state that the page records that:

   - “Great House” formed part of the *Llandough Estate sale particulars of
   1924*;
   - it was occupied by *W.G. Thomas & Sons*;
   - it comprised approximately *202 acres*;
   - it was situated in the *Parish of Llanfair*; and
   - the homestead was situated in the *Village of Llandough*.

Those particulars provide a much more specific identification than the
earlier catalogue description.

I am particularly interested in the combination of *Llandough Estate +
Parish of Llanfair + Village of Llandough + approximately 202 acres + W.G.
Thomas & Sons*.

Could you therefore confirm one narrow point from the record already
examined:

*Does the MS 3360/3 page or plan itself contain any further recorded
identifier which identifies which historical Llandough is meant — for
example the specific place-name form, neighbouring farms, estate owner,
parish/benefice wording, acreage breakdown, plan number, tenant
information, road, church or other geographical feature?*

I am not asking the Museum to make a new identification or to provide an
opinion. I am asking only whether such information is actually recorded in
the page or plan you have already examined.

There is an important reason for keeping this distinction precise. There
are *two different farmsteads in the Llandough near Penarth/Cardiff
locality* relevant to the Museum's records:

   1. *Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr*, beside St Dochdwy's Church; and
   2. *Llandough Farm*, separately described by the Museum as “opposite
   hospital”.

They must not be treated as alternative names for one farm.

There is also a separate question concerning the Museum's wording *“Llandough
Church Farm”* in the 1858 provenance of accession 63.24. I now understand
that this should not automatically be equated with either of the above
merely because it contains the word Llandough. Unless the underlying
historical evidence expressly identifies the 1858 “Church Farm”, I will
continue to record that identity as unresolved.
Filtered schedule

I also accept your clarification that the Museum does not hold a separate
recorded audit schedule showing, item by item, which search results staff
excluded after reviewing the results.

I therefore do not ask the Museum to create such a schedule.

For the purposes of the record, I will instead distinguish between:

   - records positively identified as Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr;
   - records positively identified as the separate Llandough Farm opposite
   the hospital;
   - records from other Llandoughs, including Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge;
   - and source-specific records whose location remains unresolved.

The new MS 3360/3 particulars are sufficiently distinctive to make the
underlying page and plan an important identification source, so I would be
grateful for confirmation of whether any further geographical identifier is
actually recorded there.

Thank you again for the clarification.

Cofion,
