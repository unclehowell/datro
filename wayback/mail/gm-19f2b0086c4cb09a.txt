> Dear Ms Longford,
>
> Thank you for your letter of 2 July 2026 responding to the internal review
> of my Freedom of Information request (ATISN 27021) concerning Great House
> Farm, Llandough.
>
> I appreciate the additional searches undertaken during the review and the
> explanation provided regarding the physical record series examined.
>
> However, having considered both the original response of 11 June 2026 and
> the outcome of the internal review, I would be be grateful if you could
> clarify a number of points which appear to remain unresolved.
>
> *1. Documentary basis of the original response*
>
> The original response stated that Great House Farm was visited and
> assessed by Cadw inspectors and that the building did not satisfy the
> statutory listing criteria.
>
> The internal review now confirms that no assessment, report or case file
> relating to that decision has been located and further states that any
> relevant file may have been destroyed before being entered onto the
> electronic records system.
>
> Please therefore identify the documentary basis upon which the original
> response concluded that the property was assessed and found not to meet the
> listing criteria.
>
> Specifically:
>
>    - Is that conclusion derived from a surviving record?
>    - If so, please identify that record.
>    - If not, please explain how that conclusion was reached.
>
> *2. The inspection of 29 July 1988*
>
> The original disclosure included two photographs dated 29 July 1988.
>
> Please confirm:
>
>    - whether those photographs were taken as part of a formal listing
>    inspection;
>    - whether they formed part of a wider inspection file;
>    - whether any inspection notes, recommendation forms, field notebooks,
>    photographic registers or other associated records were created; and
>    - if such records once existed, whether they are now believed to have
>    been destroyed.
>
> *3. Basis for the statement that records may have been destroyed*
>
> The review states that it is possible documentation dating from around
> 1988 was destroyed before being entered onto the electronic records system.
>
> Please clarify whether that statement is based upon:
>
>    - documentary evidence relating specifically to Great House Farm;
>    - records management documentation such as retention schedules,
>    destruction registers or disposal logs; or
>    - a general understanding of historic records management practices.
>
> If no destruction records exist for the relevant file series, please
> confirm this.
>
> *4. Scope of the searches*
>
> Please confirm whether searches extended beyond the registered listing
> files to include, where appropriate:
>
>    - ministerial correspondence;
>    - senior officials' correspondence;
>    - legal files;
>    - inspector's working papers;
>    - site visit records;
>    - archaeological or conservation files;
>    - policy records; and
>    - correspondence with external organisations, including the Vale of
>    Glamorgan Council, the former Welsh Office and other heritage bodies.
>
> If any of these categories were not searched, I would be grateful if you
> could identify which.
>
> *5. Welsh Government's position*
>
> Finally, I would be appreciate clarification of the Welsh Government's
> position regarding Great House Farm.
>
> Does the Welsh Government's position now amount to one of the following:
>
>    - that Great House Farm was never considered for statutory protection;
>    - that it was considered, but the records no longer survive; or
>    - that it is no longer possible to determine whether consideration
>    took place because the relevant records are unavailable?
>
> If none of those accurately reflects your position, I would be grateful if
> you could explain what the Welsh Government's position is.
>
> These questions arise directly from the original response and the internal
> review and are intended to clarify the historical record rather than seek
> the creation of new information.
>
> I look forward to your response.
>
> Yours sincerely,
>
> Sion Buckler
