   - Born 10 August 1913
   - Death registered March 1983, South Glamorgan (Vol 28, Page 3029)

The All Wales Medical Examiner Service was introduced on a non-statutory basis in 2019. It operated across Wales to provide independent scrutiny of deaths that were not referred to a coroner so deaths in 1983 and 1990 would not apply in this case. The service became statutory on 9 September 2024, when the death certification reforms came into force in England and Wales. From that date, independent scrutiny by a Medical Examiner became a legal requirement before registration of all non-coronial deaths. Therefore, for deaths in 1983/1990, there would have been no Medical Examiner Service. Death certification would have been carried out by the attending doctor, with referrals made to the coroner where required under the rules that existed at the time. The Medical Examiner system only arrived many decades later.

If both deaths occurred in Secondary Care, Hospitals do maintain records of deaths that occurred in their facilities, but these are not generally public records.

The official legal record of a death is the death certificate registered with the local registrar and held by the General Register Office (GRO). In Wales, you can obtain copies of death certificates dating back before 1983 if the death was registered.  Hospital medical records are usually retained under NHS records management policies and may still exist for deaths in 1983, but access is restricted because they contain confidential medical information.

If you are researching a specific death from 1990 at Llandough Hospital, possible sources are:


  1.  Death certificate from the General Register Office.
  2.  Glamorgan Archives for historical NHS administrative records.
  3.  The relevant NHS body that inherited Llandough Hospital records, now part of Cardiff and Vale University Health Board.
  4.  Coroner's records, if an inquest was held.

In 1990, Llandough Hospital was still an NHS hospital managed through the local health authority system. This was under the South Glamorgan Health Authority, which was responsible for hospitals in the Cardiff and Vale area before NHS trusts were created in the early 1990s and now the Cardiff & Vale University Health Board.

They can be contacted here:

Health Records - Cardiff and Vale University Health Board 

GP records are generally retained for a long period. Under current NHS records management guidance, GP records are typically kept for 10 years after a patient's death before being destroyed so these records would be out of scope.

I have asked our registration team to search both patients and there are no records found for either patients due to the time elapsed.
