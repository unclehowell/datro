Dear Information Commissioner's Office,

Further to my email earlier today asking for more time (same thread, internal review outcome attached), please treat this email as my formal complaint under section 50 of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 about Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales's handling of my request FOI 2026-024.

An extension of time is still my preference, so that I can send a fuller complaint. If you cannot grant one, please rely on this email as my complaint, made within six weeks of the internal review outcome of 22 September 2026.

Request: on 27 July 2026 I asked for recorded information about a discovery at Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr), Llandough-juxta-Penarth, near Cardiff (Heneb HER GGAT02038s; RCAHMW NPRN 20182). A published local history account records that a soldier's remains and his horse were found beneath the dining-room floor, that the remains were reburied in the churchyard, and that the visor and lance were given to the National Museum of Wales. I asked for accession, donation or transfer records, accession numbers and provenance notes, catalogue entries and correspondence, records of the associated remains, and any discovery or excavation reports.

Response: on 25 August 2026, one working day after the 20-working-day limit, the Museum said under section 1(1)(a) that it held no information. It also said its searches had found records of "other archaeological discoveries recorded at Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough", and that it had treated those as outside the request without describing them.

Internal review: I asked for a review on 26 August 2026. My point was that the searches were framed too narrowly around a "medieval visor, lance, helmet or other item of armour", and that the original find may have been described differently (for example as Roman). The review, issued on 22 September 2026, upheld the "not held" answer. It also said that those other records would receive "a separate response". I have not identified a response in which the Museum disclosed or described the records it set aside.

Why I believe the "not held" answer and its scope are doubtful:
1. The Museum confirms it holds records of archaeological discoveries at the same farm, yet it judged them unrelated without telling me what they are, so I cannot test that judgement. Whether they relate to the reported discovery is the question I asked.
2. Nomenclature is a major cause of confusion for all parties, and I ask the Commissioner and the Museum to take it into account. The same property appears in records as Great House Farm, Great House, Greathouse, Tŷ Mawr, Ty Mawr Farm, Tymawr, Cydfin Farm and Llandough Home Farm (1881 census). The place appears as Llandough, Llandough-juxta-Penarth, Llandough near Penarth and Llandough near Cardiff, and it is easily confused with Llandow in the Vale of Glamorgan. The Museum's own records use further terms: "Church Farm / Llandough Church Farm" in the provenance of accession 63.24 (medieval ironwork found about 1858), and "Llandough Farm (opposite hospital)", which is a separate farmstead. A search under one name or a modern object term can miss a record catalogued under another. I ask that the searches be repeated across all of these names, and across the donor names, periods and object descriptions a nineteenth-century accession might carry.
3. The reply was late (25 August against a due date of 24 August).

I ask the Commissioner to decide:
(a) whether, on the balance of probabilities, the Museum holds information within the scope of my request, including the records it set aside as "other discoveries" at Great House Farm and any record linked to accession 63.24;
(b) whether its searches were adequate, and to ask it to explain the search terms, name variants, systems and date ranges it used;
(c) that the Museum breached section 10(1) by replying late; and
(d) that the Museum should issue a fresh response covering the records it set aside.

The research behind this complaint, including the full name-variation index, is published at https://bucklerfamily.estate, and all ongoing research will continue to be published there.

The internal review outcome is attached. I can supply the original request (27 July), the Museum's response (25 August) and my review request (26 August) on request.
