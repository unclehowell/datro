Robert Fleming,

Thank you for your prompt response and for providing the helpful research
guides on land ownership records,

To clarify my original enquiry, I am specifically concerned with the
historical record surrounding the case of BP Properties Ltd v Buckler
[1987] EWCA Civ 2, which involved a disputed land dispossession at Great
House Farm in Llandough, South Glamorgan. This stemmed from earlier
proceedings in 1974, including an adjourned case in July and an appeal
attempt on October 31st that was impacted by correspondence from BP the
same day. Following the 1987 ruling, there was forced removal, demolition
of our family home, and archaeological activity on the site, despite
ongoing questions about the adjudication of ownership prior to land
registration.

My question is not solely about locating ownership or court documents
(though any guidance on whether The National Archives holds physical files
from these proceedings would be welcome), but rather whether circumstances
like these—where ownership was disputed and not fully resolved before
registration or excavation—raise any considerations for the integrity or
completeness of the archival record. For instance, do such disputes prompt
reviews or annotations in historical land records, or are there protocols
for archiving related materials from corporate entities like BP or local
authorities involved?

I understand your resources are focused on national-level holdings, but any
further direction would greatly assist my research.

Sincerely,

Sion Buckler

> It is unlikely The National Archives holds the records of this type of
> case, and the Central Land Registry
> <https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/land-registry>, who hold
> records of land registration, is a separate body. Some records of land
> ownership can be found among records at local or county archives.
>
> I recommend you consult our guide to searching for records of Land
> ownership records and deeds: an overview - The National Archives
> <https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/land-ownership-records-and-deeds-an-overview/>.
> Regarding the court cases you mention, I also recommend you consult: Civil
> court cases: Chancery Division since 1875 - The National Archives
> <https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/civil-court-cases-chancery-division-since-1875/>,
> and you may also find Civil courts: appeal cases after 1875 - The
> National Archives
> <https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/civil-courts-appeal-cases-after-1875/>
> of use to your research. These guides will help you to use *our catalogue*
> <https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/>, or other online sources,
> effectively and focus your search.
>
> Records are represented in our online catalogue by a title, a reference
> and a brief description. When you search our catalogue using keywords you
> are searching across these titles and descriptions, rather than the
> contents of the records themselves. By clicking on any search result, you
> can see whether the record is available online or if you need to visit us
> to view the original. You can also request copies of records from their
> catalogue description page.
>
> If the record is held by another archive, you will need to contact them
> directly to find out how you can access it.
