Dear Sion,

The question about archival silences is one upon which there is a voluminous literature. Practically, however, the best way of making sense of what is and is not in the archives is to refer to the reports which shaped selection policies in the modern era - see especially the Report on Departmental Records (Cmnd 9136) and the Report on Legal Records (Cmnd 3084) - and the resulting retention and disposition schedules - see for example the one for the Court of Appeal (Civil Division) published here<https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/62b47cc9d3bf7f0affd65610/court-appeal-civil-division-rrds.pdf>.

What you will see from these is that the presumption for modern records is against permanent preservation. In general, apart from small samples to demonstrate the ordinary functioning of the courts/government departments, records are only preserved if they bear witness to moments of particular legal or policy significance or change. In the case of legal records, the presumption has been that parties would be responsible for maintaining their own records.

In terms of how to continue your search, my instinct is that you should consider exploring the holdings of the relevant local archive. You can also try some keyword searches in our catalogue - see, for example, these<https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/results/r?_d=PF%7CWORK%7CBD%7CHLG&_dss=range&_sd=1978&_ed=1988&_ps=60&_hb=tna&_srt=1&_q=archaeological+NOT+bill+NOT+%22PF+309%22> search results which highlight these<https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/results/r?_q=archaeological+NOT+bill+NOT+%22PF+309%22&_d=PF%7CWORK%7CBD%7CHLG&_dss=range&_sd=1978&_ed=1988&_ps=60&_hb=tna&_srt=1&_l=3> series as potentially a relevant source of desired information. You might also wish to search the registered files of the land registry in records series LAR 1<https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C10222>. You should be advised, however, that departmental records tend to focus more on policy than upon the treatment of individual cases.

I hope that this is helpful.

Enquiry:



I note and appreciate your confirmation that the principal judicial series (J 157, J 83, J 84) contain no relevant material. Given the scale of the consequences flowing from the dispute—forced removal, demolition of an occupied dwelling, and loss of heritage fabric—the absence of supporting court or administrative files is itself significant for researchers.

Accordingly, I would be grateful for guidance on the following procedural points:

  *   how researchers are expected to interpret gaps or silence in the archival record where substantial actions plainly occurred;
  *   whether related non-judicial records (for example, departmental, planning, regulatory, or corporate records connected to land registration, planning consent, or archaeological works) are more likely to sit outside the obvious court series; and
  *   whether there is an appropriate mechanism for flagging concerns about record completeness or contextual risk, without seeking alteration of the records themselves.

For the avoidance of doubt, I am not alleging impropriety on the part of The National Archives or its staff. My concern is to ensure that the historical record is approached with appropriate caution where absence may unintentionally convey certainty or closure that did not exist at the time.
