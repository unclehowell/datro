Dear MP of Vale of Glamorgan

I am seeking your assistance in referring a complaint to the Parliamentary
and Health Service Ombudsman concerning HM Land Registry.

I have exhausted HM Land Registry's internal complaints process and have
received what they describe as their final Stage 2 response regarding title
number WA231076 and land at Great House Farm / Land on the North Side of
Leckwith Road, Llandough.

My concern is not simply that HM Land Registry disagrees with me. Rather, I
believe there has been a failure to properly engage with key evidence and
historical issues raised by my family concerning ownership claims, missing
title documentation, and the procedural history of litigation connected
with the property.

In particular:

   - HM Land Registry relied on references to court findings but did not
   clearly identify which court determination finally adjudicated the
   historical ownership claim advanced by my family.
   - My request for examination of registration issues appears to have been
   converted into a complaints process and then closed through administrative
   procedures.
   - HM Land Registry repeatedly stated that it would not "look behind"
   court decisions, yet did not address whether certain historical ownership
   issues were ever substantively determined by a court.
   - Important factual concerns were treated as irrelevant to registration
   without detailed investigation.
   - I remain concerned that potentially significant historical evidence
   and title issues have never been fully examined.

I am therefore requesting that you refer this matter to the Parliamentary
and Health Service Ombudsman for independent review of HM Land Registry's
handling of my case and its decision-making process.

I can provide the full correspondence, Land Registry references, court
materials, historical documents, and supporting evidence if required.

I would be grateful if you could consider making a referral on my behalf.

Yours sincerely,

Sion Buckler

5 Robin Hill, Dinas Powys, Vale of Glamorgan

P.S.

Having carefully reviewed HM Land Registry's responses, I remain concerned
by several matters that appear unresolved.

The responses state that the registered proprietor evidenced a good
documentary title, yet do not identify the precise chain of title relied
upon, the key documents considered, or how that documentary title was
assessed against the long-standing ownership claim advanced by my
grandmother, Mary Williams.

The responses rely upon references to court findings, but do not clearly
identify whether any court ever finally adjudicated the merits of my
grandmother's ownership claim. My understanding is that earlier proceedings
may have encountered procedural difficulties and may not have resulted in a
substantive determination of title.

HM Land Registry states that it will not "look behind" court decisions.
However, this leaves unanswered the question of whether certain historical
ownership issues were ever examined by a court in the first place.

The responses also do not explain what consideration, if any, was given to
my grandmother's repeated assertions of ownership, the documents she
believed supported that ownership, or the circumstances under which those
documents became unavailable.

Similarly, concerns raised regarding the manner in which my grandmother was
identified in historical dealings were treated as irrelevant to
registration, but the underlying factual issues were not addressed.

What I am seeking are clear answers to the following questions:

• What specific documentary chain of title was relied upon for first
registration?

• Was there ever a final judicial determination of the Williams ownership
claim on its merits, and if so, what was the case, court, and order?

• What records are held by public authorities relating to documents relied
upon by Mary Williams when asserting ownership?

• Have the historical ownership issues raised by my family ever been
independently investigated, or have they been treated as settled by
reference to later proceedings concerning different legal questions?

These questions have remained unanswered despite many years of enquiries
and are central to understanding whether my family's concerns have ever
been fully examined.
