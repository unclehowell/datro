I'm researching my birthplace and former family farm, Great House Farm, Llandough (also known as Ty Mawr), which had been our home since 1667. Our family is Buckler (formerly Williams).
If anyone has community stories, memories, photographs, or documents relating to the farm or family, I'd really appreciate hearing from you.
From everything I have been able to uncover, I believe the land is still rightfully ours, despite BP and the British state obtaining possession through the courts in 1987. We were then removed from the property, and our 800-year-old home was demolished within hours. At the time, the press labelled us "squatters being evicted."
CADW failed to list our 10-bedroom former coach/courthouse as Grade II, leaving it without protection from demolition. The Land Registry also registered the land in BP's name in 1983, despite the dispute. I believe this represents a major historical injustice.
Several members of our family died while challenging BP's registration in 1983 and the planning permission granted in 1991. The trauma has left much of my family unable to speak publicly about what happened. My pregnant mother even suffered a dislocated shoulder while serving an injunction in an attempt to stop the demolition of our occupied home, according to declassified ITV footage that remained suppressed for 38 years.
Much of the site's history has also been overlooked or forgotten. A Roman soldier buried in full armour, discovered and reported by our family in 1880; Guglielmo Marconi stayed with us while carrying out his pioneering wireless experiments in 1897; and Peerless Jim Driscoll fought his first bout in our barns.
Archaeologically, the site is extraordinary. A Roman villa was discovered there in 1979, and later excavations identified what has been described as Wales' largest and most significant burial site. There has also been little effort to formally investigate the monastery believed to lie beneath the land.
Today there is not even a plaque or information board explaining the historical and archaeological significance of the site. Church View Close now occupies the land with around 20 characterless houses.
For me, this is not just an injustice to my family, but to the history and heritage of the local community and of Wales itself.
