Dear Mr Egan,

Thank you for your email of 11 October 2026. It contains several separate matters, so I have answered each under its own number. Please respond to each separately. Nothing below withdraws or narrows my original request of 21 September, and attendance at a meeting is not a condition of my information rights.

I am not asking the Council to decide the family's title dispute or to make findings about any individual, and I am not alleging wrongdoing by you. I ask that records be identified, searched for and disclosed, and that the matters in section 4 be considered.

1. INTERNAL REVIEW OF THE 11 OCTOBER REFUSAL (FOIA s.12; EIR reg. 11)

Please treat this as a formal request for the internal review you offered, of the section 12 refusal of my request of 21 September. To the extent any information is environmental, please also treat it as representations under regulation 11 of the EIR 2004.

My request had three categories: (a) records created after the 2002 deposit concerning Great House Farm / Tý Mawr, Church View Close, the village green (including VG 41) or the roadside verge by the churchyard wall; (b) photographs, postcards or images of the farm, barn, green or church surroundings, of any date; (c) records showing what, if anything, transferred from Penarth Town Council at the 1982 reorganisation, and where pre-1982 records were deposited. I ask the reviewer to address:

(i) Scope. The estimate covers only the reading of 224 sets of minutes for 2002-2016. Please explain why that is necessary and sufficient for each category, particularly (b) and (c), and what other searches were considered: correspondence and email files, photograph collections, transfer or deposit records, any index or electronic copy of the minutes, and sampling.

(ii) Arithmetic. The letter gives 20 minutes to read plus 10 minutes to assess each set, which is 30 minutes x 224 = 6,720 minutes (112 hours), but states 2,240 minutes (37 hours), which is 10 minutes x 224. Please confirm which calculation was used and which activities under regulation 4 of the Fees Regulations it includes. I accept the limit is exceeded on either figure.

(iii) Section 12(2). Please say whether the cost of determining whether the information is held would itself exceed the limit, or whether the estimate concerns locating and extracting information once its existence is established.

(iv) EIR. The green, verge, trees, woodland and land boundaries are likely to be environmental information. Please say which parts were assessed under the EIR. The FOIA cost limit is not an EIR refusal ground; if regulation 12(4)(b) is relied on, please give the reasons, the public interest assessment and the presumption in favour of disclosure.

(v) Advice and assistance (s.16 FOIA; reg. 9 EIR). Limiting the minutes to 2002-2006 does not address (b) or (c). Please say whether any part can be answered within the limit and suggest a practical refinement.

(vi) Without prejudice to the review: if the refusal is maintained, please treat parts (b) and (c) alone as a refined request, assessed under the correct regime, so that no time is lost.

Please have the review carried out by someone who took no part in the original decision, with the outcome and reasons in writing. I would be grateful for the outcome within 20 working days.

2. NEW REQUEST FOR RECORDED INFORMATION (FOIA and, where environmental, EIR)

This is a separate request, not a refinement of the one under review. Please search for Council-held or controlled minutes, agendas, reports, correspondence, complaints, notes or referrals:

(i) from approximately 1986 to 1990 concerning Great House Farm / Tý Mawr, its occupation, title or tenure, development or demolition, including any concerns raised by my late father, William "Billy" Buckler, or other family members. I have family information that my father raised concerns at Council meetings around 1988, described within the family as fraud-related. I cannot verify whether he did, what was said or what followed, and I do not present it as established fact. I ask whether the Council's records can establish what, if anything, was reported, how it was handled, and whether it was referred to any other body;

(ii) of any date concerning the village green, roadside verge, trees and adjoining woodland, and any reported extension of Church View Close gardens beyond their boundaries into the woodland, including correspondence with the charity that holds the woodland, residents or other authorities;

(iii) concerning the 2002 deposit: please confirm whether the Council retains or controls any copies of the minutes deposited with Glamorgan Archives, and identify any record of that transfer, its date, and any archival reference or destination known to the Council. I am separately pursuing the deposited volumes with Glamorgan Archives.

I assert a proprietary interest in Parcel A and the adjoining woodland, but I do not ask the Council to adjudicate title. If nothing is held after appropriate searches, please say so. If anything is withheld, please identify the statutory basis and reasons.
