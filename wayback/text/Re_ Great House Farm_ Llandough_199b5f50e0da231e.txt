Dear Mr Buckler,

Thank you for getting in touch and for sharing such a detailed account of
your family’s case concerning Great House Farm, Llandough. I’ve read
through your summary and understand the points you raise about the way the
courts treated BP’s different corporate entities.

From a legal perspective, however, it appears extremely unlikely that a
case dating back to 1987 could now be reopened. The rule you refer to — CPR
52.30 — only applies in truly exceptional circumstances, such as where
there is clear evidence of fraud, corruption, or a fundamental procedural
defect that undermined the fairness of the original hearing. A disagreement
over how the law was applied, or inconsistencies in reasoning, would not
usually be enough to meet that standard.

The other difficulty is the time that has passed. Nearly forty years on,
the courts place very strong emphasis on finality of litigation,
particularly in property cases. Land ownership and related interests will
almost certainly have changed hands since then, and the law is reluctant to
disturb settled titles unless there is absolute proof of fraud or
concealment.

While section 32 of the Limitation Act 1980 allows some exceptions where
facts were deliberately hidden, this applies only if there was intentional
concealment by a party, not simply later discovery of information that
might cast doubt on the judgment.

For those reasons, any attempt to reopen the 1987 decision or pursue
rectification of title would almost certainly be refused by the courts.

I appreciate the time and research you’ve put into this and the historical
importance it holds for your family, but in practical terms the matter is
considered legally closed.

With best wishes,
Tom Sinclair
