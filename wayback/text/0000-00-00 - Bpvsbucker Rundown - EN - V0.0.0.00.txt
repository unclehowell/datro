Western Ground Rents Ltd was a Cardiff-based ground rent company that operated one of the most extensive and controversial property portfolios in South Wales during the mid-twentieth century. The company's business model centered on acquiring freehold reversions from original property developer (Ideal Homes Wales Ltd (now part of Persimmon Homes)), thereby becoming an intermediary landlord that collected ground rents from leasehold occupiers while maintaining ultimate ownership of the underlying land. This operational approach generated substantial profits—shareholders could recover their capital more than twice within three years through dividends of 50% (1951–52, 1952–53), 25% (1953–56), a capital distribution equivalent to 60% (1956–57), and 75% (1957, 1958)—but also attracted intense criticism from tenants, politicians, and housing reform advocates .
The company's litigation strategy in the early 1960s reflected systematic enforcement of ground rent claims. Rather than accepting negotiated settlements, Western Ground Rents pursued aggressive legal action to recover possession from tenants in arrears. The 1962 action against Mr. Frederick Buckler and Mrs. Buckler (sued as Mrs. Williams) for possession of Great House Farm must be understood within this broader pattern of enforcement activity, with the Cardiff County Court serving as the primary venue for the company's numerous possession claims

--- DISCREPANCY NOTES ON EXISTING CONTENT ---

[A] LINE 3 — "1215 – Great House Farm House was built"
    DISCREPANCY: Multiple sources (Wikipedia, RCAHMW, People's Collection
    Wales) attribute the construction to Sir Mathew Cradock (died 1531),
    not 1215. The house is described as "Late C15 or early C16 hall house"
    in the British Listed Buildings entry (Grade II, ref 15765).
    CORRECTION: c.1500–1530 construction date is more defensible than 1215.

[B] LINE 16 — "1667 – Williams family in occupation (family claim)"
    DISCREPANCY: This is family oral tradition supported by a 1980s newspaper
    article quoting Mrs Mary Williams. There is NO surviving registered title
    or conveyance from 1667. The Bute Estate Records (NLW D 219) show the
    Herbert/Pembroke/Bute estate treating itself as manorial lord through at
    least 1829. The 1667 date should be treated as "claimed possession" not
    "proved ownership."

[C] LINE 164 — "1935–39 - Says online Mr. William Dies around this time"
    DISCREPANCY: This contradicts the BP v Buckler judgment itself, which
    states Mr Williams and his wife Mrs Esther Williams were "also living
    there in 1955, but they died." If Mr Williams was born circa 1860-1870
    (to be "elderly" by the mid-1930s and to have a daughter Mary who was an
    adult by 1916), death in 1955 at age ~85 is consistent. The "1935-39"
    death date appears to be an error.

[D] LINE 170 — "This 1938 sale might be the moment the Paper Title was severed"
    DISCREPANCY: The BP v Buckler judgment (paragraph 36) states: "In 1938
    the reversion on that tenancy was conveyed to a company called Western
    Ground Rents Ltd." This was NOT a sale of the farm — it was the sale of
    the REVERSION on the existing yearly tenancy. The Williams/Buckler
    family continued in physical occupation. The 1938 Stephenson & Alexander
    sale (DSA/6/1158) may reference a different transaction. These two events
    may be the same or different — archival verification needed.

[E] LINE 303 — "October 1964: The agricultural tenancy officially came to an end"
    DISCREPANCY: The court judgment timeline shows: Feb 1916 tenancy to
    Williams; Williams fell into arrears mid-1930s and late 1940s; new yearly
    tenancy granted Feb 2, 1949 to Frederick Buckler; protected by
    Agricultural Holdings Act; notice to quit expiring Feb 2, 1955.
    The "October 1964" date has no basis in the court records.

[F] LINE 227-228 — Western Ground Rents mentions "Ideal Homes Wales Ltd"
    DISCREPANCY: The 1987 Court of Appeal judgment does NOT mention Ideal
    Homes. WGR is described simply as the company to which "the reversion on
    that tenancy was conveyed" in 1938. The "Ideal Homes" reference is
    unsupported.

[G] LINE 474-477 — "Ghost License" argument
    NOTE: The court did NOT find that the licence expired upon Mrs Williams'
    death in 1983. Dillon LJ held that a licence had been granted to
    "Mrs Buckler" which defeated the adverse possession claim. The "Ghost
    License" framing is a rhetorical argument, not an accepted legal finding.

[H] LINE 1014 — "purchased in 1670 by Lord Cardiff"
    DISCREPANCY: The accepted history is that John, Lord Mountstuart married
    Charlotte Windsor in 1766 and the Bute family acquired the Glamorgan
    estates then. The 1670 date in the Godfrey Francis letter may refer to
    a different transaction or may be an error.

[I] LINE 249-250 — DXJF/64/30-31 filed under Corbett Family of Cogan Pill
    NOTE: These items are sketches by Mrs. Nancy Buckler of Great House Farm,
    Llandough. That they are filed under the Corbett collection suggests a
    custodial relationship worth investigating, but the "squatter vs.
    historical authority" argument based on this is speculative.

[J] LINE 142 — "In 1790 the building was purchased by Lord Bute"
    VERIFIED: Wikipedia, British Listed Buildings, and RCAHMW all confirm
    this. Aligns with accepted history.



--- MISSING EVIDENCE THAT SHOULD BE APPENDED ---

[K] THE PEOPLE'S COLLECTION WALES PHOTOGRAPH (CRITICAL)
    Reference: https://www.peoplescollection.wales/items/871416
    Description: "A black and white photograph of tree planting by John
    Williams (Grandfather) to prove land ownership at Great House Farm,
    Llandough."
    Significance: This is CRITICAL primary evidence. John Williams performed
    a ceremonial tree planting as physical proof of possession — a common
    Welsh folk practice where planted trees served as permanent markers of
    tenure. The tree planting appears to have occurred circa 1916, around
    the time the Marquis formalised the yearly agricultural tenancy.
    This photograph should have been entered as evidence in the 1987 case.

[L] ARCHIVAL HOLES — SPECIFIC GAPS IN THE BUTE ESTATE RECORDS FOR LLANDOUGH
    1. Manorial records (NLW GB 0210 BUTE): [1373]-1847.
       NOTHING for Llandough manorial court after 1847.
    2. Glamorgan estate rentals: 1728-1893.
       No rental records for Great House Farm after 1893.
    3. Cardiff estate rentals: 1842-1895. Gap after 1895.
    4. Estate correspondence: Mainly 1817-1863. Virtually nothing
       for post-1900 Llandough matters.
    5. Family settlements: [early 17th cent.]-1902. None after 1902.
    6. Family probate: 1612-1900. Nothing after 3rd Marquis's death.
    7. DBDT collection: Contains Llandough Street leases (DBDT58/1,
       58/2 — both 1883) but NOTHING on the Great House Farm tenancy
       of 1916.
    CONCLUSION: The documentary gap runs c.1895-1938 for the Llandough/
    Great House Farm area. The 1916 tenancy to John Williams falls in
    the dead centre of this gap.
