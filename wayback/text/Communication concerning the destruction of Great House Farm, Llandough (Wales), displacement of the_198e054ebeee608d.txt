This was not just an eviction — it’s a layered heritage injustice:

Displacement of an indigenous family (Bucklers) under imposed UK

Erasure of cultural memory (Roman burials, child graves, farming heritage).

Suppression of scientific history (Marconi’s stay, linked to the invention

II. Facts of the Case

1. Eviction and Demolition (1987):

The Buckler family were evicted from Great House Farm following litigation
brought by BP, supported by UK authorities.

The farm was demolished against the wishes of the local community.

My family maintained we always owned it. I believe the whole legal case was
a kangaroo court. And my family and the public were outsmarted. I believe
evidence exists proving my family actually did become equitable title
holders of the farm. And did own it. We were not squatters living rent free
for decades as was suggested in the court documents. Which is itself an
absurd claim.

2. Imposed Nationality:

At the time of eviction, Mrs. Buckler was a Welsh national with ancestral
ties to the land.

She did not freely consent to become a UK citizen; British citizenship was
imposed under the British Nationality Act 1948 and 1981.

This imposition extinguished her indigenous status in law, but not in fact.

The court proceedings never considered her as an indigenous Welsh person,
nor her ancestral rights to the land.


3. Cultural & Scientific Significance:

Great House Farm is historically significant as a residence of Guglielmo
Marconi during his pioneering experiments leading to the invention of radio.

The site also contained Roman burials and child graves, representing unique
archaeological heritage.

Despite this, no plaque or marker exists on the new development to
acknowledge Marconi’s presence or the heritage of the land.



4. Community Opposition:

Local residents petitioned against the demolition of the farm in 1987.

Objections raised by the community were disregarded.

The demolition destroyed not only a building, but also the intangible
cultural heritage of the community’s relationship with the farm and its
history.


CADW refused to list the building as protected.

III. Grounds for Complaint

Violation of the 1970 UNESCO Convention: The destruction and concealment of
cultural property (Roman burials, graves, artifacts) constitutes illicit
removal and suppression of heritage.

Violation of the 1972 World Heritage Convention: Sites of outstanding
universal value (Marconi’s residence, archaeological burials) were
demolished without recognition or protection.

Violation of Indigenous/Minority Rights: The Buckler family, as indigenous
Welsh nationals, were displaced through the imposition of foreign
nationality law and denied ancestral rights to their land.

Violation of Intangible Heritage (2003 Convention): The petitions and
objections of the community are part of cultural memory, yet no recognition
or preservation exists.
