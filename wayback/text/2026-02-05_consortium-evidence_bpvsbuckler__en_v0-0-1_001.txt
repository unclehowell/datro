*Kate Johal,*

I appreciate your prompt reply and thank you. I assure you I'm not out to
waste your departments or anyone's time. I just finished 22 years in Oath
in 2024 as a public servant, employed at the Vale of Glamorgan Council,
under the Welsh Government. I'm not only bona fine, I left my military
career beforehand - with honours. I truly believe I have the most
befitting case, that I understand your department specifically exists to
handle.

Is it not PACAC's role to ensure that the *machinery of the state*—the
courts, the Land Registry, and planning authorities—operates with
transparency and protects the constitutional rights of citizens against
administrative abuse? My case relates specifically to *maladministration*
and *constitutional overreach*: it involves the judicial "erasure" of a
320-year ancestral lineage through the tactical misnaming of Mary Williams
to "Mrs. Buckler", the administrative suppression of a known national
heritage site (the 1888 Roman find) to facilitate demolition permits, and
the suspicious disappearance of public title records from state-run
libraries. By focusing on how these public institutions failed to maintain
accurate records and fair procedures, I am  presenting a case of *systemic
failure in public administration*, which is the core reason I
understand your Committee to exist.

I must formally contest the assertion that this matter falls outside the
Committee’s remit. While the origin of this case involves a dispute with a
private entity (BP), the core of my grievance concerns *constitutional
issues* and the *quality of administration provided by government-funded
bodies*, specifically regarding the protection of national heritage and the
integrity of land registration.

I ask the Committee to review this matter under the following three pillars
of your mandate:

*1. Constitutional Issues: The Erasure of Customary and Indigenous Title* The
1987 Court of Appeal ruling (*BP Properties Ltd v Buckler*) created a
significant legal precedent regarding "unilateral licenses". My family
asserts that the administration of justice in this case allowed for the
tactical erasure of the "Williams" identity—a lineage with de facto
possession dating back to 1667—to facilitate a corporate land grab. The use
of the judicial system to overwrite centuries of documented continuity with
a "paper title" is a constitutional matter regarding the rights of citizens
against aggressive corporate-state interests.

*2. Standards of Administration: Suppression of Heritage by Public Bodies*
The Committee examines the quality of administration. My investigation
reveals that:

   -

   *Archaeological Suppression:* Evidence of a Roman soldier was discovered
   in 1888, providing "actual notice" of the site's significance. This was
   reportedly suppressed or ignored by planning authorities and the
   Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust (GGAT) to allow for demolition.

   -

   *Misrepresentation in Planning:* The 13th-century Great House Farm was
   administratively classified as a "dilapidated utility building" to bypass
   heritage protections.
   -

   *Missing Records:* Vital title evidence and Bute Estate deeds
   disappeared from the public library system—a failure of public record
   administration.


*3. Accountability of the Land Registry and Parliamentary Oversight* The
registration of this land under BP’s title occurred during a period of
transition in Welsh land law (c. 1982). If public registries accepted title
without disclosing active disputes or the presence of a known national
monument (the Roman/Medieval site), this represents a failure in the
"standards of administration" by the Civil Service. Furthermore, this
matter was raised in the House of Commons by MP Ted Rowlands in 1980, yet
the administrative failures continued unabated.

*Conclusion* I am not asking the Committee to act as a court of appeal, but
to investigate the *systemic administrative failures* that allowed a
historically significant site and a family's ancestral rights to be
"buried" through procedural maneuvers and the suppression of evidence.

If the Committee exists to ensure the Civil Service and constitutional
frameworks protect the public interest, then the demolition of an
800-year-old heritage site under a cloud of administrative "silence" must
be within your purview.


I look forward to your reconsidered response.


Sincerely,


Sion Buckler

> Dear Committee Members,
>
> I am writing to draw your attention to a historical land matter which I
> believe may illustrate potential vulnerabilities in public administration,
> record-keeping, and the governance of land registration. My purpose is
> solely to provide factual information for consideration, rather than to
> request remedial action.
>
> The matter concerns land formerly known as *Great House Farm, Llandough,
> Vale of Glamorgan*, later developed for residential use and historically
> associated with *BP v Buckler (Court of Appeal, 1987)*.
>
> *Chronology of relevant events (as reflected in public records):*
>
>    - *July 1974:* Court proceedings involving my grandmother were
>    adjourned without determination of ownership.
>    - *31 October 1974:* A subsequent hearing did not proceed
>    substantively, following correspondence received from BP entities that same
>    day. No judgment on ownership was issued.
>    - Throughout this period, my grandmother was not included in dealings
>    concerning the land, while the opposing party continued to assert ownership.
>    - *1982:* The land was registered in the name of BP entities, despite
>    unresolved competing claims and no final judicial adjudication.
>    - *1983:* My grandmother passed away after the registration, with
>    ownership never conclusively adjudicated.
>    - *1987 Court of Appeal:* The record reflects an apparent
>    inconsistency in the treatment of correspondence and corporate entities.
>    Specifically:
>
>
>    - The court noted that my grandmother had replied to correspondence on
>       31 October, asserting ownership.
>       - The reply was treated as directed to the “wrong” corporation (BP
>       Pensions Trust Ltd rather than BP Properties Ltd).
>       - In a later appeal by my father, the same judge acknowledged that
>       both BP entities were effectively treated as the same for the purposes of
>       the ruling.
>       - These circumstances resulted in the court upholding the opposing
>       party’s registration despite prior correspondence demonstrating an
>       assertion of ownership.
>
>
>    - Following these proceedings, my father’s application to the *European
>    Court of Human Rights* was blocked.
>    - The family home was demolished, preventing re-entry, even for the
>    retrieval of personal belongings.
>    - Despite its historical significance and a petition to protect it,
>    the property was not preserved. The house had prior heritage interest,
>    including documented connections to Marconi.
>    - *1991:* My father passed away at just 41.
>    - *1994:* The land was subsequently excavated, with items of
>    archaeological interest removed.
>
> Taken together, these events demonstrate a situation in which *land
> registration and subsequent reliance by public bodies proceeded without
> definitive adjudication of ownership*, raising questions regarding
> administrative safeguards, procedural coherence, and record integrity.
>
> I am submitting this information purely for institutional awareness. I am *
> not making allegations of misconduct*, nor requesting investigation. I am
> able to provide a *concise factual timeline and references to public
> records* should it assist the Committee’s understanding.
