Simon Ghahary posted: The idea had left the living room.

Now it needed to prove itself.

I'd moved back to Brighton and got a meeting with John Holland, owner of the Great Escape — a nightclub directly opposite the Palace Pier. He wanted to refit the venue. I brought the HousePods and convinced him they were the way forward.

But I'd been thinking about something bigger for Brighton.
I was obsessed with the legendary New York club sound systems. David Mancuso's Loft. Larry Levan's Paradise Garage. The folklore around how those systems were positioned, how the frequencies were split, and how sound could transform a space into something almost sacred. These weren't just nightclubs. They were places where sound did something to people that they couldn't explain.

That's what I wanted to build. Our team helped me realize it. 

The club size demanded a more powerful Pod. I evolved the original form — extending the design to accommodate a second driver and sculpting the bottom from the spine. It suited the application; the Pods would be mounted higher, more exposed and working harder. That design became the TechnoPod.

For the bass, we excavated into the chalk cliff face behind the club and built a wall of it. Twenty-four B&W 801 drivers. The same drivers used to master orchestra scores at Abbey Road. There are two in a standard pair. We had twenty-four. My arms vibrated for days after. This must have been the biggest subwoofer built to date.

We had nine days to finish everything. It was all hands on deck: Dave Roberts and Marc Weaver, busy building pods. We all pitched in, no rest.

I remember being there until the very last moment — covered in dust, running on no sleep, alongside Martin De Saulles and Laurence Dickie. Outside, a queue of club members and VIPs was already building. We finished at 10.26 pm. The club was supposed to open at 10.

Watching people flood in as the system came to life was something I'll never forget.

The opening night of Brighton's Escape Club was baptized by one of the founders of house music, producer and DJ legend Todd Terry. The Face called it a "fierce sound."

It was. But more than that — it was proof.

The living room idea could fill a room of five hundred people and make them feel it in their bones.

That proof opened the next door.
