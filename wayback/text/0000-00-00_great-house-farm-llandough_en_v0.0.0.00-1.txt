Good Sirs,

I'm writing to you both as elected representatives because, frankly, the
institutional dysfunction I am uncovering is becoming quite extraordinary.

My family — including the last remaining members of my dispossessed Welsh
family in South Wales — have spent 38 years living with the consequences of
what happened to our home and land. They have been frightened into silence,
while the institutions responsible for safeguarding justice, heritage and
public accountability appear, time and again, to have preferred discretion,
obfuscation and exquisitely worded emails to the rather unfashionable
concept of actually answering the questions.

I have now spent considerable time reconstructing what happened. The deeper
I dig, the more the story appears to unravel.

We were not merely separated from a house. We were separated from ancestral
land, family history and heritage. And now the woodland on that land is
being cleared for a development of 133 homes.

One might reasonably ask: *whose history is being developed over, and whose
history is being erased?*

Whatever one's political position, the fact remains that possession being
awarded through historical court proceedings does not magically make every
underlying question of title, evidence, procedure or institutional conduct
disappear. A court order is not an archaeological dig, a title
investigation, or a certificate of immaculate institutional behaviour.

I attach a summary of my grievances concerning CADW, particularly its
historic involvement in this matter. I also attach a mock court application
which I have been developing alongside AI-assisted legal research, not as a
substitute for legal advice, but as a means of stress-testing the evidence
and identifying the questions that require proper legal determination.

I hope you will read them carefully.
