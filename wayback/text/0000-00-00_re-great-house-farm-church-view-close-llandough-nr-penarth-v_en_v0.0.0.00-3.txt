Hi Mike,

Appreciate the reply.

I kindly request an internal review of the response provided under ATISN
27021.

I am grateful for the release of the two inspection photographs dated 29
July 1988. However, I am not satisfied that the searches undertaken were
sufficient to establish that no other relevant records are held or can be
accounted for.

The response states that Great House Farm was visited and assessed for
listing by Cadw inspectors, but that the only surviving records are two
photographs. It further states that any associated records may have been
transferred to the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments
of Wales or destroyed under the Public Records Act 1958.

I respectfully submit that this conclusion appears difficult to reconcile
with the contemporary evidence.

Contemporary newspaper reports from December 1988 record that:

Cadw was actively considering emergency listing ("spot-listing") procedures
in relation to Great House Farm;

Cadw inspectors visited the site;
Cadw assessed the architectural and archaeological significance of the
property;
Cadw communicated a decision that the building did not warrant statutory
protection.

These activities would ordinarily be expected to generate records such as
inspection notes, recommendations, assessments, correspondence, memoranda,
listing files, briefing notes, internal communications, or decision records.

In addition, a surviving family witness who was present during the
inspection recalls that a written assessment was produced and supplied to
the family. The witness recalls that the assessment concluded that,
although the site was old, the building had undergone substantial
alteration over the centuries and was therefore not considered sufficiently
significant for listing.

This recollection appears broadly consistent with the reasoning attributed
to Cadw in contemporary press reports.
I therefore request clarification of the following:

Were searches undertaken for inspection notes, site notes, assessment
reports, recommendations, listing files, briefing papers, internal
memoranda, correspondence, and decision records relating to Great House
Farm?
Were accession records, transfer schedules, disposal schedules, or archive
catalogues searched to determine whether such records once existed?

If records were transferred to RCAHMW, please identify any transfer
references, accession numbers, file references, or other metadata recording
that transfer.
If records were destroyed, please identify the disposal authority,
retention schedule, and date of destruction.

If no written assessment, recommendation, report, or decision record ever
existed, please explain how Cadw reached and communicated conclusions
regarding the architectural and archaeological significance of the property.

I am not seeking reconsideration of the 1988 listing decision itself.
Rather, I seek clarification regarding the apparent absence of records
relating to a matter that was publicly reported at the time as being under
active consideration by Cadw and which resulted in a communicated decision.

Thank you for undertaking an internal review.

Yours faithfully,

Sion Buckler (Ret'd Sergenant)
Cyber & Electronic Warfare Professional
Life Member of the Royal Signals
