VALE OF GLAMORGAN BOROUGH COUNCIL
Town and Country Planning Act 1971 / Town and Country Planning General Development Order 1988
OUTLINE PLANNING PERMISSION — Application No. 89/01396/OUT
Agent: Debenham Tewson Ltd., 3, Castle Street, Cardiff. CF1 2RJ
Applicant: BP Properties Ltd., Britannic House, Moor Lane, London. EC2Y 9BU
Residential development including new access road. at Land at Great House Farm, Leckwith Road, Llandough, Penarth

The Council in pursuance of its powers under the above mentioned Act and Order hereby GRANTS OUTLINE PERMISSION for the carrying out of the proposed development as described above and in accordance with the application and plans received by the Council on 8th November, 1989 subject to the following condition(s):

1. Approval of the details of the siting, design and external appearance of the building(s), the means of access thereto and the landscaping of the site (hereinafter called "the reserved matters") shall be submitted to and approved by the Local Planning Authority before any development is commenced.
2. Application for approval of the reserved matters hereinbefore referred to must be made not later than the expiration of three years beginning with the date of this permission.
3. The development to which this permission relates must be begun not later than whichever is the later of the following dates: (a) The expiration of five years from the date of this permission. (b) The expiration of two years from the date of the final approval of the reserved matters or, in the case of approval on different dates the final approval of the last such matters to be approved.
4. All structures, including proposed dwellings, garages and other buildings sited within the curtilages of individual dwellings, shall be erected on the level areas of land and no structures shall be erected on the steep sloped or wooded areas of the site.
5. The detail plans to be submitted shall provide for an area of 0.25 acres, located adjacent to Leckwith Road and the boundary with St. Dochdwys Church, to be laid out and Landscaped as a village green/amenity area prior to the development hereby approved being brought into beneficial use.
6. Vehicular access to the development, with sightlines of 4.5m x 60m shall be provided at the north western edge of the site onto the A4055 Leckwith Road.
7. Any estate roads for the development shall be a minimum of 5.5m in width with a minimum of 1.8m wide footways and 10.5m kerb radii at its junction with the A4055.
8. The proposed development shall not infringe upon or obstruct the existing public right of way which runs along the north western boundary over the site.
9. A landscape scheme for the site showing the trees and shrubs to be retained and further planting, the details of paved areas, verges and other open spaces, shall be submitted to and approved by the Local Planning Authority before any work commences on site. The scheme shall be implemented not later than the first planting season after and thereafter maintained and replaced as necessary for a period of 5 years to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority.
10. Before the commencement of the development hereby permitted, a scheme showing the means by which foul sewage and surface water run off are to be catered for shall be be submitted to and approved in writing by the Local Planning Authority.
11. Details of proposed finished floor levels in relation to the existing ground levels and the finished levels of the site, including cross sections, shall be submitted to and agreed in writing, with the Planning Authority before development commences.
12. The detail plans shall provide for parking facilities to meet the standards of the Local Planning Authority.
13. The proposed dwellings shall be provided with private garden amenity space based on a minimum standard of 200 sq. ft. per habitable room.
14. The proposed dwelling(s) shall be designed so that no main windows of habitable rooms shall directly overlook the main windows of habitable rooms of other dwellings at a distance of less than 70 feet.
15. The existing stone wall fronting the site, where not directly affected by building operations or the proposed access, shall be retained and made good, in accordance with a scheme to be agreed in writing with the Local Planning Authority, before the development hereby approved is occupied.
16. The developer shall afford access at all reasonable times to any archaeologist nominated by the Local Planning Authority, and shall allow him/her to observe the excavations and record items of interest and finds.
17. No development shall take place until a field evaluation survey and archaeological assessment report has been carried out by a qualified archaeologist to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority. This work shall be carried out within three months of the date of this consent.
18. The detail plans to be submitted shall pay full regard to the results and findings of the archaeological investigations referred to in condition 17 above.

The reason(s) for the Council's decision to grant permission for the proposed development, subject to compliance with the condition(s) hereinbefore specified are:

1. To comply with the requirement of Section 42 of the Town and Country Planning Act, 1971.
2. To comply with the requirements of Section 42 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1971.
3. To comply with the requirements of Section 42 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1971.
4. To ensure a satisfactory form of development.
5. To retain and enhance the existing registered village green.
6. In the interests of highway safety.
7. In the interests of highway safety.
8. To ensure that no obstruction takes place on the public right of way.
9. To safeguard local visual amenities.
10. To ensure the satisfactory drainage of the site.
11. To ensure a satisfactory form of development.
12. To provide satisfactory off street parking facilities.
13. To ensure the provision of satisfactory private amenity space.
14. To safeguard the privacy of adjoining occupiers.
15. To safeguard local visual amenities.
16. To enable satisfactory archaeological investigation.
17. To enable satisfactory archaeological investigation.
18. To ensure that the future development of the site has regard to the results of the archaeological investigations.

Dated: 13th March, 1990
[signature] Chief Executive

- 2: outline, all matters reserved; "A plan has nevertheless been submitted indicating a form of layout with 18 dwellings and access to Leckwith Rd."
- 3 Planning history: "8996/3337 13 No houses. Same site. Approved conditionally 25th July 1972".
- 4 Consultations (pp.1–2): Llandough C.C. — "no objection … but wish to point out that part of the land in question is registered village green" (register plan attached). Director of Engineering & Building Services — foul sewer only; surface water elsewhere. National Rivers Authority and County Engineer — typed sheets "attached" (not in this set). Cardiff Bay Development Corporation — no objection; hopes the north-west slope trees and the public right of way are retained. "The Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust has enclosed a detailed document with their comments and recommendations, and has requested that the information be conveyed to Members of Committee. This is attached as Appendix A to this report. Following discussions with the Trust, further comments were received on the 8th February 1990 and a copy of these are attached as Appendix 'B'." Welsh Water — liaise on foul drainage; no connection to the Eastern District pumping main.
- 5 Representations (p.2): "Two letters have been received from the Barry Preservation Society and Civic Trust and a local County Councillor (who is a member of the Glamorgan Gwent Archaeological Trust) making representations in respect of this application", Appendices C and D. The applicants' agents answered GGAT's report: Appendix E.
- 6 Report (p.2): the land is mostly allocated for housing in the East Vale Local Plan (Policy H.2). "The information supporting the policy states that buildings on the site should be retained, however they were unfortunately demolished a little while ago." Site includes a registered village green, a public right of way, and a small TPO area. "Following the correspondence between myself, the Applicants' Agents and the Glamorgan Gwent Archaeological Trust Ltd., if the Council be minded to approve this application, I would suggest that a condition should be imposed which is the standard condition for this situation recommended by Appendix B to Welsh Office Circular 1/85."
- 8 Recommendation (pp.3–4): "Approve", 16 numbered conditions in draft. Draft condition 4 ties structures to level ground; condition 5 the village green ("0.1 ha (0.25 acre)"); the archaeology entry is a single coded condition, "A132 (Archaeological con[dition])". Appendices A–E are not in this set.

EV-0113  comments_from_archiology.pdf  GGAT comments and recommendations (5 pages; written June 1989 – February 1990)
Scan: https://wayback.bucklerfamily.estate/pdf/planning/1989-10-00%20-%20GGAT%20comments%2089-01396-OUT%20Great%20House%20Farm%20Llandough%20-%20EN%20-%20V0.0.0.00.pdf
======================================================================
Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust Ltd, Ferryside Warehouse, Bath Lane, Swansea. Undated. It cites the County Structure Plan "(June 1989)" and the IFA Directory of Members "1988-9", and the officer's report attaches it as Appendix A, so it was written between June 1989 and early February 1990 (GGAT's further comments of 8 Feb 1990 are a separate Appendix B, not in this set). The wayback date "1988" is wrong.
- Cover (p.1): "Land at Great House Farm, Llandough. Planning Application 89/01396/OUT. Comments and recommendations by the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust Ltd". Back cover photo caption (p.5): "St Dochdwy's church and the former Ty-Mawr farm buildings".
- 2.1 (p.2): "The churchyard is almost certainly on the site of an early christian monastic foundation which may have been founded as early as 650 AD; the cross shaft near the church dates to the tenth or eleventh century, and is of a scale and style that would mark an important religious site."
- 2.2 (p.2): "In 1979 during the course of the Wales and West Housing Association Development to the south of the church, emergency archaeological excavation revealed evidence of high quality. In summary part of an Iron Age farmstead was located, together with a Roman villa of high status, a dark age cemetery, a medieval monastic grange belonging to Tewkesbury Abbey and a late medieval village and farm. It is important to note that there was no visible trace of the farmstead, villa, cemetery or grange prior to the commencement of development at this site. Although valuable information was recovered during the excavation, the circumstances of discovery were unsatisfactory to both archaeological interests and those of the developer. It would not be in the interests of archaeologists, developers or the local community were such a situation to arise again."
- 2.3 (p.2): "At present our information on the Great House Farm area is limited to the records of the recently demolished property which was listed as a 'county treasure'. There is a possibility that archaeological deposits relating to earlier activity on this site may survive, and we believe that this possibility should be tested by a suitably qualified archaeologist." Trial work by machine under supervision; "investigation and report could be completed within a period of two weeks at a cost of £2,000."
- 3 (p.3): quotes the South Glamorgan Structure Plan (June 1989) and East Vale Local Plan; Welsh Office Circular 61/81 s.26 ("the desirability of preserving the monument and its setting is a material consideration", "whether scheduled or unscheduled"); SI 1988 No 1812 art.4 (power to direct further information); Circular 1/85 Appendix B s.3. 3.4: "even an unscheduled monument is a 'material consideration'"; "We therefore recommend that the application should not be determined until BP Properties have provided an archaeological assessment report prepared by a qualified archaeologist."
- 3.5 (p.4): GGAT endorses the British Archaeologists and Developers Liaison Group Code of Practice. "Negotiations concerning archaeological assessment of this site were undertaken with BP Properties via their agents Debenham Tewson & Chinnocks, prior to the current application for planning permission. Unfortunately no agreement was reached although the Llandough Community Council offered £100 towards the cost of an evaluation. BP Properties are not strictu sensu the developers as they wish to sell on the land with outline planning permission."
- 4.1–4.3 (p.4): assessment and report needed; "the Local Planning Authority should not determine this application until an archaeological assessment report prepared by a qualified archaeologist has been provided by BP Properties"; the Code of Practice "should be used to provide the means by which BP Properties can acquire the necessary archaeological information".
- Appendix (p.4): "Extract from letter to J Maitland Evans 1 October 1982 from GGAT: 'Ty Mawr Farm (H2.1) is designated as a three acre residential site. … Recent excavations at Llandough by the Trust revealed early occupation, part of the villa and medieval grange buildings. There remains a possibility that occupation continues to the north of the church; provision should be made, therefore, for thorough investigative work prior to the development.'"
- Not mentioned: Cadw, scheduling of this site, burials north of the church, preservation in situ, the Buckler family, the 1987 case.
