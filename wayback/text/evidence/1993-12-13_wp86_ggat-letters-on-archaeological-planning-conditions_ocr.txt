Dear Sir,
Re: Great House Farm, Llandough

With regards to the above development, I have today received from
Ideal Homes, a detailed site plan showing the proposed layout of
the development, access roads, houses, etc.

I have studied the plan most carefully and I am of the opinion
that little attempt has been made at the detailed design stage to
take account of the known and significant archaeological resource
of this site, which was fully demonstrated in the field evalua-
tion of 1990.

Accordingly, I cannot confirm that the detailed plans, which were
submitted to yourselves by Ideal Homes have paid regard to the
results and findings of the archaeological investigations, i.e.
in the report dated August 1990. Unfortunately, therefore,
condition 18 has not been fulfilled and cannot be discharged by
your Authority.

It is clear that should the development proceed unchecked,
significant and rare Early Medieval archaeology, including human
skeletal remains (please note the provisions of the Disused
Burial Grounds (Amendment) Act 1981) will be needlessly or
thoughtlessly destroyed.

Accordingly, I recommend that the developer should, as a matter
of urgency, now seek professional archaeological advice in order
to draw together an archaeological mitigation strategy, which
will effectively protect the archaeological resource (either in
situ or by record), ensure the fulfillment of planning condition
18 and enable the development to proceed. I will, of course, be
pleased to comment on the mitigation strategy on your behalf in
due course. If you require any clarification or further

information, please do not hesitate to contact me.

Thank you for consulting us on this application. We are most grateful for this
opportunity to comment.

We have checked the area of the proposed development against the County Sites
and Monuments Record, which is held and curated by this Trust. From the
information available to us we can now advise that the site has already been
subject to an archaeological assessment, the results of which have demonstrated
that significant archaeological material is known to exist on the site.
Brief details of the known archaeological interest of this site are
given below.

Archaeological Interest

An archaeological assessment, ‘Land at Great House Farm, Llandough'
(August 1990), in the form of a field evaluation, demonstrated conclusively
that significant archaeological deposits exist within the proposed development
area, though the potential varies over the site. Deposits of medieval
and possibly earlier date have been confirmed, including a quantity of
human bone. In general, the archaeological material of highest potential
exists in a broad band in the central southern area of the site, adjacent
to the Church of St Dochdwy.

Recommendation

Because of the fact that the proposed development area contains archaeological
material of known significance, we would urge most strongly that the applicant
should consider mitigatory measures to protect the known archaeological
interests. This could include excavation in order to ‘preserve by record’
the archaeological potential. Such work should be carried out in advance
of development in the areas of high archaeological sensitivity.

This recommendation is made following the guidance of the recently-issued
Welsh Office Planning Policy Guidance note (PPG 16), ‘Archaeology and Planning’.

1.0 INTRODUCTION

1.1 It is the intention of Ideal Homes Ltd to develop Great House Farm,
Llandough for residential purposes. In order to protect and preserve the
known and significant archaeological remains including Early Medieval
burials, The Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust recommended that an
archaeological mitigation strategy should be devised. this however
proved impossible and consequently the need to preserve the archaeologi-
cal remains "by record" has become a requirement.

This brief has been prepared by the Curatorial Section of the Glamorgan-
Gwent Archaeological Trust, the archaeological advisors to Vale of
Glamorgan Borough Council, to ensure that a suitable programme of ar-
chaeological investigation is devised to ensure full archaeological
excavation. post-excavation analysis and publication.

2.0 ARCHAEOLOGICAL INTEREST

The archaeological importance of the site was outlined in an archaeolo-
gical evaluation (GGAT 1990) and has been further highlighted in excava-
tion work by Cotswold Archaeological Trust.

3.0 PRESENT USE OF SITE AND AREA OF DETAILED ARCHAEOLOGICAL WORK

The site has been cleared of structures and there are no known re-
straints to excavation. The archaeological work will entail the excava-
tion of the entire extent of the burial ground and other related fea-
tures, within the proposed development area.

4.0 EXECUTION OF WORKS

3.1 The Vale of Glamorgan Borough Council will require that all ar-
chaeological work, as is required to preserve the archaeology ‘by
record’, will be executed in a proper and professional manner as defined
by the Institute of Field Archaeologists Code of Conduct and the Code of
Approved Practice for the Regulation in Field Archaeology, also English
Heritage’s ‘Management of Archaeology Projects’ (hereafter MAP2) docu-
ment.

5.0 THE PROJECT DESIGN

5.1 The archaeological contractor should undertake all the necessary
archaeological work in a logical and phased manner. The archaeological
contractor must prepare a Project Design on the lines given in MAP2,
which should be submitted to LPA for agreement prior to the commencement
of the work.

5.2 The Project Design Brief should consist of an Excavation Research
Design (Stage 1), a Post-Excavation Assessment Design (Stage 2) and a
Post-Excavation Research Design (Stage 3)- The archaeological contrac—
tor should prepare the first two of these documents in detail prior to
any award of contract and the latter immediately on the completion of
the excavation. The Project Design will be accompanied by detailed cost
estimates for the excavation stages and outline costs for the post-
excavation assessment and research designs. Detailed costs for stages 2
and 3 will be provided immediately on the completion of the preceding
excavation stage.

6.0 METHODOLOGY

6.1 The archaeological contractor will be required to devise an excava-
tion strategy to examine and record the evidence for all human and human
derived activity on the site in such a way to give a maximum information
return of the cultural heritage resources that are present and will be
destroyed by the development. All archaeological material unaffected by
the development must remain intact and protected.

6.2 As part of the excavation strategy the archaeological contractor
will be expected to devise an appropriate sampling strategy that will
allow sufficient information to be extracted about the site, its func-
tions, character and date. This will apply to the recovery of artefact-
ual, ecofactual and environmental data and, if necessary, to the selec-
tion of features for full or partial excavation and recording.

6.3 The archaeological contractor will be expected to excavate fully
and record in detail all the significant archaeological deposits. The
excavation areas may initially be excavated by machine (using a tooth-
less blade) to just above the archaeological horizon. All archaeologi-
cal and structural remains will thereafter be excavated by hand to the
natural deposits, which must be proven. All areas will be cleaned to an
appropriate standard to prove the presence, or absence, of archaeologi-
cal features and to determine their significance.

7.0 RECORDING

7.1 All archaeological contexts will be recorded using a continuous
numbered context system on pro-forma recording sheets.

7.2 Written, drawn and photographic records (b&w and colour slides) of
an appropriate level of detail will be maintained throughout the course
of the project. Plans (other than excavation location plans) will be at
a minimum scale of 1:20; section drawings will be at a minimum scale of
1:10; photographic records will be at a minimum 35mm format.

7.3 Drawn records will be related to Ordnance Survey datum and published
boundaries where appropriate.

7.4 All artifacts recovered during the project will be retained and be
related to the contexts from which they derived wherever possible. All
typologically distinct and closely datable finds will be recorded three-
dimensionally where possible.

7.5 Any human remains that are discovered must be excavated following
the procedures outlined in IFA Technical Paper 13 "Excavation and Post-
Excavation Treatment of Cremated and Inhumed Human Remains", and must
comply with the relevant Home Office regulations.

7.6 All human burials must be accurately planned and/or recorded using
photogrametric techniques.

7.7. Any features containing deposits of environmental or technological
significance will be sampled.

7.8 The requirements for the conservation of artifacts and samples will
be unpredictable until after the completion of the fieldwork. The ar-
chaeological contractor will ensure, however, that at least minimum
acceptable standards are achieved (the UK Institute of Conservation,s
"Guidelines for the Treatment of Finds from Archaeological Sites" should
be used as guidance).

8.0. MONITORING

8.1 The archaeological advisors to the LPA (the Curatorial Section of
the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust) will monitor the work, and it
is therefore recommended that the earliest possible notice be given, in
writing, before each stage of the work commences.

8.2 The archaeological advisors to the LPA will be responsible for
monitoring, to ensure compliance with the brief, until the deposition of
the site archive and finds (see 7.9). To facilitate this, the Specifica-
tion should include a projected timescale for the work.

8.3 No area will be back-filled until it has been inspected by the
LPA’s archaeological advisors, unless written permission is granted in
advance.

9.0 REPORTING

9.1 The report should be fully representative of the archaeological
information gained from the total excavation.

9.2 The report will contain at least one plan showing the Site Location
in respect of the local topography, as well as the position of all
excavated areas.

9.3 Where necessary, the report will also contain suitably selected
plans and sections of significant archaeological features.

9.4 Pottery will be analysed to the standards outlined in ‘Draft Guide-
lines for the Preparation of Pottery Archives’ as prepared by the Study
Group for Roman Pottery in consultation with the I.F.A. All other
material will be analysed following the advice given the ‘The Institute
of Field Archaeologists’: Guidelines for Finds Work’.

9.5 Provision should be made prior to the commencement of the project
for the publication of relevant archaeological finds and costs outlined
to carry out this work. Detailed costs should be submitted at Stage 3
of the Design Brief.

9.6 The completed project archive will be prepared in accordance with
the guidelines outlined in Appendix 3 of ‘Management of Archaeological
Projects’ (i.e. MAP2, after English Heritage, 1991).

9.7 An interim report of the work should be submitted to LPA within six
weeks of the completion of the fieldwork.

9.8 A summary report of the work should be submitted for publication to
a national journal (eg Archaeology in Wales) no later then one year
after the completion of the work.

9.9 The full report of the work, for publication, to a national journal
or other publishing body, should be submitted no later than twelve
months after the completion of the fieldwork.

9.10 Arrangements should be made with an appropriate organisation, such
as the National Museum of Wales or the Royal Commission on Ancient and
Historical Monuments (Wales) for the deposition of the paper and finds
the archives. No matter where the archive is finally deposited, a copy
of the report should be provided for the Regional Sites and Monuments
Record.

10.0 SUITABLE ARCHAEOLOGICAL CONTRACTOR

10.1 The work will be undertaken by the staff of a competent and profes-
sional archaeological body (hereafter the archaeological contractor).

10.2 The archaeological contractor will have considerable experience of
archaeological field evaluation, be preferably managed by a Member of

Dear Sir,

GREAT HOUSE FARM, LLANDOUGH

With reference to the above development and further to our
excellent meeting on 16 March 1994, I am now able to
comment further on the proposals to protect the known
archaeological resources of this significant site as
demonstrated by the field evaluation of August 1990.

Firstly, I do not understand the comment made in the
letter by Paul Vining of Wyn Thomas and Partners that
your approval of the Project Design is ‘not required
under the conditions of the planning permission’. I would
argue the contrary - that until the condition is properly
discharged and the protection of the archaeological resource
assured (in this case ‘protection by record') then your
Authority undoubtedly has the main responsibility for
the protection of the archaeology of this sensitive site,
which is enshrined in PPG 16, Section 14.

In general terms I welcome the initiatives of the Developer,
who has secured archaeological advice and an archaeological
contractor to carry out the necessary work on his behalf.

My initial reaction, however, must be that the Detailed
Project Design is a response to an identified need for
full archaeological excavation of part of the site in
advance of development. The preference would have been
to present a detailed archaeological mitigation strategy
which could potentially have minimised the amount of
expensive archaeological excavation required. This strategy
could perhaps have protected the most sensitive archaeo-
logical deposits in situ, whilst at the same time enabling
the permitted development to proceed.

Unfortunately the Developer has been unable to present such
a mitigation strategy and therefore the second best option
of archaeological excavation, post-excavation analysis and
publication, which invariably are more costly, has become

a requirement.

Ae regards the Project Design supplied by the Cotswold Archaeological
Trust, it is an adequate document, though admittedly such a Design
can only be as good as the brief originally supplied. I have no doubt
that the brief for this work, which was used in the tendering process,
should have been approved by the Vale of Glamorgan Borough Council
as the regulatory body, who alone can ensure the satisfactory discharging
of the planning conditions which it imposed.

On the Project Design itself, I will confine my comments to the following
main issues:

1. The Timetable
A site strip and excavation period of only ‘up to three weeks’
seems optimistic in my view, as stratified deposits and articulated

buried human remains, which may appear, are extremely time-consuming
to excavate and process.

2. Post-Excavation Analysis and Publication

It is my view that inadequate allowance of man-days and resources
has been made for the reporting stage of the project. The comment
that 'due to the limited area of investigation the results may
warrant summary reporting’ is unacceptable. The results of the
archaeological work may warrant more detailed post-excavation
analysis and publication. This alternative seems not to have
been catered for in the Project Design and as a consequence the
financial allocation may have to be increased.

3. Watching Brief

I am concerned that the low-intensity watching brief of 5 visits only
may be insufficient and a degree of flexibility will be required
in the event of important archaeological deposits being unexpectedly
uncovered.

4. Contingency

As a consequence of the comments in 1, 2 and 3 above, I recommend
that a contingency be considered to ensure the full excavation,
recording and publication of the area identified for excavation.

As the archaeological advisors to the Local Planning Authority we

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 This document sets out details of the project design by
Cotswold Archaeological Trust for the targeted recording (excava-
tion) at the site of Great House Farm, Llandough, Penarth.

1.2 It is based upon the brief supplied to CAT by Countryside
Planning & Management, and the conclusions of the evaluation
report by Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust.

2. STAFF

2.1 This project will be under the general supervision of G
Walker, Senior Project Officer, Cotswold Archaeological Trust. Mr
Walker has wide experience of performing, monitoring and managing
field work projects of different periods in Britain.

2.2 The staffing structure will be organised thus: the Senior
Project Officer will visit the excavation during the period of
fieldworks. Principal responsibility however will rest with the
Project Officer who will be on-site throughout the project. As
the precise timing of the fieldworks has yet to be determined it
is not possible to state at this time which officer will take
charge of the project, although this information will be supplied
prior to the commencement of fieldworks. All CAT officers are

4. ACADEMIC OBJECTIVES
4.1 The academic objectives of the project are:

4.1.1 To elucidate the nature of any medieval or earlier deposits
surviving within the excavation area.

4.1.2 To ascertain the nature of the human remains: in particular
their date, and whether they are in situ or a dumped deposit.
Their position outside the churchyard wall suggests they are
likely to be either comparatively early in date, or else more
recent (post-medieval) dumping.

5. METHODS

5.1 Open area excavation will. be undertaken on the area marked
on the plan. This has been designed to concentrate on areas
within the footprints of the proposed residential units. It also
aims to completely excavate those areas where human remains might
be expected. Analysis of geotechnical information indicates that
the stripping for road construction will not penetrate modern
deposits, except in one area where a nineteenth century building
has already destroyed any earlier deposits. A total area of ap-
proximately 0.lha will therefore be examined.

5.2 A watching brief will be conducted during the laying of the
sewer down the road line. A low intensity watching brief (maximum
5 visits) will be maintained over the rest of the development

area.

5.3 A mechanical excavator equipped with toothless grading bucket
will be used to remove modern overburden down onto the surface of
archaeological deposits. Excavation will then continue by hand.
Examination of buildings will concentrate on recovering the plan
and structural sequence. Some degree of sampling may need to be
instigated although a priority will be placed on obtaining de-
tails of the principal structural periods including the earliest.
All discrete features (post holes, pits) will be sampled by hand
excavation unless their common/repetitious nature suggests they
are unlikely to yield significant new information. All linear

===== page 18 of 43 =====
features (ditches) will as a minimum be hand sectioned. Priority
will be attached to features which yield sealed assemblages which
can be related to the structural sequence.

5.4 Due care will be taken to identify any deposits (of medieval
or earlier date) which may have environmental potential. Bulk
samples of up to 30 litres will be collected on site for subse-
quent flotation for carbonised remains. Mollusc samples to be

collected if appropriate.

5.5 A priority will be attached to the identification of deposits
which contain human remains, and the elucidation of their date
and origin. Appropriate samples will be taken for possible radio-
carbon dating, although this analysis will only be undertaken if
it is likely to yield closer chronological precision than that
obtainable from the ceramic evidence. All articulated burials
will be fully recorded prior to removal.

5.6 All recording will follow the Cotswold Archaeological Trust
field manual. Each context will be recorded on a pro-forma con-
text sheet by verbal and measured description; principal deposits
will be recorded by drawn plans and sections (normally at a scale
of 1:20). Photographs (black and white print; colour slide) will
be taken at intervals as the excavation progresses. All finds and
samples will be bagged separately with unique numbers related to
the context record.

5.7: Following completion of the excavation an ordered, indexed,
and internally consistent site archive will be compiled in ac-
cordance with the specification presented in Appendix 3, MAP2.
Once details of phasing have been worked out artefacts will be
dispatched to the appropriate specialists. The results of all
specialist studies will be integrated and assessed prior to the
completion of the site narrative discussion and description. A
short summary report will be prepared for immediate inclusion in
Archaeology in Wales and Cotswold Archaeological Trust Annual
Review. Following completion of the archive an assessment will be
made of the importance of the discoveries, and a programme of
research and analysis commensurate with the importance of the
remains devised. This will itemise the sequence of tasks to be
performed; their duration, and the critical project path. It is
anticipated that due to the limited area of investigation the
results may warrant summary reporting, with referencing back to
the archive. It is likely that such a report (of possibly compar-
atively short length) will be submitted for publication in an
appropriate journal.

5.8 An allowance of 49 man-days has been made for the completion
of the site archive and production of a draft publication report,
with a further 8 days for final editing and drafting. Specialist
analyses of pottery, bone and environmental samples are addition-
al to this. The academic quality of the report will be refereed
by one or more CAT Directors (in this case probably Mick Aston
and Trevor Rowley).

there are now three concentrations of burials:

disposition of the burials allows the existence of a former
curvilinear boundary to the ecclesiastical centre postulated to
lie beneath the existing church to be reconstructed. There are a
number of intercutting burials in this area, including one on a
north-south alignment which is cut by several aligned east-west.

B: to the south-west of this area a collection of graves on
regular alignments and spacings has been found.

C: to the north of A a dense concentration of burials, including
a marked preponderance of juveniles, has been-located.

Project Statement applied. Cotswold Archaeological Trust has now
been contracted to excavate this new area, and the attached plan
shows the revised areas of excavation. Outside of this area, a

The matter of whether the site should be scheduled is obviously
one for Cadw: Welsh Historic Monuments to consider. However,
should the forthcoming Project Statement from the developer,
designed to ‘protect the site by record' (including the
commitment to publish in full the results of the fieldwork)
be umsatisfactory, I have no doubt that Cadw themselves
will wish to consider their own position with regard to
spot scheduling this rare and important site of national
significance under the criteria laid down in the 1979
legislation.

Our recommendations have consistently been for the developer to
adopt a mitigation strategy (ie, ‘preservation in situ’
which could have enabled the development without the need
for high archaeological costs); nevertheless, is has been
the developer's wish to proceed with ‘preservation by record’,
which in this case we accept as the second best option.

We remain very concerned about the quality of the work being
carried out by the Cotswold Archaeological Trust. This,
and related matters, may in due course be referred to the
Institute of Field Archaeologists. We trust that the work
will be fully resourced by the developer, including the
probable high cost of the post-excavation and publication
requirements which will undoubtedly follow the fieldwork
stages.

Whilst the work is being carried out on a so-called ‘voluntary
basis', because the site is a 'Dark Age' cemetery, no development
can take place until the site has been archaeologically
cleared to the highest professional standards. I have no
doubt that the developer is fully aware of this point, though
it would perhaps have been churlish for us to have pointed
this out at the meeting. We are grateful for the developer's
initiatives, though we wish to ensure that the site will
be fully and professionally excavated and with the full
resourcing of the ongoing post-excavation stages, which
will occur as the permitted development itself lawfully
proceeds.

Your council’s objective for this site - and that of GGAT - is one of
‘preservation by record’.

IHW has volunteered to carry out preservation by record and has appointed
CAT as its specialist archaeologist contractor for that purpose. CAT is well
qualified to carry out the work (and is a Member of the Institute of Field
Archaeologists) and IHW has fully resourced the project, at considerable
cost.

The work is being carried out on a voluntary basis having regard not only
to the field evaluation survey and archaeological assessment report
prepared by GGAT in August 1990, but also to the more extensive features
identified recently on site by CAT as part of its initial excavation. The
appropriate framework for such work in this circumstance is paragraph 31
of PPG16(Wales) Archaeology and Planning.

CAT has obtained (and renewed) a licence from the Home Secretary
authorising the disinterment of human remains found on the site. The work
is being carried out ‘with due reverence’, as required by the licence. The
licence does not expire until 20 July 1994, by which time the on-site
excavation work will have been completed.

In a spirit of cooperation, IHW will arrange for a statement of the work being
undertaken by CAT to be sent to you. This document will be provided for
information purposes only and will not be subject to approval either by the
council or GGAT. | hope to let you have the document as soon as it has
been formulated by IHW’s advisors and approved by IHW. The statement
will take account of the draft Brief prepared by GGAT and faxed to me on
3 May 1994, but will omit reference to any requirement for the work to be
approved or controlled by either your council or GGAT. Confirmation was
given that, in other respects, work on site is proceeding generally in
accordance with the content of the draft Brief and with accepted
archaeological practice for such excavations.

All present at the meeting agreed that interest in the site by the press and
other media would not be conducive to preservation by record, as evidenced
by events over the last weekend following the article in the South Wales
Echo on 29 April 1994. Any press releases to be issued by the interested
parties will be agreed between IHW and the council. In view of the recent
disturbances, my client will now be employing security at the site.
