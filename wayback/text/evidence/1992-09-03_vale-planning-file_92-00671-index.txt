KEY FACTS (page references are to the scans)

THE APPLICATION
Application 92/00671/RES was an application for approval of reserved matters, not a full planning application. Form D is ticked “Approval of reserved matters following the grant of outline permission” and names the outline permission as “Date 13 March 1990, Number 89/01396/OUT”. The applicant was Ideal Homes Wales; the agent was Wyn Thomas + Partners of Cardiff. The proposal: “Erection of 20 detached houses including garages, fences, sewers and all associated engineering works” at “Great House Farm, Leckwith Road, Llandough, Penarth”. The form is dated 14 May 1992. [EV-0127, pages 1–2]

THE DECISION
The council approved the reserved matters on 3 September 1992. The developer’s agent wrote on 4 February 1994 that the plans “were approved by your council under application no. 92/00671/RES dated 3 September 1992”, and the site location plan carries the stamp “Approved subject to compliance with conditions if any … 3 Sep 1992”. On 11 August 1994 the council’s Chief Planning and Development Officer wrote to the local MP that “The reserved matters application was approved (on behalf of Ideal Homes Wales) in September, 1992”. The reserved-matters decision notice itself is not in this file. [EV-0131 pages 24 and 29; EV-0129 page 1]

ARCHAEOLOGY CONDITIONS
The outline permission 89/01396/OUT of 13 March 1990 carried three archaeology conditions. Condition 16 gave access to an archaeologist nominated by the council (referred to in the file as the watching-brief condition). Condition 17 required that “no development shall take place until a field evaluation survey and archaeological assessment report has been carried out by a qualified archaeologist to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority”. Condition 18 required: “The detail plans to be submitted shall pay full regard to the results and findings of the archaeological investigations referred to in condition 17 above.” At reserved-matters stage, the agent recorded, “no additional archaeological conditions were imposed”. [EV-0201 page 3; EV-0126 page 3; EV-0131 pages 24–25, 38, 41]

THE 1990 EVALUATION
The Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust (GGAT) assessment of August 1990 was commissioned by the site owners, B P Properties Ltd, through their agents Debenham Tewson, after the demolition of Great House Farm in December 1988. GGAT stated that it “has throughout played a dual role; on the one hand it has advised the Local Planning Authority, and on the other provided comparable advice to the applicant”. Trench 4 “contained numerous human bones … Most of the bones appeared to be disarticulated … There was no evidence of associated graves.” The report identified “a zone of medium to high archaeological potential to the north of the churchyard”, and advised that the Disused Burial Grounds (Amendment) Act 1981 might apply and that “advice should be sought by BP Properties Ltd on any legal requirements which may pertain to the human skeletal remains”. A figure is captioned “St Dochdwy’s church and the former Ty-Mawr farm buildings”. [EV-0126 pages 3, 10, 12, 15, 17]

GGAT’S RESPONSE TO THE 1992 APPLICATION
On 27 July 1992 GGAT, as the council’s archaeological adviser, wrote that the 1990 evaluation “demonstrated conclusively that significant archaeological deposits exist within the proposed development area … Deposits of medieval and possibly earlier date have been confirmed, including a quantity of human bone”, with the highest potential “adjacent to the Church of St Dochdwy”. It recommended: “we would urge most strongly that the applicant should consider mitigatory measures to protect the known archaeological interests. This could include excavation in order to ‘preserve by record’ the archaeological potential. Such work should be carried out in advance of development in the areas of high archaeological sensitivity.” The approval followed 38 days later. Page 2 of this letter is not in the file. [EV-0131 page 4]

AFTER BUILDING STARTED
On 13 December 1993 GGAT’s Principal Archaeological Officer (Curatorial) wrote that “development has commenced on this site”, that he was not aware that conditions 16 and 18 had been fulfilled, and recommended “that a Breach of Condition notice is issued immediately on this development to protect the known fragile archaeological resource”. On 15 December 1993 he wrote that “condition 18 has not been fulfilled and cannot be discharged by your Authority” and that “should the development proceed unchecked, significant and rare Early Medieval archaeology, including human skeletal remains … will be needlessly or thoughtlessly destroyed”. [EV-0131 pages 1–2]

THE DEVELOPER’S POSITION AND THE COUNCIL’S
On 4 February 1994 the agent wrote that leading counsel had advised “that condition 18 has been discharged and that the approval of the detailed plans and the notice issued by your council are decisive as to that”. The agent’s note of a meeting on 4 May 1994 records that conditions 17 and 18 “have been discharged, as confirmed in your letters of 15 October 1990 … and 15 February 1994”, that “it is not your council’s intention that our client should stop work on site”, and that the excavation by Cotswold Archaeological Trust was being carried out “on a voluntary basis”. GGAT replied on 18 May 1994 that “because the site is a ‘Dark Age’ cemetery, no development can take place until the site has been archaeologically cleared to the highest professional standards”. [EV-0131 pages 25, 41–42, 39]

THE 1994 EXCAVATION
Cotswold Archaeological Trust’s preliminary report for Ideal Homes Wales (December 1994) states: “In March 1992 full planning permission was granted for residential development, with no conditions requiring further archaeological work” and “At an early stage in the excavation it became clear that the site contained numerous burials which continued beyond the bounds of the excavation area into the zone defined, as a result of the evaluation, as being of non-existent to low archaeological potential. Many of the burials lay only a few centimetres below the topsoil.” Work ceased on site in September 1994. [EV-0209 pages 4–5]

HOW THIS SITS WITH THE 2005 PUBLICATION
Holbrook and Thomas (Medieval Archaeology 49, 2005) repeat the preliminary report’s wording that full permission was granted in March 1992 “with no condition requiring further archaeological work”. The council file shows the permission was a reserved-matters approval of 3 September 1992 under the 1990 outline consent; no new archaeology condition was added at that stage, but outline conditions 16 to 18 applied, and GGAT advised in July 1992 and December 1993 that the archaeology required mitigation. The drawings submitted with the application are dated March 1992. [EV-0127, EV-0129, EV-0131, EV-0209]

NAMES AND PLACES IN THE FILE
Great House Farm appears throughout; the site was later named Church View Close (Ideal Homes, 22 June 1994). B P Properties Ltd and Debenham Tewson appear in the 1990 assessment and the agent’s 1994 letter. The file does not mention Western Ground Rents, title numbers WA231076 or WA240304, or the 1987 court case.
