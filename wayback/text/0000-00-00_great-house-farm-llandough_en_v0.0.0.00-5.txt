Subject: Great House Farm, Llandough
From: Requester

------------------------------------------------------------

Mr Wigley,

I am writing to make a *formal complaint to Cadw* concerning the handling
and substantive response to my correspondence regarding Great House Farm
(Tŷ Mawr), Llandough, and particularly Cadw's 1988 consideration of whether
the building should be protected by listing.

I am making this complaint directly to Cadw at this stage because I
consider that Cadw should be given a proper opportunity to review and
address the matters before any escalation to the wider Welsh Government
complaints process.

For the avoidance of doubt, I am *not making a further Freedom of
Information request* and I am not simply asking Cadw to produce records
which it has already said no longer exist. I accept that Cadw has stated
that all records it currently holds concerning the 1988 listing decision
have been released.

My complaint concerns what can properly be established from the surviving
evidence, whether the responses I have received have adequately addressed
that evidence, and whether the historical record can now be described
accurately where the underlying file has been lost.
1. The newly identified 1982 evidence

I have now identified a 1 October 1982 consultation letter from the
Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust concerning the East Vale Local Plan
and the proposed Ty Maw Farm site, identified as *H2.1*.

The letter states:

“Ty Maw Farm (H2.1) is designated as a three-acre residential site. As you
will see from the enclosed information the settlement at Llandough
developed considerably from farming and fishing... Recent excavations at
Llandough by the Trust revealed early occupation, part of the villa and
medieval grange buildings. There remains a possibility that occupation
continues to the north of the church; provision should be made, therefore,
for thorough investigative work prior to the development.”

I do not suggest that this document, by itself, establishes that the
standing farmhouse was required to be listed.

Its significance is narrower and more demonstrable: *six years before the
1988 demolition, the archaeological and planning system already contained
site-specific information identifying Ty Maw Farm as a proposed development
site and recording substantial archaeological sensitivity in the immediate
Llandough area, including possible continued occupation north of the
church.*

That materially affects the historical context in which Cadw's 1988
assessment needs to be understood.

I therefore ask Cadw to address:

   1.

   Whether this 1982 information was held by, available to, or capable of
   being obtained by Cadw in the period leading to the 1988 decision.
   2.

   Whether Cadw has any surviving evidence showing what archaeological,
   planning or historic-building information was available to the inspectors
   or decision-makers in 1988.
   3.

   Whether there was communication between Cadw and the relevant
   planning/archaeological authorities concerning the proposed development of
   Ty Maw Farm and the need for investigation or protection.
   4.

   If it is no longer possible to establish whether this evidence was
   actually considered, whether Cadw will expressly acknowledge that
   uncertainty rather than treating the absence of the historic file as
   evidence that the relevant information was absent.

2. The 1974 RCAHMW survey

I have previously drawn Cadw's attention to the 26 April 1974 Royal
Commission survey by H.J. Thomas at ST16817331.

That survey recorded Great House Farm as a historic building, including
features such as the late seventeenth-century character of the house, its
cross-passage arrangement, adze-worked beams, ogee stops and barrel-vaulted
cellar.

Cadw has stated that only two photographs from its relevant historic
material survive and that it cannot now establish whether the 1974 survey
was taken into account in the 1988 listing consideration.

I accept that this may now be incapable of being proved from the surviving
file.

However, there is an important distinction between:

*“Cadw cannot now establish whether the information was considered”*

and

*“there is no evidence that the information was available or relevant.”*

The surviving RCAHMW material demonstrates that professional
historic-building recording existed well before the 1988 decision.

I therefore ask Cadw to address specifically whether that material was
available to Cadw through the inter-agency heritage system at the time and,
if so, what can now be established about its availability.
3. Cadw's own evidence that listing was actively being considered in 1988

The surviving contemporary material indicates that in December 1988 Cadw
was actively considering emergency protection/spot-listing of Great House
Farm, including inspection and consultation concerning the building's
historic significance.

That is important because it demonstrates that this was not retrospectively
invented as a heritage issue decades later. Cadw itself was considering
protection immediately before the building was demolished.

Cadw's present position is that the historical listing file no longer
survives and that its retention arrangements at the time cannot now be
established with certainty.

I am not asking Cadw to speculate about what a destroyed file contained.

I am asking Cadw to distinguish clearly between:

   - what the surviving evidence positively establishes;
   - what information was demonstrably available by 1988;
   - what actions by Cadw in 1988 can still be established;
   - what cannot now be established because the historic record has been
   lost; and
   - any conclusions Cadw considers cannot responsibly be drawn.

That distinction is particularly important when assessing whether the
surviving record provides a sufficiently reliable account of what happened.
4. The planning authority and temporary protection

I also wish to clarify the position concerning temporary protection.

I am not suggesting that Cadw itself necessarily possessed the statutory
power to serve a Building Preservation Notice. I understand that such
protection was a matter for the relevant local planning authority under the
legislation then in force.

The question for Cadw is therefore whether, during the 1988
consideration, *Cadw
advised, discussed or communicated with the relevant planning authority
concerning any available temporary protection mechanism or the need to
prevent demolition pending consideration of listing.*

If Cadw cannot establish what was discussed because the records no longer
survive, that should be stated expressly.
5. The later description of Great House Farm as “a listed building”

I have also identified later archaeological/planning documentation in which
the former site is described in terms including:

“The site formerly contained Great House Farm (a listed building).”

I am not asserting that this wording conclusively proves that Great House
Farm was formally listed at that time.

I am asking Cadw to explain, insofar as the surviving evidence permits,
whether there is any known provenance for that description and whether Cadw
considers it accurate, erroneous, or simply impossible now to determine.

This is relevant because it illustrates the existence of potentially
conflicting historical accounts concerning the building's protection status.
6. The significance of the loss of the 1988 record

Cadw has understandably stated that the historic file cannot now be
produced.

However, the loss of that file creates a historical evidential limitation;
it should not automatically resolve questions arising from the surviving
evidence in Cadw's favour.

The appropriate conclusion may simply be that some aspects of the 1988
decision *cannot now be reconstructed with certainty*.

I would therefore like Cadw to consider whether its previous responses have
drawn that distinction sufficiently clearly.
7. What I am asking Cadw to do

I am asking Cadw to treat this as a formal complaint and provide a
substantive response addressing the following:

*A.* Whether the newly identified 1982 GGAT evidence materially changes
Cadw's understanding of the information environment surrounding Ty Maw Farm
before the 1988 demolition.

*B.* Whether Cadw can establish that the 1974 RCAHMW survey was available
to it in 1988.

*C.* Whether Cadw can establish what archaeological, planning and
historic-building information was available to its officers when the
building was being considered for protection.

*D.* What can now be established about Cadw's communications with the local
planning authority and other heritage bodies during the 1988 listing
consideration.

*E.* Whether temporary protection was discussed or advised upon and, if not
capable of being established, whether Cadw will state that plainly.

*F.* What can be established about the inspection of Great House Farm and
the ultimate listing decision.

*G.* Whether Cadw accepts that the loss of the historic file means that
some aspects of the 1988 decision can no longer be reconstructed reliably.

*H.* Whether Cadw considers the surviving documentary evidence, including
the 1974 survey and 1982 archaeological/planning evidence, should be
reflected in any relevant contemporary heritage record or historical
account.

*I.* Whether Cadw can explain the provenance, insofar as it is capable of
doing so, of the later description of the former site as containing “a
listed building.”

*J.* Whether Cadw considers that its previous responses have adequately
distinguished between the absence of surviving evidence and evidence that
something did not happen.
8. Scope of this complaint

I want to be clear that I am not asking Cadw to determine my family's wider
property or title claims, nor am I asking Cadw to reach a predetermined
conclusion about the historical ownership of the land.

Those matters are separate.

This complaint is confined to *Cadw's own heritage-related role, the 1988
protection/listing process, the information that can now be established as
having existed, and the adequacy and accuracy of Cadw's present explanation
of that history.*

I am also not asking Cadw to speculate about missing documents. On the
contrary, I ask that the absence of those documents be treated as an
evidential limitation and expressly identified as such.
9. Requested outcome

I would like Cadw to:

   1.

   Register this as a formal complaint and provide a complaint reference.
   2.

   Have the matter reviewed by an appropriate person who has not simply
   repeated the previous FOI position.
   3.

   Provide a point-by-point response to the matters above, taking account
   of the newly identified 1982 evidence.
   4.

   Correct or clarify any part of Cadw's previous correspondence that is
   found to have been incomplete, inaccurate or insufficiently qualified.
   5.

   State clearly which matters can now be established, which cannot, and
   which remain uncertain because the historical records no longer survive.

I regard that as a proportionate and reasonable opportunity for Cadw to
address the matter directly before I consider whether escalation through
the Welsh Government complaints process is necessary.
