Hi Sion,

Thank you for your detailed follow-up and for sharing more about your
background and the historical context surrounding Great House Farm. I can
see how deeply this case matters to you and your family, and it’s clear
that you’ve invested considerable time and research into gathering evidence
and preserving your family’s story.

While developments in AI and digital archives have certainly made it easier
to uncover historic material and cross-reference records, the legal
framework for reopening a judgment remains essentially unchanged. Courts
still apply the same very strict criteria under CPR 52.30 and the
Limitation Act 1980, and they would require evidence of fraud, deliberate
concealment, or procedural impropriety at the time of the original
proceedings — not just new access to historical material or
reinterpretation through technology.

Even with emerging evidence, after nearly forty years the doctrine of
finality in litigation makes it exceptionally difficult to revisit property
cases that have long been settled. The courts are unlikely to reopen such a
matter unless the new evidence conclusively proves that the original
judgment was obtained through deception or corruption.

That said, the historical and human side of your family’s story — including
the Marconi connection and the loss of the farm — is genuinely fascinating.
It may have cultural or documentary value even if it has no remaining legal
remedy. If you do decide to pursue a documentary or historical feature, I’d
be happy to keep in touch and follow any developments from a reporting
standpoint.

Thank you again for writing, and I wish you every success in your efforts
to document and preserve this important piece of Welsh history.

Best regards,
Tom Sinclair
