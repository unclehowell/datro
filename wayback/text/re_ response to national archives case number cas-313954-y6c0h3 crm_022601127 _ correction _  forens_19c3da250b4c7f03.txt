New Evidence
Since my last email, I have obtained a Forensic Evaluation and Assessment
of Restitutionary Claims (dated February 8, 2026) regarding the
dispossession of the Williams family from Great House Farm, Llandough. This
professional assessment provides additional context that may assist in
understanding the archival gaps previously identified.
The forensic report specifically examines:

   1. The "Mrs. Buckler" nomenclature: The analysis characterises the
   consistent use of "Mrs. Buckler" in court records and correspondence as
   "strategic misnaming" rather than clerical error, given that Mary Williams
   never adopted her husband's surname. This raises questions about whether
   The National Archives holds any records indicating her formal objection to
   this identification or her consistent use of "Mary Williams" in legal
   proceedings.
   2. The 1974 license letters: The report notes that letters dated
   October/November 1974 were addressed to a "non-existent legal persona,"
   facilitating a judicial finding of "silence as consent." I ask again
   whether The National Archives holds any corporate records from BP
   Properties Ltd or BP Pension Trust Ltd regarding this correspondence, or
   any indication that such records were considered for preservation.
   3. The 1974 adjournment: The forensic assessment questions why
   proceedings were adjourned part-heard on July 3, 1974, given your
   confirmation that principal judicial series contain no relevant material. I
   request clarification on whether any Cardiff County Court records from
   1974-1975 were transferred to The National Archives under subsequent
   protocols, or whether they remain with the court.

>> is against permanent preservation. In general, apart from small samples to
>> demonstrate the ordinary functioning of the courts/government departments,
>> records are only preserved if they bear witness to moments of particular
>> legal or policy significance or change. In the case of legal records, the
>> presumption has been that parties would be responsible for maintaining
