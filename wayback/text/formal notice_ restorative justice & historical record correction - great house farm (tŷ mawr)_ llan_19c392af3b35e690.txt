Williams family maintained de facto possession and a continuous indigenous
presence on the land from at least 1667.

*Official Narrative vs. Family Account:* While corporate records often

shifts the family's status from "occupiers" to "owners," positioning them

1888 – Williams Family Discover Roman Soldier

*The Discovery:* The Roman soldier in full armor was discovered under the
living room floor of Tŷ Mawr (Great House Farm) in 1888. This discovery
occurred while the Williams family was replacing the old stone flagstones
with a timber floor. This private find happened exactly one hundred years
before the house was demolished in 1988 and over a century before the
"official" 1994 excavations that claimed to first reveal the site's true
historical depth.

*Context of the Discovery:*

   - The Artifacts: The soldier was reportedly found in a stone-lined grave
   (cist) wearing what appeared to be bronze or iron armor, which aligns with
   later academic findings of a high-status Roman Villa and an early medieval
   cemetery on the same grounds.
   - The "Official" Narrative vs. Reality: While academic reports often
   focus on the "rescue" digs of 1978 and 1994, our family's 19th-century
   discovery serves as evidence that the historical significance of the site
   was known locally for generations.
   - Archaeological Significance: The burial was likely part of the
   Llandough Roman Villa estate, which was later used as a sacred monastic
   burial ground.

*Contested Suppression:* Physical evidence found by residents was dismissed
or "lost" until a developer's project forced a formal, taxpayer-funded
excavation. It is therefore well-documented in local lore and early
antiquarian records that Llandough was a Roman and early Christian hub. The
discovery of a "Roman soldier in full armor" (often associated with the
high-status burials of the Llandough Roman Villa) should have legally
triggered an immediate halt to any development.

*Suppression of Known Archaeological Significance:* The discovery of the
Roman soldier in 1888 by the Williams family served as "actual notice" of
the site's historical importance. If the authorities or developers later
claimed the site's significance was "unknown" until the 1978 excavations to
avoid preservation costs, this could constitute a fraudulent
misrepresentation to the planning office.

*Possible Conspiracy:* By ensuring the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust
(GGAT) "missed" the 1888 records, the developers ensured they could
demolish the site before anyone could declare it a protected heritage site.
This is a common tactic: "Demolish first, ask for forgiveness later."

*Contested Licence Letters:*

*Identity Fraud – "Mrs Buckler":* My grandmother, Mary Williams, was

*The Mechanism:* This fictional identity was not her title or lineage but a

accept a licence, thereby forfeiting any ownership claim. While my
grandmother's de facto, animus possidendi, and adverse possession claim to
the land could not transfer to her husband - His ineligibility and
potential forfeiture (due to intent to accept license) as a Buckler, rather
than a Williams—was effectively being fraudulently ascribed unto her.

*The Legal Reality:* This is not a legalistic strawman argument, it's just
a matter of fact that my late grandmother never inherited or adopted her
husband's surname. She also publicly rejected the licence offer and
consistently asserted the farm (at least home and garden) was hers.
Frederick's animus possidendi was legally dismissed as irrelevant (but due

*The Trap of "Unilateral Licence":*

Whereby unilaterally means to decide without consulting the other party.
Used a lot in the phrase, "unilateral disarmament".

The interest in a license, expressed by Mr Frederick Buckler before his
death in late 1960's, died with him. And so Mrs Williams was falsely

Furthermore the license was alleged to be accepted by her silence, as no
expressed rejection or acceptance occurred.

*The Act:* Addressing legal license letters to a "Mrs. Buckler" when your
grandmother was Mary Williams.

*The Intent:* By using her deceased husband's surname—which she did not

*The Trap:* If she replied, she would be acknowledging the name and, by

*The Injustice:* This wasn't a clerical error; it was a tactical erasure of

*Contested Framing:* The Use of the Term "Squatter": The legal proceedings

formal Land Registration Act processes over the historical context.

*Misrepresentation of Identity:* Just says "Mrs Mary Buckler, who was also
from time to time referred to as Mrs Williams", had conceived the notion

grandfather and that there were documents in existence which proved her

*The Decisive Inconsistency:* Notably, the appeal was dismissed on the

rejection of BP's asserted superior title would not have counted because
the rejection had been directed at BP Pension Trust Ltd—an entity we had
rejected for decades—rather than BP Properties Ltd, which the court treated
as a separate company. That distinction was held to be decisive and is the
stated reason the case was lost.

*Contested Legal Finding:* This finding stands notwithstanding that the
