INFECTIOUS DISEASES.—At a meeting of the Cardiff Rural Sanitary Authority on Wednesday, Dr. Prichard reported that during the past month there were four deaths from the infectious disease of scarlatina, two occurring in one house at Maindy, and two in Llancarfan parish. Since the last meeting 22 cases of infectious disease had been notified to him. Twenty were of scarlet sever, 1 typhoid of a very mild type in Llancarfan, and the other enteric fever at Wernddu Cottages, this last having been imported from Caerphilly. The cases of scarlet fever were distributed over a wide area, seven being in Llan- daff, two in Llanishen, seven in Penmark, two in St. Bride's-super-Ely, and two in Sully. There had been T no fresh cases reported from Maindy during the past three weeks, and he presumed that the disease was dying out an that village. The source of the outbreak at Penmark was not evident. A child living at Rhoose and .another at Tredogan Cottage were taken ill shortly after returning from Porthkerry day school. It was not clear how that could give rise to scarlet fever, as all the houses where the disease existed a short time ago were disinfected by the Board's in- spector. Some of the school-books, however, might have escaped being effectually disinfected, but it was almost impossible to do this work thoroughly without the aid of special apparatus.

    NEW BAPTIST CHAPEL FOR BARRY DOCK. On Monday afternoon an important epoch in the history of the Baptist cause at Cadoxton-Barry was marked by the opening of a new Baptist chapel at Holton. For some time services have been held from house to house, commencing on February 9th, 1890, at a private house in Richard-street, since which date the services have grown and the u audiences expanded, until it was felt desirable to erect a suitable place to worship in. A prosperous Sunday School is also in existence connected with the Church which, it is hoped further to develop. The new building, which is situated on the Holton- road, has a very neat appearance, and was erected at a cost of between £500 and £550. Towards this about jgll2 has been raised. The building will seat over 300 people, and has been erected by Mr. Menaton, from plans prepared by Messrs. Richards and Gethin. The opening services were held on Tuesday afternoon, when a sermon was preached by the Rev. Benjamin Thomas, of New- port, to a large congregation. A tea followed at the Welsh Baptist Chapel, kindly lent for the occasion. About <500 sat down, and they were in- defatigably waited upon by a number of friends, including Mesdames Westacott, Flux, Jones,Franks, and Newport, and the Rev. L. Ton Evans (pastor), Mr. Menaton, &c. A public meeting was held in the evening, when there was again a good attend- ance. Dr. Lloyd Edwards presided, and in his opening address congratulated the Church on the amount of enterprise shown and the success achieved, and wished them every prosperity in the future. Mr. Benjamin Edmonds and Mr. William Hart next gave the financial statement, and the pastor (Rev. L. Ton Evans) followed with a short but interesting address. Commencing, he referred to the small beginning the Church at Barry Dock had at the time Mr. Reed kindly lent them a building. Their friends at Canton, Cardiff, had been very kind in the matter of weekly supplies, as had also been brethren connected with the Bible Christian and Wesleyan bodies, and their own denomination in the neighbourhood. That branch at Holton had worked harmoniously with the mother Church at Cadoxton, and nothing had been done with which the Cadoxton Church had expressed the slightest disapproval. Before closing his remarks he would assure them that the work being spiritual and divine no methods or means would be adopted for the erasing of the debt that were not in har- mony with the Christianity they professed. They would not adopt bazaars, lotteries, Old English or or Japanese fairs, or the latest development in this district, the midnight dance, the latter of which necessitated an extension of time for the sale of intoxicants, the unholy profits of which, he con- sidered, should not be used for the maintenance of a holy religion. The Rev. W. J. Davies (Penarth), Rev. Ingli Jones (Ponthir), Rev. J. Honey, and Messrs. Green (Cardiff), Guy (superintendent of Sunday School, Penarth), D. Howells (senior deacon, Cadoxton), and other gentlsmen addressed the meeting, the addresses being interspersed with hymns tastefully rendered, under the conductor- ship of Mr. T. S. Williams (Cadoxton). At the conclusion the usual votes of thanks to the chair- man, speakers, ladies who had served the tea, and to the Welsh Baptist Chapel Authorities for the loan of the chapel, were carried unanimously..

    BARRY RAILWAYMEN'S GRIEVANCES. TO THE EDITOR OF THE SOUTH WALES STAR. SIR,—I noticed in your last week's issue an article on the Barry Railwaymen's Grievances, and your correspondent claims it a grievance to have to run our manager up the line. If we had nothing more to complain of than this I think we could be well satisfied..1 should like to know whether our officials are to get the permission of the men when they want a run up the line. I say, no they are quite at 'liberty to go whenever they please. And I am glad to tell you there are drivers that would willingly run our esteemed manager to any part of the line after completing their day's work without grumbling. I should like to ask your correspondent if our manager ever asked any of the men to run -him about without being paid for it. He also says that the passenger men have only one Saturday afternoon off in every three. There are mineral men who don't get one off in six. Thanking you for inserting this, I am, &c., ANOTHER OF THE MEN.
