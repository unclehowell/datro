    ABERGAVENNY. IMPROVEMENT COMMISSIONERS.—At the meeting of this board on Wednesday Mr. Bishop took the chair. A claim had been made by the Inland Revenue Department, for an amount of £134 3s. Id., being a surcharge of Income- tax extending over several years. The claim was made on the ground that the assessment of the commissioners' property was not on a correct basis. The board considered that, as the officers of the Inland Revenue had had every opportunity of inquiring into the assessment from time to time, no surcharge, if any were made, should apply to back years. Scarlet fever having broken out in the adjoining district, precautions had been taken by Dr. Steel, the medical officer, for preventing the carrying of the disease into the Abergavenny district. The board directed the notice board in the Maindu Mead, declaring the path by the river to be a public one, to be put up again, the board having been taken down by some person or per90ns and placed in an orchard adjoining. A complaint had been made of the annoyance caused to two persons, who were certified by medical men to be dangerously ill, by a steam roundabout and organ placed on private land adjoining Baker-street. The board expressed their sympathy and a desire to take any steps to prevent the nuisance, if it could be shown they had the power to do so. The water supply was stated to be 24 gallons per head per day of the population. The board decided to retain in their own hands the tolls of the markets for the coming year.

    LLANDOUGH. DEATH OF MR. THOMAS REES. — We regret to announce the death of the above-named gentle- man, which took place on Tuesday, at the advanced age of 89. Mr. Rees descended from an old Welsh family, who resided at Glynogwr. He removed from there to Llandough Farm, which ho rented from the Marquess of Bute, and which he held until his death, and we believe that he was one of the oldest tenants on the estate. He was an excellent farmer, a good neighbour, kind to the poor, his hand and heart being always ready to help them in the hour of need, a kind parent. and a faithful friend. He filled the office of churchwarden for upwards of forty years with fidelity, and in politics he was an uncompro- mising Conservative; and he was a man who never hesitated to express his opinion and speak his mind. He was a true specimen of a Welsh farmer of the olden time, who have nearly all passed away in South Wales, frank, upright, and outspoken, and without any guile. In Mr. Rees's death the link which connected the present to the past generation has been severed.

    THE MUNICIPAL ELECTIONS. The municipal elections for England and Wales to fill vacancies in the Town Councils caused by the retirement in rotation of one-third of their directly elected members took place on Monday. The elections this year were marked by two promi- nent features, the first being that the number of contests was far fewer than usual, and the second that the hours of polling in places where there were contests extended tc eight p.m. in all boroughs for the first time. Last year it was only the larger boroughs which enjoyed the extended hours, and it was an Act of 1835 which extendecllhe privilege to every borough in the kingdom. fin making any attempt to analyse the returns it bus to be borne in mind that in many boroughs the elections are dis- tinctly stated to have turned upon local or personal and not upon political considerations, and an ele- ment of complication has this year, for the first time, besn introduced, in the fact that the Unionist Liberals figure as a distinct factor in some of the contests. Additional interest attaches to the return in that one half of the aldermen retire by trienffial rotation on the ninth, and their re-election or otherwise largely depends upon the results now made known. At the elections of three years since, which may, because of this particular, be compared with those of to-day, the Conserva- tives scored a distinct gain; but the Liberals were still predominant in most of the Town Councils, especially the large boroughs, whiber,last year the Conservatives made extensive gains in the large boroughs, notably at Manchester. Salford, and Bootle. Owing to the lateness of the hour at which the poll closed, the result in several boroughs will not be declared until this (Tuesday) morning. An analysis of the returns up to mid- night showed that the Conservatives had slightly the advantage. Their most conspicuous successes were at Liverpool, where they gained five seats, one of them without a contest, and at Huddersfield, where five seats also were gained.
