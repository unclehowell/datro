    DARING ESCAPE FROM A POLICE CELL. A Warrington telegram dated Monday says :-A daring escape was made in the night from the Warrington police cells by Edward Wicks, a notorious cornerman, who bad been locked up on a charge of assaulting the police. Shortly before dusk it was discovered that the prisoner had escaped. He had wrenched a plank bed 12 feet long and weighing three cwt. from its fastenings, dragged it from a cell, and, rearing it against a 20 feet wali, managed to climb to the top and drop into the street on the other side, getting clear away. Later in the evening the police found his whereabouts and surrouuded the house in which he was taking refuge. All entrance was made, and Wicks was found inan attic, into which he had drawn the ladder by which he climbed there from t he trapdoor. He looked down on the police and detied them to take him. After some delay the police burst open the door with a pole. The prisoner then surrendered. The river Mersey runs past the bouse, and Wicks' friends bad a boat in readiness to convey him away after dark. He was' brought befoee the borough magistrates on Monday afternoon, and sentenced to three months' bard labour.
