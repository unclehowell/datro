Dear Disclosure & Library Team,

Thank you for your response.
I am requesting an internal review of FOI 250628005 on the basis that I do not consider the search scope and archival coverage to have been adequately evidenced in relation to historic case-linked material.

I note your confirmation that a search identified only the Court of Appeal judgment in BP Properties Ltd v [requester] [1987] and that no further departmental records were located.

However, the request also concerned:
Ministerial correspondence (including correspondence involving Alun Michael MP and the Lord Chancellor’s Department)
Internal departmental records relating to the case and associated enforcement/possession proceedings
Any related administrative handling or correspondence streams
Given that the matter proceeded through:
High Court proceedings
Court of Appeal (1987)
House of Lords refusal (1988)
and was subsequently the subject of an application to the European Commission of Human Rights (Application No. 14464/88, inadmissibility decision dated 14 April 1989)
it is reasonable to expect that some level of governmental or departmental engagement, case referencing, or archival correspondence indexing may have existed at the time, even if subsequently destroyed under retention policies.

1. Search adequacy and archival handling
Please confirm in the internal review:
Whether searches included legacy file series and archived departmental correspondence systems (not only current MoJ holdings)
Whether any cross-referencing was undertaken using case identifiers, party names, or linked litigation references
Whether record destruction policies were applied generally or whether any exceptions exist for litigation-linked ministerial correspondence

2. Retention and destruction assertion
The response states that relevant 1988 policy records “would have been destroyed” and refers to retention schedules (including ministerial correspondence retention periods).
For the purpose of review, please clarify:
Whether this conclusion is based on a file-by-file audit or a general retention policy assumption
Whether any retention exemptions applied to litigation-linked or European-level human rights correspondence arising from domestic proceedings

3. Reasonable expectation of trace records
Given the formal judicial and ECHR procedural history, I would expect at minimum the possibility of:
indexing records
correspondence registers
case tracking references
or archived departmental metadata even where full files no longer exist

If none exist, please confirm whether this conclusion is based on a structured archive search or policy inference.
I request that the internal review focuses specifically on whether the search methodology was sufficiently broad to capture archived or legacy case-related material, rather than only extant MoJ operational records.

Yours sincerely,
