FREEDOM OF INFORMATION ACT 2005 – INTERNAL REVIEW
1. I am writing in response to your email of 25 August 2026 in which you asked for an
internal review of MOD’s processing of your request for information under the Freedom of
Information Act 2000 (the Act) via whatdotheyknow.com1. The purpose of this review is to
consider whether the requirements of the Act have been fulfilled. Its scope is defined by
Part 5 of the Code of Practice2 under section 45 of the Act. I apologise for the delay in
responding to you.
2. In conducting my review of the handling of your request, I have focussed on the
following provisions in the Act.
a. Section 1(1)(a) which, subject to certain exclusions, gives any person
making a request for information to a public authority the entitlement to be
informed in writing by the public authority whether it holds information of the
description specified in the request;
b. Section 1(1)(b) which, subject to certain exemptions, creates an entitlement
to receive the information held by the public authority;
c. Section 10(1) which states that, subject to certain provisions allowing
extensions of time, the public authority must comply with the requirements
of section 1(1) promptly, and in any event not later than the twentieth
working day following the date of receipt; and
d. Section 16(1) which states that it is the duty of a public authority to provide
help and assistance, so far as it would be reasonable to expect the authority
to do so, to persons who propose to make, or have made, requests for
information to it.

4. Section 10(1) of the Act requires that you receive a response by no later than the
twentieth working day following date of receipt by the relevant public authority (MOD), which
in this case was by 2 June 2026. The Department wrote to you on 29 May 2026, and
therefore within statutory timescale. The response confirmed under section 1 of the Act that
no information in scope of your request was held. Additionally, the response neither
confirmed nor denied under section 23(5) (information provided by or relating to a security
body) whether any information in scope of your request was held relating to Special Forces
involvement in the incident. You were correctly informed of your right to appeal.
5. In summary, your request was handled in accordance with the Act.
Substance
6. As part of this review, I have considered your request again from first principles and
my findings are below.
No information held
7. Section 1(1) of the Act states that any person making a request for information to a
public authority is entitled to be informed in writing by the public authority whether it holds
information relevant to the request, and if so, to have that information communicated to
them, subject to any exemptions that may apply.
8. Searches for information within the scope of your request were conducted across the
Ministry of Defence archives using a comprehensive keyword search strategy designed to
capture all relevant records. In addition, targeted searches were carried out within the
pertinent areas of the Department, specifically the Army Secretariat and the
2
Counterterrorism and United Kingdom (CT&UK) branches, to address the Military Aid to the
Civil Authorities (MACA) element of your request. Despite these efforts, no information
matching the criteria of your request was located in any of these sources.
9. I am therefore satisfied that no information in scope of your request is held.
Use of Section 23(5) (information supplied by, or relating to, bodies dealing with
security matters)
10. Section 23(5) of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 states that the duty to confirm
or deny does not arise if, or to the extent that, compliance with section 1(1)(a) would involve
the disclosure of any information (whether or not already recorded) which was directly or
indirectly supplied to the public authority by, or relates to, any of the bodies specified in
subsection (3).
11. The Ministry of Defence neither confirms nor denies whether it holds information
relating to Special Forces involvement in the incident. In doing so, it has engaged the
‘neither confirm nor deny’ (NCND) provision under section 23(5). This is an absolute
exemption, meaning that requests for information falling within this category can be refused
without consideration of the public interest.
12. Given the direct reference to UK Special Forces in the wording of your request, the
Department is satisfied that the exemption at section 23(5) was correctly engaged in order
to honour the long-held British Government convention never to comment on or confirm the
activities of Special Forces. Notwithstanding the declaration above that the information
requested in relation to this request is not held, this response should not be taken as an
indication that any information relating to Special Forces within the scope of your request is
or is not held.
Request for personal information
13. I note that a part of your email of appeal you have requested personal information, in
particular at parts 1 and 5. I can advise that these elements of your appeal have been
raised as a subject access request and forwarded to the relevant area of the Department to
be processed under the Data Protection Act.
Conclusion
14. In summary, I find that:
a. Your request was not handled in accordance with the timeliness requirements of
the Act.
b. No information in scope of your request is held.
c. Section 23(5) information supplied by, or relating to, bodies dealing with security
matters) was correctly applied to your request.
d. Elements of your appeal have been taken forward by the Department to be
processed as a subject access request under the Data Protection Act.
