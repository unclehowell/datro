      How to read the board

      One square per record, in time order. Each row band is an era (oldest at the top, marked on the left like the ranks of a chessboard); within an era the squares read left to right, earliest first. The last band, Law and remedies, holds the statute and case-law squares, kept apart from the land timeline. Black and white alternate as on a real board, so the two records run side by side. Zoom out to see the whole board; zoom in to read a square; tap it for the full text and its source.

      Black squares ·

      Actions against Williams/Buckler ownership: sales, proceedings, orders, eviction, demolition, registrations, refusals
      Legal framing (grey text): possessor, proprietor, licence, “permissive” occupation, “whole farm”, adverse possession
      Omissions (white text): records missing, withheld, not held, not notified, cut or destroyed
      Records that cut against the case, shown honestly

      White squares ·

      Evidence of ownership, occupation and the grounds to reopen the case
      Heritage and lineage: the house, the Williams line, the Cedwyn estate
      Trees, woodland and the land the family worked

      The text inside the squares

      The whole text of the free archive edition of the Legal Wiki (version 0.1.0, 2,033 pages) and the public Great House Farm sites is spread across the squares: 9,652 extracts in time order. Black squares carry the counter narrative, the BP / authorities side drawn from the court record and official documents. White squares carry the Williams/Buckler record. Every square of a colour holds an equal share, to within one paragraph: about 4,800 characters in each black square and about 12,700 in each white square. Zoom right into a square to read its text in columns, or tap the square to read it in the side panel. Personal details are withheld, and the wording follows the board's house style. The verbatim originals are in the archive edition and on the Legal Wiki.

      The sudoku board

      Switch to Sudoku to see the same 808 squares in time order, poured into ten 9×9 sudoku grids. Each puzzle has exactly one solution. Each correct number lights the next squares of the story and opens a floating card with the next extract in time order, so finishing all ten puzzles unfolds the whole record. Dim cells are squares not yet reached. Your progress saves on this device. Reveal cell and Reveal puzzle are there if you'd rather read than play.
