Applicant, agent, owner and site
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Applicant: BP Properties Ltd, Britannic House, Moor Lane, London EC2Y 9BU. Agent: Debenham Tewson & Chinnocks, 3 Castle Street, Cardiff. Certificate A, signed for the agent “on behalf of BP Properties Ltd” on 16 Oct 1989: “nobody, except the applicant, was the owner of any part of the land to which the application relates” (EV-0123 p.3). Site: 3.4 acres at Great House Farm, Leckwith Road, Llandough; existing use “Agricultural”. The approved site plan (EV-0207 p.6) shows the red line around the land north of St Dochdwy’s churchyard, continuing down the east side of the churchyard; the Ordnance Survey base inside the red line is labelled “Ty Mawr Farm”. The form answers “None” to buildings to be demolished (EV-0123 p.2).

The farmhouse
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

GGAT: “At present our information on the Great House Farm area is limited to the records of the recently demolished property which was listed as a ‘county treasure’” (EV-0113 p.2). Officer’s report: “The site formerly contained a number of buildings including Great House Farm, but all buildings on the site have now been demolished”, and the Local Plan text “states that buildings on the site should be retained, however they were unfortunately demolished a little while ago” (EV-0125 pp.1–2). GGAT’s back cover photograph is captioned “St Dochdwy’s church and the former Ty-Mawr farm buildings” (EV-0113 p.5).

What GGAT advised
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

“In 1979 during the course of the Wales and West Housing Association Development to the south of the church, emergency archaeological excavation revealed evidence of high quality. In summary part of an Iron Age farmstead was located, together with a Roman villa of high status, a dark age cemetery, a medieval monastic grange belonging to Tewkesbury Abbey and a late medieval village and farm. It is important to note that there was no visible trace of the farmstead, villa, cemetery or grange prior to the commencement of development at this site” (EV-0113 p.2, §2.2). The churchyard “is almost certainly on the site of an early christian monastic foundation which may have been founded as early as 650 AD” (§2.1).

“We therefore recommend that the application should not be determined until BP Properties have provided an archaeological assessment report prepared by a qualified archaeologist” (§3.4). “Negotiations concerning archaeological assessment of this site were undertaken with BP Properties via their agents Debenham Tewson & Chinnocks, prior to the current application for planning permission. Unfortunately no agreement was reached although the Llandough Community Council offered £100 towards the cost of an evaluation. BP Properties are not strictu sensu the developers as they wish to sell on the land with outline planning permission” (EV-0113 p.4, §3.5).

GGAT’s appendix quotes its letter of 1 October 1982 to J Maitland Evans: “Ty Mawr Farm (H2.1) is designated as a three acre residential site. … There remains a possibility that occupation continues to the north of the church; provision should be made, therefore, for thorough investigative work prior to the development” (EV-0113 p.4).

Who else was consulted
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

From the officer’s report (EV-0125 pp.1–2): Llandough Community Council, no objection, “part of the land in question is registered village green”; Director of Engineering & Building Services, foul sewer only; National Rivers Authority and County Engineer, typed sheets attached (not in the file); Cardiff Bay Development Corporation, no objection, trees on the north-west slope and the public right of way to be kept; Welsh Water, no connection to the Eastern District pumping main. GGAT asked that its document “be conveyed to Members of Committee”; it went as Appendix A, and GGAT’s further comments of 8 Feb 1990 as Appendix B. Representations came from the Barry Preservation Society and Civic Trust and from “a local County Councillor (who is a member of the Glamorgan Gwent Archaeological Trust)” (Appendices C and D); the agents answered GGAT (Appendix E). Cadw does not appear in the file.

The archaeology conditions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The officer recommended approval with one archaeology condition, “the standard condition for this situation recommended by Appendix B to Welsh Office Circular 1/85” (EV-0125 p.2; draft condition “A132”, p.3). The decision notice of 13 March 1990, signed over the title Chief Executive, carries three (EV-0124 pp.2–3):

- **16.** “The developer shall afford access at all reasonable times to any archaeologist nominated by the Local Planning Authority, and shall allow him/her to observe the excavations and record items of interest and finds.” Reason: “To enable satisfactory archaeological investigation.”
- **17.** “No development shall take place until a field evaluation survey and archaeological assessment report has been carried out by a qualified archaeologist to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority. This work shall be carried out within three months of the date of this consent.” Reason: “To enable satisfactory archaeological investigation.”
- **18.** “The detail plans to be submitted shall pay full regard to the results and findings of the archaeological investigations referred to in condition 17 above.” Reason: “To ensure that the future development of the site has regard to the results of the archaeological investigations.”

The other conditions: 1–3 reserved matters and time limits; 4 no structures on the steep or wooded areas; 5 a 0.25 acre village green “adjacent to Leckwith Road and the boundary with St. Dochdwys Church”; 6–7 access and estate roads onto the A4055; 8 the public right of way on the north-western boundary; 9 landscaping; 10 foul and surface water; 11 floor levels; 12 parking; 13 garden space; 14 privacy distances; 15 the stone wall fronting the site to be “retained and made good”. The full text of all 18 is in the `transcripts <https://wayback.bucklerfamily.estate/text/evidence/1990-03-13_vale-planning-file_89-01396-out-transcripts.txt>`__.
