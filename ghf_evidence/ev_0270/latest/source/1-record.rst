**Quotation (verbatim):** “The woodland was sold to the LAW by BP Pension Fund which owned it until 1988” (SWE 26 Jun 1990). Seven woods “in Llandough and Leckwith from the Land Authority for Wales”: Reservoir Wood (Llandough), Coed Sion Hywel, Coed Cwm Cydfin, Coed Creigiaidd, Coed y Stofer, Factory Wood, West Hill Wood (SWE 11 Sep 1990). LAW advertised “Lewis Road Llandough … acres of Prime Residential Building Land For Sale by Private Treaty” (SWE 9 Nov 1989; OCR reads “488”, probably 4.88: UNCONFIRMED). 127 acres “between Leckwith Hill [and] Llandough including 18 acres of special scientific interest” to the county council (SWE 7 Nov 1994).

**Fits with:** Traces the WGR/BP estate residue around the farm after the 1987 judgment: BP Pension Fund → Intercontinental (Oct 1988) → LAW (Nov 1988) → South Glamorgan CC (1994–95). LAW also held Ynyston Farm, Leckwith, a former WGR farm let to W. S. Coles from 1934 (DBDT121/2). A LAW abstract of title would recite WGR’s root.

**Cuts against:** The 1988 buyer was a private joint venture (Helical Bar and Richard Morgan & Co) chosen by open tender from about 800 parties, which cuts against reading the sale as a concealed government transfer. The Great House Farm site itself stayed with BP Properties Ltd. LAW spent £9.5m on land across all of Wales in 1988–89, so Llandough was ordinary business. The press is inconsistent on whether the 1994–95 transfer was a gift or a sale. Snippets may splice neighbouring columns.
