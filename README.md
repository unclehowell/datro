# Great House Farm — Llandough / Bute Estate Title Research

## Branch: `greathousefarm`

This branch documents the research into the chain of title for **Great House Farm, Llandough, near Penarth** (formerly Ty Mawr), and the identified gaps in the Marquess of Bute's Glamorgan estate records.

---

## Background

Great House Farm at Llandough (near Penarth, Vale of Glamorgan) was originally a medieval monastic site, later a fortified manor house built c.1500–1530 by Sir Mathew Cradock. The property passed through the Herbert family (Earls of Pembroke), was purchased by the Earl of Bute in c.1790, and remained part of the Bute Glamorgan estate through the 19th and early 20th centuries.

The Williams family claimed continuous occupation from as early as 1667, though this is based on family oral tradition rather than surviving documentary proof. In February 1916, the Marquess of Bute granted a formal yearly agricultural tenancy of the farm to **John Williams**. In 1938, the reversion on that tenancy was sold to **Western Ground Rents Ltd**, which had itself purchased the Bute leasehold portfolio via **Mountjoy Ltd**. BP Pension Trust later acquired the interest, leading to the landmark case **BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2**.

The house was demolished in November 1988 following the Court of Appeal ruling, despite a 1,700-signature public petition and active Cadw listing consideration.



## The "Gaps" in the Bute Estate Records

The Bute Estate Records (held primarily at the **National Library of Wales, Aberystwyth** as GB 0210, with related material at the **Glamorgan Record Office** as GB 0214) contain critical documentary lacunae for the Llandough/Great House Farm area:

| Record Series | Coverage | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Manorial records (NLW GB 0210) | [?1373]–1847 | Nothing after 1847 |
| Glamorgan estate rentals | 1728–1893 | No rentals after 1893 |
| Cardiff estate rentals | 1842–1895 | Gap after 1895 |
| Estate correspondence | 1787–1910 (mainly 1817–1863) | Silent on post-1900 Llandough |
| Family settlements | [early 17th cent.]–1902 | None after 1902 |
| Family probate records | 1612–1900 | Nothing after 3rd Marquis's death |
| Debenham Tewson (GB 0214 DBDT) | 1849–1976 | Has Llandough St leases (1883) but nothing for 1916 tenancy |

**Conclusion:** The documentary gap runs approximately **1895–1938** for the Llandough/Great House Farm area. The February 1916 Williams tenancy falls squarely in the dead centre of this gap.

## Chain of Title (Reconstructed)

1. **Pre-1793:** Herbert family (Earls of Pembroke) → Llandough manor
2. **c.1790:** Lord Bute purchases Great House Farm
3. **1847–1900:** 3rd Marquis period (trustee administration 1848–1868)
4. **1900:** 3rd Marquis dies; estate divided between 4th Marquis and Colum
5. **1909–1924:** Systematic sale of Bute urban lands, collieries, coalfield farms
6. **1926:** Remaining property incorporated as Mountjoy Ltd
7. **Feb 1916:** Marquis grants yearly agricultural tenancy of Great House Farm to John Williams
8. **1938:** Reversion conveyed to Western Ground Rents Ltd
9. **1949:** New yearly tenancy to Frederick Buckler (Williams son-in-law)
10. **1953/55:** Tenancy terminated; adverse possession arguments begin
11. **1962:** Possession order (Judge Temple Morris QC) — never fully enforced
12. **1969:** Western Ground Rents sells to BP Pension Trust
13. **1974:** Unilateral licence letters sent; community petition (1,700 signatures)
14. **1983:** Mary Williams (née Buckler née Williams) dies
15. **1987:** Court of Appeal — BP wins; licence defeats adverse possession
16. **Nov 1988:** House demolished
