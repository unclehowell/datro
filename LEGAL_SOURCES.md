## Rules applied

- **FOIA internal review is not statutory.** The ICO's section 17 guidance: "There is no statutory requirement to offer an internal review under the FOIA", but authorities should have one under the s.45 Code. The ICO normally expects the authority to have been asked to look again before a complaint (s.50(2)(a) lets the regulator decline otherwise). Shown as *Expected*.
- **EIR regulation 11 is statutory and separate.** Representations within 40 working days of the failure; decision within 40 working days of receipt. Never merged with a FOIA review at the same authority.
- **ICO complaint timing is guidance.** "Within six weeks of receiving your final response or last substantive contact" is the ICO's request, not statute; labelled as guidance.
- **Tribunal:** 28 days after the decision notice is sent (GOV.UK guidance); late appeals need the tribunal's agreement. Never held back for other routes.
- **Personal data:** UK GDPR Art 15 (one month, up to two further months; clarification stops the clock; ICO guidance updated 16 Jul 2026 for the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025) is distinct from DPA 2018 Part 3 s.45 law-enforcement access (one month, s.54(2), extendable by two months under s.54(3A)) and from deceased patients' records under AHRA 1990 s.3 (40 days; 21 where all entries are recent).
- **HM Land Registry:** OC1 / OC2 / HC1 are records routes. The complaints procedure (two stages, then the ICR) is a service route; the ICR "has no power to review or overturn any of our decisions about land registration issues, or to comment on points of law". Registration challenges (rectification, indemnity, judicial review served on the Chief Land Registrar) are legal routes needing independent advice.
- **Court records:** CPR r.5.4C(1) gives a non-party statements of case and judgments or orders made in public; other documents need permission. Not FOI, not litigation disclosure.
- **Accountability routes** (MPs, MSs, councillors, scrutiny, standards, ombudsmen) are parallel and never a prerequisite.

## Evidence statuses

`drafted`, `sent`, `acknowledged`, `awaiting`, `clarification`, `disclosed`, `disclosed-partial`, `redacted`,
`refused`, `not-held-asserted`, `not-located`, `destruction-asserted`, `destruction-evidenced`,
`review-pending`, `complaint-pending`, `regulator-pending`, `tribunal-pending`, `court-pending`, `completed`.
"Never existed" is not a status: a failed search does not show it. A destruction statement stays attributed to the
institution until a disposal record or equivalent reliable evidence supports it. A search log is asked for where
relevant; its absence is not assumed to be a breach.

## Open legal points (not resolved here)

- FOIA s.50 and related provisions now read "Commission" (Information Commission) after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025; ICO pages still say "ICO". Labels use "Information Commission (ICO)".
- Public-interest extension under FOIA s.10(3): the Act says "reasonable"; no fixed maximum is stated in the provisions read.
- Each ombudsman's and regulator's remit and time limits (PSOW, PHSO, IOPC/PCC, Monitoring Officers) were not re-read on 22 Oct 2026 and must be checked before use.
- Limitation and judicial-review time limits are not computed here; they need independent legal advice.
- The roadmap's eleven-step layout (`public/data/roadmap.json`) is generated elsewhere; each step now carries its legal character in the UI, but the generator still emits one ordered list per lane.
