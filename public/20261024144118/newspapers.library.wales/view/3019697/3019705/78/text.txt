LLANDOUGH. ALLEGED FARMYARD THEFT.—Robert Williams, a mason's labourer, was on Tuesday charged (before Mr. John Duncan and Mr. J. S. Corbett) at the offices of the clerk to the Penarth magistrates, High-street, Cardiff, with stealing three geese, the property of Mr. John Williams, Great House Farm, Llandough, on November 11, 1891.-Sergeant Ferris, of the Cardiff Borough Police Force, deposed that he saw the prisoner early cn the morning in question in Clive- street, Grangetown, carrying the geese on his back. He at once started after Williams, who dropped the geese and got away.—Police-constable Morgan, of the county constabulary, said he arrested the prisoner when he was leaving Cardiff Gaol on Monday. The prisoner, when charged with stealing the geese, replied, All right.The prisoner was remanded until Monday at Penarth. LLANCARFAN. INFECTIOUS DISEASES.—At a meeting of the Cardiff Rural Sanitary Authority on Wednesday, Dr. Prichard reported that during the past month there were four deaths from the infectious disease of scarlatina, two occurring in one house at Maindy, and two in Llancarfan parish. Since the last meeting 22 cases of infectious disease had been notified to him. Twenty were of scarlet sever, 1 typhoid of a very mild type in Llancarfan, and the other enteric fever at Wernddu Cottages, this last having been imported from Caerphilly. The cases of scarlet fever were distributed over a wide area, seven being in Llan- daff, two in Llanishen, seven in Penmark, two in St. Bride's-super-Ely, and two in Sully. There had been T no fresh cases reported from Maindy during the past three weeks, and he presumed that the disease was dying out an that village. The source of the outbreak at Penmark was not evident. A child living at Rhoose and .another at Tredogan Cottage were taken ill shortly after returning from Porthkerry day school. It was not clear how that could give rise to scarlet fever, as all the houses where the disease existed a short time ago were disinfected by the Board's in- spector. Some of the school-books, however, might have escaped being effectually disinfected, but it was almost impossible to do this work thoroughly without the aid of special apparatus.
