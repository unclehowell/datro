What We Can Reconstruct / What Is Still Missing¶

What we can reconstruct¶

  1. CORROBORATED Cedwyn and Cydfin are two spellings used for the same brook and wooded dell on the Ely between Leckwith and Llandough (Baring-Gould & Fisher 1908; OS 2nd edition 1898–1908; Bute sale 1902).

  2. DOCUMENTED A farm called Cydwin in the manor of Llandough was let for 1,000 years from 20 June 1547 by Sir George Herbert of Swansea to Edmund Vaughan of Llandough, and the lease was assigned to Thomas Mathews of Llandaf in 1739/40 for £1066 13s. 4d. (NLW Lockwood L 11).

  3. DOCUMENTED The Bute archive holds leases of “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm (107 a.)” in Llandough from 1552 to 1824 (NLW Bute D 219).

  4. DOCUMENTED In 1806–07 Mark Wood of Llandaf Court exchanged “Ty Mawr Farm otherwise Ked Fen Farm” (126 a.) with the Marquess of Bute (NLW Lockwood D 1/104; Bute D205/6–7).

  5. DOCUMENTED In 1824 Bute’s surveyor called the farm “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm” (Glamorgan Archives DB/E/1).

  6. INFERENCE Cydwin, Cydfin, Ked Fen, Red Fen and Cedfin are one farm, Tŷ Mawr, and “Red Fen” is a misreading of “Ked Fen”.

  7. INFERENCE The farm passed Herbert (lessor) → Vaughan (lessee, 1547–1740) → Mathews (1740) → Wood (c. 1798) → Bute (1807), with its title deeds.

  8. LEGEND A giant, Cedwyn Gawr, lived at Ynys Cedwyn on the Tawe in Arthur’s time and was not killed by him (Peniarth MS 118, c. 1600). The legend belongs to Ynyscedwyn, not to Llandough.

  9. DOCUMENTED A saint, Cedwyn, son of Gwgon Gwron, is patron of Llangedwyn; a lost church of his, Lann Cetguinn, stood in Ystrad Yw (Book of Llandaf). His pedigree is in pre-Iolo manuscripts as well as in Iolo’s.
