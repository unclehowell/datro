The Title: From Herbert to Bute¶

The 1547 lease of Cydwin¶

NLW, Lockwood estate records, L 11 — “Assignment of a lease for 1000 years of Cydwin in the manor of Llandough”, 1739/40, Feb. 13

live catalogue, 20 October 2026.

DOCUMENTED (catalogue entry; the deed itself unread)

Assignment by (1) Edmond Vaughan of p. Llandough, co. Glam., gent., to (2) Thomas Mathews of Llandaf, co. Glam., esq., of a lease for 1000 years dated 20 June 1547 between George Herbert of Swansea, kt, and Edmund Vaughan of Llandough, yeoman, of a farm called Cydwin (fields named) in the manor of Llandough, co. Glam. Consideration: £1066 13s. 4d. Endorsed: receipt of 1 for £1066 13s. 4d.

What the entry establishes, and what it does not:

  • Parties and date. Lessor: Sir George Herbert of Swansea, knight. Lessee: Edmund Vaughan of Llandough, yeoman. Date: 20 June 1547. By 1545 Sir George Herbert held the manor of Llandough, “as appears by a rent-roll of that date” (J. Hobson Matthews (ed.), Cardiff Records, vol. 2 (1900), “The manors of Cardiff district”, British History Online).

  • Term. A lease for 1,000 years, so a leasehold term running to 20 June 2547, not a freehold. Earlier notes calling it a “1,000-year freehold” are corrected here.

  • The name. The catalogue spells the farm Cydwin. Whether the deed itself writes Cedwyn, Cydwin or another form is SOURCE PENDING: it needs a reading of L 11 at the National Library.

  • Rent, covenants, field names. Not in the catalogue; all SOURCE PENDING.

  • The original 1547 counterpart. Not separately catalogued; it is known only from the 1740 recital.

How Bute came to hold the Herbert lease’s farm¶

INFERENCE Read together, the catalogues give a chain:

  1. Sir George Herbert, lord of Llandough by 1545, leases Cydwin to the Vaughans for 1,000 years in 1547.

  2. The Vaughans hold it until 1739/40, when Edmond Vaughan assigns the lease to Thomas Mathews of Llandaf.

  3. The Mathews estate passes to Mark Wood of Llandaf Court around 1798, and with it this farm.

  4. In 1806–07 Wood exchanges “Ty Mawr Farm otherwise Ked Fen Farm” with the Marquess of Bute, who by then also owns the manor (bought 1793).

  5. The title deeds go with the land, which explains why the Bute archive holds leases of “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm” from 1552, decades before Bute owned anything at Llandough.

Steps 1, 2, 4 and the dates are documented. Step 3 and the identity of Cydwin with Ked Fen and Tŷ Mawr are inference. The test is to compare the field names in L 11 with the 1806 agreement, D219/1 and the 1824 plan.

Questions for a solicitor¶

Our case, prospective. Not a finding of any court.

  • Was the 1547 lease term still running in 1807, and did it pass to Bute in the exchange as a term of years, alongside the manor Bute had bought in 1793? If Bute then held both the term and the freehold reversion, did the two merge?

  • If any part of the 1,000-year term survived separately, could a statutory enlargement of a long lease into a freehold (Law of Property Act 1925, s. 153) ever have applied, given its conditions on the original length, the unexpired term and the rent?

  • None of this has been tested. The Cedwyn Estate Working Theory on the Legal Wiki sets out the wider hypothesis; this book records only what the documents show.
