TRAMPS AT LLANDOUGH. I Found Sleeping in a Lime Kiln I Five worn-out looking seamen, of no fixed abode, were placed in the dock at Penarth Police-court to-day (before Colonel Thornley and Mr. John Cory) charged with sleeping out. They were James Lockler, William Wright, Peter Hockenson, David Phillips, and Charles W.liams.—Police-congtable Charles Morgan (105), and Police-constable Jones found the defendants, in company with seven otihers, about midnight on Tuesday sleeping in Llandough lime kilns. One of the defendants told the bench that he had been out of work for over six weeks, and could not get the price of a night's lodg- ings. He had lost his discharge book when the eteam trawler, on board of which he was working, sank near Milford. Another of the party only came out of Merthyr Union Workhouse on the previous morning. He was suffering from a bulIt I wound, which, he said, he received whilst travelling in the train coming from Bryn- mawr. Colonel Thornley: But, surely, you were not looking for a ship in Llandough? (Laugh- ter). Defendant: No, sir, we were there for a. Bleep. Hockenson was sentenced to a week's imprisonment. He had been previously con- victed for begging. The other men were dis- charged with a, warning to clear out of the district.
