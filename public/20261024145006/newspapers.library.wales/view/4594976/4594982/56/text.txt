PENARTH POLICE COURT.

PENARTH POLICE COURT. MONDAY.—Before Mr James Ware (in the chair) and Colonel Guthrie. WARRANT IN AN AFFILIATION CASE.—Upon the application of Margaret Price, Cogan, a single woman, a warrant was ordered to be issued for the arrest of William Tuckett, engine driver on the Taff Vale Railway at Cardiff, for disobedience of a bastardy order. HEAVY FINK FOR DRUNKENNESS. — Patrick Culley and John Kingston, dock labourers, were charged by Mr Superintendent Wake with being drunk ana refusing to quit the Albion Hotel, Penarth, on the 2nd instant. Defendants having Eleaded, guilty, Mr Ware remarked that it would e better for them to apply their money to a good purpose rather than spend it in the payment of police lines. Kelly (who had repeatedly been con- victed for the same offence) was fined £ 2 and costs in default a month Kingston (who bad also been previously convicted for drunkenness) was ordered to pay a fine of £1 and costs, or undergo a month. STEALING APPLES AT LLANDOUGH. — Herbert Woollacott, a ragged youth from Cardiff, was brought up in custody charged with robbing an orchard at Llandough on the previous evening. P.C. Charles H. Morgan said that about five o'clock on Sunday afternoon last he was on duty in the neighbourhood of the orchard belonging to Mr John Williams, Great House Farm, Llan- dough, when he met prosecutor's son, who told him that prisoner had been stealing apples from his father's orchard. He consequently arrested the accused, and found a number of apples upon him. The stolen apples, he believed, were-worth about three-pence.—Mr Ware (to the prisoner): What have you got to say to this ?—Prisoner: Nothing, sir.—Mr Ware? Are you sorry?—Pri- soner Yes, sir.—Mr Ware Then you may go this time.—Colonel Guthrie But take care you don't appear here again.—Prisoner (who presented a sadly poverty-striken appearance) then left the dock manifestly grateful for the clemency of the Bench.
