Llandough-juxta-Barry: separate historical occurrences

Two separate historical sources use this or a closely related wording. They are recorded here as separate occurrences, not as proof of one place, holding or property.

2 occurrences, oldest first · ⓘ Compare the Penarth parish-unit entry

  1. “Llandough-juxta-Barry or Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge (Landogh)”

    3 Nov 1440 (writ 29 Dec 1439) secondary transcription

    Mapping the Medieval Countryside, E-CIPM 25-330, Isabel, Countess of Warwick; inquisition at Gloucester; TNA C 139/96/3 mm.45–46 and E 149/169/6 m.9. Read the transcription.

    The transcription describes a knight’s fee formerly held by the heirs of John de Wynton, valued at £20. It explicitly offers “Barry or Cowbridge”. The original membranes have not been inspected. No connection is established to the later Penarth parish or Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr.

  2. “Llandough-juxta-Barry”

    14 Dec 1922 catalogue

    The National Archives, Bute Glamorgan Estates catalogue, DB/17: Great Western Railway Co.; lands in St John, Cardiff and Llandough-juxta-Barry. Bute catalogue.

    Catalogue description only. The DB/17 file, plans and parcels have not been inspected or identified on the ground. The catalogue wording does not establish that this railway land was Great House Farm or that it is the same locality as the 1440 fee.

Not established as the same place or property

  • Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr beside St Dochdwy’s Church — No source links the 1440 fee or DB/17 railway land to the farmhouse or its parcels. See the separate Penarth parish record.
  • Llandough Juxta Penarth Parish (Vision of Britain unit 10484613) — The 1831 Vision of Britain alternate-name association is a distinct database entry. It does not prove that the 1440 holding or 1922 GWR lands are the Penarth parish or the farmhouse. Unit record.
  • Llandough Parish / Llandough Juxta Cowbridge (Vision of Britain unit 10484870) — A separate modern parish-unit record. The medieval transcription’s “Barry or Cowbridge” wording does not establish identity with that later unit. Unit record.

Research status: both entries require original-source inspection. The 1440 transcription and the 1922 archive catalogue are different evidential layers; neither is treated as a record of the farmhouse.
