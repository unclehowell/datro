Description :
The church of Llandough is on a site of considerable antiquity. Excavations in the area immediately to the N revealed a cemetery which may have started in the 4th century and lasted possibly into the 14th century (Thomas and Holbrook 1994); this seems to have originated in a Roman villa which was occupied from the 2nd to the 4th century and was itself a development from an Iron Age farmstead (Owen-John 1988).
It is identified as the monastery Sancti Docguinni of the Book of Llandaff (PRN77s; RCAHMW 1976, 17 no 828), and the elaborate Class E pillar cross of late 10th or early 11th century date which stands in the churchyard is certainly compatible with such a status (PRN 76s; RCAHMW 61-2 no 938). In 1173 the church was granted to Tewksbury Abbey (Crouch 1988, 28-31). In 1563 is described as a chapel with burial rights annexed to Leckwith (Prichard 1969, 42).
The present church was built in 1866 to replace the medieval church which stood immediately to its S, to the W of the churchyard cross. The medieval church was described by Glynne (1901, 258-9), who visited it in 1853, as consisting of nave and chancel with a W bellcote; it had by this time been almost wholly rebuilt in 1843 (ex inf incumbent) and it is the 1843 neo-Norman chancel arch, noted by Glynne, which was taken down from the old church and reset slightly heightened between the S aisle and the organ chamber (Thorne 1975, 34). Also preserved from the old church is an early Victorian royal arms and one grave slab, now in the floor of the organ chamber; there is also an 18th century mule chest, which was brought from Cogan Pill (ex inf incumbent).
Evans 1998 GGAT 51 Historic Churches Project
he present church was designed by by S C Fripp of Bristol and consists of nave, separate chancel, SE tower with saddleback roof, W porch and vestry. It has a polychrome interior. Remains of previous church noted in churchyard: Medieval/early 19th century church was on terrace to S of present church; site has been used for grave digging (Evans 1998).
Llandough Church rebuilt on two occasions during the nineteenth century, the first time about 1848, and again in 1865 when the old church was taken down and a new church was built to the east side of the former site. The Norman chancel arch with chevron moulding was removed from the original 12th century church and re-erected at the east end of the southern aisle of the rebuilt church of 1865-6 (Orrin, GR, 2004)
