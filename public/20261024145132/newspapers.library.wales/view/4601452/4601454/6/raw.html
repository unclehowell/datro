    The Man About Town. The political deluge is past; the rush of meetings and speeches has subsided, and other topics are beginning to show them- selves. Political delegates no longer inun- date the Welsh Metropolis, ftnd the Ark of the National l,iberal Federation has been removed to Parliament-street. The sub- sidence has left behind for me a little pile of letters from correspondents who are anxious to gain the public ear through a column which has known them not for one whole week The College authorities may be short of adequate buildings and the necessary funds wherewith to erect a magnificent structure worthy of the Centre of Learning in the Principality for that is what we must make Cardiff.bnt they can- not be charged with any want of foresight in making the best of their opportunities. They have managed to pilot two members of the Government over the Institution during the visit of the Federation, and if assistance is not received from the Imperial Government to aid a national movement working for the good of the nation, I do not think it will be the fault of the Premier-who adroitly parried Lord Aberdarc's direct innuendo-or of Mr James Bryce, the President of theBoard of Trade. The Principal, Mr Viriamu Jones, has set his heart on a fine building for the most important of the three constituent colleges of the University of Wale-an excellent augury for success. Though the town has m(1; rcaiaikable progress and its development has been phenomenal, we are still in the pioneer stages in secondary and higher education, a fact which should not be overlooked in taking a survey of Cardiff. The people and its public men are not absolutely and entirely absorbed in the race for wealth, as witness its system of elementary education, which is as efficient as any in the United Kingdom. When the new Free Library is opened Cardiff will be an excellent model for any or the larger and older towns to imitate in the provision of literature for its citizens. A new Museum is only a matter of two or three years. To complete the town's educational institutions is needed a worthy University College Building, and then Cardiff will bo the Athens of Wales," fit home for the Offices of the University of Wales, the National Museum, and those National Centres hinted at by Mr T. E. Ellis at the banquet on Thursday night.
