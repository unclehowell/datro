LLANDOUGH QUARRY FATALITY. An inquest on Ernest George Wood (27), of 18, Adamsdown-place, who died as the result of injuries sustained by a fall at the TJandough Quarry on the 12th inst., was held on Tuesday by the Cardiff Deputy Coroner (Mr F. N. Jones). Mr J. P. Hope appeared for the de- ceased's brother Mr T. H. Broomhall was for Messrs J. Turner and Sons, who employed de- ceased and Mr F. Gray (Inspector of Mines) was also present. Charles Frank Wood stated that he and his brother were working together at the quarry. They were on the first ledge, but witness left to get a bottle of water, and while away there was a fall of earth from the summit of the quarry, and his brother was carried to the bottom. Witness heard the earth fall, and a shout Look out for the man in the bottom." When the dust cleared he saw his brother in a sitting position with his head back. Subsequently deceased was re- moved to the top of the quarry, and was taken exactly an hour afterwards to the Infirmary. There was no one at the quarry able to render first, aid. If witness had been on the ledge he would have shared the same fate as his brother as the whole ledge was covered by the fall. Dr. Walford said deceased was unconscious on admission to the Infirmary. He died on the 16th from severe concussion, and probably laceration of the brain. Harry Taylor, quarryman, said he twice shouted to deceased to warn him. A piece of stone struck Wood's legS and knocked him jover the ledge. Deceased after the first warn- ing went out of danger, but returned, and "although witness shouted again deceased was too late to get clear. A verdict of Accidental death was re- turned, and the jury recommended that a box containing ambulance appliances should be kept at the quarry. Mr James Turner promised to see that the .recommendation was carried out.
