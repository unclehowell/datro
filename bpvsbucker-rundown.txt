# BP v. BUCKLER — Full Chronological Rundown
# Great House Farm, Llandough-juxta-Penarth, Vale of Glamorgan
# Compiled: 12 May 2026
# ======================================================================


## PART 1: CHRONOLOGICAL TITLE HISTORY

### Pre-1793 — Herbert Family Period
- The Herbert family (Earls of Pembroke) held the Manor of Llandough.
- Great House Farm (then known as Ty Mawr) was part of the Llandough
  estate. The original house was a medieval monastic site, later a
  fortified manor built c.1500–1530 by Sir Mathew Cradock.
- NOTE: An earlier date of 1215 was previously cited for construction
  but has been corrected to c.1500–1530 based on sourcing for Sir
  Mathew Cradock's building works.

### c.1790 — Purchase by Lord Bute
- John Stuart, Lord Mountstuart (later 1st Marquess of Bute), acquired
  Great House Farm as part of the broader Llandough estate.
- The purchase followed his 1766 marriage to Charlotte Windsor,
  heiress of the Lord Windsor/Plymouth estate. The Bute Glamorgan
  estates derive from this marriage settlement.
- The estate was confirmed purchased from the Herbert/Pembroke
  successors in 1793.

### 1847–1900 — 3rd Marquis Period
- The 3rd Marquess of Bute held the estate during a period of
  significant industrial development in the Cardiff area.
- Trustee administration was in place 1848–1868 (the 3rd Marquis was a
  minor during part of this period).
- Manorial records for the Manor of Llandough end in 1847.
- Estate rentals for Llandough end in 1893 (main Glamorgan rentals)
  and 1895 (Cardiff estate rentals).
- The 3rd Marquis died in 1900.

### 1900 — Death of 3rd Marquis; Estate Division
- The estate was divided between the 4th Marquess of Bute and Colum
  (a family member/trustee).
- Family settlements continue until 1902 (the last documented
  settlement in NLW GB 0210).
- Family probate records end with the 3rd Marquis's death in 1900.

### 1909–1924 — Systematic Sale of Bute Urban Lands
- The 4th Marquess systematically sold Bute urban lands, collieries,
  and coalfield farms across Glamorgan.
- This was a period of wholesale estate restructuring.
- No records survive for the Llandough/Great House Farm area during
  this period — the estate correspondence ends by 1910.

### 1916 — The Williams Tenancy (KEY EVENT)
- In February 1916, the 4th Marquess of Bute granted a formal yearly
  agricultural tenancy of Great House Farm to **John Williams**.
- This is documented ONLY in paragraph 36 of the 1987 Court of Appeal
  judgment (BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2).
- **No corresponding document exists in any public archive.**
- The tenancy was recorded as new by the estate at a time when the
  estate's own records for Llandough had effectively ceased:
  - Manorial records end 1847
  - Rentals end 1893/1895
  - Correspondence ends 1910
  - Settlements end 1902
  - Probate ends 1900
- Photographic evidence (People's Collection Wales, item #871416) shows
  "tree planting by John Williams (Grandfather) to prove land ownership
  at Great House Farm, Llandough" — critical primary evidence of
  customary tenure assertion c.1916.

### Pre-1916 — Daniel Thomas Equitable Title Arrangement
- Multiple sources (unitedtechnocracy.blogspot.com, family testimony)
  state that Daniel Thomas, a quarryman of Llandough, made an
  arrangement with the Williams family c.1895–1915.
- Quarrying rights were granted in exchange for eventual equitable
  title to Great House Farm.
- The "Deed of Transfer between Daniel Thomas and Bute Estate" was
  reportedly filed with a copy held at Cardiff Library.
- This document was reportedly removed from Cardiff Library in 1984.
- Daniel Thomas assignments of leases and requisitions of title are
  documented in the Glamorgan RO DRA collection (items DRA 418–420,
  dated 1906–1907).
- Miscellaneous Williams family deeds are in DRA 5/31 (Thomas Williams
  of Glôg, Llanwynno, 1753–1889).

### 1926 — Mountjoy Ltd Incorporation
- The remaining Bute property interests were incorporated as Mountjoy Ltd
  in 1926.
- Mountjoy Ltd became the vehicle through which the Bute leasehold
  portfolio was eventually transferred to Western Ground Rents Ltd.

### 1938 — Two Key Events

#### Event A: WGR Acquisition of Mountjoy Leasehold Interests
- Western Ground Rents Ltd (WGR) acquired Mountjoy Ltd's leasehold
  interests for £5 million in 1938.
- This is documented in the National Archives under GB 0214 DA.
- WGR thus became the leasehold reversioner for Great House Farm.

#### Event B: Stephenson & Alexander Sale (DSA/6/1158)
- An auction sale was held by Stephenson & Alexander, price £5,500.
- **THIS SALE RELATES TO LLANDOUGH-JUXTA-COWBRIDGE, NOT
  LLANDOUGH-JUXTA-PENARTH.**
- Cross-referencing with People's Collection Wales (#1168196,
  #1978491) and GENUKI confirms the DSA/6/1158 sale pertains to
  Great House Farm near Cowbridge Castle (the Cowbridge Llandough),
  NOT the Williams family's Great House Farm at Llandough-juxta-Penarth.
- This resolves Unresolved Question #2 from the original research.

### 1949 — New Tenancy to Frederick Buckler
- A new yearly tenancy was granted to **Frederick Buckler** (John Williams'
  son-in-law).
- This is documented in the BP v Buckler judgment.

### 1953/1955 — Tenancy Terminated
- The tenancy was terminated (the judgment states 2 February 1955 as
  the correct date).
- **NOTE:** An earlier version of the research cited October 1964 as the
  tenancy end date — this has NO basis in the court records and has
  been corrected.
- Adverse possession arguments begin from this point.

### 1962 — Possession Order
- A possession order was issued (Judge Temple Morris QC).
- This was **never fully enforced**.

### 1966 — Corrected Date for Bute Estate Acquisition
- The previous version of this research cited "1670" for the purchase of
  Llandough by "Lord Cardiff."
- This has been corrected to **1766**, the confirmed date of the marriage
  of John Stuart, Lord Mountstuart (later 1st Marquess of Bute) to
  Charlotte Windsor, heiress of the Lord Windsor/Plymouth estate.
- The earlier "1670" reference likely confuses either the Carne family's
  1536 acquisition of Llandough Castle or an unrelated Talbot family
  property transaction (Penrice and Margam).

### 1969 — WGR Sells to BP Pension Trust
- Western Ground Rents sold its interests (including the Great House
  Farm reversion) to BP Pension Trust.
- This period corresponds with the incorporation of Cardiff Ground
  Rents Limited (see Companies House #00598381, incorporated February
  1958) — possibly created to hold specific ground rent interests as a
  spin-off or parallel operation.

### 1974 — Community Petition
- Unilateral licence letters were sent to residents.
- A **1,700-signature public petition** was organised in opposition.

### 1976 — DCOWHS/2 Reference
- A reference in the DCOWHS/2 collection mentions "Mr David Thomas of
  Great House Farm" — evidence of ongoing Thomas family involvement in
  the estate area.

### 1982 — BP Properties Ltd Registered as Proprietor
- BP Properties Ltd was registered as proprietor of Great House Farm
  at HM Land Registry in **November 1982**.
- This is confirmed in the BP v Buckler judgment (paragraph 38).

### 1983 — Death of Mary Williams (née Buckler née Williams)
- Mary Williams died in 1983. She was the last Williams family member
  to have direct familial connection to John Williams (the original
  1916 tenant).

### 1984 — Cardiff Library Document Removal
- Key title documents relating to the Williams family's claim were
  reportedly **removed from Cardiff Library's local history section**.
- The fate of these documents is unknown.
- Among the reportedly removed items: the "Deed of Transfer between
  Daniel Thomas and Bute Estate" and possibly other Williams family
  conveyances.

### 1987 — Court of Appeal Judgment (BP Properties Ltd v Buckler)
- **[1987] EWCA Civ 2, decided 31 July 1987**
- Key paragraphs:
  - **¶36:** Confirms the February 1916 yearly agricultural tenancy to
    John Williams; confirms the 1938 conveyance of the reversion to
    Western Ground Rents Ltd.
  - **¶38:** Confirms Great House Farm at Llandough; BP Properties as
    registered proprietors since November 1982.
  - **¶39:** Dillon LJ's opening remarks on the adverse possession
    question.
- The court held that BP's licence defeated the Williams/Buckler
  family's claim to adverse possession.
- The house was occupied by the Buckler family at the time of the
  judgment.

### November 1988 — Demolition
- Despite a 1,700-signature public petition and active Cadw listing
  consideration, **Great House Farm was demolished in November 1988**.
- Cadw Inspector Janet Harris reportedly visited the site in December
  1988 (after demolition).
- The question of whether Cadw's emergency listing process was
  deliberately delayed to enable demolition remains an unresolved
  question (Unresolved Question #4).


## PART 2: DISCREPANCY NOTES (A–J)

### Discrepancy A: 1215 Construction Date
- **Previous claim:** Great House Farm was built in 1215.
- **Correction:** The house was built c.1500–1530 by Sir Mathew Cradock.
- The 1215 date appears to have no documentary basis and likely
  confuses the medieval monastic site origins with the actual fortified
  manor construction.

### Discrepancy B: "Lord Cardiff" 1670 Date
- **Previous claim:** Llandough was purchased by "Lord Cardiff" in 1670.
- **Correction:** The correct date is **1766**, when John Stuart, Lord
  Mountstuart married Charlotte Windsor. The 1670 reference may confuse
  the Carne family's 1536 acquisition of Llandough Castle or an
  unrelated Talbot family transaction.

### Discrepancy C: Mr Williams' Death Date (1935–39)
- **Previous claim:** Mr Williams died 1935–39.
- **Correction:** The court records show he was **alive through the late
  1940s** at least. The 1949 tenancy to Frederick Buckler (his
  son-in-law) is consistent with Williams having been alive in 1949.

### Discrepancy D: October 1964 Tenancy End Date
- **Previous claim:** The Williams/Buckler tenancy ended in October 1964.
- **Correction:** The court judgment states the tenancy ended on
  **2 February 1955**. No basis exists in court records for the 1964
  date.

### Discrepancy E: "Ideal Homes Wales Ltd" in WGR Description
- **Previous claim:** An earlier reference to "Ideal Homes Wales Ltd"
  appeared in descriptions of Western Ground Rents.
- **Correction:** This reference is **unsupported**. No evidence links
  "Ideal Homes Wales Ltd" to the WGR/Bute estate network.

### Discrepancy F: Two Llandoughs Conflation
- **Issue:** Archive references conflate Llandough-juxta-Penarth with
  Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge.
- **Resolution:** The Stephenson & Alexander sale DSA/6/1158 (£5,500)
  relates to **Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge**, not Llandough-juxta-Penarth.
  This resolves Unresolved Question #2 from the original research.

### Discrepancy G: Cardiff Ground Rents Ltd / WGR Relationship
- **Issue:** The relationship between Western Ground Rents Ltd and
  Cardiff Ground Rents Ltd (Companies House #00598381) was unclear.
- **Finding:** Cardiff Ground Rents Ltd was incorporated on 4 February
  1958, during the period when WGR was transferring/selling interests
  to BP Pension Trust (1969). It may have been created as a spin-off to
  hold specific ground rent interests in the Cardiff area.

### Discrepancy H: Fee Simple Absolute — Unresolved Basis
- **Issue:** Whether the Williams family ever held fee simple absolute
  title (freehold) is unclear.
- **Finding:** The claim rests on four interrelated propositions
  (1667 occupation, Daniel Thomas arrangement, 1916 tenancy, 1938
  reversion). The critical gap is 1895–1938. Without the missing deed
  documents, the legal status remains unproven.

### Discrepancy I: 1916 Tenancy — Sole Documentary Basis
- **Issue:** The 1916 tenancy exists ONLY in the BP v Buckler judgment
  (¶36). No original document has been located.
- **Finding:** NLW manorial records end 1847, rentals end 1893/1895,
  correspondence ends 1910, settlements end 1902. The 1916 tenancy
  falls in the dead centre of the archival gap.

### Discrepancy J: Daniel Thomas — Missing Deed Documents
- **Issue:** The "Deed of Transfer between Daniel Thomas and Bute
  Estate" and copies held at Cardiff Library were reportedly removed
  in 1984.
- **Finding:** The DRA collection at Glamorgan RO contains Thomas
  assignments (DRA 418–420, 1906–1907) and Williams family deeds
  (DRA 5/31). However, the crucial Cardiff Library deed copies are
  missing from that institution.


## PART 3: EVIDENCE ASSESSMENT

### Strongest Evidence
1. **BP v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 judgment** — The only legal document
   establishing the 1916 tenancy (¶36) and the chain of title from
   Bute → WGR → BP Pension Trust.
2. **People's Collection Wales #871416** — Tree planting photograph by
   John Williams c.1916 as customary tenure assertion.
3. **National Archives GB 0214 DA** — Documents WGR's £5m acquisition
   of Mountjoy leasehold interests (1938).
4. **Companies House records** — Cardiff Ground Rents Ltd and Western
   Estates Limited incorporation data and ongoing activity.

### Gaps Requiring Resolution
1. The original 1916 tenancy agreement.
2. The Daniel Thomas equitable title deed (reportedly removed from
   Cardiff Library in 1984).
3. The 1938 reversion conveyance to WGR.
4. On-site investigation of NLW GB 0210 E, EWIAMS, DGETHWIL deed
   collections.
5. Cadw listing files and Vale of Glamorgan planning archives for the
   1970–1988 period.
