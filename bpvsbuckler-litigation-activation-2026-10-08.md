## Current legal routes to test

### 1. 1955 excess/wrongful execution

Counsel must identify the actual cause of action, defendant, limitation regime, effect of any intervening adverse possession/registration, and whether any present declaratory, proprietary or damages remedy survives.

### 2. HMLR Schedule 4

If a current registered title wrongly includes the exact former Parcel-A woodland, the modern register-mistake route must be considered separately from the historical 1955 cause of action. HM Land Registry guidance recognises alteration/rectification for qualifying register mistakes, including wrongly included land.

### 3. HMLR Schedule 8

Preserve a separate indemnity route if a qualifying register mistake has caused loss and rectification is unavailable or inappropriate.

### 4. Protective notice / standstill

If the exact woodland polygon is identifiable and a legally protectable proprietary interest still exists, counsel should decide whether to lodge an HMLR unilateral notice and/or seek a voluntary standstill from Forest of Cardiff before any 2027 sale. This is a legal decision, not an automatic filing.

### 5. VG41 correction

If the original commons/green registration file reveals an actual statutory registration mistake, the Welsh Government's correction regime can be tested. The existence of that procedure is not evidence that VG41 was wrongly registered.

## Critical legal insight: 1955 may be both the wrong and the limitation start

The physical taking must not be treated as automatically establishing a recoverable present title. Under the historic Limitation Act 1939, where an entitled person was dispossessed, the right of action to recover land could accrue on dispossession, and the ordinary period for recovery by a non-Crown claimant was 12 years. The precise sections, interruptions, adverse-possession character and transitional rules must be checked by counsel against the actual facts.

Accordingly the 1955 evidence has two jobs:

1. establish whether WGR took Parcel-A woodland/VG41-area land without lawful entitlement or beyond the execution authority; and
2. establish when and how any adverse-possession limitation period began, changed, was interrupted, or became subject to an exception.

A present land claim therefore requires a separate answer to the limitation/discovery question. The project must not assume “wrongful taking in 1955 = title recoverable in 2026”.

The 1970 VG41 registration is especially important because it may identify who claimed ownership and what evidence was being relied upon about fifteen years after the taking. It may support or undermine different theories; its evidential result must be accepted either way.
